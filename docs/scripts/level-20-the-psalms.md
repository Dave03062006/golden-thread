# Level 20 script: *The Psalms*

**Bible:** the Psalms (8, 13, 16, 19, 2, 22, 23, 32, 42, 100, 103, 110, 118, 121, 122, 130, 133, 134, 150)
**Age:** VI · The Kingdom
**Length:** about 45 minutes (6 episodes, then the quiz, then reflection and prayer)
**Status:** draft for Dave's approval.

## How to read this script

- Each **scene** is a checkpoint. Its numbered lines are the narration beats; the player taps to move from one to the next.
- *On screen* describes what the scene shows. It's the brief for the art in both styles.
- **[Q]** marks an exact quotation from the Bible. Its wording in both translations is in the verse table at the end. Everything else is our own retelling.
- **Play** is the episode's interactive moment. **Thread** is its golden thread. **Read** is the passage the game sends the player to.
- "LORD" in capitals stands for God's holy name, printed in small capitals in the RSV.

### What this level is about

The Psalms are the prayer book of the Bible: 150 songs that God's people have sung for three thousand years, and still sing every day. Many were first sung by David, the shepherd boy who became king (Levels 18 and 19). This level doesn't tell one story. Instead, each episode visits a different kind of psalm, in the place where it might first have been sung:
- songs of **praise**, under the stars and in the Temple courts;
- **the shepherd's psalm**, on the hills of Bethlehem;
- **laments**, from a cave in the dark;
- songs of **repentance**, on the city wall, waiting for the dawn;
- songs of **the King who is to come**, at a coronation and on the Temple building site;
- and the **songs of the road**, sung by pilgrim families climbing up to Jerusalem.

The Psalms give words to every feeling: joy, fear, sorrow, guilt, longing and hope. Jesus prayed them all his life. He sang them at the Passover, quoted them in the Temple, and prayed them from the cross. He said that "everything written about me in... the psalms must be fulfilled."

**How it's shown:** Episode 3 begins with a short note. It quotes the psalm Jesus prayed on the cross; nothing is shown.

## Episode 1 · Songs of Praise

**Psalms 8, 19, 100 and 150 · about 7 minutes · 3 scenes**

### Scene 1.1 · The heavens are telling
*On screen:* Night on the hills of Bethlehem. Young David sits on a rock above his sleeping flock with his lyre, looking up at a sky crowded with stars. The moon rises over the hills.

1. Many of the Psalms were first sung by David, the shepherd boy who became king. Out on the hills at night, with his sheep asleep around him, he looked up.
2. **[Q] Psalm 8:1** "O LORD, our Lord, how majestic is thy name in all the earth! Thou whose glory above the heavens is chanted"
3. **[Q] Psalm 8:3** "When I look at thy heavens, the work of thy fingers, the moon and the stars which thou hast established;"
4. **[Q] Psalm 8:4** "what is man that thou art mindful of him, and the son of man that thou dost care for him?"
5. **[Q] Psalm 19:1** "The heavens are telling the glory of God; and the firmament proclaims his handiwork."

### Scene 1.2 · Make a joyful noise
*On screen:* Jerusalem, by the tent of the ark. Levites in white robes stand in rows with harps, lyres and cymbals; children stand among them, singing. King David leads them, his arms raised.

1. When David brought the ark of God to Jerusalem, he appointed Levites to sing and play before the LORD every day. The songs they sang became the Psalms, the songbook of God's people.
2. **[Q] Psalm 100:1** "Make a joyful noise to the LORD, all the lands!"
3. **[Q] Psalm 100:3** "Know that the LORD is God! It is he that made us, and we are his; we are his people, and the sheep of his pasture."
4. **[Q] Psalm 100:4** "Enter his gates with thanksgiving, and his courts with praise! Give thanks to him, bless his name!"
5. Even the children sang. A psalm says that God's glory is "chanted by the mouth of babes and infants."

### Scene 1.3 · Let everything that breathes
*On screen:* The Temple courts in full sunlight, crowded with worshipers. One by one the instruments join in: a ram's-horn trumpet, a lute and a harp, timbrels and dancers, strings and pipes, and great bronze cymbals. *(Play: praise him!)*

1. **[Q] Psalm 150:1** "Praise the LORD! Praise God in his sanctuary; praise him in his mighty firmament!"
2. *(Play: praise him!)*
3. **[Q] Psalm 150:3** "Praise him with trumpet sound; praise him with lute and harp!"
4. **[Q] Psalm 150:4** "Praise him with timbrel and dance; praise him with strings and pipe!"
5. **[Q] Psalm 150:5** "Praise him with sounding cymbals; praise him with loud clashing cymbals!"
6. **[Q] Psalm 150:6** "Let everything that breathes praise the LORD! Praise the LORD!"

### Play: praise him!
- **What it is:** rhythm (spec §13.7, kind 5: "psalms on the lyre").
- **How it plays:** a drum keeps the beat. On each beat the player taps, and the next instrument of Psalm 150 joins the song: the trumpet, the lute and harp, the timbrel and the dancers, the strings and pipe, and last the cymbals. When all five have joined, the whole crowd sings: "Let everything that breathes praise the LORD!"
- **Teaches:** that praise is for everyone and everything, and that God loves to be praised with music and joy.
- **Gentle mode:** any tap counts; the beat waits for the player.
- **Accessibility:** a pointer, or Space; the instruments are named as they join.

**Thread:** *"Chanted by the mouth of babes and infants" → the children in the Temple cry "Hosanna to the Son of David!"* The glowing object is the children singing among the Levites.
- **[Q] Psalm 8:2:** "by the mouth of babes and infants, thou hast founded a bulwark because of thy foes, to still the enemy and the avenger."
- **[Q] Matthew 21:15:** "But when the chief priests and the scribes saw the wonderful things that he did, and the children crying out in the temple, "Hosanna to the Son of David!" they were indignant;"
- **[Q] Matthew 21:16:** "and they said to him, "Do you hear what these are saying?" And Jesus said to them, "Yes; have you never read, 'Out of the mouth of babes and sucklings thou hast brought perfect praise'?""
- **What it shows:** when Jesus came into the Temple, the grown-ups argued, but the children understood and sang his praise. Jesus answered with Psalm 8: God's praise is perfect on the lips of children. No one is too small to praise God.
- **Source:** Matthew 21:14–16; Psalm 8; Catechism 2589, 2639.

**Where and when:**
- **150 psalms.** The book of Psalms is divided into five books, like the five books of Moses. It was gathered over many centuries, from David's time (about 1000 BC) to after the exile.
- **"Psalms."** From the Greek for songs sung to a harp. In Hebrew the book is called *Tehillim*, "praises."
- **David's singers.** David appointed Levites "to invoke, to thank, and to praise the LORD" with harps, lyres and cymbals (1 Chronicles 16:4–5). Some psalms are named after his singers, Asaph and the sons of Korah.
- **The Temple instruments.** The trumpet was the *shofar*, a ram's horn. The lute and harp were stringed instruments like the lyre.

**Read:** Psalm 8; Psalm 100; Psalm 150.

## Episode 2 · The Lord Is My Shepherd

**Psalm 23 · about 8 minutes · 3 scenes**

### Scene 2.1 · I shall not want
*On screen:* Dawn on the hills of Bethlehem. Young David leads his flock out of the fold, his staff in his hand and his sling at his belt. The sheep follow him, not driven but called.

1. David knew what a good shepherd does. He finds grass and water for his sheep, guides them on safe paths, and protects them from danger. So when he sang about God, he sang about a shepherd.
2. **[Q] Psalm 23:1** "The LORD is my shepherd, I shall not want;"
3. **[Q] Psalm 23:2** "he makes me lie down in green pastures. He leads me beside still waters;"

### Scene 2.2 · The valley of the shadow
*On screen:* A wide landscape of the Judean hills: green grass in the morning, a quiet pool, a winding path along a ridge, then a deep, shadowy ravine, and beyond it, in the evening light, a house with its door open. *(Play: paths of righteousness.)*

1. **[Q] Psalm 23:3** "he restores my soul. He leads me in paths of righteousness for his name's sake."
2. *(Play: paths of righteousness.)*
3. **[Q] Psalm 23:4** "Even though I walk through the valley of the shadow of death, I fear no evil; for thou art with me; thy rod and thy staff, they comfort me."

### Scene 2.3 · My cup overflows
*On screen:* Evening. The flock is safe in the fold. In the doorway of a lit house, a table is set with bread and a cup filled to overflowing; a lamp hangs over the door, and the door stands open.

1. **[Q] Psalm 23:5** "Thou preparest a table before me in the presence of my enemies; thou anointest my head with oil, my cup overflows."
2. **[Q] Psalm 23:6** "Surely goodness and mercy shall follow me all the days of my life; and I shall dwell in the house of the Lord for ever."
3. A thousand years later, Jesus took up David's song: "I am the good shepherd" (Level 18). And on the night before he died, he promised his friends a place in his Father's house.

### Play: paths of righteousness
- **What it is:** a courier route (spec §13.7, kinds 2 and 15).
- **How it plays:** the player leads David's flock through Psalm 23, stop by stop: the green pastures, the still waters, the right paths along the ridge, down into the dark valley (where the shepherd's staff glows and the sheep stay close), past the table, and home to the open door of the house. At each stop, the line of the psalm appears.
- **Teaches:** that God, our shepherd, is with us everywhere: in the good places and in the dark ones.
- **Gentle mode:** the flock follows the path on its own.
- **Accessibility:** a pointer, or the arrow keys; each stop is announced.

**Thread:** *"I shall dwell in the house of the Lord for ever" → "In my Father's house are many rooms… I go to prepare a place for you."* The glowing object is the open door of the house.
- **[Q] Psalm 23:6:** "Surely goodness and mercy shall follow me all the days of my life; and I shall dwell in the house of the Lord for ever."
- **[Q] John 14:2:** "In my Father's house are many rooms; if it were not so, would I have told you that I go to prepare a place for you?"
- **[Q] John 14:3:** "And when I go and prepare a place for you, I will come again and will take you to myself, that where I am you may be also."
- **What it shows:** the psalm ends at home, in God's house, for ever. Jesus, the Good Shepherd, promised that he would go ahead of us and prepare that home himself, and come back to bring us there. The table set in the psalm, and the overflowing cup, have long been read by the Church as signs of the Eucharist, the meal Jesus prepares for us on the way.
- **Source:** John 10:11–16; John 14:1–3; Revelation 7:17; Catechism 754, 1335, 2794.

**Where and when:**
- **The Judean hills.** Dry hills east of Bethlehem, green only after the winter rains. A shepherd had to know where the grass and the water were.
- **The valley of the shadow of death.** The hills are cut by deep, dark ravines, where wild animals and robbers could hide.
- **The rod and the staff.** The rod was a short club to drive off wolves; the staff was a long stick, sometimes with a crook, to guide the sheep and pull them out of trouble.
- **"Thou anointest my head with oil."** Shepherds rubbed olive oil on their sheep's heads to soothe cuts and keep flies away; hosts poured oil on an honored guest's head.

**Read:** Psalm 23; John 10:1–16.

## Episode 3 · Songs of Lament

**Psalms 13, 42 and 22 · about 8 minutes · 3 scenes**

> **A note before this episode:** This episode quotes the psalm Jesus prayed on the cross. Nothing is shown.

### Scene 3.1 · "How long, O LORD?"
*On screen:* Night, deep inside a cave. David, on the run from King Saul (Level 18), sits alone by a tiny fire, his head in his hands. As the play goes on, the fire grows, and light fills the cave. *(Play: how long, O LORD?)*

1. Not every psalm is happy. More psalms are laments than any other kind: prayers from people who are sad, afraid or hurting, telling God exactly how they feel.
2. **[Q] Psalm 13:1** "How long, O LORD? Wilt thou forget me for ever? How long wilt thou hide thy face from me?"
3. *(Play: how long, O LORD?)*
4. **[Q] Psalm 13:5** "But I have trusted in thy steadfast love; my heart shall rejoice in thy salvation."
5. Laments almost always turn, as this one does, from "How long?" to "But I have trusted." God can take our honest prayers, even our angry ones.

### Scene 3.2 · As a hart longs for flowing streams
*On screen:* A dry riverbed in the summer heat. A deer, thin and tired, searches along the cracked stones. Then, around a bend, a spring bubbles up, and the deer drinks.

1. **[Q] Psalm 42:1** "As a hart longs for flowing streams, so longs my soul for thee, O God."
2. **[Q] Psalm 42:2** "My soul thirsts for God, for the living God. When shall I come and behold the face of God?"
3. **[Q] Psalm 42:5** "Why are you cast down, O my soul, and why are you disquieted within me? Hope in God; for I shall again praise him, my help"

### Scene 3.3 · "My God, my God"
*On screen:* A bare hill outside a city at noon. The sky slowly darkens, and the sun is hidden. Nothing else is shown; then, at the end, the sun breaks out again, and its light reaches across the whole earth.

1. One psalm of David begins with the saddest cry in the Bible.
2. **[Q] Psalm 22:1** "My God, my God, why hast thou forsaken me? Why art thou so far from helping me, from the words of my groaning?"
3. It goes on to describe a man surrounded and mocked by his enemies, whose hands and feet are pierced, and who sees his clothes divided by the casting of lots.
4. But the psalm doesn't end in darkness. It ends in praise, with the whole world turning to God:
5. **[Q] Psalm 22:27** "All the ends of the earth shall remember and turn to the LORD; and all the families of the nations shall worship before him."

### Play: how long, O LORD?
- **What it is:** ordering (spec §13.7, kind 9).
- **How it plays:** the four parts of Psalm 13 are scattered on the cave floor like stones. The player puts them in the order the psalm prays them: the cry ("How long, O LORD?"), the plea ("Consider and answer me, O LORD my God"), the trust ("But I have trusted in thy steadfast love") and the praise ("I will sing to the LORD"). With each one, the fire grows brighter.
- **Teaches:** the shape of a lament. We can bring God our sorrow, and the psalms show us how to move from crying out to trusting him.
- **Gentle mode:** the next part glows.
- **Accessibility:** a pointer, or the arrow keys and Space.

**Thread:** *"My God, my God, why hast thou forsaken me?" → Jesus, on the cross, prays the first words of Psalm 22.* The glowing object is the sun breaking out again.
- **[Q] Psalm 22:1:** "My God, my God, why hast thou forsaken me? Why art thou so far from helping me, from the words of my groaning?"
- **[Q] Matthew 27:46:** "And about the ninth hour Jesus cried with a loud voice, "Eli, Eli, lama sabach-thani?" that is, "My God, my God, why hast thou forsaken me?""
- **What it shows:** on the cross, Jesus prayed the first line of this psalm aloud. He made our cry of loneliness his own, so that no one who prays it is ever alone. And everyone who knew the psalm knew how it ends: in victory, with "all the families of the nations" worshiping God. The cross was not the end.
- **Source:** Matthew 27:35–46; John 19:23–24; Psalm 22; Catechism 603, 2605.

**Where and when:**
- **The cave.** David hid in the caves of Adullam and En-gedi (Level 18). Two psalms are titled "when he was in the cave" (Psalms 57 and 142).
- **A hart.** A male deer. In the dry summer the streams of Judea dry up, and animals search far for water.
- **"Eli, Eli, lama sabachthani?"** Jesus prayed the psalm in his own language, Aramaic, as Matthew records.
- **Psalm 22 and the cross.** The Gospels quote it several times: the mocking crowd, the soldiers casting lots for Jesus' clothes, and his cry.

**Read:** Psalm 13; Psalm 42:1–5; Psalm 22.

## Episode 4 · Songs of Repentance

**Psalms 130, 32 and 103 · about 7 minutes · 3 scenes**

### Scene 4.1 · Out of the depths
*On screen:* Night on the walls of Jerusalem. A watchman paces the wall with a lamp; below him the city sleeps. Far to the east, the hills are black against a starry sky.

1. Some psalms are prayers of sorrow for sin. The Church calls seven of them the penitential psalms. Level 19 told of the greatest of them, Psalm 51.
2. **[Q] Psalm 130:1** "Out of the depths I cry to thee, O LORD!"
3. **[Q] Psalm 130:3** "If thou, O LORD, shouldst mark iniquities, Lord, who could stand?"
4. **[Q] Psalm 130:4** "But there is forgiveness with thee, that thou mayest be feared."

### Scene 4.2 · More than watchmen for the morning
*On screen:* The same wall, in the last hour of the night. The watchman looks east, waiting. First a gray light, then rose, then gold along the hills, and the sun rises. *(Play: watchmen for the morning.)*

1. **[Q] Psalm 130:5** "I wait for the LORD, my soul waits, and in his word I hope;"
2. *(Play: watchmen for the morning.)*
3. **[Q] Psalm 130:6** "my soul waits for the Lord more than watchmen for the morning, more than watchmen for the morning."
4. **[Q] Psalm 130:7** "O Israel, hope in the LORD! For with the LORD there is steadfast love, and with him is plenteous redemption."
5. **[Q] Psalm 130:8** "And he will redeem Israel from all his iniquities."

### Scene 4.3 · As far as the east is from the west
*On screen:* Morning over the whole land, from the sunrise in the east to the sea in the west. The sunlight sweeps across the hills, and the shadows run away to the west.

1. **[Q] Psalm 32:1** "Blessed is he whose transgression is forgiven, whose sin is covered."
2. **[Q] Psalm 103:8** "The LORD is merciful and gracious, slow to anger and abounding in steadfast love."
3. **[Q] Psalm 103:10** "He does not deal with us according to our sins, nor requite us according to our iniquities."
4. **[Q] Psalm 103:12** "as far as the east is from the west, so far does he remove our transgressions from us."
5. **[Q] Psalm 103:13** "As a father pities his children, so the LORD pities those who fear him."

### Play: watchmen for the morning
- **What it is:** timing, a quiet waiting play (spec §13.7, kind 16).
- **How it plays:** the watchman looks east into the dark. The player waits; three times the sky changes, gray, then rose, then gold, and each time the player taps to call it out from the wall: "The morning is coming!" A tap in the dark does nothing. The waits are long, on purpose.
- **Teaches:** that waiting for God is part of prayer, and that his mercy comes as surely as the morning.
- **Gentle mode:** the light waits for the player's tap.
- **Accessibility:** a pointer, or Space; each change of light is announced.

**Thread:** *"He will redeem Israel from all his iniquities" → "You shall call his name Jesus, for he will save his people from their sins."* The glowing object is the rising sun.
- **[Q] Psalm 130:8:** "And he will redeem Israel from all his iniquities."
- **[Q] Matthew 1:21:** "she will bear a son, and you shall call his name Jesus, for he will save his people from their sins.""
- **What it shows:** Psalm 130 waits in the dark for the one who will redeem Israel from all its sins. The angel told Joseph the name of that Redeemer: Jesus, which means "the LORD saves." He is the morning the watchmen waited for: "the dayspring from on high" (Luke 1:78).
- **Source:** Matthew 1:20–21; Luke 1:68–79; Catechism 430, 1846.

**Where and when:**
- **The seven penitential psalms.** Psalms 6, 32, 38, 51, 102, 130 and 143. The Church has prayed them together since the early centuries.
- ***De profundis.*** Psalm 130 is called from its first words in Latin, "Out of the depths." The Church prays it for the dead.
- **Watchmen.** Jerusalem's walls had watchmen through the night, who called out the hours and watched for danger and for the dawn.
- **East and west.** In the land of Israel, east is the desert and the sunrise; west is the Great Sea, where the sun sets. They never meet.

**Read:** Psalm 130; Psalm 32:1–5; Psalm 103.

## Episode 5 · The King Who Is to Come

**Psalms 2, 110, 118 and 16 · about 8 minutes · 3 scenes**

### Scene 5.1 · "You are my son"
*On screen:* The courtyard of the palace in Jerusalem. A young king of David's family kneels before the priest, who pours oil on his head and sets a crown on it. Trumpets sound, and the people shout. The king turns, and for a moment he is lit by a great light from above.

1. Some psalms were sung when a new king of David's family was crowned. But they promise far more than any ordinary king could be.
2. **[Q] Psalm 2:7** "I will tell of the decree of the LORD: He said to me, "You are my son, today I have begotten you."
3. **[Q] Psalm 110:1** "The LORD says to my lord: "Sit at my right hand, till I make your enemies your footstool.""
4. **[Q] Psalm 110:4** "The LORD has sworn and will not change his mind, "You are a priest for ever after the order of Melchizedek.""

### Scene 5.2 · The stone which the builders rejected
*On screen:* A building site on Mount Zion. Builders lift great stones with ropes and beams; one rough stone has been pushed aside into the dust. The arch of the gate stands unfinished, with one gap at the top, the head of the corner. *(Play: the head of the corner.)*

1. **[Q] Psalm 118:22** "The stone which the builders rejected has become the head of the corner."
2. *(Play: the head of the corner.)*
3. **[Q] Psalm 118:23** "This is the LORD's doing; it is marvelous in our eyes."
4. **[Q] Psalm 118:24** "This is the day which the LORD has made; let us rejoice and be glad in it."

### Scene 5.3 · Blessed be he who enters
*On screen:* The finished gate. A festival crowd streams through it, waving branches and singing. Inside, the Temple courts shine.

1. **[Q] Psalm 118:26** "Blessed be he who enters in the name of the LORD! We bless you from the house of the LORD."
2. Psalm 118 was sung at every Passover. On Palm Sunday, the crowds sang it again as Jesus rode into Jerusalem: "Hosanna!", which means "Save us!", and "Blessed is he who comes in the name of the Lord!"
3. **[Q] Psalm 16:10** "For thou dost not give me up to Sheol, or let thy godly one see the Pit."
4. **[Q] Psalm 16:11** "Thou dost show me the path of life; in thy presence there is fulness of joy, in thy right hand are pleasures for evermore."
5. After he rose from the dead, Jesus told his disciples that everything written about him "in the psalms" must be fulfilled.

### Play: the head of the corner
- **What it is:** aiming (spec §13.7, kind 4).
- **How it plays:** the builders' crane swings the rejected stone back and forth above the unfinished arch. The player lets go (tap, or Space) when it is over the gap at the top. Too early or too late, and it simply swings again. When it drops into place, the whole arch locks together, and light shines through the gate.
- **Teaches:** that the one people throw away can be the one God makes most important. Jesus was rejected, and God made him the cornerstone of everything.
- **Gentle mode:** the gap is wide, and the stone swings slowly.
- **Accessibility:** a pointer, or Space.

**Thread:** *"The stone which the builders rejected has become the head of the corner" → Jesus: "Have you never read in the scriptures…?"* The glowing object is the stone at the head of the corner.
- **[Q] Psalm 118:22:** "The stone which the builders rejected has become the head of the corner."
- **[Q] Matthew 21:42:** "Jesus said to them, "Have you never read in the scriptures: 'The very stone which the builders rejected has become the head of the corner; this was the Lord's doing, and it is marvelous in our eyes'?"
- **[Q] Acts 4:11:** "This is the stone which was rejected by you builders, but which has become the head of the corner."
- **What it shows:** Jesus quoted this psalm about himself, a few days before he was rejected and crucified. After the Resurrection, Peter told the same leaders: you rejected him, but God has made him the head of the corner. Everything the Church is, is built on him.
- **Source:** Matthew 21:42; Acts 4:10–12; 1 Peter 2:4–7; Ephesians 2:20; Catechism 756.

**Where and when:**
- **Royal psalms.** Psalms 2, 72, 89, 110 and others were sung for the kings of David's family. When the kingdom ended (Level 29), God's people kept singing them, waiting for the King they promised.
- **Melchizedek.** The priest-king of Salem who blessed Abraham with bread and wine (Level 3). Psalm 110 promises a king who will also be "a priest for ever."
- **The most quoted psalm.** The New Testament quotes Psalm 110 more than any other passage of the Old Testament.
- **The Hallel.** Psalms 113–118, sung at Passover. After the Last Supper, Jesus and his disciples "sang a hymn" (Matthew 26:30): very likely these psalms.

**Read:** Psalm 2; Psalm 110; Psalm 118:19–29.

## Episode 6 · Songs of the Road to Jerusalem

**Psalms 121, 122, 133 and 134 · about 7 minutes · 3 scenes**

### Scene 6.1 · I lift up my eyes to the hills
*On screen:* Spring. A pilgrim family walks on the dusty road with a crowd of others: a father, a mother, a grandmother on a donkey, and a boy of twelve. Ahead, the road climbs steeply into the hills toward Jerusalem.

1. Three times a year, families from all over the land went up to Jerusalem for the great feasts. On the long climb they sang the Songs of Ascents, fifteen short psalms for the road.
2. **[Q] Psalm 121:1** "I lift up my eyes to the hills. From whence does my help come?"
3. **[Q] Psalm 121:2** "My help comes from the LORD, who made heaven and earth."
4. **[Q] Psalm 121:3** "He will not let your foot be moved, he who keeps you will not slumber."
5. **[Q] Psalm 121:8** "The LORD will keep your going out and your coming in from this time forth and for evermore."

### Scene 6.2 · I was glad
*On screen:* The city on its hill, gold in the evening sun. The family reaches the gate, and climbs the great stairway up to the Temple, step after step, singing. *(Play: songs of ascents.)*

1. **[Q] Psalm 122:1** "I was glad when they said to me, "Let us go to the house of the LORD!""
2. *(Play: songs of ascents.)*
3. **[Q] Psalm 122:2** "Our feet have been standing within your gates, O Jerusalem!"
4. **[Q] Psalm 122:6** "Pray for the peace of Jerusalem! "May they prosper who love you!"

### Scene 6.3 · Who stand by night in the house of the LORD
*On screen:* The Temple courts at night, lit by great lamps. The pilgrim families sit together in the courts; the Levites on the steps lift up their hands and sing. The boy looks up at the Temple, wide-eyed.

1. **[Q] Psalm 133:1** "Behold, how good and pleasant it is when brothers dwell in unity!"
2. **[Q] Psalm 134:1** "Come, bless the LORD, all you servants of the LORD, who stand by night in the house of the LORD!"
3. **[Q] Psalm 134:2** "Lift up your hands to the holy place, and bless the LORD!"
4. **[Q] Psalm 134:3** "May the LORD bless you from Zion, he who made heaven and earth!"

### Play: songs of ascents
- **What it is:** ordering and memory (spec §13.7, kinds 9 and 14).
- **How it plays:** the family climbs the Temple stairway, and on each step the player chooses the next song of the road, in the order a pilgrim would sing them: from first seeing the hills, to arriving at the gates, to standing together in the courts, to blessing the LORD at night. The family climbs a step with each one.
- **Teaches:** that life with God is a journey toward his house, and that we make it together, singing.
- **Gentle mode:** the next song glows.
- **Accessibility:** a pointer, or the arrow keys and Space.

**Thread:** *"I was glad when they said to me, 'Let us go to the house of the LORD!'" → "His parents went to Jerusalem every year at the feast of the Passover."* The glowing object is the Temple gate.
- **[Q] Psalm 122:1:** "I was glad when they said to me, "Let us go to the house of the LORD!""
- **[Q] Luke 2:41:** "Now his parents went to Jerusalem every year at the feast of the Passover."
- **[Q] Luke 2:42:** "And when he was twelve years old, they went up according to custom;"
- **What it shows:** Mary and Joseph made this journey every year, and when Jesus was twelve, he went up with them, singing these same songs on the road. In the Temple, he called it "my Father's house." Every Sunday, Christians make the same journey on a small scale: "Let us go to the house of the LORD!"
- **Source:** Luke 2:41–51; Psalm 84; Hebrews 12:22–24; Catechism 534, 2179.

**Where and when:**
- **Three feasts.** God's law told every man of Israel to go up to the sanctuary three times a year: at Passover, at Pentecost and at the feast of Booths (Deuteronomy 16:16).
- **"Up" to Jerusalem.** Jerusalem stands about 750 meters above the sea. From Jericho, the road climbs more than a kilometer in thirty kilometers.
- **The Songs of Ascents.** Psalms 120 to 134. A Jewish tradition says the Levites sang them on the fifteen steps that led up into the Temple courts.
- **Brothers dwelling in unity.** On the feasts, families from every tribe camped together around the city.

**Read:** Psalms 121, 122, 133 and 134; Luke 2:41–52.
