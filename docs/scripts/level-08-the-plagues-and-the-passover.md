# Level 8 script: *The Plagues and the Passover*

**Bible:** Exodus 5–13
**Age:** III · Out of Egypt
**Length:** about 50 minutes (6 episodes, then the quiz, then reflection and prayer)
**Status:** draft, waiting for Dave's approval.

## How to read this script

- Each **scene** is a checkpoint. Its numbered lines are the narration beats; the player taps to move from one to the next.
- *On screen* describes what the scene shows. It's the brief for the art in both styles.
- **[Q]** marks an exact quotation from the Bible. Its wording in both translations is in the verse table at the end. Everything else is our own retelling.
- **Play** is the episode's interactive moment. **Thread** is its golden thread. **Read** is the passage the game sends the player to.
- "LORD" in capitals stands for God's holy name, printed in small capitals in the RSV.

### What this level is about

Moses and Aaron go to Pharaoh with God's word: "Let my people go." Pharaoh refuses, and makes the slaves' work harder still. But God has promised to bring his people out, and he keeps his promise. Nine times he sends a sign upon Egypt, and nine times Pharaoh hardens his heart. Then God tells his people to get ready: each family takes a lamb, marks its doorposts with the lamb's blood, and eats the meal dressed to travel. That night the LORD passes over the marked houses, and Pharaoh at last lets the people go. They walk out of Egypt by night, free, with God going before them in a pillar of cloud and fire.

God tells his people never to forget this night. Every year they keep the Passover, and tell their children what God did. Christians keep it too, in a new way: Jesus celebrated the Passover with his friends the night before he died, and he is himself the Lamb whose blood saves us. That is why at every Mass we hear, "Behold the Lamb of God."

The plagues are frightening, and the night of the Passover is a night of sorrow in Egypt. The level tells them gently, and shows children that God hears the cry of the oppressed, that no power on earth can stand against him, and that he always makes a way to save his people.

## Episode 1 · "Let My People Go"

**Exodus 5:1–5; 6:1–9; 7:1–13 · about 8 minutes · 3 scenes**

### Scene 1.1 · Before Pharaoh
*On screen:* Pharaoh's great hall, columns painted with gods and kings. Pharaoh on his throne, his officials around him. Moses, an old man with a shepherd's rod, and Aaron beside him, stand before the throne.

1. Moses and Aaron came back to Egypt, and went to the most powerful man in the world.
2. **[Q] Exodus 5:1** "Afterward Moses and Aaron went to Pharaoh and said, "Thus says the LORD, the God of Israel, 'Let my people go, that they may hold a feast to me in the wilderness.'""
3. **[Q] Exodus 5:2** "But Pharaoh said, "Who is the LORD, that I should heed his voice and let Israel go? I do not know the LORD, and moreover I will not let Israel go.""
4. **[Q] Exodus 5:4** "But the king of Egypt said to them, "Moses and Aaron, why do you take the people away from their work? Get to your burdens.""

### Scene 1.2 · "I am the LORD"
*On screen:* Outside, at evening, Moses alone, looking up. The first stars come out, and words of light fall from the sky like stars. *(Play: God's promises.)*

1. **[Q] Exodus 6:2** "And God said to Moses, "I am the LORD."
2. **[Q] Exodus 6:6** "Say therefore to the people of Israel, 'I am the LORD, and I will bring you out from under the burdens of the Egyptians, and I will deliver you from their bondage, and I will redeem you with an outstretched arm and with great acts of judgment,"
3. **[Q] Exodus 6:7** "and I will take you for my people, and I will be your God; and you shall know that I am the LORD your God, who has brought you out from under the burdens of the Egyptians."
4. *(Play: God's promises.)*
5. **[Q] Exodus 6:9** "Moses spoke thus to the people of Israel; but they did not listen to Moses, because of their broken spirit and their cruel bondage."

### Scene 1.3 · Aaron's rod
*On screen:* Pharaoh's hall again. Aaron casts down his rod and it becomes a serpent. Pharaoh's magicians, in leopard skins, cast down their rods, and theirs become serpents too. Then Aaron's serpent swallows theirs.

1. **[Q] Exodus 7:10** "So Moses and Aaron went to Pharaoh and did as the LORD commanded; Aaron cast down his rod before Pharaoh and his servants, and it became a serpent."
2. **[Q] Exodus 7:11** "Then Pharaoh summoned the wise men and the sorcerers; and they also, the magicians of Egypt, did the same by their secret arts."
3. **[Q] Exodus 7:12** "For every man cast down his rod, and they became serpents. But Aaron's rod swallowed up their rods."
4. **[Q] Exodus 7:13** "Still Pharaoh's heart was hardened, and he would not listen to them; as the LORD had said."

### Play: God's promises
- **What it is:** catching (spec §13.7, kind 13).
- **How it plays:** night over Egypt. God's promises fall from the sky like stars, one after another: "I will bring you out," "I will deliver you," "I will redeem you," "I will take you for my people," "I will be your God," "I will bring you into the land." The player moves Moses' open hands to catch each one as it falls (drag, or the arrow keys), and each promise caught shines and stays, until all six are held.
- **Teaches:** God's promises to his people, in his own words; that he keeps them.
- **Gentle mode:** the promises fall slowly, and Moses' hands move to meet them by themselves.
- **Accessibility:** a pointer (drag), the arrow keys, or Space to catch the next.

**Thread:** *"Let my people go" → Jesus: "If the Son makes you free, you will be free indeed."* The glowing object is the rod in Moses' hand.
- **[Q] John 8:34:** "Jesus answered them, "Truly, truly, I say to you, every one who commits sin is a slave to sin."
- **[Q] John 8:36:** "So if the Son makes you free, you will be free indeed."
- **What it shows:** God sent Moses to set his people free from slavery in Egypt. Jesus came to set all people free from a deeper slavery: sin, which keeps us from loving God and each other. He is the Son who makes us free.
- **Source:** John 8:31–36; Luke 4:18; Catechism 1741.

**Where and when:**
- **Pharaoh's power.** Pharaoh was thought of as a god himself, the son of the sun god. When he asks, "Who is the LORD?", he means that he answers to no one.
- **"A feast in the wilderness."** Moses first asks only for three days to worship God in the desert. Pharaoh will not give even that.
- **Moses and Aaron's ages.** Moses was eighty, and Aaron eighty-three (Exodus 7:7).

**Read:** Exodus 5:1–5; 6:2–9; 7:8–13.

## Episode 2 · Bricks Without Straw

**Exodus 5:6–23; 6:1 · about 7 minutes · 3 scenes**

### Scene 2.1 · No more straw
*On screen:* The brick pits at dawn. A taskmaster reads Pharaoh's order from a scroll. The Hebrews scatter across the harvested fields, bent over, gathering stubble. *(Play: gathering stubble.)*

1. **[Q] Exodus 5:6** "The same day Pharaoh commanded the taskmasters of the people and their foremen,"
2. **[Q] Exodus 5:7** ""You shall no longer give the people straw to make bricks, as heretofore; let them go and gather straw for themselves."
3. **[Q] Exodus 5:9** "Let heavier work be laid upon the men that they may labor at it and pay no regard to lying words.""
4. **[Q] Exodus 5:12** "So the people were scattered abroad throughout all the land of Egypt, to gather stubble for straw."
5. *(Play: gathering stubble.)*
6. **[Q] Exodus 5:13** "The taskmasters were urgent, saying, "Complete your work, your daily task, as when there was straw.""

### Scene 2.2 · The foremen cry out
*On screen:* The Hebrew foremen, tired and dusty, kneel before Pharaoh. He turns away. Outside, they meet Moses and Aaron, and turn on them angrily.

1. The foremen were punished, because the people could not make as many bricks as before.
2. **[Q] Exodus 5:15** "Then the foremen of the people of Israel came and cried to Pharaoh, "Why do you deal thus with your servants?"
3. **[Q] Exodus 5:17** "But he said, "You are idle, you are idle; therefore you say, 'Let us go and sacrifice to the LORD.'"
4. **[Q] Exodus 5:21** "and they said to them, "The LORD look upon you and judge, because you have made us offensive in the sight of Pharaoh and his servants, and have put a sword in their hand to kill us.""

### Scene 2.3 · "Why didst thou ever send me?"
*On screen:* Moses alone by the river at evening, his face lifted to the sky, his hands open.

1. **[Q] Exodus 5:22** "Then Moses turned again to the LORD and said, "O LORD, why hast thou done evil to this people? Why didst thou ever send me?"
2. **[Q] Exodus 5:23** "For since I came to Pharaoh to speak in thy name, he has done evil to this people, and thou hast not delivered thy people at all.""
3. **[Q] Exodus 6:1** "But the LORD said to Moses, "Now you shall see what I will do to Pharaoh; for with a strong hand he will send them out, yea, with a strong hand he will drive them out of his land.""
4. When things got worse, Moses did not give up. He took his question straight to God, and God answered him.

### Play: gathering stubble
- **What it is:** herding and gathering (spec §13.7, kind 2).
- **How it plays:** a harvested field under a hot sun, with stubble left in it here and there. The player gathers the stubble (tap each patch, or sweep across them) and carries it back to the brick pit. A tally shows the bricks the people can make with it, and beside it the number Pharaoh still demands, "the same number as before." However much is gathered, it is never enough.
- **Teaches:** how unjust the burden was; and that God sees it.
- **Gentle mode:** a sweep anywhere gathers the nearest patch.
- **Accessibility:** a pointer (tap or sweep), or Space to gather the next patch.

**Thread:** *"Let heavier work be laid upon the men" → Jesus: "Come to me, all who labor and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest."* The glowing object is Moses' open hands as he prays.
- **[Q] Matthew 11:28:** "Come to me, all who labor and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest."
- **[Q] Matthew 11:29:** "Take my yoke upon you, and learn from me; for I am gentle and lowly in heart, and you will find rest for your souls."
- **What it shows:** Pharaoh made the burdens heavier and heavier. Jesus does the opposite: he calls everyone who is tired and weighed down, and gives them rest. Whatever is heavy for us, we can bring to him.
- **Source:** Matthew 11:28–30; Catechism 1658, 2612.

**Where and when:**
- **Straw in bricks.** Chopped straw mixed into the mud held a brick together as it dried, and kept it from cracking. Without it, the work was slower and harder.
- **Foremen.** Hebrews set over the other Hebrew workers. They were punished when the count of bricks was short.
- **Stubble.** The short stalks left in a field after the harvest. Gathering enough of it for a day's bricks took hours.

**Read:** Exodus 5:6–6:1.

## Episode 3 · The First Nine Plagues

**Exodus 7:14–10:29 · about 10 minutes · 4 scenes**

> **Content note shown before this episode:** "In this episode God sends nine plagues on Egypt: the river turns to blood, and frogs, insects, sickness, hail, locusts and darkness come upon the land. They are told gently."

### Scene 3.1 · The river turns to blood
*On screen:* Morning on the bank of the Nile. Pharaoh comes down to the water with his servants. Moses holds out his rod over the river, and the water turns deep red from bank to bank.

1. **[Q] Exodus 7:16** "And you shall say to him, 'The LORD, the God of the Hebrews, sent me to you, saying, "Let my people go, that they may serve me in the wilderness; and behold, you have not yet obeyed.""
2. **[Q] Exodus 7:20** "Moses and Aaron did as the LORD commanded; in the sight of Pharaoh and in the sight of his servants, he lifted up the rod and struck the water that was in the Nile, and all the water that was in the Nile turned to blood."
3. **[Q] Exodus 7:21** "And the fish in the Nile died; and the Nile became foul, so that the Egyptians could not drink water from the Nile; and there was blood throughout all the land of Egypt."
4. **[Q] Exodus 7:23** "Pharaoh turned and went into his house, and he did not lay even this to heart."

### Scene 3.2 · Frogs, gnats and flies
*On screen:* Frogs everywhere: on the palace steps, in the courtyard, in the kneading bowls. Then a cloud of tiny gnats, then swarms of flies, over Egypt, but not over the green fields of Goshen, where the Hebrews live.

1. Then God sent frogs. They came up out of the river and filled the land: the houses, the beds, even the ovens.
2. **[Q] Exodus 8:6** "So Aaron stretched out his hand over the waters of Egypt; and the frogs came up and covered the land of Egypt."
3. Then the dust became gnats. Pharaoh's magicians tried to do the same, and could not.
4. **[Q] Exodus 8:19** "And the magicians said to Pharaoh, "This is the finger of God." But Pharaoh's heart was hardened, and he would not listen to them; as the LORD had said."
5. **[Q] Exodus 8:22** "But on that day I will set apart the land of Goshen, where my people dwell, so that no swarms of flies shall be there; that you may know that I am the LORD in the midst of the earth."

### Scene 3.3 · Sickness, hail, locusts and darkness
*On screen:* The plagues one after another: the Egyptians' cattle lying down sick; sores; a storm of hail and fire; a cloud of locusts darkening the sky; then darkness over Egypt, and light in the Hebrews' houses. *(Play: the plagues in order.)*

1. Again and again, Pharaoh promised to let the people go, and again and again he hardened his heart. The cattle of Egypt fell sick; boils broke out on people and animals; a storm of hail and fire flattened the fields; locusts ate every green thing that was left.
2. *(Play: the plagues in order.)*
3. **[Q] Exodus 10:22** "So Moses stretched out his hand toward heaven, and there was thick darkness in all the land of Egypt three days;"
4. **[Q] Exodus 10:23** "they did not see one another, nor did any rise from his place for three days; but all the people of Israel had light where they dwelt."

### Scene 3.4 · A hardened heart
*On screen:* Pharaoh in his darkened hall, a single lamp beside him. Moses before him. Pharaoh points to the door.

1. **[Q] Exodus 9:27** "Then Pharaoh sent, and called Moses and Aaron, and said to them, "I have sinned this time; the LORD is in the right, and I and my people are in the wrong."
2. But each time the plague stopped, Pharaoh changed his mind. His heart grew harder and harder.
3. **[Q] Exodus 10:28** "Then Pharaoh said to him, "Get away from me; take heed to yourself; never see my face again; for in the day you see my face you shall die.""
4. **[Q] Exodus 10:29** "Moses said, "As you say! I will not see your face again.""

### Play: the plagues in order
- **What it is:** ordering (spec §13.7, kind 9).
- **How it plays:** the nine plagues are shown as pictures scattered across a scroll: blood, frogs, gnats, flies, the sick cattle, boils, hail, locusts, darkness. The player puts them in the order they came, choosing the next one each time from three (tap, or the arrow keys and Space). Each one chosen takes its place along the scroll, with a word about it; a wrong choice gets a gentle "not yet." When all nine are in place, the scroll rolls up.
- **Teaches:** the order of the plagues; that God gave Pharaoh chance after chance.
- **Gentle mode:** the right plague glows among the choices.
- **Accessibility:** a pointer, or the arrow keys and Space.

**Thread:** *Moses' first sign turns the water of the Nile into blood → Jesus' first sign turns water into wine.* The glowing object is the river.
- **[Q] John 2:9:** "When the steward of the feast tasted the water now become wine, and did not know where it came from (though the servants who had drawn the water knew), the steward of the feast called the bridegroom"
- **[Q] John 2:11:** "This, the first of his signs, Jesus did at Cana in Galilee, and manifested his glory; and his disciples believed in him."
- **What it shows:** Moses' first great sign in Egypt turned water into blood, and brought death to the river. Jesus' first sign, at a wedding in Cana, turned water into wine, and brought joy. The signs of Moses showed God's judgment on Pharaoh; the signs of Jesus show God's life and joy for everyone.
- **Source:** John 2:1–11; Catechism 1335, 2618.

**Where and when:**
- **The gods of Egypt.** The Egyptians worshiped the Nile, the frog, the cattle, the sun. Each plague showed that the LORD is greater than all of them (Exodus 12:12).
- **"The finger of God."** Jesus later uses the same words: he says he casts out demons "by the finger of God" (Luke 11:20).
- **Goshen spared.** From the plague of flies on, Exodus often notes that Goshen, where the Hebrews lived, was spared: from the flies, the sickness of the cattle, the hail and the darkness.

**Read:** Exodus 7:14–24; 8:16–24; 10:21–29.

## Episode 4 · The Passover Lamb

**Exodus 11:1–12:28 · about 9 minutes · 4 scenes**

### Scene 4.1 · One plague more
*On screen:* Moses among the Hebrew elders at evening in Goshen. He raises his rod as he speaks; their faces are grave and hopeful.

1. **[Q] Exodus 11:1** "The LORD said to Moses, "Yet one plague more I will bring upon Pharaoh and upon Egypt; afterwards he will let you go hence; when he lets you go, he will drive you away completely."
2. God warned that at midnight the firstborn in every house of Egypt would die, from Pharaoh's own son to the son of the poorest servant. But his own people would be kept safe.
3. **[Q] Exodus 11:7** "But against any of the people of Israel, either man or beast, not a dog shall growl; that you may know that the LORD makes a distinction between the Egyptians and Israel."

### Scene 4.2 · A lamb for a household
*On screen:* A Hebrew family in front of their house: the father chooses a young white lamb from the flock; the children stroke it.

1. **[Q] Exodus 12:3** "Tell all the congregation of Israel that on the tenth day of this month they shall take every man a lamb according to their fathers' houses, a lamb for a household;"
2. **[Q] Exodus 12:5** "Your lamb shall be without blemish, a male a year old; you shall take it from the sheep or from the goats;"
3. **[Q] Exodus 12:7** "Then they shall take some of the blood, and put it on the two doorposts and the lintel of the houses in which they eat them."
4. **[Q] Exodus 12:8** "They shall eat the flesh that night, roasted; with unleavened bread and bitter herbs they shall eat it."

### Scene 4.3 · Ready to go
*On screen:* Evening. The father marks the doorposts and the lintel with a bunch of hyssop. Inside, the family stands around the table, belts tied, sandals on, staffs in hand. *(Play: the Passover night.)*

1. **[Q] Exodus 12:11** "In this manner you shall eat it: your loins girded, your sandals on your feet, and your staff in your hand; and you shall eat it in haste. It is the LORD's passover."
2. *(Play: the Passover night.)*
3. **[Q] Exodus 12:13** "The blood shall be a sign for you, upon the houses where you are; and when I see the blood, I will pass over you, and no plague shall fall upon you to destroy you, when I smite the land of Egypt."

### Scene 4.4 · The people did so
*On screen:* The whole street at night, every door marked, lamps glowing inside. The people bow their heads in prayer.

1. **[Q] Exodus 12:21** "Then Moses called all the elders of Israel, and said to them, "Select lambs for yourselves according to your families, and kill the passover lamb."
2. **[Q] Exodus 12:28** "Then the people of Israel went and did so; as the LORD had commanded Moses and Aaron, so they did."
3. They trusted God, and did what he said, though they could not yet see how he would save them.

### Play: the Passover night
- **What it is:** serving and distributing (spec §13.7, kind 19).
- **How it plays:** the family gets ready for the Passover, as God told them, step by step. First the father dips the hyssop and marks the two doorposts and the lintel (tap each one). Then the player sets the meal on the table: the roasted lamb, the unleavened bread, the bitter herbs. Last, the family gets ready to go: a belt for each, sandals on their feet, a staff in each hand. Each thing done glows, until the house is ready, and the lamp is lit.
- **Teaches:** what God told his people to do on the Passover night, and why each thing mattered.
- **Gentle mode:** the next thing to do glows.
- **Accessibility:** a pointer, or Space for the next step.

**Thread:** *The Passover lamb, without blemish, whose blood saved God's people → Jesus, the Lamb of God, our Passover.* The glowing object is the blood on the doorposts.
- **[Q] John 1:29:** "The next day he saw Jesus coming toward him, and said, "Behold, the Lamb of God, who takes away the sin of the world!"
- **[Q] 1 Corinthians 5:7:** "Cleanse out the old leaven that you may be a new lump, as you really are unleavened. For Christ, our paschal lamb, has been sacrificed."
- **What it shows:** The Passover lamb had to be without blemish, and not one of its bones could be broken (Exodus 12:46). Jesus died at Passover time, the innocent Lamb of God, and not one of his bones was broken either (John 19:36). His blood saves us, as the lamb's blood saved God's people. That is why, at every Mass, the priest holds up the Host and says, "Behold the Lamb of God."
- **Source:** John 1:29; 1 Corinthians 5:7; John 19:36; Catechism 608, 1340, 1364.

**Where and when:**
- **The month of Abib.** God made the Passover month the first month of the year (Exodus 12:2). It falls in spring, in March or April, at the full moon.
- **Hyssop.** A small bushy plant whose stems hold liquid well, used for sprinkling. At the cross, the soldiers raised a sponge of wine to Jesus on hyssop (John 19:29).
- **Unleavened bread.** Bread made without yeast, quick to bake, for people who must leave in a hurry.

**Read:** Exodus 12:1–13; 12:21–28.

## Episode 5 · The Night of Departure

**Exodus 12:29–42; 13:17–22 · about 8 minutes · 4 scenes**

> **Content note shown before this episode:** "In this episode the firstborn of Egypt die on the night of the Passover, as God had warned. It is told gently, and nothing is shown."

### Scene 5.1 · Midnight
*On screen:* The Hebrew street at midnight, the marked doors closed and lamps glowing inside. Then Pharaoh's palace, lamps lit in every window, and Pharaoh standing in the night calling for Moses.

1. At midnight, as God had said, the firstborn of Egypt died. There was a great cry in the land. But in the houses marked with the blood of the lamb, God's people were safe.
2. **[Q] Exodus 12:31** "And he summoned Moses and Aaron by night, and said, "Rise up, go forth from among my people, both you and the people of Israel; and go, serve the LORD, as you have said."
3. **[Q] Exodus 12:32** "Take your flocks and your herds, as you have said, and be gone; and bless me also!""

### Scene 5.2 · In haste
*On screen:* The Hebrews streaming out of their houses by moonlight, carrying kneading bowls wrapped in their cloaks, children on their shoulders, flocks and herds among them. A great column sets out along the road. *(Play: the march out of Egypt.)*

1. **[Q] Exodus 12:34** "So the people took their dough before it was leavened, their kneading bowls being bound up in their mantles on their shoulders."
2. **[Q] Exodus 12:37** "And the people of Israel journeyed from Rameses to Succoth, about six hundred thousand men on foot, besides women and children."
3. *(Play: the march out of Egypt.)*
4. **[Q] Exodus 12:38** "A mixed multitude also went up with them, and very many cattle, both flocks and herds."

### Scene 5.3 · Four hundred and thirty years
*On screen:* The column stretches back toward Egypt under the full moon. An old man looks back once at the land where his family were slaves, then turns and walks on.

1. **[Q] Exodus 12:40** "The time that the people of Israel dwelt in Egypt was four hundred and thirty years."
2. **[Q] Exodus 12:41** "And at the end of four hundred and thirty years, on that very day, all the hosts of the LORD went out from the land of Egypt."
3. **[Q] Exodus 12:42** "It was a night of watching by the LORD, to bring them out of the land of Egypt; so this same night is a night of watching kept to the LORD by all the people of Israel throughout their generations."

### Scene 5.4 · The pillar of cloud and fire
*On screen:* Dawn in the wilderness. Ahead of the people, a tall pillar of cloud, glowing gold at its heart; at nightfall it shines as a pillar of fire over the camp.

1. God did not lead them the short way, along the coast, where they would meet war. He led them through the wilderness, toward the sea.
2. **[Q] Exodus 13:21** "And the LORD went before them by day in a pillar of cloud to lead them along the way, and by night in a pillar of fire to give them light, that they might travel by day and by night;"
3. **[Q] Exodus 13:22** "the pillar of cloud by day and the pillar of fire by night did not depart from before the people."

### Play: the march out of Egypt
- **What it is:** rhythm (spec §13.7, kind 5).
- **How it plays:** the column of God's people sets out by moonlight, to the beat of a drum. The player taps along with the beat (or presses Space), and with each step in time, more families join the march: a father with a child on his shoulders, a mother with a kneading bowl, a shepherd and his flock, until the whole multitude is on the road. A step out of time doesn't stop anyone; the next beat comes round again.
- **Teaches:** the joy of freedom; that God brought out all his people together.
- **Gentle mode:** any tap counts as a step in time.
- **Accessibility:** a pointer (tap), or Space; the beat also shows as a pulse on screen.

**Thread:** *God brings his people out of Egypt in the great exodus → Jesus speaks of his own "departure," his exodus, which he would accomplish in Jerusalem.* The glowing object is the pillar of fire.
- **[Q] Luke 9:30:** "And behold, two men talked with him, Moses and Elijah,"
- **[Q] Luke 9:31:** "who appeared in glory and spoke of his departure, which he was to accomplish at Jerusalem."
- **What it shows:** On the mountain of the Transfiguration, Moses spoke with Jesus about his "departure": in Greek, his *exodus*. Moses had led God's people out of slavery in Egypt; Jesus, by his death and resurrection, would lead all God's people out of the slavery of sin and death, into the Father's house.
- **Source:** Luke 9:28–36; Catechism 1151, 1340.

**Where and when:**
- **Rameses and Succoth.** Rameses was the store-city the slaves had built; Succoth ("booths") was the first camp on the way out of Egypt, in the east of the Nile delta.
- **Six hundred thousand.** The Bible counts the men; with women and children, a great multitude, and with them a "mixed multitude" of other peoples who chose to go too.
- **Four hundred and thirty years.** As God had told Abraham long before, his descendants would be strangers in a land not theirs, and then come out (Genesis 15:13–14).

**Read:** Exodus 12:29–42; 13:17–22.

## Episode 6 · Remember This Day

**Exodus 12:14–17, 24–27; 13:3–16 · about 8 minutes · 4 scenes**

### Scene 6.1 · A memorial day
*On screen:* The camp in the wilderness at evening. Moses speaks to the people, and they listen.

1. Even before they left Egypt, God told his people to remember this night for ever.
2. **[Q] Exodus 12:14** ""This day shall be for you a memorial day, and you shall keep it as a feast to the LORD; throughout your generations you shall observe it as an ordinance for ever."
3. **[Q] Exodus 12:17** "And you shall observe the feast of unleavened bread, for on this very day I brought your hosts out of the land of Egypt: therefore you shall observe this day, throughout your generations, as an ordinance for ever."

### Scene 6.2 · Remember this day
*On screen:* Moses raises his rod over the people, and behind him the pillar of fire glows.

1. **[Q] Exodus 13:3** "And Moses said to the people, "Remember this day, in which you came out from Egypt, out of the house of bondage, for by strength of hand the LORD brought you out from this place; no leavened bread shall be eaten."
2. **[Q] Exodus 13:8** "And you shall tell your son on that day, 'It is because of what the LORD did for me when I came out of Egypt.'"

### Scene 6.3 · When your children ask
*On screen:* Years later, a family at the Passover table: the father, the mother, the grandparents, and the youngest child, who looks up and asks. On the table: a roasted lamb, flat bread, bitter herbs, a staff leaning against the wall. *(Play: when your children ask.)*

1. **[Q] Exodus 12:26** "And when your children say to you, 'What do you mean by this service?'"
2. *(Play: when your children ask.)*
3. **[Q] Exodus 12:27** "you shall say, 'It is the sacrifice of the LORD's passover, for he passed over the houses of the people of Israel in Egypt, when he slew the Egyptians but spared our houses.'" And the people bowed their heads and worshiped."

### Scene 6.4 · Every year since
*On screen:* The family table again, and then, softly, another table: Jesus at supper with his friends, holding the bread.

1. Jewish families still keep the Passover every spring. At the meal, the youngest child asks, "Why is this night different from all other nights?", and the family tells the story of what God did.
2. Jesus kept the Passover with his friends, the night before he died. He took the bread and the cup, and gave them a new meaning.
3. **[Q] Exodus 13:14** "And when in time to come your son asks you, 'What does this mean?' you shall say to him, 'By strength of hand the LORD brought us out of Egypt, from the house of bondage."

### Play: when your children ask
- **What it is:** matching and naming (spec §13.7, kind 10).
- **How it plays:** the youngest child points to each thing on the Passover table in turn and asks, "What does this mean?" The player chooses the answer that belongs to it: the lamb, "The LORD passed over our houses"; the unleavened bread, "We left Egypt in a hurry, before the dough could rise"; the bitter herbs, "Slavery in Egypt was bitter"; the staff, "We were ready to go when God brought us out." Each right answer is told to the child, who smiles and points to the next.
- **Teaches:** what the signs of the Passover mean, and that faith is handed on from parents to children.
- **Gentle mode:** the right answer glows.
- **Accessibility:** a pointer, or the arrow keys and Space.

**Thread:** *"This day shall be for you a memorial day" → Jesus at the Last Supper: "Do this in remembrance of me."* The glowing object is the bread on the table.
- **[Q] Luke 22:19:** "And he took bread, and when he had given thanks he broke it and gave it to them, saying, "This is my body which is given for you. Do this in remembrance of me.""
- **What it shows:** God told his people to keep the Passover every year in memory of how he saved them. At the Passover meal before he died, Jesus gave the bread and the cup a new meaning: his own Body and Blood, given for us. Every Mass is that memorial, the new Passover: not only remembering, but receiving the Lamb of God himself.
- **Source:** Luke 22:7–20; 1 Corinthians 11:23–26; Catechism 1339–1340, 1363.

**Where and when:**
- **The Seder.** The Passover meal Jewish families keep today is called the *Seder*, "order," because it follows the same order every year.
- **Bitter herbs.** Exodus says the Egyptians made the Hebrews' lives "bitter with hard service" (Exodus 1:14). Jewish tradition sees that bitterness in the herbs.
- **Easter.** The Church's Easter is the Christian Passover: in many languages it is even called by the same name, like *Pascua* in Spanish and *Paskah* in Indonesian.

**Read:** Exodus 12:14–17; 12:24–27; 13:3–16.
