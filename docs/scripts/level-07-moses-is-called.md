# Level 7 script: *Moses Is Called*

**Bible:** Exodus 1–4
**Age:** III · Out of Egypt
**Length:** about 40 minutes (5 episodes, then the quiz, then reflection and prayer)
**Status:** approved by Dave on 2026-10-22 ("approve").

## How to read this script

- Each **scene** is a checkpoint. Its numbered lines are the narration beats; the player taps to move from one to the next.
- *On screen* describes what the scene shows. It's the brief for the art in both styles.
- **[Q]** marks an exact quotation from the Bible. Its wording in both translations is in the verse table at the end. Everything else is our own retelling.
- **Play** is the episode's interactive moment. **Thread** is its golden thread. **Read** is the passage the game sends the player to.
- "LORD" in capitals stands for God's holy name, printed in small capitals in the RSV.

### What this level is about

Level 7 opens a new Age: *Out of Egypt*. Joseph's family has grown into a great people in Egypt, and a new Pharaoh, afraid of them, makes them slaves. He even orders their baby boys to be killed. But two brave midwives obey God rather than the king, and one little boy is hidden in a basket among the reeds of the Nile, found by Pharaoh's own daughter, and raised in the palace: Moses.

Moses grows up, tries to help his people in his own way, and has to flee into the desert. For many years he is a shepherd in Midian. Then, at the mountain of God, a bush burns and is not burnt up, and God calls him by name. God has seen his people's suffering, and he is sending Moses to set them free. Moses is afraid: "Who am I?" God answers, "I will be with you," and tells him his name: "I AM WHO I AM."

The level shows children that God sees those who suffer and hears their cry; that he works through ordinary, brave people, and through those who feel too small for what he asks; and that the God who spoke from the bush is the one Jesus revealed: "Before Abraham was, I am."

## Episode 1 · Slavery and the Brave Midwives

**Exodus 1 · about 8 minutes · 4 scenes**

> **Content note shown before this episode:** "In this episode Pharaoh makes the Hebrews slaves, and orders that their baby boys be killed. Two brave women save them. Nothing is shown."

### Scene 1.1 · A new king
*On screen:* The green land of Goshen, crowded with the tents and houses of a great people: families, children, flocks. Then the scene darkens, and a new Pharaoh looks out from his palace with a frown.

1. Joseph died, and his brothers, and all that generation. But their families stayed in Egypt.
2. **[Q] Exodus 1:7** "But the descendants of Israel were fruitful and increased greatly; they multiplied and grew exceedingly strong; so that the land was filled with them."
3. **[Q] Exodus 1:8** "Now there arose a new king over Egypt, who did not know Joseph."
4. **[Q] Exodus 1:9** "And he said to his people, "Behold, the people of Israel are too many and too mighty for us."

### Scene 1.2 · Mortar and brick
*On screen:* A building site under a hot sun: the Hebrews mixing mud and straw, carrying bricks, building great storehouses, while Egyptian taskmasters stand over them with sticks.

1. **[Q] Exodus 1:11** "Therefore they set taskmasters over them to afflict them with heavy burdens; and they built for Pharaoh store-cities, Pithom and Raamses."
2. **[Q] Exodus 1:12** "But the more they were oppressed, the more they multiplied and the more they spread abroad. And the Egyptians were in dread of the people of Israel."
3. **[Q] Exodus 1:14** "and made their lives bitter with hard service, in mortar and brick, and in all kinds of work in the field; in all their work they made them serve with rigor."

### Scene 1.3 · Shiphrah and Puah
*On screen:* Pharaoh on his throne, two women before him: Shiphrah and Puah, the Hebrew midwives. Then night in the Hebrew quarter: narrow streets, lamps in the windows, Pharaoh's watchmen walking with lanterns. *(Play: the midwives' night.)*

1. Then Pharaoh sent for two Hebrew midwives, Shiphrah and Puah, who helped the mothers when their babies were born. He commanded them: let the baby girls live, but kill the baby boys.
2. **[Q] Exodus 1:17** "But the midwives feared God, and did not do as the king of Egypt commanded them, but let the male children live."
3. *(Play: the midwives' night.)*
4. **[Q] Exodus 1:18** "So the king of Egypt called the midwives, and said to them, "Why have you done this, and let the male children live?""
5. **[Q] Exodus 1:19** "The midwives said to Pharaoh, "Because the Hebrew women are not like the Egyptian women; for they are vigorous and are delivered before the midwife comes to them.""

### Scene 1.4 · God dealt well with the midwives
*On screen:* Morning in the Hebrew quarter: Shiphrah and Puah among happy families, babies in their mothers' arms. Then Pharaoh, angry, points toward the river.

1. **[Q] Exodus 1:20** "So God dealt well with the midwives; and the people multiplied and grew very strong."
2. **[Q] Exodus 1:21** "And because the midwives feared God he gave them families."
3. **[Q] Exodus 1:22** "Then Pharaoh commanded all his people, "Every son that is born to the Hebrews you shall cast into the Nile, but you shall let every daughter live.""
4. The Bible remembers the names of the two brave midwives, Shiphrah and Puah. It doesn't even tell us the name of the powerful Pharaoh.

### Play: the midwives' night
- **What it is:** stealth and escape (spec §13.7, kind 17).
- **How it plays:** night in the Hebrew quarter. Shiphrah and Puah go from house to house, where mothers are waiting for their babies to be born, and bring the newborn sons safely home to their families. Pharaoh's watchmen walk the streets with lanterns. The player moves the midwives along the street while the lanterns look away (hold to walk), and stops them in the shadow of a doorway when a light swings near (let go). Each house they reach, a lamp is lit in its window. Five houses, five babies safe.
- **Teaches:** the courage to obey God rather than an unjust command; that every child's life is precious.
- **Gentle mode:** the watchmen are slow and sleepy, and nobody can be caught: if a light comes near, the midwives simply wait in a doorway until it passes.
- **Accessibility:** a pointer (hold), or Space (hold).

**Thread:** *Pharaoh orders the Hebrew baby boys killed → King Herod orders the baby boys of Bethlehem killed, and the child Jesus is saved.* The glowing object is a lamp in a window, where a baby is safe.
- **[Q] Matthew 2:13:** "Now when they had departed, behold, an angel of the Lord appeared to Joseph in a dream and said, "Rise, take the child and his mother, and flee to Egypt, and remain there till I tell you; for Herod is about to search for the child, to destroy him.""
- **[Q] Matthew 2:16:** "Then Herod, when he saw that he had been tricked by the wise men, was in a furious rage, and he sent and killed all the male children in Bethlehem and in all that region who were two years old or under, according to the time which he had ascertained from the wise men."
- **What it shows:** Moses was born when a king was killing baby boys, and God saved him. Jesus was born when a king was killing baby boys too, and God saved him, by sending his family into Egypt. Moses would lead God's people out of slavery; Jesus would lead all people out of the slavery of sin. The Church remembers the little boys of Bethlehem as the Holy Innocents, on December 28.
- **Source:** Matthew 2:13–18; Hebrews 11:23; Catechism 530.

**Where and when:**
- **When.** Exodus doesn't name the Pharaoh. Many scholars think of the 1200s BC and Pharaoh Ramesses II, who built a great city in the Nile delta; others place the events earlier. Genesis 15:13 had foretold it: Abraham's descendants would be "sojourners in a land that is not theirs."
- **Pithom and Raamses.** Store-cities in the eastern Nile delta, near Goshen, where grain and supplies were kept. Their ruins have been searched for by archaeologists for over a century.
- **Mud bricks.** Egyptian bricks were made of Nile mud mixed with straw, pressed in wooden molds and dried in the sun. Paintings from the time show slaves making them.

**Read:** Exodus 1.

## Episode 2 · The Baby in the Basket

**Exodus 2:1–10 · about 8 minutes · 4 scenes**

### Scene 2.1 · A goodly child
*On screen:* Inside a small mud-brick house at night, an oil lamp burning. A mother holds her newborn son and hushes him. His sister, a girl of about ten, keeps watch at the door.

1. **[Q] Exodus 2:1** "Now a man from the house of Levi went and took to wife a daughter of Levi."
2. **[Q] Exodus 2:2** "The woman conceived and bore a son; and when she saw that he was a goodly child, she hid him three months."
3. For three months they kept him hidden, and hushed him whenever he cried. But babies grow, and they get louder.

### Scene 2.2 · A basket of bulrushes
*On screen:* At the river's edge, early morning. The mother weaves a little basket of papyrus reeds, seals it with dark pitch, lays the baby in it, and sets it among the reeds. Then she goes, and the sister hides among the tall papyrus to watch. *(Play: a basket of bulrushes.)*

1. **[Q] Exodus 2:3** "And when she could hide him no longer she took for him a basket made of bulrushes, and daubed it with bitumen and pitch; and she put the child in it and placed it among the reeds at the river's brink."
2. *(Play: a basket of bulrushes.)*
3. **[Q] Exodus 2:4** "And his sister stood at a distance, to know what would be done to him."

### Scene 2.3 · Pharaoh's daughter
*On screen:* The princess comes down to the river with her maidens, in fine white linen. A maid lifts the basket out of the reeds; the princess opens it, and the baby cries. The sister steps out from the reeds.

1. **[Q] Exodus 2:5** "Now the daughter of Pharaoh came down to bathe at the river, and her maidens walked beside the river; she saw the basket among the reeds and sent her maid to fetch it."
2. **[Q] Exodus 2:6** "When she opened it she saw the child; and lo, the babe was crying. She took pity on him and said, "This is one of the Hebrews' children.""
3. **[Q] Exodus 2:7** "Then his sister said to Pharaoh's daughter, "Shall I go and call you a nurse from the Hebrew women to nurse the child for you?""
4. **[Q] Exodus 2:8** "And Pharaoh's daughter said to her, "Go." So the girl went and called the child's mother."
5. **[Q] Exodus 2:9** "And Pharaoh's daughter said to her, "Take this child away, and nurse him for me, and I will give you your wages." So the woman took the child and nursed him."

### Scene 2.4 · Drawn out of the water
*On screen:* Years later: the boy, now about four, is brought by his mother to the palace. The princess kneels to welcome him, and he looks back once at his mother.

1. His own mother was paid to care for him. God had given him back to her.
2. **[Q] Exodus 2:10** "And the child grew, and she brought him to Pharaoh's daughter, and he became her son; and she named him Moses, for she said, "Because I drew him out of the water.""
3. So Moses grew up in Pharaoh's palace, as a prince of Egypt. But he never forgot that he was a Hebrew.

### Play: a basket of bulrushes
- **What it is:** building and stacking (spec §13.7, kind 3).
- **How it plays:** the mother's hands weave the little basket. The player lays the reeds row by row, over and under, as each row swings into place (tap when it lines up), until the basket rises from its base to its rim. Then the player sweeps dark pitch over it, inside and out, so no water can come in. The baby is laid in it, wrapped in a blanket, and the lid is closed, and the basket floats safely among the reeds.
- **Teaches:** a mother's love and faith; she did all she could, and trusted God with the rest.
- **Gentle mode:** every tap lays a row, whatever the timing, and a sweep anywhere spreads the pitch.
- **Accessibility:** a pointer (tap, then sweep), or Space for each row and to spread the pitch.

**Thread:** *Pharaoh's daughter takes the Hebrew baby as her own son → In Christ, God makes us his own children.* The glowing object is the baby in the princess's arms.
- **[Q] Galatians 4:4:** "But when the time had fully come, God sent forth his Son, born of woman, born under the law,"
- **[Q] Galatians 4:5:** "to redeem those who were under the law, so that we might receive adoption as sons."
- **What it shows:** A baby nobody could protect was drawn out of the water and taken into the king's own family. That is a picture of what God does for us: through Jesus, and through the water of Baptism, he takes us into his own family, as his sons and daughters.
- **Source:** Galatians 4:4–7; Romans 8:15; Catechism 1265, 1279.

**Where and when:**
- **Papyrus.** The "bulrushes" were papyrus, a tall reed that grew thick along the Nile. Egyptians made boats, baskets, mats and paper from it.
- **The same word as Noah's ark.** The Hebrew word for this basket, *tebah*, is used only one other time in the Bible: for Noah's ark. Both saved a life from the waters.
- **The sister.** She isn't named here. Later the Bible tells us that Moses' sister was Miriam (Exodus 15:20; Numbers 26:59).
- **The name Moses.** In Egyptian, *mose* means "child" or "is born" (as in Ramesses, "Ra is born"). In Hebrew it sounds like *mashah*, "to draw out."

**Read:** Exodus 2:1–10.

## Episode 3 · Flight to Midian

**Exodus 2:11–25 · about 8 minutes · 4 scenes**

> **Content note shown before this episode:** "In this episode Moses kills an Egyptian who was beating a Hebrew slave. It is told in a few words, and nothing is shown."

### Scene 3.1 · Among his people
*On screen:* Moses, grown up, in the fine clothes of an Egyptian prince, walks among the Hebrew slaves at the brick pits. He sees a taskmaster raise his stick over a worker.

1. **[Q] Exodus 2:11** "One day, when Moses had grown up, he went out to his people and looked on their burdens; and he saw an Egyptian beating a Hebrew, one of his people."
2. Moses looked this way and that. He was angry, and he struck the Egyptian, and the man died. Moses hid him in the sand.
3. He wanted to help his people. But he did it in his own way, with violence, and in secret.

### Scene 3.2 · "Who made you a prince?"
*On screen:* The next day, two Hebrew workers quarrel. Moses steps between them; one turns on him and points. Moses steps back, afraid, and flees into the desert.

1. **[Q] Exodus 2:13** "When he went out the next day, behold, two Hebrews were struggling together; and he said to the man that did the wrong, "Why do you strike your fellow?""
2. **[Q] Exodus 2:14** "He answered, "Who made you a prince and a judge over us? Do you mean to kill me as you killed the Egyptian?" Then Moses was afraid, and thought, "Surely the thing is known.""
3. **[Q] Exodus 2:15** "When Pharaoh heard of it, he sought to kill Moses. But Moses fled from Pharaoh, and stayed in the land of Midian; and he sat down by a well."

### Scene 3.3 · At the well
*On screen:* A stone well in the desert of Midian, with troughs beside it. Seven young women draw water for their flock; rough shepherds push them away. Moses stands up. *(Play: watering the flock.)*

1. **[Q] Exodus 2:16** "Now the priest of Midian had seven daughters; and they came and drew water, and filled the troughs to water their father's flock."
2. **[Q] Exodus 2:17** "The shepherds came and drove them away; but Moses stood up and helped them, and watered their flock."
3. *(Play: watering the flock.)*
4. **[Q] Exodus 2:19** "They said, "An Egyptian delivered us out of the hand of the shepherds, and even drew water for us and watered the flock.""
5. **[Q] Exodus 2:20** "He said to his daughters, "And where is he? Why have you left the man? Call him, that he may eat bread.""
6. Moses stayed with Reuel, the priest of Midian, and married his daughter Zipporah.
7. **[Q] Exodus 2:22** "She bore a son, and he called his name Gershom; for he said, "I have been a sojourner in a foreign land.""

### Scene 3.4 · God heard their groaning
*On screen:* Years pass over Midian: Moses, older, with his flock under the stars. Far away in Egypt, the slaves still toil, and lift their faces to the sky.

1. **[Q] Exodus 2:23** "In the course of those many days the king of Egypt died. And the people of Israel groaned under their bondage, and cried out for help, and their cry under bondage came up to God."
2. **[Q] Exodus 2:24** "And God heard their groaning, and God remembered his covenant with Abraham, with Isaac, and with Jacob."
3. **[Q] Exodus 2:25** "And God saw the people of Israel, and God knew their condition."

### Play: watering the flock
- **What it is:** pouring and water (spec §13.7, kind 12).
- **How it plays:** the seven daughters' sheep crowd around the empty troughs. The player helps Moses draw up the bucket from the well (hold), and pour it into a trough (let go), until each trough is full to its mark. As each trough fills, the sheep at it drink and settle, and one of the daughters smiles her thanks. Seven troughs, one for each sister's part of the flock.
- **Teaches:** standing up for those who are pushed aside, and helping with our hands.
- **Gentle mode:** the bucket fills by itself, and every pour fills a trough.
- **Accessibility:** a pointer (hold), or Space (hold).

**Thread:** *Moses' own people reject him: "Who made you a prince and a judge over us?" → Jesus came to his own, and his own did not receive him; yet God made each of them a deliverer.* The glowing object is the well in Midian.
- **[Q] Acts 7:35:** ""This Moses whom they refused, saying, 'Who made you a ruler and a judge?' God sent as both ruler and deliverer by the hand of the angel that appeared to him in the bush."
- **[Q] John 1:11:** "He came to his own home, and his own people received him not."
- **What it shows:** St. Stephen, the first martyr, told this story to show that God's people had often refused the very ones God sent to save them. Moses, refused by his own, was sent back as their deliverer. Jesus, refused by his own, became the Savior of all.
- **Source:** Acts 7:23–36; John 1:10–12; Catechism 530, 2575.

**Where and when:**
- **Midian.** The land of the Midianites, east of the Gulf of Aqaba, in what is now north-western Saudi Arabia. They were desert herders, and distant relatives of Israel: Midian was a son of Abraham (Genesis 25:2).
- **Forty years.** St. Stephen says Moses was forty when he fled, and stayed in Midian forty years (Acts 7:23, 30).
- **Two names.** Moses' father-in-law is called Reuel here, and Jethro in the next chapters. Many people then had more than one name, or a name and a title.
- **Gershom.** The name sounds like *ger sham*, "a stranger there."

**Read:** Exodus 2:11–25.

## Episode 4 · The Burning Bush

**Exodus 3:1–4:17 · about 10 minutes · 5 scenes**

### Scene 4.1 · At Horeb, the mountain of God
*On screen:* A rocky mountain in the wilderness. Moses, now an old shepherd with a gray beard and a staff, leads his flock up the slope. On a ledge, a small thorny bush is wrapped in flame, but its leaves stay green. *(Play: turn aside and see.)*

1. **[Q] Exodus 3:1** "Now Moses was keeping the flock of his father-in-law, Jethro, the priest of Midian; and he led his flock to the west side of the wilderness, and came to Horeb, the mountain of God."
2. **[Q] Exodus 3:2** "And the angel of the LORD appeared to him in a flame of fire out of the midst of a bush; and he looked, and lo, the bush was burning, yet it was not consumed."
3. **[Q] Exodus 3:3** "And Moses said, "I will turn aside and see this great sight, why the bush is not burnt.""
4. *(Play: turn aside and see.)*

### Scene 4.2 · Holy ground
*On screen:* Close to the bush, its light on Moses' face. He takes off his sandals and kneels, and hides his face in his cloak.

1. **[Q] Exodus 3:4** "When the LORD saw that he turned aside to see, God called to him out of the bush, "Moses, Moses!" And he said, "Here am I.""
2. **[Q] Exodus 3:5** "Then he said, "Do not come near; put off your shoes from your feet, for the place on which you are standing is holy ground.""
3. **[Q] Exodus 3:6** "And he said, "I am the God of your father, the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob." And Moses hid his face, for he was afraid to look at God."

### Scene 4.3 · "I have seen the affliction of my people"
*On screen:* In the flame, like a picture, the slaves in Egypt at their bricks; then, beyond them, a green land of hills and streams, with flocks and beehives.

1. **[Q] Exodus 3:7** "Then the LORD said, "I have seen the affliction of my people who are in Egypt, and have heard their cry because of their taskmasters; I know their sufferings,"
2. **[Q] Exodus 3:8 (in part)** "and I have come down to deliver them out of the hand of the Egyptians, and to bring them up out of that land to a good and broad land, a land flowing with milk and honey"
3. **[Q] Exodus 3:10** "Come, I will send you to Pharaoh that you may bring forth my people, the sons of Israel, out of Egypt.""

### Scene 4.4 · "I AM WHO I AM"
*On screen:* Moses, still kneeling, looks up at the flame. The flame grows brighter and taller, and fills the screen with gold.

1. **[Q] Exodus 3:11** "But Moses said to God, "Who am I that I should go to Pharaoh, and bring the sons of Israel out of Egypt?""
2. **[Q] Exodus 3:12** "He said, "But I will be with you; and this shall be the sign for you, that I have sent you: when you have brought forth the people out of Egypt, you shall serve God upon this mountain.""
3. **[Q] Exodus 3:13** "Then Moses said to God, "If I come to the people of Israel and say to them, 'The God of your fathers has sent me to you,' and they ask me, 'What is his name?' what shall I say to them?""
4. **[Q] Exodus 3:14** "God said to Moses, "I AM WHO I AM." And he said, "Say this to the people of Israel, 'I AM has sent me to you.'""
5. God's name means that he simply *is*: he was before everything, and always will be, and he is always there for his people.

### Scene 4.5 · "What is that in your hand?"
*On screen:* Moses stands with his shepherd's staff. He throws it down, and it becomes a serpent; he picks it up by the tail, and it is a staff again. Far off, a figure walks toward the mountain: his brother Aaron.

1. **[Q] Exodus 4:1** "Then Moses answered, "But behold, they will not believe me or listen to my voice, for they will say, 'The LORD did not appear to you.'""
2. **[Q] Exodus 4:2** "The LORD said to him, "What is that in your hand?" He said, "A rod.""
3. **[Q] Exodus 4:3** "And he said, "Cast it on the ground." So he cast it on the ground, and it became a serpent; and Moses fled from it."
4. God gave Moses signs to show the people: the rod that became a serpent and then a rod again, and his hand that turned white and was healed.
5. **[Q] Exodus 4:10** "But Moses said to the LORD, "Oh, my Lord, I am not eloquent, either heretofore or since thou hast spoken to thy servant; but I am slow of speech and of tongue.""
6. **[Q] Exodus 4:12** "Now therefore go, and I will be with your mouth and teach you what you shall speak.""
7. Moses still asked God to send someone else. So God gave him his brother Aaron to speak for him.
8. **[Q] Exodus 4:17** "And you shall take in your hand this rod, with which you shall do the signs.""

### Play: turn aside and see
- **What it is:** light and reveal (spec §13.7, kind 11).
- **How it plays:** the bush burns on the mountainside, and Moses comes closer. With each step the player takes (tap, or hold to walk), the flame's light reaches further, and shows more: the rocks lit gold, the thorny branches, the green leaves untouched by the fire. Moses stops where the light is brightest. When he is close enough, the voice comes (the story goes on).
- **Teaches:** wonder, and turning aside to look for God, who calls each of us by name.
- **Gentle mode:** Moses walks on by himself after a moment.
- **Accessibility:** a pointer (tap or hold), or Space.

**Thread:** *God tells Moses his name: "I AM WHO I AM" → Jesus says, "Before Abraham was, I am."* The glowing object is the flame in the bush.
- **[Q] John 8:58:** "Jesus said to them, "Truly, truly, I say to you, before Abraham was, I am.""
- **What it shows:** God told Moses his name: I AM. Centuries later, Jesus used those same words about himself, and the people who heard him understood: he was saying that he is God. The one who spoke from the bush is the one who became a man in Jesus.
- **Source:** John 8:56–59; John 18:5–6; Catechism 205–213, 589.

**Where and when:**
- **Horeb.** Another name for Mount Sinai, where God would later give the Ten Commandments (Exodus 19). Tradition places it in the south of the Sinai peninsula, at Jebel Musa, "Moses' Mountain," where St. Catherine's Monastery has stood since the 500s.
- **Shoes off.** Taking off one's shoes was a sign of reverence, as in many places of prayer today.
- **The holy name.** In Hebrew, God's name is written with four letters, YHWH. Out of reverence, Jews do not say it aloud, but say "the Lord" (*Adonai*) instead. That is why the RSV prints it as LORD.
- **Milk and honey.** A land good for flocks (milk) and for bees and fruit trees (honey): a land of plenty.

**Read:** Exodus 3:1–4:17.

## Episode 5 · Return to Egypt

**Exodus 4:18–31 · about 6 minutes · 3 scenes**

### Scene 5.1 · "Go in peace"
*On screen:* Moses' tent in Midian. He says goodbye to Jethro, then sets out with Zipporah and their sons on a donkey, the rod of God in his hand, along a road that winds toward Egypt.

1. **[Q] Exodus 4:18** "Moses went back to Jethro his father-in-law and said to him, "Let me go back, I pray, to my kinsmen in Egypt and see whether they are still alive." And Jethro said to Moses, "Go in peace.""
2. **[Q] Exodus 4:19** "And the LORD said to Moses in Midian, "Go back to Egypt; for all the men who were seeking your life are dead.""
3. **[Q] Exodus 4:20** "So Moses took his wife and his sons and set them on an ass, and went back to the land of Egypt; and in his hand Moses took the rod of God."

### Scene 5.2 · Two brothers
*On screen:* At the foot of the mountain of God, Aaron comes running and embraces Moses. They sit together on a rock as Moses tells him everything.

1. **[Q] Exodus 4:27** "The LORD said to Aaron, "Go into the wilderness to meet Moses." So he went, and met him at the mountain of God and kissed him."
2. **[Q] Exodus 4:28** "And Moses told Aaron all the words of the LORD with which he had sent him, and all the signs which he had charged him to do."
3. Moses was not alone any more. God had given him a brother to stand beside him.

### Scene 5.3 · The people believed
*On screen:* Back in Goshen, at evening: the elders of Israel gathered in a circle, gray-bearded and tired. Aaron speaks; Moses shows the signs. One by one, the elders bow their heads. *(Play: the signs.)*

1. **[Q] Exodus 4:29** "Then Moses and Aaron went and gathered together all the elders of the people of Israel."
2. **[Q] Exodus 4:30** "And Aaron spoke all the words which the LORD had spoken to Moses, and did the signs in the sight of the people."
3. *(Play: the signs.)*
4. **[Q] Exodus 4:31** "And the people believed; and when they heard that the LORD had visited the people of Israel and that he had seen their affliction, they bowed their heads and worshiped."
5. They didn't know yet how God would set them free. But after so many years, they knew he had seen them, and was coming.

### Play: the signs
- **What it is:** ordering (spec §13.7, kind 9).
- **How it plays:** the elders watch, doubtful. The player shows them the signs God gave, in the order God gave them: first the rod, cast down to become a serpent and taken up to become a rod again; then the hand, put into the cloak and taken out white as snow, and put back and taken out healed; then the water from the Nile, poured on the dry ground. The player chooses which sign comes next; each one plays out, and more of the elders lean in, until all of them bow their heads.
- **Teaches:** the signs God gave Moses, in order, and that God gives us what we need for what he asks.
- **Gentle mode:** the next sign glows among the choices.
- **Accessibility:** a pointer, or the arrow keys and Space.

**Thread:** *"Go back to Egypt; for all the men who were seeking your life are dead" → an angel tells Joseph, "Go to the land of Israel, for those who sought the child's life are dead."* The glowing object is the rod of God in Moses' hand.
- **[Q] Matthew 2:20:** ""Rise, take the child and his mother, and go to the land of Israel, for those who sought the child's life are dead.""
- **What it shows:** Matthew tells Jesus' childhood with Moses' words. Like Moses, Jesus was saved from a king who killed baby boys, lived in exile, and came back when those who wanted him dead had died. Jesus is the new Moses, who leads God's people into true freedom.
- **Source:** Matthew 2:13–23; Hosea 11:1; Catechism 527–530.

**Where and when:**

- **The elders.** The heads of Israel's families and tribes, who spoke for the people.
