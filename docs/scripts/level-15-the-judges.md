# Level 15 script: *The Judges*

**Bible:** Judges 2–8; 10–11; 13–14; 16–18; 20–21
**Age:** V · The Promised Land
**Length:** about 50 minutes (6 episodes, then the quiz, then reflection and prayer)
**Status:** draft for Dave's approval.

## How to read this script

- Each **scene** is a checkpoint. Its numbered lines are the narration beats; the player taps to move from one to the next.
- *On screen* describes what the scene shows. It's the brief for the art in both styles.
- **[Q]** marks an exact quotation from the Bible. Its wording in both translations is in the verse table at the end. Everything else is our own retelling.
- **Play** is the episode's interactive moment. **Thread** is its golden thread. **Read** is the passage the game sends the player to.
- "LORD" in capitals stands for God's holy name, printed in small capitals in the RSV.

### What this level is about

Joshua is dead, and a new generation grows up "who did not know the LORD." Again and again the same thing happens: the people forget God and serve the gods of their neighbors; enemies come and take their harvests; the people cry out to God; and God, moved to pity, raises up a "judge," a leader who saves them. Then the land has rest, until the judge dies and it all starts again.

The judges are not heroes in shining armor. Deborah is a prophetess who sits under a palm tree. Gideon is the least man in the weakest clan, threshing wheat in hiding. Jephthah is an outcast whose rash vow ends in grief. Samson is the strongest man alive, and the weakest at keeping his promises. By the end of the book, the people have forgotten so much that "every man did what was right in his own eyes." And yet God never gives up on them. The letter to the Hebrews names four of these judges among the great men of faith, people who "won strength out of weakness":

- **[Q] Hebrews 11:32:** "And what more shall I say? For time would fail me to tell of Gideon, Barak, Samson, Jephthah, of David and Samuel and the prophets—"
- **[Q] Hebrews 11:34:** "quenched raging fire, escaped the edge of the sword, won strength out of weakness, became mighty in war, put foreign armies to flight."

The book ends by waiting for a king. The next level, Ruth, happens "in the days when the judges ruled," in the little town of Bethlehem, where that king's family begins.

**How it's shown:** the book of Judges is one of the darkest in the Bible, and the game tells it honestly but gently. Battles are not shown; we see the trumpets, the torches, the rain, and the enemy running away. Three episodes begin with a short note for parents and players: Jephthah's vow (Episode 4), Samson's end (Episode 5), and the crime and civil war at the end of the book (Episode 6). Nothing in them is shown, only told in a sentence or two.

## Episode 1 · The Cycle of Forgetting

**Judges 2:6–3:31 · about 8 minutes · 3 scenes**

### Scene 1.1 · A generation who did not know the LORD
*On screen:* The hill country of Ephraim, green and quiet. Old Joshua's tomb among the olive trees; the elders who knew him grow old and gray, one by one, and their places at the gate are taken by young men. The children play around the twelve stones at Gilgal, but nobody tells them what the stones mean any more.

1. **[Q] Judges 2:7** "And the people served the LORD all the days of Joshua, and all the days of the elders who outlived Joshua, who had seen all the great work which the LORD had done for Israel."
2. **[Q] Judges 2:8** "And Joshua the son of Nun, the servant of the LORD, died at the age of one hundred and ten years."
3. **[Q] Judges 2:10** "And all that generation also were gathered to their fathers; and there arose another generation after them, who did not know the LORD or the work which he had done for Israel."

### Scene 1.2 · Around and around
*On screen:* A Canaanite hilltop shrine with a stone pillar and a carved wooden pole. Israelite families bring offerings to it. Then raiders come over the hills and carry off the harvest; the people kneel among the empty fields and cry out, and a light comes down on one man among them. *(Play: around and around.)*

1. **[Q] Judges 2:11** "And the people of Israel did what was evil in the sight of the LORD and served the Baals;"
2. **[Q] Judges 2:12** "and they forsook the LORD, the God of their fathers, who had brought them out of the land of Egypt; they went after other gods, from among the gods of the peoples who were round about them, and bowed down to them; and they provoked the LORD to anger."
3. **[Q] Judges 2:14** "So the anger of the LORD was kindled against Israel, and he gave them over to plunderers, who plundered them; and he sold them into the power of their enemies round about, so that they could no longer withstand their enemies."
4. **[Q] Judges 2:16** "Then the LORD raised up judges, who saved them out of the power of those who plundered them."
5. *(Play: around and around.)*
6. **[Q] Judges 2:18** "Whenever the LORD raised up judges for them, the LORD was with the judge, and he saved them from the hand of their enemies all the days of the judge; for the LORD was moved to pity by their groaning because of those who afflicted and oppressed them."
7. **[Q] Judges 2:19** "But whenever the judge died, they turned back and behaved worse than their fathers, going after other gods, serving them and bowing down to them; they did not drop any of their practices or their stubborn ways."

### Scene 1.3 · Othniel and Ehud
*On screen:* The circle, turning: a king from far away rules over Israel; the people cry out; Othniel, a young man of Judah, rises up with the Spirit of the LORD on him, and the land is green and at peace. Then the circle turns again, and a left-handed man, Ehud, sets the people free from Moab.

1. **[Q] Judges 3:7** "And the people of Israel did what was evil in the sight of the LORD, forgetting the LORD their God, and serving the Baals and the Asheroth."
2. **[Q] Judges 3:9** "But when the people of Israel cried to the LORD, the LORD raised up a deliverer for the people of Israel, who delivered them, Othni-el the son of Kenaz, Caleb's younger brother."
3. **[Q] Judges 3:10** "The Spirit of the LORD came upon him, and he judged Israel; he went out to war, and the LORD gave Cushan-rishathaim king of Mesopotamia into his hand; and his hand prevailed over Cushan-rishathaim."
4. **[Q] Judges 3:11** "So the land had rest forty years. Then Othni-el the son of Kenaz died."
5. Then the people forgot God again, and Eglon king of Moab ruled over them for eighteen years. And again they cried to the LORD.
6. **[Q] Judges 3:15** "But when the people of Israel cried to the LORD, the LORD raised up for them a deliverer, Ehud, the son of Gera, the Benjaminite, a left-handed man. The people of Israel sent tribute by him to Eglon the king of Moab."
7. **[Q] Judges 3:30** "So Moab was subdued that day under the hand of Israel. And the land had rest for eighty years."

### Play: around and around
- **What it is:** ordering (spec §13.7, kind 9).
- **How it plays:** a great wheel, like a millstone, with five places on its rim. Five pictures lie beside it: the people bowing to idols; raiders taking the harvest; the people crying out; God raising up a judge; the land at rest. The player sets them on the wheel in order (drag, or the arrow keys and Space), and the wheel turns once. Then it turns again, faster, for Othniel, and again for Ehud. Each time the wheel comes round to "the people forgot," it creaks a little lower.
- **Teaches:** the pattern of the whole book of Judges, which the player will see again in every episode: sin, suffering, crying out, rescue, rest, and forgetting.
- **Gentle mode:** the next picture glows, and a wrong one slides back by itself.
- **Accessibility:** a pointer, or the arrow keys and Space.

**Thread:** *Israel forgets God again and again, but every time they cry out, he hears them and saves them → "If we are faithless, he remains faithful."* The glowing object is the light that comes down on Othniel as the Spirit of the LORD comes upon him.
- **[Q] 2 Timothy 2:13:** "if we are faithless, he remains faithful— for he cannot deny himself."
- **What it shows:** the circle in the book of Judges is a sad one, but it shows something wonderful. The people break their promises to God again and again. God never breaks his. Each time they cry out, "the LORD was moved to pity," and he sends someone to save them. Saint Paul says the same of us: even when we are faithless, God stays faithful, because he cannot stop being himself. The judges saved Israel for a while; Jesus came to break the circle of sin for good.
- **Source:** 2 Timothy 2:11–13; Romans 3:3–4; Catechism 210–211, 1428.

**Where and when:**
- **Judges.** In Hebrew, *shofetim*: not only judges in a court, but leaders whom God raised up to rescue his people and rule them for a time. The book names twelve of them.
- **About 1200–1050 BC.** The judges lived in the two centuries between Joshua and the first king, Saul. The tribes lived apart in their own lands, with no capital and no king.
- **The Baals and the Ashtaroth.** Baal was the Canaanite god of storms and rain, and Ashtart a goddess of fertility. People hoped that serving them would bring good harvests.
- **Othniel.** A nephew of Caleb, the brave old spy of Levels 12 and 14.

**Read:** Judges 2:6–3:11.

## Episode 2 · Deborah and Barak

**Judges 4–5 · about 9 minutes · 3 scenes**

### Scene 2.1 · Under the palm of Deborah
*On screen:* A tall date palm in the hill country, between Ramah and Bethel. Under it sits Deborah, a prophetess, and people stand in line to bring her their quarrels. Far to the north, on the plain, nine hundred iron chariots glint in the sun. Deborah sends for Barak, a soldier from the north, and he comes and stands before her.

1. **[Q] Judges 4:3** "Then the people of Israel cried to the LORD for help; for he had nine hundred chariots of iron, and oppressed the people of Israel cruelly for twenty years."
2. **[Q] Judges 4:4** "Now Deborah, a prophetess, the wife of Lappidoth, was judging Israel at that time."
3. **[Q] Judges 4:5** "She used to sit under the palm of Deborah between Ramah and Bethel in the hill country of Ephraim; and the people of Israel came up to her for judgment."
4. **[Q] Judges 4:6** "She sent and summoned Barak the son of Abino-am from Kedesh in Naphtali, and said to him, "The LORD, the God of Israel, commands you, 'Go, gather your men at Mount Tabor, taking ten thousand from the tribe of Naphtali and the tribe of Zebulun."
5. **[Q] Judges 4:7** "And I will draw out Sisera, the general of Jabin's army, to meet you by the river Kishon with his chariots and his troops; and I will give him into your hand.'""
6. **[Q] Judges 4:8** "Barak said to her, "If you will go with me, I will go; but if you will not go with me, I will not go.""
7. **[Q] Judges 4:9** "And she said, "I will surely go with you; nevertheless, the road on which you are going will not lead to your glory, for the LORD will sell Sisera into the hand of a woman." Then Deborah arose, and went with Barak to Kedesh."

### Scene 2.2 · Down from Mount Tabor
*On screen:* Mount Tabor, round and green, rising out of the plain. Ten thousand men wait on its slopes. Below, Sisera's chariots line up along the dry bed of the river Kishon. Storm clouds roll in; rain pours down, the river rises and floods the plain, and the chariot wheels sink in the mud. Barak's men run down the mountain, and Sisera's army scatters. *(Play: the torrent Kishon.)*

1. **[Q] Judges 4:14** "And Deborah said to Barak, "Up! For this is the day in which the LORD has given Sisera into your hand. Does not the LORD go out before you?" So Barak went down from Mount Tabor with ten thousand men following him."
2. *(Play: the torrent Kishon.)*
3. **[Q] Judges 5:20** "From heaven fought the stars, from their courses they fought against Sisera."
4. **[Q] Judges 5:21** "The torrent Kishon swept them away, the onrushing torrent, the torrent Kishon. March on, my soul, with might!"
5. **[Q] Judges 4:15** "And the LORD routed Sisera and all his chariots and all his army before Barak at the edge of the sword; and Sisera alighted from his chariot and fled away on foot."

### Scene 2.3 · The song of Deborah
*On screen:* Evening. Sisera, alone and on foot, reaches a tent of black goat hair. The tent from outside, at night, a lamp inside, then dark. In the morning, Deborah and Barak stand on the hill and sing, and the sun rises over Mount Tabor in glory.

1. **[Q] Judges 4:17** "But Sisera fled away on foot to the tent of Jael, the wife of Heber the Kenite; for there was peace between Jabin the king of Hazor and the house of Heber the Kenite."
2. Jael welcomed him in, gave him milk, and covered him with a rug. And while he lay asleep, she killed him. As Deborah had said, the enemy's general fell "into the hand of a woman."
3. **[Q] Judges 5:1** "Then sang Deborah and Barak the son of Abino-am on that day:"
4. **[Q] Judges 5:3** ""Hear, O kings; give ear, O princes; to the LORD I will sing, I will make melody to the LORD, the God of Israel."
5. **[Q] Judges 5:7** "The peasantry ceased in Israel, they ceased until you arose, Deborah, arose as a mother in Israel."
6. **[Q] Judges 5:12** ""Awake, awake, Deborah! Awake, awake, utter a song! Arise, Barak, lead away your captives, O son of Abino-am."
7. Far away, Sisera's mother waited at her window for a son who would never come home.
8. **[Q] Judges 5:31** ""So perish all thine enemies, O LORD! But thy friends be like the sun as he rises in his might." And the land had rest for forty years."

### Play: the torrent Kishon
- **What it is:** pouring and water (spec §13.7, kind 12).
- **How it plays:** the plain below Mount Tabor, the dry riverbed of the Kishon winding across it, and Sisera's chariots drawn up along its banks. Storm clouds gather over the hills. The player taps the clouds (or Space) to send the rain down, and steers each stream down the hillsides into the river (drag, or the arrow keys). The river rises, overflows, and turns the plain to mud; one by one the chariots sink to their axles. When the water is high, Deborah cries "Up!", and Barak's men run down the mountain.
- **Teaches:** that Israel won not because they were strong, but because "the LORD went out before" them; nine hundred chariots of iron could not stand in the mud.
- **Gentle mode:** the streams find their own way to the river.
- **Accessibility:** a pointer, or the arrow keys and Space.

**Thread:** *"But thy friends be like the sun as he rises in his might" → "Then the righteous will shine like the sun in the kingdom of their Father."* The glowing object is the sun rising over Mount Tabor as Deborah's song ends.
- **[Q] Matthew 13:43:** "Then the righteous will shine like the sun in the kingdom of their Father. He who has ears, let him hear."
- **What it shows:** Deborah's song ends with a prayer that God's friends would shine like the sunrise. Jesus promises that this will truly happen: at the end of time, those who love God will shine like the sun in their Father's kingdom. Mount Tabor, where Barak's men waited, is the mountain where Christians remember the Transfiguration, when Jesus' face "shone like the sun," and his friends saw a glimpse of that glory.
- **Source:** Matthew 13:43; 17:1–2; Daniel 12:3; Catechism 554–556, 1042.

**Where and when:**
- **Mount Tabor.** A round mountain, 575 meters high, standing alone at the edge of the Jezreel Valley in Galilee. Since the 300s, Christians have honored it as the mountain of the Transfiguration.
- **The river Kishon.** It runs across the Jezreel Valley to the sea near Mount Carmel. In summer it is almost dry, but winter storms can flood the whole plain in a few hours.
- **Chariots of iron.** The tanks of the ancient world, unbeatable on hard, flat ground, and useless in mud.
- **Deborah.** The only judge who is also called a prophetess, and the only woman among the judges. Her song in Judges 5 is one of the oldest poems in the Bible.
- **Jael.** A woman of the Kenites, the family of Moses' father-in-law. *How it's shown:* we see only her tent from outside, at night.

**Read:** Judges 4; 5:1–31.

## Episode 3 · Gideon's Fleece and Three Hundred Men

**Judges 6–8 · about 10 minutes · 3 scenes**

### Scene 3.1 · In the wine press
*On screen:* Harvest time, but the fields are bare: the Midianites have come "like locusts," with tents and camels without number, and eaten everything. In a stone wine press in the hillside, hidden from view, a young man, Gideon, beats out a little wheat. Under the oak above him sits a stranger with a staff, shining faintly.

1. **[Q] Judges 6:6** "And Israel was brought very low because of Midian; and the people of Israel cried for help to the LORD."
2. **[Q] Judges 6:11** "Now the angel of the LORD came and sat under the oak at Ophrah, which belonged to Joash the Abiezrite, as his son Gideon was beating out wheat in the wine press, to hide it from the Midianites."
3. **[Q] Judges 6:12** "And the angel of the LORD appeared to him and said to him, "The LORD is with you, you mighty man of valor.""
4. **[Q] Judges 6:13** "And Gideon said to him, "Pray, sir, if the LORD is with us, why then has all this befallen us? And where are all his wonderful deeds which our fathers recounted to us, saying, 'Did not the LORD bring us up from Egypt?' But now the LORD has cast us off, and given us into the hand of Midian.""
5. **[Q] Judges 6:14** "And the LORD turned to him and said, "Go in this might of yours and deliver Israel from the hand of Midian; do not I send you?""
6. **[Q] Judges 6:15** "And he said to him, "Pray, Lord, how can I deliver Israel? Behold, my clan is the weakest in Manasseh, and I am the least in my family.""
7. **[Q] Judges 6:16** "And the LORD said to him, "But I will be with you, and you shall smite the Midianites as one man.""
8. Fire sprang from the rock and burned up Gideon's offering, and the angel vanished. That night Gideon pulled down his father's altar to Baal, and built an altar to the LORD.

### Scene 3.2 · The fleece and the water
*On screen:* A threshing floor at night, under the stars, with a fleece of white wool laid in the middle. In the morning the fleece is soaked and the ground is dry; Gideon wrings out a whole bowl of water. The next morning, the fleece is dry and the ground is wet with dew. Then the spring of Harod, at the foot of a mountain: thousands of men kneel to drink, and a few scoop the water up in their hands.

1. **[Q] Judges 6:37** "behold, I am laying a fleece of wool on the threshing floor; if there is dew on the fleece alone, and it is dry on all the ground, then I shall know that thou wilt deliver Israel by my hand, as thou hast said.""
2. **[Q] Judges 6:38** "And it was so. When he rose early next morning and squeezed the fleece, he wrung enough dew from the fleece to fill a bowl with water."
3. **[Q] Judges 6:39** "Then Gideon said to God, "Let not thy anger burn against me, let me speak but this once; pray, let me make trial only this once with the fleece; pray, let it be dry only on the fleece, and on all the ground let there be dew.""
4. **[Q] Judges 6:40** "And God did so that night; for it was dry on the fleece only, and on all the ground there was dew."
5. **[Q] Judges 7:2** "The LORD said to Gideon, "The people with you are too many for me to give the Midianites into their hand, lest Israel vaunt themselves against me, saying, 'My own hand has delivered me.'"
6. Twenty-two thousand men who were afraid went home. Ten thousand stayed, and still God said, "The people are still too many."
7. **[Q] Judges 7:6** "And the number of those that lapped, putting their hands to their mouths, was three hundred men; but all the rest of the people knelt down to drink water."
8. **[Q] Judges 7:7** "And the LORD said to Gideon, "With the three hundred men that lapped I will deliver you, and give the Midianites into your hand; and let all the others go every man to his home.""

### Scene 3.3 · For the LORD and for Gideon
*On screen:* Night. The Midianite camp fills the valley below, tents and camels as far as the eye can see. Three hundred men creep down the hills in three companies, each with a trumpet and a clay jar with a torch hidden inside. Gideon lifts his trumpet; the jars smash, three hundred torches blaze in the dark, the trumpets sound, and the whole camp wakes and runs away. *(Play: torches and trumpets.)*

1. **[Q] Judges 7:16** "And he divided the three hundred men into three companies, and put trumpets into the hands of all of them and empty jars, with torches inside the jars."
2. **[Q] Judges 7:17** "And he said to them, "Look at me, and do likewise; when I come to the outskirts of the camp, do as I do."
3. *(Play: torches and trumpets.)*
4. **[Q] Judges 7:20** "And the three companies blew the trumpets and broke the jars, holding in their left hands the torches, and in their right hands the trumpets to blow; and they cried, "A sword for the LORD and for Gideon!""
5. **[Q] Judges 7:21** "They stood every man in his place round about the camp, and all the army ran; they cried out and fled."
6. **[Q] Judges 8:22** "Then the men of Israel said to Gideon, "Rule over us, you and your son and your grandson also; for you have delivered us out of the hand of Midian.""
7. **[Q] Judges 8:23** "Gideon said to them, "I will not rule over you, and my son will not rule over you; the LORD will rule over you.""
8. But Gideon took the Midianites' gold and made a priestly garment, an ephod, and set it up in his town. Before long the people were worshiping it, and it became a snare to Gideon and his family.
9. **[Q] Judges 8:28** "So Midian was subdued before the people of Israel, and they lifted up their heads no more. And the land had rest forty years in the days of Gideon."

### Play: torches and trumpets
- **What it is:** timing (spec §13.7, kind 16; the spec names Gideon's trumpets and torches).
- **How it plays:** the dark hills around the Midianite camp, with Gideon's three companies hidden in a ring. Gideon said, "Look at me, and do likewise," and that is the game: the player watches Gideon on the hill. When he lifts his trumpet, the player blows (tap, or Space); when he smashes his jar, the player smashes too, and the torches flare. Company by company, all around the camp, the lights spread in the dark until the valley is ringed with fire, and the player taps for the great shout, "A sword for the LORD and for Gideon!"
- **Teaches:** that God gave the victory to three hundred men with jars and trumpets, so that no one could say, "My own hand has delivered me."
- **Gentle mode:** any tap near Gideon's signal counts, and he waits for the player.
- **Accessibility:** a pointer, or Space.

**Thread:** *Torches hidden in clay jars blaze out when the jars are broken → "We have this treasure in earthen vessels."* The glowing object is the torches, shining out of the broken jars.
- **[Q] 2 Corinthians 4:6:** "For it is the God who said, "Let light shine out of darkness," who has shone in our hearts to give the light of the knowledge of the glory of God in the face of Christ."
- **[Q] 2 Corinthians 4:7:** "But we have this treasure in earthen vessels, to show that the transcendent power belongs to God and not to us."
- **What it shows:** Gideon's men carried their light hidden in ordinary clay jars, and it only shone out when the jars were broken. Saint Paul says that we are like those jars: plain, fragile, easily broken. But God has put inside us a great treasure, the light of Christ, so that everyone can see that the power is God's and not ours. Gideon was the least in the weakest clan, and God chose him for exactly that reason.
- **Source:** 2 Corinthians 4:6–11; 12:9; 1 Corinthians 1:27–29; Catechism 272–273.

**Where and when:**
- **A wine press.** A pit cut in the rock, where grapes were trodden. Wheat was normally threshed on an open hilltop where the wind could carry off the chaff; Gideon hid in the wine press so the Midianites wouldn't see him.
- **"The LORD is peace."** The name Gideon gave his altar: in Hebrew, *Adonai Shalom*.
- **The fleece.** A sheepskin with the wool still on it. Christians have long seen in Gideon's fleece, wet with dew from heaven while the ground was dry, a picture of Mary, who received the Son of God from heaven. Psalm 72:6, "like rain that falls on the mown grass," was read in the old Latin Bible as "like rain upon the fleece," and is sung in Advent.
- **The spring of Harod.** At the foot of Mount Gilboa, in the Jezreel Valley. It still flows today, at Ein Harod.
- **Camels.** The Midianites were among the first raiders to ride camels, which let them cross the desert and strike far from home.
- **The middle watch.** The night was divided into three watches. The middle watch began around ten o'clock, when the new guards had just settled down.

**Read:** Judges 6:1–8:28.

## Episode 4 · Jephthah's Vow

**Judges 10:6–11:40 · about 8 minutes · 3 scenes**

> **A note before this episode:** this is one of the saddest stories in the Bible. Jephthah makes a careless promise to God, and his daughter pays for it. The game tells what the Bible says in one plain sentence, and shows nothing.

### Scene 4.1 · "Only deliver us"
*On screen:* The hills of Gilead, east of the Jordan. Families carry their idols out of their houses and throw them down. Then a camp of outlaws in the wild land of Tob, and a tough, scarred man at its fire: Jephthah. The elders of Gilead come walking up the road to him, hats in their hands.

1. **[Q] Judges 10:10** "And the people of Israel cried to the LORD, saying, "We have sinned against thee, because we have forsaken our God and have served the Baals.""
2. **[Q] Judges 10:15** "And the people of Israel said to the LORD, "We have sinned; do to us whatever seems good to thee; only deliver us, we pray thee, this day.""
3. **[Q] Judges 10:16** "So they put away the foreign gods from among them and served the LORD; and he became indignant over the misery of Israel."
4. **[Q] Judges 11:1** "Now Jephthah the Gileadite was a mighty warrior, but he was the son of a harlot. Gilead was the father of Jephthah."
5. **[Q] Judges 11:2** "And Gilead's wife also bore him sons; and when his wife's sons grew up, they thrust Jephthah out, and said to him, "You shall not inherit in our father's house; for you are the son of another woman.""
6. When the Ammonites made war on Gilead, the elders came looking for the man they had driven away.
7. **[Q] Judges 11:7** "But Jephthah said to the elders of Gilead, "Did you not hate me, and drive me out of my father's house? Why have you come to me now when you are in trouble?""
8. **[Q] Judges 11:11** "So Jephthah went with the elders of Gilead, and the people made him head and leader over them; and Jephthah spoke all his words before the LORD at Mizpah."

### Scene 4.2 · The message to Ammon
*On screen:* Jephthah's messengers ride east across the hills to the Ammonite king's tent. Over the scene, a map unrolls: the way Israel came long ago, from Egypt through the wilderness, around Edom and Moab, to the river Arnon. *(Play: the way we came.)*

1. **[Q] Judges 11:12** "Then Jephthah sent messengers to the king of the Ammonites and said, "What have you against me, that you have come to me to fight against my land?""
2. **[Q] Judges 11:13** "And the king of the Ammonites answered the messengers of Jephthah, "Because Israel on coming from Egypt took away my land, from the Arnon to the Jabbok and to the Jordan; now therefore restore it peaceably.""
3. *(Play: the way we came.)*
4. **[Q] Judges 11:27** "I therefore have not sinned against you, and you do me wrong by making war on me; the LORD, the Judge, decide this day between the people of Israel and the people of Ammon.""
5. **[Q] Judges 11:28** "But the king of the Ammonites did not heed the message of Jephthah which he sent to him."

### Scene 4.3 · The vow
*On screen:* Jephthah on the road to battle, stopping to make his vow. Then evening at his house in Mizpah, and the door opening: his only daughter runs out to meet him, with a timbrel and dancing. He tears his clothes. Then the mountains, and a group of young women walking together among the hills.

1. **[Q] Judges 11:29** "Then the Spirit of the LORD came upon Jephthah, and he passed through Gilead and Manasseh, and passed on to Mizpah of Gilead, and from Mizpah of Gilead he passed on to the Ammonites."
2. **[Q] Judges 11:30** "And Jephthah made a vow to the LORD, and said, "If thou wilt give the Ammonites into my hand,"
3. **[Q] Judges 11:31** "then whoever comes forth from the doors of my house to meet me, when I return victorious from the Ammonites, shall be the LORD's, and I will offer him up for a burnt offering.""
4. **[Q] Judges 11:32** "So Jephthah crossed over to the Ammonites to fight against them; and the LORD gave them into his hand."

### Play: the way we came
- **What it is:** courier routes (spec §13.7, kind 15).
- **How it plays:** the king of Ammon claims that Israel stole his land. Jephthah answers by telling the story of how Israel really came, and the player traces it on the map as he speaks (drag, or the arrow keys and Space): out of Egypt to the Red Sea; through the wilderness to Kadesh; around Edom and Moab, never through them; across the Arnon; and up to the Jabbok, the land of Sihon, who attacked them. If the path tries to cut through Edom or Moab, it stops at the border, and Jephthah says, "The king of Edom would not listen."
- **Teaches:** that Jephthah tried words before war, and knew what God had done for his people. The player remembers the journey from Levels 9–13.
- **Gentle mode:** the path is drawn faintly, and the player only follows it.
- **Accessibility:** a pointer, or the arrow keys and Space.

**Thread:** *Jephthah makes a careless vow, trying to bargain with God for victory → Jesus: "Let what you say be simply 'Yes' or 'No.'"* The glowing object is his daughter's timbrel, remembered with love by the daughters of Israel.

- **[Q] Matthew 5:37:** "Let what you say be simply 'Yes' or 'No'; anything more than this comes from evil."
- **What it shows:** God had already given Jephthah his Spirit, and needed nothing from him. A true promise to God, a vow, is made freely and carefully, for something good.

**Where and when:**

- **Mizpah of Gilead.** Its name means "watchtower." It may be the place where Jacob and Laban set up a heap of stones and said, "The LORD watch between you and me" (Level 4).
- **The Ammonites.** A people east of Gilead. Their capital, Rabbah, is Amman, the capital of Jordan today.
- **The Arnon and the Jabbok.** Two rivers flowing west into the Jordan and the Dead Sea, in deep gorges. Jacob wrestled with God at the Jabbok (Level 4).
- **What happened to Jephthah's daughter?** The Bible says only that her father kept his vow, and that "she had never known a man." Most readers, Jewish and Christian, have understood that he offered her as a sacrifice. Since the Middle Ages, some have thought instead that he gave her to God's service for life, so she never married. Either way, the vow was wrong.

**Read:** Judges 10:6–11:40.

**Where and when:**

- **A Nazirite.** Someone set apart for God by a special promise: no wine, no razor on the head, and no touching a dead body (Numbers 6). Most Nazirites made the promise for a time; Samson was to keep it all his life.
- **The Philistines.** A people from across the sea who settled on the coast around 1175 BC, in five cities: Gaza, Ashkelon, Ashdod, Ekron and Gath. They knew how to work iron, and the land of Israel later took its Roman name, Palestine, from them.
- **Dagon.** The Philistines' chief god, probably a god of grain.
- **Gaza.** The southernmost Philistine city, near the sea, still a city today.

- **How it plays:** Micah's shrine, with everything he has gathered in it: the carved idol, the cast idol, the ephod, the household gods, his son dressed as a priest, and the hired Levite.
