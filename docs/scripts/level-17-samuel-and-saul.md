# Level 17 script: *Samuel and Saul*

**Bible:** 1 Samuel 1–10; 13; 15
**Age:** VI · The Kingdom
**Length:** about 50 minutes (6 episodes, then the quiz, then reflection and prayer)
**Status:** approved by Dave on 2026-11-11 ("Approved, build it").

## How to read this script

- Each **scene** is a checkpoint. Its numbered lines are the narration beats; the player taps to move from one to the next.
- *On screen* describes what the scene shows. It's the brief for the art in both styles.
- **[Q]** marks an exact quotation from the Bible. Its wording in both translations is in the verse table at the end. Everything else is our own retelling.
- **Play** is the episode's interactive moment. **Thread** is its golden thread. **Read** is the passage the game sends the player to.
- "LORD" in capitals stands for God's holy name, printed in small capitals in the RSV.

### What this level is about

The age of the judges ends with a mother's prayer. Hannah, who has no children, pours out her heart at Shiloh, and God gives her a son, Samuel, whom she gives back to him. In the night, the boy hears God call his name and answers, "Speak, for thy servant hears." Samuel becomes the last of the judges and a great prophet. But Israel loses the ark of God to the Philistines, and when it comes home, the people still want something else: "a king to govern us like all the nations." God gives them Saul, tall and handsome, anointed with oil. But Saul will not wait for God and will not obey him, and Samuel has to tell him: "to obey is better than sacrifice."

Hannah's song is the first song in the Bible about God's "anointed" king, the Messiah. Mary would sing it again, almost word for word, when she carried Jesus.

**How it's shown:** Episodes 3 and 6 begin with a short note. The battle where the ark is lost and the war against Amalek are told in a sentence or two, and not shown.

## Episode 1 · Hannah's Prayer

**1 Samuel 1:1–2:26 · about 9 minutes · 3 scenes**

### Scene 1.1 · At Shiloh
*On screen:* The house of the LORD at Shiloh, a tent-shrine on a hill, at feast time. A family sits at a sacrificial meal: Elkanah, his wife Peninnah with her many children, and Hannah, alone, not eating. Peninnah whispers something cruel. Hannah gets up and goes to pray. *(Play: pouring out her soul.)*

1. **[Q] 1 Samuel 1:2** "He had two wives; the name of the one was Hannah, and the name of the other Peninnah. And Peninnah had children, but Hannah had no children."
2. **[Q] 1 Samuel 1:6** "And her rival used to provoke her sorely, to irritate her, because the LORD had closed her womb."
3. **[Q] 1 Samuel 1:10** "She was deeply distressed and prayed to the LORD, and wept bitterly."
4. **[Q] 1 Samuel 1:11** "And she vowed a vow and said, "O LORD of hosts, if thou wilt indeed look on the affliction of thy maidservant, and remember me, and not forget thy maidservant, but wilt give to thy maidservant a son, then I will give him to the LORD all the days of his life, and no razor shall touch his head.""
5. *(Play: pouring out her soul.)*

### Scene 1.2 · Only her lips moved
*On screen:* Old Eli, the priest, sits on a seat by the doorpost of the temple, watching Hannah. Her lips move, but no sound comes. He frowns, then listens, then blesses her. A year later, Hannah holds a baby boy.

1. **[Q] 1 Samuel 1:13** "Hannah was speaking in her heart; only her lips moved, and her voice was not heard; therefore Eli took her to be a drunken woman."
2. **[Q] 1 Samuel 1:15** "But Hannah answered, "No, my lord, I am a woman sorely troubled; I have drunk neither wine nor strong drink, but I have been pouring out my soul before the LORD."
3. **[Q] 1 Samuel 1:17** "Then Eli answered, "Go in peace, and the God of Israel grant your petition which you have made to him.""
4. **[Q] 1 Samuel 1:20** "and in due time Hannah conceived and bore a son, and she called his name Samuel, for she said, "I have asked him of the LORD.""

### Scene 1.3 · Lent to the LORD
*On screen:* Hannah climbs the hill to Shiloh again, holding the hand of a small boy, with a bull, a sack of flour and a skin of wine. She gives Samuel to Eli. Then she lifts her hands and sings, and the light rises around her. Years pass: every year she brings Samuel a new little robe, a little bigger each time.

1. **[Q] 1 Samuel 1:24** "And when she had weaned him, she took him up with her, along with a three-year-old bull, an ephah of flour, and a skin of wine; and she brought him to the house of the LORD at Shiloh; and the child was young."
2. **[Q] 1 Samuel 1:27** "For this child I prayed; and the LORD has granted me my petition which I made to him."
3. **[Q] 1 Samuel 1:28** "Therefore I have lent him to the LORD; as long as he lives, he is lent to the LORD." And they worshiped the LORD there."
4. **[Q] 1 Samuel 2:1** "Hannah also prayed and said, "My heart exults in the LORD; my strength is exalted in the LORD. My mouth derides my enemies, because I rejoice in thy salvation."
5. **[Q] 1 Samuel 2:2** ""There is none holy like the LORD, there is none besides thee; there is no rock like our God."
6. **[Q] 1 Samuel 2:8** "He raises up the poor from the dust; he lifts the needy from the ash heap, to make them sit with princes and inherit a seat of honor. For the pillars of the earth are the LORD's, and on them he has set the world."
7. **[Q] 1 Samuel 2:10** "The adversaries of the LORD shall be broken to pieces; against them he will thunder in heaven. The LORD will judge the ends of the earth; he will give strength to his king, and exalt the power of his anointed.""
8. **[Q] 1 Samuel 2:19** "And his mother used to make for him a little robe and take it to him each year, when she went up with her husband to offer the yearly sacrifice."
9. **[Q] 1 Samuel 2:26** "Now the boy Samuel continued to grow both in stature and in favor with the LORD and with men."

### Play: pouring out her soul
- **What it is:** a quiet interaction (spec §13.7, kind 20).
- **How it plays:** Hannah kneels at the door of the house of the LORD. The player presses and holds (the pointer, or Space) while she prays in silence: her lips move, and a soft light rises from her, a little higher the longer she prays. Letting go is fine; she rests, and prays on when the player holds again. When the light reaches the top, the prayer is finished.
- **Teaches:** that prayer can be silent, from the heart, and that God hears it.
- **Gentle mode:** one tap and she prays on by herself.
- **Accessibility:** a pointer, or Space.

**Thread:** *Hannah's song: "He raises up the poor from the dust" → Mary's Magnificat: "he has put down the mighty from their thrones, and exalted those of low degree."* The glowing object is the light around Hannah as she sings.
- **[Q] Luke 1:46:** "And Mary said, "My soul magnifies the Lord,"
- **[Q] Luke 1:47:** "and my spirit rejoices in God my Savior,"
- **[Q] Luke 1:52:** "he has put down the mighty from their thrones, and exalted those of low degree;"
- **[Q] Luke 1:53:** "he has filled the hungry with good things, and the rich he has sent empty away."
- **What it shows:** Hannah, who had been mocked for having no child, sang that God lifts up the lowly and brings down the proud, and she ended her song with the "anointed" king, the Messiah. A thousand years later, Mary, a young woman of Nazareth, sang almost the same song when she was carrying Jesus, the Anointed One himself. The Church sings Mary's song, the Magnificat, every evening.
- **Source:** Luke 1:46–55; Catechism 489, 2619, 2675.

**Where and when:**
- **Shiloh.** The town in the hills of Ephraim where the tabernacle had stood since Joshua's time (Level 14). By now it had a "temple," with doorposts.
- **About 1100 BC.** The last years of the judges (Level 15), soon after Ruth's time (Level 16).
- **"No razor shall touch his head."** Hannah promised that her son would be a Nazirite, set apart for God, like Samson (Level 15).
- **"Samuel."** Hannah said, "I have asked him of the LORD." The name sounds like the Hebrew for "heard of God."
- **The little robe.** Each year Hannah made Samuel a new one, as he grew.

**Read:** 1 Samuel 1:1–2:11; 2:18–26.

## Episode 2 · "Speak, Lord, for Your Servant Hears"

**1 Samuel 3 · about 7 minutes · 3 scenes**

### Scene 2.1 · The lamp of God
*On screen:* Night in the house of the LORD at Shiloh. The golden lampstand burns low beside the curtain, and behind it, in the dark, the ark of the covenant. Old Eli lies in his room, nearly blind. The boy Samuel sleeps on a mat near the ark.

1. **[Q] 1 Samuel 3:1** "Now the boy Samuel was ministering to the LORD under Eli. And the word of the LORD was rare in those days; there was no frequent vision."
2. **[Q] 1 Samuel 3:2** "At that time Eli, whose eyesight had begun to grow dim, so that he could not see, was lying down in his own place;"
3. **[Q] 1 Samuel 3:3** "the lamp of God had not yet gone out, and Samuel was lying down within the temple of the LORD, where the ark of God was."

### Scene 2.2 · "Samuel! Samuel!"
*On screen:* A voice calls, and Samuel sits up and runs to Eli. Again, and again. The third time, Eli sits up slowly and understands. Samuel goes back and lies down, wide awake, listening. *(Play: here I am.)*

1. **[Q] 1 Samuel 3:4** "Then the LORD called, "Samuel! Samuel!" and he said, "Here I am!""
2. **[Q] 1 Samuel 3:7** "Now Samuel did not yet know the LORD, and the word of the LORD had not yet been revealed to him."
3. *(Play: here I am.)*
4. **[Q] 1 Samuel 3:10** "And the LORD came and stood forth, calling as at other times, "Samuel! Samuel!" And Samuel said, "Speak, for thy servant hears.""

### Scene 2.3 · A prophet of the LORD
*On screen:* Morning. Samuel opens the doors of the house of the LORD. Then the years pass: Samuel grows into a young man, and people come from all over Israel to hear him.

1. That night God told Samuel a hard thing: Eli's sons had robbed God's offerings and done evil at Shiloh, and Eli had not stopped them, so judgment would come on his house. In the morning Samuel was afraid to tell Eli, but he told him everything.
2. **[Q] 1 Samuel 3:19** "And Samuel grew, and the LORD was with him and let none of his words fall to the ground."
3. **[Q] 1 Samuel 3:20** "And all Israel from Dan to Beer-sheba knew that Samuel was established as a prophet of the LORD."

### Play: here I am
- **What it is:** a quiet interaction, walking toward God's voice (spec §13.7, kind 20).
- **How it plays:** the dark temple, the lamp burning low. The voice calls, "Samuel!" and the player chooses what Samuel does: run to Eli, roll over and sleep, or answer the LORD. Three times he runs to Eli, who sends him back, until Eli understands and tells him what to say. The fourth time, the player chooses Samuel's answer: "Speak, for thy servant hears."
- **Teaches:** the story of Samuel's call, and that hearing God often begins with someone who helps us recognize his voice, as Eli did.
- **Gentle mode:** the right choice glows.
- **Accessibility:** a pointer, or the arrow keys and Space.

**Thread:** *"Speak, for thy servant hears" → Jesus: "My sheep hear my voice, and I know them, and they follow me."* The glowing object is the lamp of God.
- **[Q] John 10:27:** "My sheep hear my voice, and I know them, and they follow me;"
- **What it shows:** in Samuel's time "the word of the LORD was rare," and the lamp of God was burning low. But God called a boy by name, and the boy listened. Jesus says that he still calls each of his sheep by name, and that they know his voice. We learn to hear it in prayer, in Scripture and in the Church, and, like Samuel, we often need someone like Eli to help us recognize it.
- **Source:** John 10:1–16, 27; Catechism 2578, 2653–2654, 2716.

**Where and when:**
- **The lamp of God.** The golden lampstand of the tabernacle (Level 11), kept burning "from evening to morning." It "had not yet gone out": it was still night, almost dawn.
- **Eli's sons.** Hophni and Phinehas, priests who took the best of the offerings for themselves.
- **"From Dan to Beer-sheba."** From the northernmost town to the southernmost (Level 15): all Israel.

**Read:** 1 Samuel 3:1–21.

## Episode 3 · The Ark Is Captured

**1 Samuel 4–6 · about 8 minutes · 3 scenes**

> **A note before this episode:** in this episode Israel loses a battle, and Eli and his sons die. It is told in a few sentences, and no fighting is shown.

### Scene 3.1 · The ark taken into battle
*On screen:* Israel's camp at Ebenezer, the Philistine camp at Aphek across the valley. Priests carry the ark into the camp, and the soldiers shout so loud the ground shakes. Then dust in the distance, and the ark carried away on Philistine shoulders.

1. **[Q] 1 Samuel 4:3** "And when the troops came to the camp, the elders of Israel said, "Why has the LORD put us to rout today before the Philistines? Let us bring the ark of the covenant of the LORD here from Shiloh, that he may come among us and save us from the power of our enemies.""
2. They treated the ark like a lucky charm, as if God had to win for them whether they obeyed him or not.
3. **[Q] 1 Samuel 4:10** "So the Philistines fought, and Israel was defeated, and they fled, every man to his home; and there was a very great slaughter, for there fell of Israel thirty thousand foot soldiers."
4. **[Q] 1 Samuel 4:11** "And the ark of God was captured; and the two sons of Eli, Hophni and Phinehas, were slain."

### Scene 3.2 · "The glory has departed"
*On screen:* Shiloh's gate. Old Eli sits on his seat by the road, waiting, trembling for the ark. A runner arrives with torn clothes and dust on his head. Then a house in Shiloh, and a young mother with a newborn baby.

1. **[Q] 1 Samuel 4:17** "He who brought the tidings answered and said, "Israel has fled before the Philistines, and there has also been a great slaughter among the people; your two sons also, Hophni and Phinehas, are dead, and the ark of God has been captured.""
2. **[Q] 1 Samuel 4:18** "When he mentioned the ark of God, Eli fell over backward from his seat by the side of the gate; and his neck was broken and he died, for he was an old man, and heavy. He had judged Israel forty years."
3. At that moment Phinehas' wife gave birth to a son, and as she was dying, she gave him his name.
4. **[Q] 1 Samuel 4:21** "And she named the child Ichabod, saying, "The glory has departed from Israel!" because the ark of God had been captured and because of her father-in-law and her husband."

### Scene 3.3 · Dagon falls, and the ark comes home
*On screen:* The temple of Dagon at Ashdod. The ark stands beside the great statue of Dagon. In the morning, Dagon lies face down before the ark, and the next morning he is broken. Then a new cart, two cows, and the ark on the cart, rolling up the road toward Beth-shemesh, where people reaping wheat look up and cheer. *(Play: the road to Beth-shemesh.)*

1. **[Q] 1 Samuel 5:2** "then the Philistines took the ark of God and brought it into the house of Dagon and set it up beside Dagon."
2. **[Q] 1 Samuel 5:3** "And when the people of Ashdod rose early the next day, behold, Dagon had fallen face downward on the ground before the ark of the LORD. So they took Dagon and put him back in his place."
3. **[Q] 1 Samuel 5:4** "But when they rose early on the next morning, behold, Dagon had fallen face downward on the ground before the ark of the LORD, and the head of Dagon and both his hands were lying cut off upon the threshold; only the trunk of Dagon was left to him."
4. **[Q] 1 Samuel 5:6** "The hand of the LORD was heavy upon the people of Ashdod, and he terrified and afflicted them with tumors, both Ashdod and its territory."
5. **[Q] 1 Samuel 6:7** "Now then, take and prepare a new cart and two milch cows upon which there has never come a yoke, and yoke the cows to the cart, but take their calves home, away from them."
6. *(Play: the road to Beth-shemesh.)*
7. **[Q] 1 Samuel 6:12** "And the cows went straight in the direction of Beth-shemesh along one highway, lowing as they went; they turned neither to the right nor to the left, and the lords of the Philistines went after them as far as the border of Beth-shemesh."
8. **[Q] 1 Samuel 6:13** "Now the people of Beth-shemesh were reaping their wheat harvest in the valley; and when they lifted up their eyes and saw the ark, they rejoiced to see it."

### Play: the road to Beth-shemesh
- **What it is:** pathfinding (spec §13.7, kind 6).
- **How it plays:** the road from Ekron up into the hills of Judah. The two cows pull the cart with the ark, lowing for their calves left behind, and yet they go straight on toward Israel. The player leads them along the road, bend by bend (drag, or the arrow keys; Space goes on to the next bend), and the lords of the Philistines follow behind, watching.
- **Teaches:** that the ark came home not by any army, but because God brought it.
- **Gentle mode:** the cows walk on by themselves.
- **Accessibility:** a pointer, or the arrow keys and Space.

**Thread:** *Dagon falls on his face before the ark of the LORD → "at the name of Jesus every knee should bow."* The glowing object is the ark in the temple of Dagon.
- **[Q] Philippians 2:9:** "Therefore God has highly exalted him and bestowed on him the name which is above every name,"
- **[Q] Philippians 2:10:** "that at the name of Jesus every knee should bow, in heaven and on earth and under the earth,"
- **What it shows:** the Philistines thought they had captured the God of Israel. But in the night their own god fell on his face before the ark, as if bowing down. No idol can stand before the true God. Saint Paul says that one day every knee will bow at the name of Jesus, "in heaven and on earth and under the earth."
- **Source:** Philippians 2:5–11; Isaiah 45:23; Catechism 449, 2112–2114.

**Where and when:**
- **Ashdod and Ekron.** Two of the five Philistine cities near the coast (Level 15).
- **Dagon.** The Philistines' chief god, the same god whose temple fell on Samson (Level 15).
- **Ichabod.** The name means "Where is the glory?"
- **Beth-shemesh.** "House of the sun," a town of Israel on the edge of the hills, looking down on Philistine country.

**Read:** 1 Samuel 4:1–6:16.

## Episode 4 · Israel Asks for a King

**1 Samuel 7–8 · about 8 minutes · 3 scenes**

### Scene 4.1 · Ebenezer
*On screen:* Mizpah. The people pour water out on the ground before the LORD and fast, and throw away their idols. Thunder rolls over the hills, and the Philistines run. Samuel sets up a big standing stone.

1. **[Q] 1 Samuel 7:3** "Then Samuel said to all the house of Israel, "If you are returning to the LORD with all your heart, then put away the foreign gods and the Ashtaroth from among you, and direct your heart to the LORD, and serve him only, and he will deliver you out of the hand of the Philistines.""
2. The people put away their idols and gathered at Mizpah to pray. When the Philistines attacked, the LORD thundered against them with a great voice, and they ran.
3. **[Q] 1 Samuel 7:12** "Then Samuel took a stone and set it up between Mizpah and Jeshanah, and called its name Ebenezer; for he said, "Hitherto the LORD has helped us.""
4. **[Q] 1 Samuel 7:15** "Samuel judged Israel all the days of his life."

### Scene 4.2 · "Give us a king"
*On screen:* Years later, at Ramah. Samuel is old and white-haired. His sons, judges in the south, take bribes. The elders of all Israel arrive at his door.

1. **[Q] 1 Samuel 8:1** "When Samuel became old, he made his sons judges over Israel."
2. **[Q] 1 Samuel 8:3** "Yet his sons did not walk in his ways, but turned aside after gain; they took bribes and perverted justice."
3. **[Q] 1 Samuel 8:5** "and said to him, "Behold, you are old and your sons do not walk in your ways; now appoint for us a king to govern us like all the nations.""
4. **[Q] 1 Samuel 8:6** "But the thing displeased Samuel when they said, "Give us a king to govern us." And Samuel prayed to the LORD."
5. **[Q] 1 Samuel 8:7** "And the LORD said to Samuel, "Hearken to the voice of the people in all that they say to you; for they have not rejected you, but they have rejected me from being king over them."

### Scene 4.3 · The ways of the king
*On screen:* Samuel stands before the people in a village street, and as he speaks, things are taken away one by one: young men to the chariots, daughters to the palace kitchens, the best fields, a tenth of the grain, the cattle, the flocks. The people shout anyway. *(Play: what the king will take.)*

1. **[Q] 1 Samuel 8:11** "He said, "These will be the ways of the king who will reign over you: he will take your sons and appoint them to his chariots and to be his horsemen, and to run before his chariots;"
2. *(Play: what the king will take.)*
3. **[Q] 1 Samuel 8:18** "And in that day you will cry out because of your king, whom you have chosen for yourselves; but the LORD will not answer you in that day.""
4. **[Q] 1 Samuel 8:19** "But the people refused to listen to the voice of Samuel; and they said, "No! but we will have a king over us,"
5. **[Q] 1 Samuel 8:20** "that we also may be like all the nations, and that our king may govern us and go out before us and fight our battles.""
6. **[Q] 1 Samuel 8:22** "And the LORD said to Samuel, "Hearken to their voice, and make them a king." Samuel then said to the men of Israel, "Go every man to his city.""

### Play: what the king will take
- **What it is:** ordering (spec §13.7, kind 9).
- **How it plays:** Samuel's warning, item by item. The player chooses what the king will take next, in the order Samuel says it (choose, or the arrow keys and Space): your sons, for his chariots; your daughters, as cooks and bakers; your best fields and vineyards; a tenth of your grain; your servants and cattle; a tenth of your flocks. As each is chosen, it is taken from the village.
- **Teaches:** what Samuel warned the people a king would cost them, and that they chose it anyway.
- **Gentle mode:** the next one glows.
- **Accessibility:** a pointer, or the arrow keys and Space.

**Thread:** *Samuel's stone, Ebenezer: "Hitherto the LORD has helped us" → "The Lord is my helper, I will not be afraid."* The glowing object is the stone.
- **[Q] Hebrews 13:5:** "Keep your life free from love of money, and be content with what you have; for he has said, "I will never fail you nor forsake you.""
- **[Q] Hebrews 13:6:** "Hence we can confidently say, "The Lord is my helper, I will not be afraid; what can man do to me?""
- **What it shows:** after the people turned back to God, he helped them, and Samuel set up a stone so they would remember: "Hitherto," up to here, "the LORD has helped us." The people soon forgot, and asked for a king to fight their battles. The letter to the Hebrews reminds Christians of the same promise: "I will never fail you nor forsake you." Every Ebenezer in our own lives, every time God has helped us, is a reason to trust him for what is still to come.
- **Source:** Hebrews 13:5–6; Psalm 118:6; Catechism 301, 2828–2830.

**Where and when:**
- **Mizpah and Ramah.** Two towns in the hills of Benjamin, north of Jerusalem. Ramah was Samuel's home; every year he went around to Bethel, Gilgal and Mizpah to judge Israel.
- **"Like all the nations."** God had meant Israel to be different from the nations, with God himself as their king (Level 15, "the LORD will rule over you").
- **Ebenezer.** "Stone of help." The same name as the place where the ark was lost: now it was a place of victory.

**Read:** 1 Samuel 7:2–17; 8:1–22.

## Episode 5 · Saul Anointed

**1 Samuel 9–10 · about 9 minutes · 3 scenes**

### Scene 5.1 · The lost donkeys
*On screen:* The hill country of Benjamin. A tall young man, Saul, and his servant search the hills for his father's lost donkeys, day after day. At a town gate they meet an old man, Samuel, who has been expecting them.

1. **[Q] 1 Samuel 9:2** "and he had a son whose name was Saul, a handsome young man. There was not a man among the people of Israel more handsome than he; from his shoulders upward he was taller than any of the people."
2. **[Q] 1 Samuel 9:3** "Now the asses of Kish, Saul's father, were lost. So Kish said to Saul his son, "Take one of the servants with you, and arise, go and look for the asses.""
3. **[Q] 1 Samuel 9:16** ""Tomorrow about this time I will send to you a man from the land of Benjamin, and you shall anoint him to be prince over my people Israel. He shall save my people from the hand of Philistines; for I have seen the affliction of my people, because their cry has come to me.""
4. **[Q] 1 Samuel 9:17** "When Samuel saw Saul, the LORD told him, "Here is the man of whom I spoke to you! He it is who shall rule over my people.""
5. **[Q] 1 Samuel 9:20** "As for your asses that were lost three days ago, do not set your mind on them, for they have been found. And for whom is all that is desirable in Israel? Is it not for you and for all your father's house?""

### Scene 5.2 · A vial of oil
*On screen:* Dawn on the edge of town. Samuel sends the servant ahead, takes out a small flask of oil, and pours it on Saul's head. The flask glows. Saul goes home a different man.

1. **[Q] 1 Samuel 10:1** "Then Samuel took a vial of oil and poured it on his head, and kissed him and said, "Has not the LORD anointed you to be prince over his people Israel? And you shall reign over the people of the LORD and you will save them from the hand of their enemies round about. And this shall be the sign to you that the LORD has anointed you to be prince over his heritage."
2. **[Q] 1 Samuel 10:6** "Then the spirit of the LORD will come mightily upon you, and you shall prophesy with them and be turned into another man."
3. **[Q] 1 Samuel 10:9** "When he turned his back to leave Samuel, God gave him another heart; and all these signs came to pass that day."

### Scene 5.3 · Among the baggage
*On screen:* All the tribes gathered at Mizpah. The lot falls on Benjamin, on the family of Matri, on Saul, but Saul is nowhere to be seen. He is hiding among the piles of baggage at the edge of the camp. They find him and bring him out, a head taller than everyone. *(Play: hidden among the baggage.)*

1. **[Q] 1 Samuel 10:20** "Then Samuel brought all the tribes of Israel near, and the tribe of Benjamin was taken by lot."
2. **[Q] 1 Samuel 10:21** "He brought the tribe of Benjamin near by its families, and the family of the Matrites was taken by lot; finally he brought the family of the Matrites near man by man, and Saul the son of Kish was taken by lot. But when they sought him, he could not be found."
3. *(Play: hidden among the baggage.)*
4. **[Q] 1 Samuel 10:22** "So they inquired again of the LORD, "Did the man come hither?" and the LORD said, "Behold, he has hidden himself among the baggage.""
5. **[Q] 1 Samuel 10:23** "Then they ran and fetched him from there; and when he stood among the people, he was taller than any of the people from his shoulders upward."
6. **[Q] 1 Samuel 10:24** "And Samuel said to all the people, "Do you see him whom the LORD has chosen? There is none like him among all the people." And all the people shouted, "Long live the king!""

### Play: hidden among the baggage
- **What it is:** searching (spec §13.7, kind 8).
- **How it plays:** the edge of the camp, with piles of baggage: sacks, saddles, water jars, rolled tents. Saul is hiding behind one of them. The player searches the piles one by one (tap a pile, or Space for the next) until a pair of very tall feet gives him away.
- **Teaches:** that Saul began humbly, even shy of being king.
- **Gentle mode:** the right pile rustles.
- **Accessibility:** a pointer, or Space.

**Thread:** *Samuel anoints Saul with oil: the LORD's "anointed" → Jesus: "The Spirit of the Lord is upon me, because he has anointed me."* The glowing object is the vial of oil.
- **[Q] Luke 4:18:** ""The Spirit of the Lord is upon me, because he has anointed me to preach good news to the poor. He has sent me to proclaim release to the captives and recovering of sight to the blind, to set at liberty those who are oppressed,"
- **What it shows:** kings of Israel were anointed with oil as a sign that God had chosen them and given them his Spirit. In Hebrew, "anointed one" is *Messiah*; in Greek, *Christos*. Saul was the first of Israel's anointed kings, and he failed. Jesus is the Anointed One who never fails: at Nazareth he read Isaiah's words and said they were about him. At Confirmation, Christians too are anointed with holy oil, to share in his Spirit.
- **Source:** Luke 4:16–21; Isaiah 61:1; Catechism 436, 695, 1289.

**Where and when:**
- **Benjamin.** The smallest tribe (Level 15, Episode 6), between Judah and Ephraim. Saul's home was Gibeah.

### Play: the bleating of the sheep
- **What it is:** spot the twist (spec §13.7, kind 20).
- **How it plays:** Saul tells his side of the story, and the player compares it with what really happened, choosing the true answer each time (choose, or the arrow keys and Space). "I have performed the commandment of the LORD": but what does Samuel hear? "The people spared the best": but who was the king? "To sacrifice to the LORD": but what did God want? Each excuse falls apart.
- **Teaches:** how Saul made excuses and blamed others instead of admitting his sin, the opposite of David in Level 19.
- **Gentle mode:** the true answer glows.
- **Accessibility:** a pointer, or the arrow keys and Space.

**Thread:** *"To obey is better than sacrifice" → Jesus: "Lo, I have come to do thy will, O God."* The glowing object is the light around Samuel as he speaks God's word.
- **[Q] Hebrews 10:7:** "Then I said, 'Lo, I have come to do thy will, O God,' as it is written of me in the roll of the book.""
- **What it shows:** Saul thought he could make up for disobeying God by offering him sacrifices. But God doesn't want our gifts instead of our hearts. The letter to the Hebrews says that Jesus came into the world saying, "I have come to do thy will": his whole life, and his death on the cross, were one great act of obedience and love, the one sacrifice that truly pleased God.
- **Source:** Hebrews 10:5–10; Psalm 40:6–8; Psalm 51:16–17; Catechism 614, 2100.

**Where and when:**
- **Gilgal.** The old camp near Jericho (Level 14), where Saul had been made king before all Israel.
- **The Amalekites.** Desert raiders who attacked Israel at Rephidim (Level 9). God's command against them belongs with the hardest passages of the Bible, like the fall of Jericho (Level 14); the Church reads these wars in the light of Jesus, who taught us to love our enemies.
- **"A man after his own heart."** God's next king: David, a shepherd boy of Bethlehem (Level 18).

**Read:** 1 Samuel 13:5–14; 15:1–35.
