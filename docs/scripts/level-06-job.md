# Level 6 script: *Job*

**Bible:** the Book of Job
**Age:** II · The Patriarchs
**Length:** about 35 minutes (6 episodes, then the quiz, then reflection and prayer)
**Status:** approved by Dave on 2026-10-22 ("Approved").

## How to read this script

- Each **scene** is a checkpoint. Its numbered lines are the narration beats; the player taps to move from one to the next.
- *On screen* describes what the scene shows. It's the brief for the art in both styles.
- **[Q]** marks an exact quotation from the Bible. Its wording in both translations is in the verse table at the end. Everything else is our own retelling.
- **Play** is the episode's interactive moment. **Thread** is its golden thread. **Read** is the passage the game sends the player to.
- "LORD" in capitals stands for God's holy name, printed in small capitals in the RSV.

### What this level is about

The Book of Job is not a story about a family, like the rest of Genesis, but a long poem about one man's suffering. Job is good, rich and happy, until in a single day he loses everything: his flocks, his servants, and his ten children. Then his own health. His friends come to comfort him, and end up arguing that he must have done something to deserve it. Job knows he hasn't. He cries out to God: "Why?" And yet, in the darkest place, he says, "I know that my Redeemer lives." At last God answers him, not with an explanation, but out of a whirlwind, showing him the wonders of creation. Job bows before God's greatness and is at peace, and God restores him.

The book doesn't tell children that suffering is easy, or that it always makes sense. It tells them that God is near, that it is all right to bring our sorrow and even our questions to him, and that he is good. The Church reads Job's words at funerals, and Christians have always heard in them the voice of Jesus, the innocent one who suffered, and who is our Redeemer.

The level is set apart in time: Job lived long ago, in the days of the patriarchs. That is why it is placed here, after Joseph and before Moses.

## Episode 1 · The Trial

**Job 1–2:10 · about 7 minutes · 4 scenes**

> **Content note shown before this episode:** "In this episode Job loses everything he has, even his children, in one day. It is told gently, and nothing is shown."

### Scene 1.1 · A man in the land of Uz
*On screen:* A rich farmstead under a wide sky: tents and a stone house, flocks and herds spreading over the hills, servants at work. Job, a dignified man with a gray beard, prays at an altar at dawn while his children sleep. *(Play: Job's blessings.)*

1. **[Q] Job 1:1** "There was a man in the land of Uz, whose name was Job; and that man was blameless and upright, one who feared God, and turned away from evil."
2. **[Q] Job 1:2** "There were born to him seven sons and three daughters."
3. *(Play: Job's blessings.)*
4. **[Q] Job 1:3** "He had seven thousand sheep, three thousand camels, five hundred yoke of oxen, and five hundred she-asses, and very many servants; so that this man was the greatest of all the people of the east."
5. Every time his children had held a feast, Job rose early and offered a sacrifice for each of them, in case they had sinned. He loved God, and he loved his children.

### Scene 1.2 · In the court of heaven
*On screen:* Above the clouds, a great light, the court of heaven. A dark shadow stands before the light: Satan, "the accuser."

1. The Bible lets us see what Job could not: a day when the angels came before God, and Satan came among them.
2. **[Q] Job 1:8** "And the LORD said to Satan, "Have you considered my servant Job, that there is none like him on the earth, a blameless and upright man, who fears God and turns away from evil?""
3. **[Q] Job 1:9** "Then Satan answered the LORD, "Does Job fear God for nought?"
4. **[Q] Job 1:11** "But put forth thy hand now, and touch all that he has, and he will curse thee to thy face.""
5. **[Q] Job 1:12** "And the LORD said to Satan, "Behold, all that he has is in your power; only upon himself do not put forth your hand." So Satan went forth from the presence of the LORD."

### Scene 1.3 · The messengers
*On screen:* Back at the farmstead. A messenger runs up, dusty and out of breath; then another, and another. The flocks on the hills are gone. Job stands, then tears his robe and falls to his knees.

1. One day, messenger after messenger came running to Job. Raiders had taken the oxen and donkeys. Fire had fallen on the sheep. The camels had been carried off.
2. Then the worst news of all: a great wind had struck the house where his sons and daughters were feasting, and they had died.
3. **[Q] Job 1:20** "Then Job arose, and rent his robe, and shaved his head, and fell upon the ground, and worshiped."
4. **[Q] Job 1:21** "And he said, "Naked I came from my mother's womb, and naked shall I return; the LORD gave, and the LORD has taken away; blessed be the name of the LORD.""
5. **[Q] Job 1:22** "In all this Job did not sin or charge God with wrong."

### Scene 1.4 · Among the ashes
*On screen:* Evening. Job sits alone among the ashes outside the town, wrapped in sackcloth, his skin covered with sores. His wife stands beside him, grieving and angry.

1. Satan came before God again, and God allowed Job's own body to be struck, but not his life.
2. **[Q] Job 2:8** "And he took a potsherd with which to scrape himself, and sat among the ashes."
3. **[Q] Job 2:9** "Then his wife said to him, "Do you still hold fast your integrity? Curse God, and die.""
4. **[Q] Job 2:10** "But he said to her, "You speak as one of the foolish women would speak. Shall we receive good at the hand of God, and shall we not receive evil?" In all this Job did not sin with his lips."
5. His wife had lost her children too. Her words came from grief. Job did not stop loving her, or God.

### Play: Job's blessings
- **What it is:** light and reveal (spec §13.7, kind 11).
- **How it plays:** dawn over Job's lands, still dim. The player taps to light up each of Job's blessings, one by one, and each glows as it is found: his seven sons, his three daughters, his sheep, his camels, his oxen, his donkeys, his servants. With each, a soft word of thanks rises, the way Job thanked God each morning. When everything is lit, the sun rises (the story goes on).
- **Teaches:** that all we have comes from God, and gratitude; and it makes what Job is about to lose felt.
- **Gentle mode:** the next blessing glows, and lights itself after a moment.
- **Accessibility:** a pointer, or Space to light the next.

**Thread:** *Satan asks to test Job, and God sets a limit → Jesus to Peter: "Satan demanded to have you… but I have prayed for you."* The glowing object is Job praying at his altar.
- **[Q] Luke 22:31:** ""Simon, Simon, behold, Satan demanded to have you, that he might sift you like wheat,"
- **[Q] Luke 22:32:** "but I have prayed for you that your faith may not fail; and when you have turned again, strengthen your brethren.""
- **What it shows:** Satan could not touch Job without God's leave, and God set a limit: "only spare his life." Jesus told Peter that Satan had asked to test him too, and promised, "I have prayed for you." God never leaves us alone in a trial: he sets its limits, and Jesus prays for us.
- **Source:** Luke 22:31–32; 1 Corinthians 10:13; Catechism 395, 2851.

**Where and when:**
- **When.** The Book of Job does not say. Job lives like the patriarchs, as head of his family, offering sacrifices himself, with wealth counted in flocks.
- **The land of Uz.** No one knows exactly where it was. The Book of Lamentations links Uz with Edom (Lamentations 4:21), south-east of the Dead Sea; others place it farther east, toward the desert.
- **"Satan."** In Hebrew, *ha-satan*, "the accuser" or "the adversary." He is not God's equal: he can do nothing without God's permission.

**Read:** Job 1:1–2:10.

## Episode 2 · Job's Lament

**Job 2:11–3:26 · about 5 minutes · 3 scenes**

### Scene 2.1 · Three friends
*On screen:* Three travelers in fine robes come over the hills from three directions: Eliphaz, Bildad and Zophar. They see Job from far off and do not recognize him. They weep, tear their robes, and sit on the ground around him. *(Play: seven days.)*

1. **[Q] Job 2:11** "Now when Job's three friends heard of all this evil that had come upon him, they came each from his own place, Eliphaz the Temanite, Bildad the Shuhite, and Zophar the Naamathite. They made an appointment together to come to condole with him and comfort him."
2. **[Q] Job 2:12** "And when they saw him from afar, they did not recognize him; and they raised their voices and wept; and they rent their robes and sprinkled dust upon their heads toward heaven."
3. *(Play: seven days.)*
4. **[Q] Job 2:13** "And they sat with him on the ground seven days and seven nights, and no one spoke a word to him, for they saw that his suffering was very great."
5. Sometimes the best comfort is not words at all, but simply being there.

### Scene 2.2 · "Let the day perish"
*On screen:* Night. Job lifts his face to the dark sky and cries out. The stars seem far away.

1. **[Q] Job 3:1** "After this Job opened his mouth and cursed the day of his birth."
2. **[Q] Job 3:3** ""Let the day perish wherein I was born, and the night which said, 'A man-child is conceived.'"
3. **[Q] Job 3:20** ""Why is light given to him that is in misery, and life to the bitter in soul,"

### Scene 2.3 · No rest
*On screen:* Job, bowed, his face in his hands. Then, faintly, the first gray light of morning on the hills.

1. **[Q] Job 3:24** "For my sighing comes as my bread, and my groanings are poured out like water."
2. **[Q] Job 3:26** "I am not at ease, nor am I quiet; I have no rest; but trouble comes.""
3. Job did not curse God. He told God the truth about his pain. The Bible keeps his words, so that we know we can do the same.

### Play: seven days
- **What it is:** a quiet interaction (spec §13.7, kind 20).
- **How it plays:** Job and his three friends sit on the ground together. Nothing needs to be done. The player taps (or presses Space) to let each day pass: the sun crosses the sky, evening falls, the stars come out, and morning returns. Seven days and seven nights, and no one speaks. A small mark is scratched in the dust for each day.
- **Teaches:** the comfort of simply staying with someone who suffers.
- **Gentle mode:** the days pass on their own.
- **Accessibility:** a pointer, or Space for each day.

**Thread:** *Job cries out to God, "Why?" → Jesus on the cross: "My God, my God, why hast thou forsaken me?"* The glowing object is the first light of morning after the long night.
- **[Q] Matthew 27:46:** "And about the ninth hour Jesus cried with a loud voice, "Eli, Eli, lama sabach-thani?" that is, "My God, my God, why hast thou forsaken me?""
- **What it shows:** Job cried out to God in his pain and asked why. On the cross, Jesus too cried out in the words of a psalm, "My God, my God, why?" He took our sorrow and our questions into his own heart. When we suffer, Jesus has been there before us, and he is with us there.
- **Source:** Matthew 27:45–46; Psalm 22:1; Hebrews 4:15; Catechism 603, 2606.

**Where and when:**
- **Teman.** Eliphaz came from Teman, a town in Edom famous for its wise men (Jeremiah 49:7).
- **Seven days.** In Israel, mourning for the dead lasted seven days. The friends mourned with Job as for someone who had died.
- **Lament.** A lament is a prayer that brings sorrow honestly to God. About a third of the psalms are laments.

**Read:** Job 2:11–3:26.

## Episode 3 · The Friends' Arguments

**Job 4–31 · about 6 minutes · 3 scenes**

### Scene 3.1 · Eliphaz speaks
*On screen:* The friends' long debate, around a low fire. Eliphaz, the eldest, stands and speaks, raising a finger. Job listens, shaking his head.

1. At last the friends spoke. They were sure they knew why Job was suffering: God punishes the wicked, so Job must have done something wicked.
2. **[Q] Job 4:7** ""Think now, who that was innocent ever perished? Or where were the upright cut off?"
3. **[Q] Job 4:8** "As I have seen, those who plow iniquity and sow trouble reap the same."

### Scene 3.2 · True, or twisted?
*On screen:* Bildad and Zophar take their turns, louder and louder. Their words hang in the air like smoke. *(Play: true or twisted?)*

1. **[Q] Job 8:3** "Does God pervert justice? Or does the Almighty pervert the right?"
2. **[Q] Job 11:6** "and that he would tell you the secrets of wisdom! For he is manifold in understanding. Know then that God exacts of you less than your guilt deserves."
3. *(Play: true or twisted?)*
4. Some of what the friends said was true about God. But they used it to accuse a man God himself had called blameless.

### Scene 3.3 · Job answers
*On screen:* Job, alone after the friends turn away, looks up at the evening sky. A single star appears.

1. Job would not pretend to be guilty. He longed to bring his case before God himself.
2. **[Q] Job 16:19** "Even now, behold, my witness is in heaven, and he that vouches for me is on high."
3. **[Q] Job 23:10** "But he knows the way that I take; when he has tried me, I shall come forth as gold."

### Play: true or twisted?
- **What it is:** a quiet interaction (spec §13.7, kind 20): spot the twist, like the serpent's words in Level 1.
- **How it plays:** one by one, things the friends said float up. For each, the player decides: is this true of God, or is it twisted? "Does God pervert justice?" (God is just: true.) "Who that was innocent ever perished?" (Twisted: Job is innocent, and suffering.) "God exacts of you less than your guilt deserves." (Twisted: God had called Job blameless.) "Happy is the man whom God reproves." (True of God's correction, but not the reason Job was suffering.) Each answer shows a short explanation.
- **Teaches:** that suffering is not always a punishment; to be careful before we judge those who suffer.
- **Gentle mode:** the right answer glows.
- **Accessibility:** a pointer, or the arrow keys and Space.

**Thread:** *The friends think Job's suffering must be his own fault → Jesus' disciples ask, "Rabbi, who sinned?"* The glowing object is the evening star over Job.
- **[Q] John 9:2:** "And his disciples asked him, "Rabbi, who sinned, this man or his parents, that he was born blind?""
- **[Q] John 9:3:** "Jesus answered, "It was not that this man sinned, or his parents, but that the works of God might be made manifest in him."
- **What it shows:** Job's friends were sure that suffering meant sin. Centuries later, Jesus' disciples thought the same about a man born blind. Jesus said no: this was not a punishment, and God would show his glory in him. Then he healed him.
- **Source:** John 9:1–7; Luke 13:1–5; Catechism 1502–1505.

**Where and when:**
- **A long poem.** From chapter 3 to chapter 41, the Book of Job is written as poetry: the friends' speeches, Job's answers, and at last God's.
- **Three rounds.** The friends speak in turn, three times over, and Job answers each one. A fourth speaker, a young man named Elihu, joins in near the end (chapters 32–37).
- **Refined like gold.** Gold was purified by fire: the heat burned away everything that was not gold.

**Read:** Job 4:1–21; 23:1–17.

## Episode 4 · "I Know That My Redeemer Lives"

**Job 19 · about 5 minutes · 3 scenes**

### Scene 4.1 · Alone
*On screen:* Job sits alone among the ashes. Far off, people pass by and turn their faces away; children point and laugh. His friends have gone.

1. Job's friends had turned against him. Even his servants and his neighbors had forgotten him.
2. **[Q] Job 19:23** ""Oh that my words were written! Oh that they were inscribed in a book!"
3. **[Q] Job 19:24** "Oh that with an iron pen and lead they were graven in the rock for ever!"

### Scene 4.2 · "I know that my Redeemer lives"
*On screen:* Job lifts his head. A great rock face beside him, and as he speaks, his words are carved into it in letters of light. *(Play: carved in the rock.)*

1. Then, in the darkest place, Job said something astonishing.
2. **[Q] Job 19:25** "For I know that my Redeemer lives, and at last he will stand upon the earth;"
3. *(Play: carved in the rock.)*
4. **[Q] Job 19:26** "and after my skin has been thus destroyed, then from my flesh I shall see God,"
5. **[Q] Job 19:27** "whom I shall see on my side, and my eyes shall behold, and not another. My heart faints within me!"

### Scene 4.3 · Words graven for ever
*On screen:* The carved words glow on the rock as night falls, and stay.

1. A redeemer, in Job's time, was a close relative who stood up for you: who bought you back from slavery, or defended your name. Job believed that God himself would be his Redeemer, and that he would see him.
2. Job's wish came true. His words were written in a book, and they have been read for thousands of years.

### Play: carved in the rock
- **What it is:** memory (spec §13.7, kind 14).
- **How it plays:** the words of Job 19:25 glow on the rock: "For I know that my Redeemer lives, and at last he will stand upon the earth." Then some of the words fade, leaving gaps. The player chooses the missing words in order (Redeemer, lives, stand, earth), and each is carved back into the rock in light. Then the whole verse shines.
- **Teaches:** Job's great act of faith, learned by heart.
- **Gentle mode:** the right word glows among the choices.
- **Accessibility:** a pointer, or the arrow keys and Space.

**Thread:** *"I know that my Redeemer lives" → Jesus: "I am the resurrection and the life."* The glowing object is the carved words on the rock.
- **[Q] John 11:25:** "Jesus said to her, "I am the resurrection and the life; he who believes in me, though he die, yet shall he live,"
- **What it shows:** Job, near death, hoped to see God with his own eyes, in his own flesh. Jesus is the Redeemer Job longed for: he died and rose again in his body, and he promises that those who believe in him will rise too. That is why the Church sings Job's words at funerals, and why Handel set them to music in his *Messiah*.
- **Source:** John 11:25–26; 1 Corinthians 15:20; Catechism 988–991.

**Where and when:**
- **The redeemer.** In Hebrew, *go'el*: the family member who stood up for you. In the Book of Ruth, Boaz is Ruth's *go'el*.
- **Carved in rock.** Kings of the ancient Near East had their words cut into cliffs. One of the most famous, the Behistun inscription in Iran, is about 100 meters up a cliff face.
- **"I know that my Redeemer liveth."** These words, sung in Handel's *Messiah*, are among the best-loved in the whole Bible.

**Read:** Job 19.

## Episode 5 · God Answers from the Whirlwind

**Job 38–42:6 · about 7 minutes · 4 scenes**

### Scene 5.1 · Out of the whirlwind
*On screen:* The sky darkens; a great whirling storm sweeps across the hills, lightning inside it. Job and his friends shield their faces. Then a voice, and a light at the storm's heart.

1. Job had asked God to answer him. At last, God did.
2. **[Q] Job 38:1** "Then the LORD answered Job out of the whirlwind:"
3. **[Q] Job 38:4** ""Where were you when I laid the foundation of the earth? Tell me, if you have understanding."
4. **[Q] Job 38:7** "when the morning stars sang together, and all the sons of God shouted for joy?"

### Scene 5.2 · The wonders of creation
*On screen:* The storm opens like a curtain, and through it, the wonders God shows Job: the sea held back by its shore, the storehouses of snow, the stars of the Pleiades and Orion, a lioness with her cubs, a raven feeding its young, a war horse, an eagle on a high crag. *(Play: where were you?)*

1. **[Q] Job 38:8** ""Or who shut in the sea with doors, when it burst forth from the womb;"
2. **[Q] Job 38:11** "and said, 'Thus far shall you come, and no farther, and here shall your proud waves be stayed'?"
3. *(Play: where were you?)*
4. **[Q] Job 38:41** "Who provides for the raven its prey, when its young ones cry to God, and wander about for lack of food?"

### Scene 5.3 · I lay my hand on my mouth
*On screen:* The storm quiets. Job kneels, his hand over his mouth, looking up in wonder.

1. God did not tell Job why he had suffered. Instead, he showed Job how great and how wise he is, and how he cares for every creature he has made.
2. **[Q] Job 40:4** ""Behold, I am of small account; what shall I answer thee? I lay my hand on my mouth."

### Scene 5.4 · Now my eye sees thee
*On screen:* Morning light after the storm. Job, still kneeling, raises his hands. His face is peaceful.

1. **[Q] Job 42:2** ""I know that thou canst do all things, and that no purpose of thine can be thwarted."
2. **[Q] Job 42:5** "I had heard of thee by the hearing of the ear, but now my eye sees thee;"
3. Job still did not know why. But he had met God, and that was enough.

### Play: where were you?
- **What it is:** searching (spec §13.7, kind 8).
- **How it plays:** the whirlwind fills the screen with cloud. The player sweeps the cloud away, and in each clearing a wonder of creation is found, with God's question to Job beneath it: "Who shut in the sea with doors?" "Have you entered the storehouses of the snow?" "Can you bind the chains of the Pleiades, or loose the cords of Orion?" "Can you hunt the prey for the lion?" "Who provides for the raven its prey?" "Do you give the horse his might?" "Is it at your command that the eagle mounts up?" When all seven are found, the storm begins to still.
- **Teaches:** the greatness and wisdom of God the Creator, and his care for every creature.
- **Gentle mode:** a sweep anywhere clears the cloud; the next wonder glows faintly through it.
- **Accessibility:** a pointer (sweep), or Space to find the next wonder.

**Thread:** *God speaks out of the whirlwind, Lord of the wind and the sea → Jesus calms the storm: "Who then is this, that even wind and sea obey him?"* The glowing object is the light at the heart of the whirlwind.
- **[Q] Mark 4:39:** "And he awoke and rebuked the wind, and said to the sea, "Peace! Be still!" And the wind ceased, and there was a great calm."
- **[Q] Mark 4:41:** "And they were filled with awe, and said to one another, "Who then is this, that even wind and sea obey him?""
- **What it shows:** God asked Job, "Who shut in the sea with doors?" Only the Creator commands the wind and the waves. When Jesus, in a little boat on the Sea of Galilee, told the storm, "Peace! Be still!", and it obeyed, his friends asked who he could be. The answer is in Job: only God does this.
- **Source:** Mark 4:35–41; Psalm 89:9; Catechism 295, 548.

**Where and when:**
- **The Pleiades and Orion.** Two of the brightest star patterns in the winter sky, known to almost every ancient people. You can still find them on a clear night.
- **Behemoth and Leviathan.** God goes on to describe two mighty beasts (chapters 40–41), perhaps the hippopotamus and the crocodile, creatures no one could tame.
- **No explanation.** God never tells Job about the conversation with Satan in chapter 1. Job trusts God without knowing.

**Read:** Job 38; 42:1–6.

## Episode 6 · Restored

**Job 42:7–17 · about 5 minutes · 3 scenes**

### Scene 6.1 · "You have not spoken of me what is right"
*On screen:* The three friends stand before Job, ashamed, leading bulls and rams for an offering. Job rises to meet them.

1. **[Q] Job 42:7** "After the LORD had spoken these words to Job, the LORD said to Eliphaz the Temanite: "My wrath is kindled against you and against your two friends; for you have not spoken of me what is right, as my servant Job has."
2. **[Q] Job 42:8** "Now therefore take seven bulls and seven rams, and go to my servant Job, and offer up for yourselves a burnt offering; and my servant Job shall pray for you, for I will accept his prayer not to deal with you according to your folly; for you have not spoken of me what is right, as my servant Job has.""

### Scene 6.2 · Job prays for his friends
*On screen:* Job at the altar, his arms raised, praying for the three men who had accused him. Then green returns to the hills, and herds stream home over them. *(Play: home to the fold.)*

1. **[Q] Job 42:10** "And the LORD restored the fortunes of Job, when he had prayed for his friends; and the LORD gave Job twice as much as he had before."
2. *(Play: home to the fold.)*
3. Job prayed for the friends who had hurt him. It was then that God restored him.

### Scene 6.3 · The latter days
*On screen:* Years later: Job, white-haired, at a feast with his family, seven sons and three daughters, and grandchildren and great-grandchildren all around.

1. **[Q] Job 42:12** "And the LORD blessed the latter days of Job more than his beginning; and he had fourteen thousand sheep, six thousand camels, a thousand yoke of oxen, and a thousand she-asses."
2. **[Q] Job 42:13** "He had also seven sons and three daughters."
3. **[Q] Job 42:14** "And he called the name of the first Jemimah; and the name of the second Keziah; and the name of the third Keren-happuch."
4. **[Q] Job 42:16** "And after this Job lived a hundred and forty years, and saw his sons, and his sons' sons, four generations."
5. Then Job died, an old man, full of days.
6. The children Job had lost were not replaced: they were still his, and they were with God. The Book of Job does not say that every sorrow is undone on earth. It says that God is faithful, and that he has the last word.

### Play: home to the fold
- **What it is:** herding and gathering (spec §13.7, kind 2).
- **How it plays:** green hills, and Job's new flocks and herds spreading over them: sheep, camels, oxen and donkeys, twice as many as before. The player calls each herd home to the fold (tapping a herd, or pressing Space for the next), and they come trotting in. A counter doubles as they arrive: "seven thousand… fourteen thousand sheep."
- **Teaches:** God's generosity, and the joy of being restored.
- **Gentle mode:** the herds come home by themselves, one after another.
- **Accessibility:** a pointer, or Space for the next herd.

**Thread:** *Job's patience, and God's mercy at the end → "You have heard of the steadfastness of Job."* The glowing object is Job praying for his friends.
- **[Q] James 5:11:** "Behold, we call those happy who were steadfast. You have heard of the steadfastness of Job, and you have seen the purpose of the Lord, how the Lord is compassionate and merciful."
- **What it shows:** The New Testament itself points back to Job: an example of holding on to God in suffering, and of the Lord's compassion. Job also prayed for those who had wronged him, as Jesus taught: "pray for those who persecute you." And the restoration at the end of the book is a small picture of the great restoration to come, when God will wipe away every tear.
- **Source:** James 5:10–11; Matthew 5:44; Revelation 21:4; Catechism 309, 324.

**Where and when:**
- **Double.** Job received exactly twice his sheep, camels, oxen and donkeys. But he received the same number of children: seven sons and three daughters. Many readers see in this that his first children were not lost for ever.
- **Three daughters.** Jemimah means "dove"; Keziah is a sweet spice, cassia; Keren-happuch is a jar of eye make-up. Job gave his daughters an inheritance with their brothers, which was unusual then.
- **A hundred and forty years.** Twice the seventy years of a normal lifetime (Psalm 90:10).

**Read:** Job 42:7–17.

### Reflection and prayer

- **Verse to ponder:** **[Q] Job 1:21** "And he said, "Naked I came from my mother's womb, and naked shall I return; the LORD gave, and the LORD has taken away; blessed be the name of the LORD.""
- **Question for your life:** Job brought everything to God: his thanks, his tears, even his questions. Is there something sad or hard that you could tell God about today, honestly, the way Job did?
- **Journal:** an optional written answer, saved to the journal.
- **Prayer (original):**

  > Lord, you gave Job every good thing,
  > and you stayed with him when he lost it all.
  > When I am sad or afraid, help me to tell you the truth,
  > as Job did, and to keep holding on to you.
  > Help me to sit with those who suffer, and not to judge them.
  > Thank you for Jesus, my Redeemer, who lives,
  > who suffered for me and rose again.
  > Blessed be your name, in good times and in hard times.
  > Amen.

## Level 6 quiz bank (42 questions)

The types are MC (multiple choice), TF (true or false), WHO (who said it?), ORDER (put in order), WHERE (tap the map), FINISH (finish the verse) and THREAD (match the golden thread). The correct answer is in **bold**. For FINISH questions, the wrong words offered are listed after the right ones. Every question shows its explanation and reference after it's answered.

One place on the map is new for this level: **Edom**, where many place the land of Uz (region: edom).

### Episode 1 · The Trial
