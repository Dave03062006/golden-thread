# Level 14 script: *Joshua*

**Bible:** Joshua 1–8; 10; 14; 18; 21; 24
**Age:** V · The Promised Land
**Length:** about 50 minutes (7 episodes, then the quiz, then reflection and prayer)
**Status:** draft, waiting for Dave's approval.

## How to read this script

- Each **scene** is a checkpoint. Its numbered lines are the narration beats; the player taps to move from one to the next.
- *On screen* describes what the scene shows. It's the brief for the art in both styles.
- **[Q]** marks an exact quotation from the Bible. Its wording in both translations is in the verse table at the end. Everything else is our own retelling.
- **Play** is the episode's interactive moment. **Thread** is its golden thread. **Read** is the passage the game sends the player to.
- "LORD" in capitals stands for God's holy name, printed in small capitals in the RSV.

### What this level is about

Moses is dead, and God speaks to Joshua: "Be strong and of good courage… the LORD your God is with you wherever you go." Forty years after the sea, the people cross another river, the Jordan, on dry ground, following the ark. A woman of Jericho, Rahab, hides their spies and is saved by a scarlet cord. The walls of Jericho fall at the sound of trumpets and a shout. When one man, Achan, steals and hides what belonged to God, the whole people suffers, until the sin is brought into the light. God fights for his people at Gibeon, where the sun stands still. Then the land is shared out, tribe by tribe, and every one of God's promises comes true. At the end of his life, Joshua gathers the people and asks them to choose whom they will serve: "As for me and my house, we will serve the LORD."

Joshua's very name, Yeshua, "the LORD saves," is the name of Jesus. Rahab became one of Jesus' own ancestors. Jesus was baptized in the same river the people crossed. And the promised land points to a greater promise: "an inheritance which is imperishable… kept in heaven for you."

**How it's shown:** the wars of the book of Joshua are hard to read, and the game does not show battles. At Jericho the walls fall and the people go up into the city; at Ai and Gibeon we see Joshua praying, the people marching, and God's help, never killing. The note in Episode 3 explains the hardest idea in the book, the "ban," simply and honestly, and how the Church reads these battles: our true enemies are not people, but sin and evil (Ephesians 6:12).

## Episode 1 · Rahab and the Spies

**Joshua 1–2 · about 7 minutes · 3 scenes**

### Scene 1.1 · Be strong and of good courage
*On screen:* The plains of Moab at dawn, the camp quiet after thirty days of mourning for Moses. Joshua stands alone by the river, looking across at the land. A light speaks to him from above.

1. **[Q] Joshua 1:1** "After the death of Moses the servant of the LORD, the LORD said to Joshua the son of Nun, Moses' minister,"
2. **[Q] Joshua 1:2** ""Moses my servant is dead; now therefore arise, go over this Jordan, you and all this people, into the land which I am giving to them, to the people of Israel."
3. **[Q] Joshua 1:5** "No man shall be able to stand before you all the days of your life; as I was with Moses, so I will be with you; I will not fail you or forsake you."
4. **[Q] Joshua 1:9** "Have I not commanded you? Be strong and of good courage; be not frightened, neither be dismayed; for the LORD your God is with you wherever you go.""

### Scene 1.2 · Hidden on the roof
*On screen:* Jericho at evening: its great walls, and a house built into the wall, with a window high up. Two men slip in at the gate. Soldiers knock at Rahab's door; she hides the spies on her flat roof under stalks of flax spread out to dry. *(Play: hidden on the roof.)*

1. **[Q] Joshua 2:1** "And Joshua the son of Nun sent two men secretly from Shittim as spies, saying, "Go, view the land, especially Jericho." And they went, and came into the house of a harlot whose name was Rahab, and lodged there."
2. **[Q] Joshua 2:3** "Then the king of Jericho sent to Rahab, saying, "Bring forth the men that have come to you, who entered your house; for they have come to search out all the land.""
3. *(Play: hidden on the roof.)*
4. **[Q] Joshua 2:4** "But the woman had taken the two men and hidden them; and she said, "True, men came to me, but I did not know where they came from;"
5. **[Q] Joshua 2:9** "and said to the men, "I know that the LORD has given you the land, and that the fear of you has fallen upon us, and that all the inhabitants of the land melt away before you."
6. **[Q] Joshua 2:11** "And as soon as we heard it, our hearts melted, and there was no courage left in any man, because of you; for the LORD your God is he who is God in heaven above and on earth beneath."

### Scene 1.3 · The scarlet cord
*On screen:* Night. Rahab lets the two spies down from her window on a rope, down the outside of the wall. Before they go, they give her a cord of bright scarlet; she ties it in her window, where it glows. Then the spies, back across the river, tell Joshua everything.

1. **[Q] Joshua 2:12** "Now then, swear to me by the LORD that as I have dealt kindly with you, you also will deal kindly with my father's house, and give me a sure sign,"
2. **[Q] Joshua 2:15** "Then she let them down by a rope through the window, for her house was built into the city wall, so that she dwelt in the wall."
3. **[Q] Joshua 2:18** "Behold, when we come into the land, you shall bind this scarlet cord in the window through which you let us down; and you shall gather into your house your father and mother, your brothers, and all your father's household."
4. **[Q] Joshua 2:21** "And she said, "According to your words, so be it." Then she sent them away, and they departed; and she bound the scarlet cord in the window."
5. **[Q] Joshua 2:24** "And they said to Joshua, "Truly the LORD has given all the land into our hands; and moreover all the inhabitants of the land are fainthearted because of us.""

### Play: hidden on the roof
- **What it is:** stealth and escape (spec §13.7, kind 17).
- **How it plays:** Rahab's flat roof, with the stalks of flax laid out in rows. The king's men are coming up the stairs. The player covers the two spies with flax, bundle by bundle (tap, or the arrow keys and Space), before the soldiers reach the top; then the soldiers look around, see only flax, and leave. Later, when the guard on the wall walks the other way, the player lets the spies down the rope from the window, one at a time.
- **Teaches:** how Rahab risked her life to protect God's people, because she believed in their God.
- **Gentle mode:** the soldiers climb slowly, and the guard walks a long way before turning.
- **Accessibility:** a pointer, or the arrow keys and Space.

**Thread:** *Rahab trusts the God of Israel, and is saved by the scarlet cord in her window → "By faith Rahab… did not perish," and she became an ancestor of Jesus.* The glowing object is the scarlet cord.
- **[Q] Hebrews 11:31:** "By faith Rahab the harlot did not perish with those who were disobedient, because she had given friendly welcome to the spies."
- **[Q] Matthew 1:5:** "and Salmon the father of Boaz by Rahab, and Boaz the father of Obed by Ruth, and Obed the father of Jesse,"
- **What it shows:** Rahab was a foreigner, a sinner, living in an enemy city. But she believed that the God of Israel was "God in heaven above and on earth beneath," and she risked everything to help his people. God saved her and her whole family, and she came to live among his people. She married into the tribe of Judah, and became the great-great-grandmother of King David, and so an ancestor of Jesus. Saint Matthew names her in Jesus' family tree. The Fathers of the Church saw in her scarlet cord a sign of the blood of Christ, by which we are saved.
- **Source:** Hebrews 11:31; James 2:25; Matthew 1:5; Saint Clement of Rome, *Letter to the Corinthians* 12; Catechism 2463.

**Where and when:**
- **Jericho.** One of the oldest cities in the world, in an oasis just west of the Jordan, near the Dead Sea; people have lived there for more than ten thousand years. It is the "city of palm trees" that Moses saw from Nebo (Level 13).
- **Flax.** A plant grown for its fibers, which are spun into linen. Its cut stalks were laid out on flat roofs to dry in the sun.
- **A house in the wall.** Some cities had houses built between an inner and an outer wall, so a window could look out over the outside of the city.

**Read:** Joshua 1:1–9; 2:1–24.

## Episode 2 · Crossing the Jordan

**Joshua 3–5 · about 8 minutes · 3 scenes**

### Scene 2.1 · The ark goes first
*On screen:* The Jordan in spring flood, brown and wide, over its banks. The priests lift the ark of the covenant onto their shoulders and walk toward the water, and all the people follow at a distance. As the priests' feet touch the water, the river stops: far upstream it piles up in a heap, and the riverbed below lies dry.

1. **[Q] Joshua 3:5** "And Joshua said to the people, "Sanctify yourselves; for tomorrow the LORD will do wonders among you.""
2. **[Q] Joshua 3:6** "And Joshua said to the priests, "Take up the ark of the covenant, and pass on before the people." And they took up the ark of the covenant, and went before the people."
3. **[Q] Joshua 3:13** "And when the soles of the feet of the priests who bear the ark of the LORD, the Lord of all the earth, shall rest in the waters of the Jordan, the waters of the Jordan shall be stopped from flowing, and the waters coming down from above shall stand in one heap.""
4. **[Q] Joshua 3:15** "and when those who bore the ark had come to the Jordan, and the feet of the priests bearing the ark were dipped in the brink of the water (the Jordan overflows all its banks throughout the time of harvest),"
5. **[Q] Joshua 3:16** "the waters coming down from above stood and rose up in a heap far off, at Adam, the city that is beside Zarethan, and those flowing down toward the sea of the Arabah, the Salt Sea, were wholly cut off; and the people passed over opposite Jericho."
6. **[Q] Joshua 3:17** "And while all Israel were passing over on dry ground, the priests who bore the ark of the covenant of the LORD stood on dry ground in the midst of the Jordan, until all the nation finished passing over the Jordan."

### Scene 2.2 · Twelve stones
*On screen:* The priests stand still in the middle of the dry riverbed with the ark. Twelve men, one from each tribe, each lift a big stone from the place where the priests are standing, and carry it up the bank on his shoulder. *(Play: twelve stones from the Jordan.)*

1. **[Q] Joshua 4:2** ""Take twelve men from the people, from each tribe a man,"
2. **[Q] Joshua 4:3** "and command them, 'Take twelve stones from here out of the midst of the Jordan, from the very place where the priests' feet stood, and carry them over with you, and lay them down in the place where you lodge tonight.'""
3. *(Play: twelve stones from the Jordan.)*
4. **[Q] Joshua 4:6** "that this may be a sign among you, when your children ask in time to come, 'What do those stones mean to you?'"
5. **[Q] Joshua 4:7** "Then you shall tell them that the waters of the Jordan were cut off before the ark of the covenant of the LORD; when it passed over the Jordan, the waters of the Jordan were cut off. So these stones shall be to the people of Israel a memorial for ever.""

### Scene 2.3 · Gilgal
*On screen:* The camp at Gilgal, on the west bank, in the promised land at last. The twelve stones stand in a circle. The people keep the Passover; in the morning they eat bread from the grain of the land, and there is no manna on the ground. Then, near Jericho, Joshua meets a man with a drawn sword, and falls on his face.

1. **[Q] Joshua 4:19** "The people came up out of the Jordan on the tenth day of the first month, and they encamped in Gilgal on the east border of Jericho."
2. **[Q] Joshua 4:20** "And those twelve stones, which they took out of the Jordan, Joshua set up in Gilgal."
3. **[Q] Joshua 5:10** "While the people of Israel were encamped in Gilgal they kept the passover on the fourteenth day of the month at evening in the plains of Jericho."
4. **[Q] Joshua 5:12** "And the manna ceased on the morrow, when they ate of the produce of the land; and the people of Israel had manna no more, but ate of the fruit of the land of Canaan that year."
5. **[Q] Joshua 5:13** "When Joshua was by Jericho, he lifted up his eyes and looked, and behold, a man stood before him with his drawn sword in his hand; and Joshua went to him and said to him, "Are you for us, or for our adversaries?""
6. He answered that he came as commander of the army of the LORD. Joshua fell on his face and worshiped.
7. **[Q] Joshua 5:15** "And the commander of the LORD's army said to Joshua, "Put off your shoes from your feet; for the place where you stand is holy." And Joshua did so."

### Play: twelve stones from the Jordan
- **What it is:** building and stacking (spec §13.7, kind 3).
- **How it plays:** the dry riverbed, with the priests and the ark standing in the middle. Twelve men come down, one from each tribe; the player helps each lift a stone from the riverbed and carry it up to Gilgal (drag, or the arrow keys and Space), and sets it in its place, until twelve stones stand in a circle. Each stone shows its tribe's name as it is set.
- **Teaches:** the twelve stones as a memorial, so that children would ask "What do these stones mean?" and hear what God had done.
- **Gentle mode:** a tap on a stone carries it to its place by itself.
- **Accessibility:** a pointer, or the arrow keys and Space.

**Thread:** *Israel passes through the Jordan into the promised land → Jesus is baptized in the Jordan, and the heavens open.* The glowing object is the ark, standing in the middle of the river.
- **[Q] Mark 1:9:** "In those days Jesus came from Nazareth of Galilee and was baptized by John in the Jordan."
- **[Q] Mark 1:10:** "And when he came up out of the water, immediately he saw the heavens opened and the Spirit descending upon him like a dove;"
- **What it shows:** God's people entered the promised land through the waters of the Jordan, led by the ark. Centuries later, Jesus, whose name is Joshua, came down to that same river to be baptized, and the heavens opened. He makes the waters of Baptism the way into God's family, and into the life he promises. Saint Gregory of Nyssa wrote that the crossing of the Jordan was a picture of Baptism, the doorway into the true promised land.
- **Source:** Mark 1:9–11; Matthew 3:13–17; Saint Gregory of Nyssa, *On the Baptism of Christ*; Catechism 1222, 1223–1225.

**Where and when:**
- **The Jordan in flood.** In spring, snow melting on Mount Hermon fills the Jordan until it spills over its banks.
- **Adam.** The town where the water "rose up in a heap," about 30 kilometers upstream. Landslides there have blocked the river in later times too, most recently in 1927, for about a day.
- **Gilgal.** The first camp in the promised land, near Jericho. It became a holy place, where Israel gathered for many years.
- **The manna ceased.** Forty years of bread from heaven ended the day after the people ate the grain of the land. God had kept feeding them until they no longer needed it.

**Read:** Joshua 3:1–4:24; 5:10–15.

## Episode 3 · The Walls of Jericho

**Joshua 6 · about 7 minutes · 3 scenes**

### Scene 3.1 · Jericho shut up
*On screen:* Jericho, its gates shut and barred, soldiers on its walls. Joshua stands before the city and listens. In the camp, seven priests hold rams' horn trumpets, and the ark is lifted onto the priests' shoulders.

1. **[Q] Joshua 6:1** "Now Jericho was shut up from within and from without because of the people of Israel; none went out, and none came in."
2. **[Q] Joshua 6:2** "And the LORD said to Joshua, "See, I have given into your hand Jericho, with its king and mighty men of valor."
3. **[Q] Joshua 6:3** "You shall march around the city, all the men of war going around the city once. Thus shall you do for six days."
4. **[Q] Joshua 6:4** "And seven priests shall bear seven trumpets of rams' horns before the ark; and on the seventh day you shall march around the city seven times, the priests blowing the trumpets."
5. **[Q] Joshua 6:5** "And when they make a long blast with the ram's horn, as soon as you hear the sound of the trumpet, then all the people shall shout with a great shout; and the wall of the city will fall down flat, and the people shall go up every man straight before him.""

### Scene 3.2 · Seven times around
*On screen:* The procession around the city: soldiers, then the seven priests with trumpets, then the ark, then the people, all in silence except for the trumpets. Day after day, once around; on the seventh day, seven times. Then the long blast, the great shout, and the walls fall flat. *(Play: seven times around.)*

1. **[Q] Joshua 6:10** "But Joshua commanded the people, "You shall not shout or let your voice be heard, neither shall any word go out of your mouth, until the day I bid you shout; then you shall shout.""
2. **[Q] Joshua 6:15** "On the seventh day they rose early at the dawn of day, and marched around the city in the same manner seven times: it was only on that day that they marched around the city seven times."
3. *(Play: seven times around.)*
4. **[Q] Joshua 6:16** "And at the seventh time, when the priests had blown the trumpets, Joshua said to the people, "Shout; for the LORD has given you the city."
5. **[Q] Joshua 6:20** "So the people shouted, and the trumpets were blown. As soon as the people heard the sound of the trumpet, the people raised a great shout, and the wall fell down flat, so that the people went up into the city, every man straight before him, and they took the city."

### Scene 3.3 · Rahab's house
*On screen:* Clouds of dust over the fallen walls. One piece of wall still stands, with a window, and a scarlet cord hanging in it. The two spies lead Rahab and her whole family out to safety, to the camp of Israel.

1. In all the fallen city, one house in the wall still stood: the house with the scarlet cord.
2. **[Q] Joshua 6:25** "But Rahab the harlot, and her father's household, and all who belonged to her, Joshua saved alive; and she dwelt in Israel to this day, because she hid the messengers whom Joshua sent to spy out Jericho."
3. **[Q] Joshua 6:27** "So the LORD was with Joshua; and his fame was in all the land."

### Play: seven times around
- **What it is:** rhythm (spec §13.7, kind 5; the spec names Jericho's march).
- **How it plays:** the procession circles the walls in silence, and the player keeps the march in step (tap, or Space, on each beat), while the priests sound the trumpets. Each day is one circle; the seventh day, seven circles, faster and faster. At the end, the player holds for the long blast of the trumpets, then taps for the great shout, and the walls fall flat.
- **Teaches:** that the walls fell not by force, but by obeying God and trusting him, day after day.
- **Gentle mode:** any tap near the beat counts, and the days pass more quickly.
- **Accessibility:** a pointer, or Space.

**Thread:** *The walls of Jericho fall after seven days of marching → "By faith the walls of Jericho fell down."* The glowing object is the ark, carried around the walls.
- **[Q] Hebrews 11:30:** "By faith the walls of Jericho fell down after they had been encircled for seven days."
- **What it shows:** No army could have broken Jericho's walls. God's people simply did what he asked, marching and waiting in silence for seven days, and trusted him to keep his word. The letter to the Hebrews puts the walls of Jericho in its list of the great deeds of faith. The walls in our own lives, of fear, of sin, of things too hard for us, fall the same way: not by our strength, but by trusting God.
- **Source:** Hebrews 11:30; 2 Corinthians 10:3–5; Catechism 144–146.

**Where and when:**
- **Rams' horns.** The *shofar*, a trumpet made from a ram's horn, still blown in synagogues on the Jewish New Year (Level 11).
- **"Devoted to the LORD for destruction."** In the wars of that time, a city and everything in it could be "devoted," given wholly over to a god, so that no one took anything for himself. This is one of the hardest things in the Bible to read. The Church reads it in the light of Jesus, who taught us to love our enemies (Matthew 5:44), and sees in these battles a picture of the Christian's fight: our enemies are not people, but sin and evil, which must be given up completely (Ephesians 6:12).
- **The ruins of Jericho.** Archaeologists have dug at Jericho for more than a century, and still debate when its walls fell.

**Read:** Joshua 6.

## Episode 4 · Achan and Ai

**Joshua 7–8 · about 7 minutes · 3 scenes**

### Scene 4.1 · Defeat at Ai
*On screen:* The small town of Ai on its hill. A few thousand men of Israel go up confidently, and come running back down. In the camp, Joshua tears his clothes and lies on his face before the ark until evening, with the elders, dust on their heads.

1. **[Q] Joshua 7:1** "But the people of Israel broke faith in regard to the devoted things; for Achan the son of Carmi, son of Zabdi, son of Zerah, of the tribe of Judah, took some of the devoted things; and the anger of the LORD burned against the people of Israel."
2. **[Q] Joshua 7:4** "So about three thousand went up there from the people; and they fled before the men of Ai,"
3. **[Q] Joshua 7:6** "Then Joshua rent his clothes, and fell to the earth upon his face before the ark of the LORD until the evening, he and the elders of Israel; and they put dust upon their heads."
4. **[Q] Joshua 7:7** "And Joshua said, "Alas, O Lord GOD, why hast thou brought this people over the Jordan at all, to give us into the hands of the Amorites, to destroy us? Would that we had been content to dwell beyond the Jordan!"

### Scene 4.2 · "Do not hide it from me"
*On screen:* God's voice as light over Joshua. Then a single tent in the camp at evening, and Achan sitting at its door, avoiding everyone's eyes. Joshua comes to him gently. Inside, under the ground, something glints. *(Play: brought into the light.)*

1. **[Q] Joshua 7:10** "The LORD said to Joshua, "Arise, why have you thus fallen upon your face?"
2. **[Q] Joshua 7:11** "Israel has sinned; they have transgressed my covenant which I commanded them; they have taken some of the devoted things; they have stolen, and lied, and put them among their own stuff."
3. **[Q] Joshua 7:19** "Then Joshua said to Achan, "My son, give glory to the LORD God of Israel, and render praise to him; and tell me now what you have done; do not hide it from me.""
4. **[Q] Joshua 7:20** "And Achan answered Joshua, "Of a truth I have sinned against the LORD God of Israel, and this is what I did:"
5. **[Q] Joshua 7:21** "when I saw among the spoil a beautiful mantle from Shinar, and two hundred shekels of silver, and a bar of gold weighing fifty shekels, then I coveted them, and took them; and behold, they are hidden in the earth inside my tent, with the silver underneath.""
6. *(Play: brought into the light.)*

### Scene 4.3 · The altar on Mount Ebal
*On screen:* A heap of stones in a valley. Then Ai again, taken this time. Then, between two mountains, an altar of uncut stones; Joshua writes the law on great plastered stones, and reads it aloud to all the people, women, children and strangers among them.

1. Achan's sin had brought trouble on the whole people. He and what he had stolen were taken out of the camp, and a great heap of stones was raised over the place, called the Valley of Achor, which means "trouble."
2. **[Q] Joshua 8:1** "And the LORD said to Joshua, "Do not fear or be dismayed; take all the fighting men with you, and arise, go up to Ai; see, I have given into your hand the king of Ai, and his people, his city, and his land;"
3. **[Q] Joshua 8:30** "Then Joshua built an altar on Mount Ebal to the LORD, the God of Israel,"
4. **[Q] Joshua 8:34** "And afterward he read all the words of the law, the blessing and the curse, according to all that is written in the book of the law."
5. **[Q] Joshua 8:35** "There was not a word of all that Moses commanded which Joshua did not read before all the assembly of Israel, and the women, and the little ones, and the sojourners who lived among them."

### Play: brought into the light
- **What it is:** searching (spec §13.7, kind 8).
- **How it plays:** Achan's tent, dim. Joshua has asked him not to hide anything. The player looks with a lamp (drag, or the arrow keys) for what he buried in the ground: the beautiful mantle from Shinar, the two hundred shekels of silver, the bar of gold. Each one found is lifted out and carried to the door, into the light, and laid before the LORD.
- **Teaches:** that sin hidden harms everyone, and that God asks us to bring it into the light; and that confession is the way back.
- **Gentle mode:** the lamp is bigger, and each hidden thing glints when the lamp comes near.
- **Accessibility:** a pointer, or the arrow keys and Space.

**Thread:** *"Tell me now what you have done; do not hide it from me" → "If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just, and will forgive our sins."* The glowing object is what Achan hid, glinting in the ground.
- **[Q] 1 John 1:8:** "If we say we have no sin, we deceive ourselves, and the truth is not in us."
- **[Q] 1 John 1:9:** "If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just, and will forgive our sins and cleanse us from all unrighteousness."
- **What it shows:** Achan's hidden sin harmed the whole people, and he hid it until he was found. Jesus has given us a better way: we bring our sins into the light ourselves, and he forgives them. In the sacrament of Reconciliation, we tell our sins to God through his priest, and hear that we are forgiven. Nothing we bring to him is too heavy for his mercy.
- **Source:** 1 John 1:5–10; John 3:20–21; Catechism 1424, 1455–1458.

**Where and when:**
- **Ai.** The name means "the ruin." It stood on a hill east of Bethel, where Abraham once pitched his tent (Genesis 12:8; Level 3).
- **A mantle from Shinar.** A fine robe from Babylonia, the land of the tower of Babel (Level 2): something precious, and foreign.
- **Mount Ebal and Mount Gerizim.** Two mountains facing each other at Shechem. Moses had commanded that the blessings be read from one and the curses from the other (Deuteronomy 27).

**Read:** Joshua 7:1–26; 8:30–35.

## Episode 5 · The Sun Stands Still

**Joshua 10:6–14 · about 6 minutes · 3 scenes**

### Scene 5.1 · "Come up to us quickly"
*On screen:* A messenger runs into the camp at Gilgal, dusty and out of breath, from the city of Gibeon, which has made peace with Israel. Five kings and their armies surround Gibeon on its hill.

1. **[Q] Joshua 10:6** "And the men of Gibeon sent to Joshua at the camp in Gilgal, saying, "Do not relax your hand from your servants; come up to us quickly, and save us, and help us; for all the kings of the Amorites that dwell in the hill country are gathered against us.""
2. **[Q] Joshua 10:7** "So Joshua went up from Gilgal, he and all the people of war with him, and all the mighty men of valor."
3. **[Q] Joshua 10:8** "And the LORD said to Joshua, "Do not fear them, for I have given them into your hands; there shall not a man of them stand before you.""

### Scene 5.2 · All night from Gilgal
*On screen:* Night. Joshua and his army march up from the Jordan valley into the hills, under the stars, on the steep road to Gibeon, and arrive as the sky grows pale. *(Play: all night from Gilgal.)*

1. **[Q] Joshua 10:9** "So Joshua came upon them suddenly, having marched up all night from Gilgal."
2. *(Play: all night from Gilgal.)*
3. The armies of the kings were thrown into confusion and fled down the steep pass of Beth-horon, and a terrible storm of great hailstones fell on them as they ran.

### Scene 5.3 · "Sun, stand thou still"
*On screen:* The valley below Gibeon at midday. Joshua stands on a rock and lifts his arms. The sun hangs over Gibeon, the pale moon over the valley of Aijalon, and the light does not move. The long day goes on.

1. **[Q] Joshua 10:12** "Then spoke Joshua to the LORD in the day when the LORD gave the Amorites over to the men of Israel; and he said in the sight of Israel, "Sun, stand thou still at Gibeon, and thou Moon in the valley of Aijalon.""
2. **[Q] Joshua 10:13** "And the sun stood still, and the moon stayed, until the nation took vengeance on their enemies. Is this not written in the Book of Jashar? The sun stayed in the midst of heaven, and did not hasten to go down for about a whole day."
3. **[Q] Joshua 10:14** "There has been no day like it before or since, when the LORD hearkened to the voice of a man; for the LORD fought for Israel."
4. Joshua had kept his word to the people of Gibeon, and God kept his word to Joshua.

### Play: all night from Gilgal
- **What it is:** pathfinding (spec §13.7, kind 6).
- **How it plays:** a moonlit map of the hills between Gilgal and Gibeon. The player leads Joshua's army up through the dark valleys, finding the way between rocks and ridges (drag, or the arrow keys), with the stars to steer by, before the sky grows light. Gibeon's watch fire glows ahead as the goal.
- **Teaches:** that Joshua kept his promise to help the Gibeonites, at once and at great cost, and that God was with him.
- **Gentle mode:** the path is marked with small lights, and the army follows by itself.
- **Accessibility:** a pointer, or the arrow keys and Space.

**Thread:** *"The LORD fought for Israel" → "If God is for us, who is against us?"* The glowing object is the sun, standing still over Gibeon.
- **[Q] Romans 8:31:** "What then shall we say to this? If God is for us, who is against us?"

**Where and when:**

### Play: we will serve the LORD
- **What it is:** a quiet interaction (spec §13.7, kind 20).
- **How it plays:** the great stone under the oak, and the families of Israel gathered around. Some of them still hold little carved idols from the past.
