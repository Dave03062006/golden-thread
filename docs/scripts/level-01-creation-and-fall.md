# Level 1 script: *Creation and the Fall*

**Bible:** Genesis 1–5
**Age:** I · The Beginning
**Length:** about 55–60 minutes (6 episodes, then the quiz, then reflection and prayer)
**Status:** draft for Dave's approval (spec §15). Nothing in this level is built from unapproved text.

## How to read this script

- Each **scene** is a checkpoint. Its numbered lines are the narration beats; the player taps to move from one to the next.
- *On screen* describes what the scene shows. It's the brief for the art in both styles.
- **[Q]** marks an exact quotation from the Bible. Its wording in both translations is in the verse table at the end. Everything else is our own retelling.
- **Play** is the episode's interactive moment. **Thread** is its golden thread. **Read** is the passage the game sends the player to.
- "LORD" in small capitals stands for God's holy name, as printed in the RSV.

## Episode 1 · In the Beginning

**Genesis 1:1–2:3 · about 12 minutes · 8 scenes**

### Scene 1.1 · Before the beginning
*On screen:* Total darkness. Slowly, the faint shine of deep water and a soft breath of light moving over it, like wings.

1. Before mountains, before stars, before time itself, there was God, and God alone.
2. Then God began to create.
3. **[Q] Genesis 1:1** "In the beginning God created the heavens and the earth."
4. **[Q] Genesis 1:2** "The earth was without form and void, and darkness was upon the face of the deep; and the Spirit of God was moving over the face of the waters."
5. The Hebrew word for "moving" is the word for a bird hovering over its nest. Before anything had shape, God's Spirit was already brooding over it, ready to bring forth life.

### Scene 1.2 · Day one: light
*On screen:* Light breaks across the darkness from a single point and floods the whole scene. Then light and darkness settle side by side as day and night.

1. Then God spoke.
2. **[Q] Genesis 1:3** "And God said, 'Let there be light'; and there was light."
3. God saw that the light was good. He separated the light from the darkness, and called the light Day and the darkness Night.
4. And there was evening and there was morning, one day.

*Music cue:* Haydn's blazing C-major chord on "and there was light" lands exactly as the light breaks.
*Play, part 1:* the player calls the light (see "Let there be…" below).

### Scene 1.3 · Day two: the sky
*On screen:* A vast dome rises out of the waters, holding back the waters above it. Blue sky opens between them.

1. On the second day God made a great dome, the firmament, to separate the waters below from the waters above.
2. He called the firmament Heaven.
3. And there was evening and there was morning, a second day.

*Play, part 2:* the player lifts the sky.

### Scene 1.4 · Day three: land, seas and plants
*On screen:* The waters draw back, land rises, and the first green spreads across it: grasses, seed-bearing plants, fruit trees.

1. God gathered the waters under the sky into one place, and the dry land appeared. He called the land Earth, and the gathered waters Seas.
2. Then the earth sprouted green: plants bearing seed and trees bearing fruit, each according to its kind.
3. God saw that it was good. Evening came, and morning: a third day.

*Play, part 3:* the player raises the land, then calls up the plants.

### Scene 1.5 · Day four: sun, moon and stars
*On screen:* The sun rises in gold and the moon follows in silver. Then the stars appear, thousands of them, almost in passing.

1. God set lights in the firmament to separate the day from the night, and to mark signs and seasons, days and years.
2. The sun to rule the day, the moon to rule the night, and then, almost in passing, he made the stars as well.
3. The nations around Israel worshiped the sun, the moon and the stars as gods. Genesis calls them simply lights: made things, set in place by the one true God.
4. Evening came, and morning: a fourth day.

*Play, part 4:* the player sets the sun and moon in place and scatters the stars.

### Scene 1.6 · Day five: fish and birds
*On screen:* The seas swarm with fish and great sea creatures, and the sky fills with birds, flock after flock.

1. God said, let the waters swarm with living creatures, and let birds fly above the earth across the sky.
2. So he made the great creatures of the sea, every fish, and every winged bird.
3. God blessed them and said, be fruitful and multiply.
4. Evening came, and morning: a fifth day.

*Play, part 5:* the player releases the fish into the sea and the birds into the sky.

### Scene 1.7 · Day six: animals, and man and woman
*On screen:* Animals of every kind walk out across the land. Then everything grows quiet. A man and a woman stand in the light, the only creatures turned toward the source of the light.

1. God said, let the earth bring forth living creatures: cattle, creeping things and wild beasts, each according to its kind. And so it was.
2. Then God did something new. This time he did not say "Let there be." He said:
3. **[Q] Genesis 1:26 (in part)** "Let us make man in our image, after our likeness."
4. **[Q] Genesis 1:27** "So God created man in his own image, in the image of God he created him; male and female he created them."
5. God blessed them and gave them the earth to care for, his stewards in his creation.
6. **[Q] Genesis 1:31 (in part)** "And God saw everything that he had made, and behold, it was very good."
7. Evening came, and morning: a sixth day.

*Play, part 6:* the player welcomes the animals. The creation of the man and the woman is God's act alone, so the player only watches.

### Scene 1.8 · Day seven: God rests
*On screen:* The whole finished world, softly lit and completely still. Nothing moves except the light.

1. So the heavens and the earth were finished, in all their array.
2. On the seventh day God rested from all the work he had done. He blessed the seventh day and made it holy.
3. God did not rest because he was tired. He rested the way an artist steps back from a finished work, and he invited his creation to rest with him.
4. For Israel this day became the Sabbath. For Christians, the Lord's Day is Sunday, the day of the Resurrection and the first day of a new creation.

*Play, part 7 (stillness):* for a few moments there is nothing to do. The music plays, the light moves, and the words "Rest" appear. After about 12 seconds, Continue fades in.

### Play: "Let there be…"
- **What it is:** a quiet, wordless interaction woven through scenes 1.2–1.8. It has no fail state, no score and no timer. Its purpose is to let the order of the days sink in by doing them.
- **Each day's gesture** (touch, mouse, or keyboard with arrows and Space):

| Day | Gesture | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Press and hold anywhere | Light grows from that point until it fills the screen. Release, and day and night settle apart. |
| 2 | Swipe upward, or press Up | The firmament lifts, dividing the waters. |
| 3 | Drag the land up out of the sea, then tap the land | The land rises and green spreads where you tap. |
| 4 | Drag the sun to the day side and the moon to the night side, then sweep across the sky | The stars scatter in the wake of the sweep. |
| 5 | Swipe down into the sea and up into the sky | Shoals of fish and flocks of birds burst forth. |
| 6 | Tap the animals as they arrive | They take their places. The man and the woman are not a player action. |
| 7 | Nothing | Rest. |

- **Teaches:** the order of the days. Days 1–3 make spaces and days 4–6 fill them.
- **Gentle mode:** gestures can't fail anyway; gentle mode adds on-screen arrows showing each gesture.
- **Accessibility:** every gesture has a keyboard equivalent and a single-button option (press Space to perform the day's action).

**Thread:** *Creation by God's word → the Word through whom all things were made.* The glowing object is the first point of light from scene 1.2, which shines on in a corner of every later scene until the player finds it.
- **[Q] John 1:1–3:** "In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God. He was in the beginning with God; all things were made through him, and without him was not anything made that was made."
- **What it shows:** God created by speaking. John reveals that this Word is a person: the Son, who became flesh in Jesus. The "In the beginning" of John's Gospel deliberately echoes Genesis.
- **Source:** John 1:1–14; Catechism 291.

**Where and when:**
- **Before recorded history.** Genesis 1–11 tells of the time before any history can be dated. The Church reads these chapters as revealing true things about God, creation and humanity in symbolic language (Catechism 337, 390).
- **A careful pattern.** Days 1–3 create spaces (light and dark, sky and sea, land), and days 4–6 fill them (sun, moon and stars; fish and birds; animals and people). Day 7 crowns everything with rest.
- **Against its neighbors.** In Babylon's creation story the gods fight each other, and humans are made as slaves for them. Genesis tells of one God who creates in peace by his word, calls everything good, and makes humans in his own image.
- **"Let us."** Christian readers have long heard in "Let us make man" (1:26) a first whisper of the Trinity.

**Read:** Genesis 1:1–2:3.

## Episode 2 · The Garden of Eden

**Genesis 2:4–25 · about 8 minutes · 5 scenes**

### Scene 2.1 · Dust and breath
*On screen:* Bare ground with a mist rising from it. Two great hands of light shape a figure from the earth; then breath, like wind, and the figure opens his eyes.

1. Genesis now tells the story of creation a second time, up close. This time it's told not from the heavens down, but from the ground up, through the eyes of the first man.
2. When the LORD God made the earth and the heavens, no plant had yet sprung up, for there was no one to till the ground. A mist rose from the earth and watered it.
3. Then the LORD God bent low, the way a potter shapes clay.
4. **[Q] Genesis 2:7** "Then the LORD God formed man of dust from the ground, and breathed into his nostrils the breath of life; and man became a living being."
5. The Hebrew word for man, *adam*, sounds like the word for ground, *adamah*. We are made of earth and filled with God's own breath.

### Scene 2.2 · The garden and its rivers
*On screen:* A wide, lush garden with a great river dividing into four as it flows out of view. Two trees stand in the middle, one glowing softly with life.

1. **[Q] Genesis 2:8** "And the LORD God planted a garden in Eden, in the east; and there he put the man whom he had formed."
2. Every tree that was beautiful to see and good to eat grew there. In the middle of the garden stood the tree of life, and the tree of the knowledge of good and evil.
3. A river flowed out of Eden and divided into four: the Pishon, the Gihon, the Tigris and the Euphrates.
4. God put the man in the garden to till it and keep it. From the very beginning, work was a gift: a share in God's care for the world.

### Scene 2.3 · The one command
*On screen:* The man walking among the trees. The camera settles on the tree of the knowledge of good and evil.

1. **[Q] Genesis 2:16–17** "And the LORD God commanded the man, saying, 'You may freely eat of every tree of the garden; but of the tree of the knowledge of good and evil you shall not eat, for in the day that you eat of it you shall die.'"
2. Everything was given: every tree but one.
3. The one limit wasn't a trap. It was a reminder that the man was a creature, and that God alone decides what is good and what is evil.

### Scene 2.4 · Naming the animals
*On screen:* Animals come to the man one by one across the garden, in pairs. He greets each one. Finally the camera pulls back: every creature has its partner, and the man stands alone.

1. **[Q] Genesis 2:18** "Then the LORD God said, 'It is not good that the man should be alone; I will make him a helper fit for him.'"
2. Out of the ground the LORD God formed every beast and every bird, and brought them to the man to see what he would call them.
3. *(Play: naming the animals.)*
4. The man gave names to them all.
5. **[Q] Genesis 2:20 (in part)** "…but for the man there was not found a helper fit for him."
6. Every creature had its partner. The man stood alone.

### Scene 2.5 · The woman
*On screen:* The man asleep under a tree, with soft light around him. Then the woman, standing in the morning light. He wakes, sees her, and stands.

1. So the LORD God caused a deep sleep to fall upon the man. He took one of his ribs and closed up its place with flesh, and from the rib he made a woman, and brought her to the man.
2. **[Q] Genesis 2:23** "Then the man said, 'This at last is bone of my bones and flesh of my flesh; she shall be called Woman, because she was taken out of Man.'"
3. **[Q] Genesis 2:24** "Therefore a man leaves his father and his mother and cleaves to his wife, and they become one flesh."
4. They were naked, and they were not ashamed. Nothing stood between them, and nothing stood between them and God.

### Play: naming the animals
- **What it is:** the matching and naming kind of play. About 8 animals come to Adam one at a time. For each one, the player picks the name that fits from three descriptions, for example "strong and golden, his roar wakes the dawn" for the lion, or "keen-eyed, she builds her nest on the cliffs" for the eagle.
- **How it ends:** each named animal joins its partner and goes to its place. After the last one, the music thins and the camera pulls back to the man alone, which leads into scene 2.4's last lines.
- **Mistakes:** a wrong choice gets a gentle "Not quite" and the animal waits. There's no penalty.
- **Teaches:** Adam's authority and care over creation, and his loneliness that no animal could fill.
- **Gentle mode:** two choices instead of three.

**Thread:** *Marriage, the two becoming one flesh → Christ and the Church.* The glowing object is the light around the sleeping man in scene 2.5.
- **[Q] Ephesians 5:31–32:** "'For this reason a man shall leave his father and mother and be joined to his wife, and the two shall become one flesh.' This is a great mystery, and I mean in reference to Christ and the church."
- **What it shows:** marriage, from the very first page, is a living picture of Christ's love for his Church. Christian writers also saw the woman formed from Adam's side as a sign of the Church born from the side of Christ on the cross (John 19:34).
- **Source:** Ephesians 5:21–33; Catechism 1602, 1616.

**Where and when:**
- **Eden, "in the east."** Genesis names four rivers. The Tigris and the Euphrates still flow through modern Turkey, Syria and Iraq, the land called Mesopotamia. The Pishon and the Gihon are unknown.
- **Two accounts.** Genesis 1 and 2 tell creation from two angles, first from the heavens and then up close. Ancient writers often told one truth from more than one side.
- **Words to know.** *Adam* means man or humankind, and sounds like *adamah*, ground.

**Read:** Genesis 2:4–25.

## Episode 3 · The Serpent and the Fall

**Genesis 3:1–13 · about 7 minutes · 6 scenes**

### Scene 3.1 · The serpent's question
*On screen:* Afternoon in the garden. A serpent winds along a branch of the forbidden tree, close to the woman.

1. **[Q] Genesis 3:1** "Now the serpent was more subtle than any other wild creature that the LORD God had made. He said to the woman, 'Did God say, "You shall not eat of any tree of the garden"?'"
2. It wasn't even a lie yet, just a question. But it made God's generous gift sound like a harsh rule. God had said every tree, freely, except one. The serpent said: not *any* tree?
3. **[Q] Genesis 3:2–3** "And the woman said to the serpent, 'We may eat of the fruit of the trees of the garden; but God said, "You shall not eat of the fruit of the tree which is in the midst of the garden, neither shall you touch it, lest you die."'"
4. Later Scripture names who speaks through the serpent: **[Q] Revelation 12:9 (in part)** "that ancient serpent, who is called the Devil and Satan."

### Scene 3.2 · The lie
*On screen:* The serpent coils closer. The fruit seems to glow, and everything else in the garden dims.

1. **[Q] Genesis 3:4–5** "But the serpent said to the woman, 'You will not die. For God knows that when you eat of it your eyes will be opened, and you will be like God, knowing good and evil.'"
2. *(Play: spot the twist.)*
3. Now the lie was out in the open, and with it a darker suggestion: that God was holding something back, that he could not be trusted.

### Scene 3.3 · The fruit
*On screen:* Her hand reaching. Then the man beside her. The fruit passes between them.

1. **[Q] Genesis 3:6** "So when the woman saw that the tree was good for food, and that it was a delight to the eyes, and that the tree was to be desired to make one wise, she took of its fruit and ate; and she also gave some to her husband, and he ate."
2. Every good thing in the garden had come from God's hand. Now they reached to take it, to decide for themselves what was good.

### Scene 3.4 · Eyes opened
*On screen:* The light changes, colder now. The two turn away from each other and sew fig leaves together.

1. Their eyes were opened, but not the way the serpent had promised. They saw that they were naked, and they were ashamed. They sewed fig leaves together to cover themselves.
2. The first thing sin brought was shame, and something now stood between them.

### Scene 3.5 · "Where are you?"
*On screen:* Evening. A soft light moves through the garden, which is the presence of God, shown as light and voice. The two hide among the trees.

1. In the cool of the day they heard the sound of the LORD God walking in the garden, and they hid themselves among the trees.
2. **[Q] Genesis 3:9** "But the LORD God called to the man, and said to him, 'Where are you?'"
3. God knew where they were. The question was for them. It's the first time in the Bible that God comes seeking the lost, and he has been asking it ever since.
4. The man answered that he had heard God, and was afraid because he was naked, and had hidden himself.

### Scene 3.6 · Blame
*On screen:* The man points at the woman, and the woman points at the serpent. The light waits, patient.

1. "Have you eaten of the tree I commanded you not to eat?" God asked.
2. The man said: the woman you gave to be with me, she gave me the fruit, and I ate.
3. The woman said: the serpent deceived me, and I ate.
4. The man blamed the woman, and even God who had given her. The woman blamed the serpent. Sin had broken trust with God, and between each other.

### Play: "Spot the twist"
- **What it is:** a quiet interaction from the matching kind of play. Two panels sit side by side: on the left, **what God said** (Genesis 2:16–17); on the right, **what was said in the garden** (Genesis 3:1, 3:3, 3:4–5). The player taps each phrase on the right that bends God's words. There are four to find:
  1. **"not … any tree."** God gave *every* tree freely except one. The serpent made a generous gift sound like a total ban.
  2. **"neither shall you touch it."** God never said this. Adding to God's word made the command seem harsher than it was.
  3. **"You will not die."** A flat contradiction of God's warning.
  4. **"God knows … you will be like God."** The suggestion that God is jealous, and a lie about God's heart.
- **After each find,** one sentence explains it.
- **Teaches:** how temptation works: exaggerate the rule, doubt the warning, suspect the Giver.
- **Gentle mode:** the phrases to find are underlined faintly.

**Thread:** *Disobedience in a garden → obedience in a garden.* The glowing object is the forbidden tree.
- **[Q] Romans 5:19:** "For as by one man's disobedience many were made sinners, so by one man's obedience many will be made righteous."
- **[Q] John 18:1 (in part):** "…across the Kidron valley, where there was a garden…"
- **[Q] Luke 22:42:** "Father, if thou art willing, remove this cup from me; nevertheless not my will, but thine, be done." *(Verify the RSV-2CE wording: see the verse table.)*
- **What it shows:** Adam said "my will" in a garden. Jesus, the new Adam, said "not my will, but yours" in a garden too, the night before he died, and undid the first disobedience.
- **Source:** Romans 5:12–21; Catechism 411.

**Where and when:**
- **The serpent.** In the ancient world, serpents stood for wisdom, life and danger all at once. Revelation 12:9 identifies "that ancient serpent" as the devil.
- **"Knowing good and evil."** This means deciding for oneself what is good and what is evil, which belongs to God alone. The tree stands for the limit a creature accepts with trust (Catechism 396).

**Read:** Genesis 3:1–13.

## Episode 4 · The Promise and the Exile

**Genesis 3:14–24 · about 8 minutes · 5 scenes**

### Scene 4.1 · The serpent cursed, and the promise
*On screen:* The serpent sinks into the dust. Then, in the darkening garden, a single point of light appears and steadily grows.

1. Then the LORD God turned to the serpent. It would go on its belly, and eat dust, all the days of its life.
2. **[Q] Genesis 3:15** "I will put enmity between you and the woman, and between your seed and her seed; he shall bruise your head, and you shall bruise his heel."
3. In the darkest moment of the story, God speaks a promise. The battle isn't over. A child of the woman will strike the serpent's head, though he himself will be wounded.
4. The Church calls this verse the *first gospel*: the first announcement of the good news.

### Scene 4.2 · The consequences
*On screen:* The garden's colors fade. Thorns grow. The man bends over hard ground; sweat, and toil.

1. To the woman, God foretold pain in bearing children and a broken harmony with her husband.
2. To the man, God said that the ground would now yield bread only through toil and sweat, until he returned to the ground from which he was taken.
3. **[Q] Genesis 3:19 (in part)** "…you are dust, and to dust you shall return."
4. Every Ash Wednesday the Church marks our foreheads with these words. It's not to crush us, but to remind us who we are, and who alone can save us.

### Scene 4.3 · Eve
*On screen:* The man turns to the woman. Despite everything, a small warm light passes between them.

1. **[Q] Genesis 3:20** "The man called his wife's name Eve, because she was the mother of all living."
2. Death had entered the world, and the man answered by naming his wife Life. *Eve* means "living."

### Scene 4.4 · Garments of skins
*On screen:* Their fig leaves crumble. The light of God draws near and wraps them in warm garments.

1. **[Q] Genesis 3:21** "And the LORD God made for Adam and for his wife garments of skins, and clothed them."
2. Even in judgment, God cared for them. Their fig leaves would not last, so God himself clothed them.

### Scene 4.5 · East of Eden
*On screen:* The two walk out through the garden's eastern edge into a wide, harder land. Behind them stand the cherubim, and a flaming sword turning every way.

1. God would not let them reach for the tree of life and live forever in their broken state.
2. So the LORD God sent them out of the garden, to till the ground from which the man had been taken.
3. **[Q] Genesis 3:24** "He drove out the man; and at the east of the garden of Eden he placed the cherubim, and a flaming sword which turned every way, to guard the way to the tree of life."
4. *(Play: lights in the dark.)*
5. The way back was closed. But the promise went with them.

### Play: "Lights in the dark"
- **What it is:** a quiet interaction from the searching kind of play. As the two walk out of the garden at dusk, the player guides a small light through the darkening scene, with a finger, the mouse or the arrow keys, to find three glowing signs of God's mercy inside the judgment:
  1. **the promise** (Genesis 3:15)
  2. **the garments** (Genesis 3:21)
  3. **the name "Eve"** (Genesis 3:20)
- **Each find** brightens the path a little and recalls its line. When all three are found, the couple walks on in the light they carry.
- **Teaches:** that God's mercy runs through even this sad chapter.
- **Gentle mode:** the signs pulse more brightly, and the light is larger.

**Thread (the main thread of Level 1):** *the Woman and her offspring → Mary and Jesus.* The glowing object is the first point of light from scene 4.1.
- **[Q] John 19:26:** "When Jesus saw his mother, and the disciple whom he loved standing near, he said to his mother, 'Woman, behold, your son!'"
- **[Q] Revelation 12:1:** "And a great portent appeared in heaven, a woman clothed with the sun, with the moon under her feet, and on her head a crown of twelve stars;"
- **What it shows:** the Woman's offspring is Jesus, who crushes the serpent's power on the cross, wounded himself. The Woman beside him is Mary, the new Eve. Jesus calls her "Woman" from the cross, and Revelation shows her in glory. The first Eve said no to God; Mary said yes. Level 53 closes this loop.
- **Source:** Catechism 410–411; St. Irenaeus (the Eve–Mary parallel).

**Where and when:**
- **"He shall bruise your head."** The Hebrew points to the woman's offspring. St. Jerome's Latin translation (the Vulgate) reads "she shall crush," and the Douay-Rheims follows it. That's why so many paintings show Mary crushing the serpent. Both readings honor the same truth: the Son of the Woman, and his mother with him, defeat the serpent.
- **Cherubim.** In Scripture, cherubim guard holy places. Later, golden cherubim would stand over the ark of the covenant (Exodus 25).

**Read:** Genesis 3:14–24.

## Episode 5 · Cain and Abel

**Genesis 4:1–16 · about 9 minutes · 6 scenes**

> **Content note shown before this episode:** "This episode tells of the first murder. It is told with restraint, and nothing is shown."

### Scene 5.1 · Two brothers
*On screen:* Outside the garden, a hillside home. Two boys grow into men: one walks among sheep, the other works a field.

1. Outside the garden, life went on.
2. Eve bore a son, Cain, and said: **[Q] Genesis 4:1 (in part)** "I have gotten a man with the help of the LORD."
3. Then she bore his brother, Abel.
4. Abel became a keeper of sheep, and Cain a tiller of the ground.

### Scene 5.2 · Two offerings
*On screen:* Two altars of stone. Smoke rises from both: from Abel's, straight up into light; from Cain's, low and wavering.

1. *(Play: Abel's flock.)*
2. In time, both brothers brought offerings to the LORD. Cain brought some of the fruit of the ground.
3. **[Q] Genesis 4:4 (in part)** "…and Abel brought of the firstlings of his flock and of their fat portions."
4. The LORD had regard for Abel and his offering, but not for Cain's.
5. Why? Genesis notices the difference in the gifts: Abel gave the first and the best. The Letter to the Hebrews sees the difference in the heart: **[Q] Hebrews 11:4 (in part)** "By faith Abel offered to God a more acceptable sacrifice than Cain."

### Scene 5.3 · Sin at the door
*On screen:* Cain alone at dusk, his face fallen. In the doorway behind him, a dark shape crouches, like an animal waiting.

1. Cain was very angry, and his face fell.
2. The LORD spoke to him: why are you angry, and why has your face fallen?
3. **[Q] Genesis 4:7** "If you do well, will you not be accepted? And if you do not do well, sin is couching at the door; its desire is for you, but you must master it."
4. God warned Cain the way a father warns a son. Sin was waiting at the door like a crouching animal, but Cain could still master it. The choice was still his.

### Scene 5.4 · In the field
*On screen:* Two silhouettes walk away into a wide field at evening. The screen fades slowly to dusk. There's only the wind, and a single bird rising.

1. Cain said to his brother Abel, "Let us go out to the field."
2. And there, in the field, Cain rose up against his brother Abel and killed him.

### Scene 5.5 · "Where is your brother?"
*On screen:* Cain alone in the empty field. The light of God comes near.

1. **[Q] Genesis 4:9** "Then the LORD said to Cain, 'Where is Abel your brother?' He said, 'I do not know; am I my brother's keeper?'"
2. Once, God had asked, "Where are you?" Now he asked, "Where is your brother?" The first sin broke trust with God. This one broke the human family.

### Scene 5.6 · The voice of blood, and the mark
*On screen:* The ground where Abel fell, glowing faintly. Then Cain walking east into a barren land, a small sign of light on his forehead.

1. **[Q] Genesis 4:10** "And the LORD said, 'What have you done? The voice of your brother's blood is crying to me from the ground.'"
2. Cain would wander the earth, and the ground would no longer yield its strength to him.
3. Cain cried out that his punishment was more than he could bear, and that anyone who found him would kill him.
4. Even then, God protected him. The LORD put a mark on Cain so that no one who came upon him would kill him.
5. And Cain went away from the presence of the LORD and settled in the land of Nod, **[Q] Genesis 4:16 (in part)** "east of Eden."

### Play: Abel's flock
- **What it is:** the herding kind of play.
- **Part 1:** guide Abel's sheep home across a hillside into the fold, steering with the shepherd's staff (finger, mouse or arrow keys). A few lambs wander off and need gentle nudging back.
- **Part 2, the choice:** in the fold, the firstborn lambs are marked with a soft glow. The player chooses which to bring as the offering. Choosing the firstborn and best fulfills the offering. Any other choice gets a quiet "Abel gave the first and the best" and an invitation to choose again.
- **Teaches:** giving God our first and best, not our leftovers.
- **Gentle mode:** slower sheep, a wider fold gate, and no lambs wander.

**Thread:** *Abel's blood → the blood of Jesus.* The glowing object is the ground in scene 5.6.
- **[Q] Hebrews 12:24:** "and to Jesus, the mediator of a new covenant, and to the sprinkled blood that speaks more graciously than the blood of Abel."
- **What it shows:** Abel's innocent blood cried out for justice. Jesus' innocent blood speaks a better word: mercy, forgiveness and a new covenant. Abel, the shepherd who offered a lamb and was killed by his brother, points to Jesus, the Good Shepherd and Lamb of God. At Mass the Church still remembers "the gifts of your servant Abel the just."
- **Source:** Hebrews 11:4 and 12:24; the Roman Canon (Eucharistic Prayer I).

**Where and when:**
- **The land of Nod, "east of Eden."** *Nod* sounds like the Hebrew word for *wandering*.
- **Offerings.** Shepherds offered their firstborn animals and farmers their first fruits. Giving the first was a way of saying that everything belongs to God.

**Read:** Genesis 4:1–16.

## Episode 6 · From Adam to Noah

**Genesis 4:17–5:32 · about 6 minutes · 5 scenes**

### Scene 6.1 · Cain's line
*On screen:* A city rising: walls, tents and herds, then a lyre and pipe, then sparks from a forge. Then one man, Lamech, boasting to his two wives.

1. Cain's family grew. He built a city and named it Enoch, after his son.
2. His descendants were makers and builders: Jabal, father of those who live in tents and keep herds; Jubal, father of all who play the lyre and pipe; and Tubal-cain, who forged bronze and iron.
3. But skill did not make them good. Lamech, of Cain's line, boasted to his wives that he had killed a man for wounding him. If Cain would be avenged seven times, he said, then Lamech seventy-seven.
4. One day Jesus would turn Lamech's boast inside out. Forgive, he would say, **[Q] Matthew 18:22 (in part)** "not … seven times, but seventy times seven."

### Scene 6.2 · Seth
*On screen:* Adam and Eve, older now, holding a newborn. Later, a family gathered at an altar at dusk, lifting their hands.

1. Adam and Eve had another son. Eve called him Seth, and said: **[Q] Genesis 4:25 (in part)** "God has appointed for me another child instead of Abel, for Cain slew him."
2. Seth had a son, Enosh.
3. **[Q] Genesis 4:26 (in part)** "At that time men began to call upon the name of the LORD."
4. Two families now walked the earth: one building cities and boasting of revenge, the other calling on the name of the LORD.

### Scene 6.3 · Ten generations
*On screen:* A great family tree grows across the screen, generation by generation, from Adam to Noah.

1. "This is the book of the generations of Adam," Genesis says, and it lists them one by one.
2. *(Play: the family tree.)*
3. Ten generations: Adam, Seth, Enosh, Kenan, Mahalalel, Jared, Enoch, Methuselah, Lamech and Noah. Each lived, had children, and died.
4. The great ages in this chapter, Adam 930 years and Methuselah 969, have puzzled readers for centuries, and they have been understood in different ways. Whatever their meaning, Genesis makes its point: generation after generation, the line of the promise goes on.

### Scene 6.4 · Enoch walked with God
*On screen:* Enoch walking along a path that rises gently into light. Then the path is empty, glowing.

1. One name breaks the pattern. Of every other man in the list it says, "and he died." But of Enoch it is written:
2. **[Q] Genesis 5:24** "Enoch walked with God; and he was not, for God took him."
3. The Letter to the Hebrews says: **[Q] Hebrews 11:5 (in part)** "By faith Enoch was taken up so that he should not see death."
4. Enoch shows what human life was meant to be: walking with God.

### Scene 6.5 · Noah is born
*On screen:* A weary farmer at the end of a long day lifts a newborn son toward the evening sky. The ground around him is hard and full of thorns, but the light on the child is warm.

1. Methuselah's son was named Lamech too, a different Lamech from Cain's line. He had a son.
2. **[Q] Genesis 5:29** "…and called his name Noah, saying, 'Out of the ground which the LORD has cursed this one shall bring us relief from our work and from the toil of our hands.'"
3. The ground was cursed and life was toil, and a father hoped this child would bring relief. He would, in a way no one could imagine. That is the next level's story.

### Play: the family tree
- **What it is:** the ordering kind of play. Ten name-leaves drift down, and the player places each one on the tree in order from Adam to Noah.
- **Each correct placement** grows the next branch and shows one short fact:
  - Adam: "lived 930 years"
  - Enosh: "in his days people began to call upon the LORD"
  - Enoch: "walked with God"
  - Methuselah: "969 years, the longest life in the Bible"
  - Noah: "this one shall bring us relief"
- **Mistakes:** a misplaced leaf floats back with a hint, such as "Seth came after Adam."
- **Teaches:** the line from Adam to Noah, and that God's promise travels through generations.
- **Gentle mode:** the next correct leaf glows.

**Thread:** *the family line from Adam → Jesus.* The glowing object is the root of the family tree.
- **[Q] Luke 3:38:** "the son of Enos, the son of Seth, the son of Adam, the son of God."
- **What it shows:** Luke traces Jesus' family tree all the way back through these very names (Enos, or Enosh, then Seth and Adam) to God. The promise of Genesis 3:15 travels down this line of real people until it is fulfilled in him.
- **Source:** Luke 3:23–38.

**Where and when:**
- **The first civilization.** Genesis credits Cain's line with the first cities, herding, music and metalwork.
- **Ten generations.** Ancient family lists often used ten generations to cover a whole age. The list from Shem to Abraham in Genesis 11 has ten as well.

**Read:** Genesis 4:17–5:32.

**Verse count.** Level 1 quotes 46 passages, about 52 verses counting ranges, some only in part. At this rate the whole game would quote roughly 2,750 verses.
