# Level 4 script: *Isaac and Jacob*

**Bible:** Genesis 24–36
**Age:** II · The Patriarchs
**Length:** about 50 minutes (7 episodes, then the quiz, then reflection and prayer)
**Status:** draft, waiting for Dave's approval.

## How to read this script

- Each **scene** is a checkpoint. Its numbered lines are the narration beats; the player taps to move from one to the next.
- *On screen* describes what the scene shows. It's the brief for the art in both styles.
- **[Q]** marks an exact quotation from the Bible. Its wording in both translations is in the verse table at the end. Everything else is our own retelling.
- **Play** is the episode's interactive moment. **Thread** is its golden thread. **Read** is the passage the game sends the player to.
- "LORD" in capitals stands for God's holy name, printed in small capitals in the RSV.

### What this level is about

Level 3 ended with Abraham burying Sarah, holding the promised land only in faith. Level 4 follows the promise through his son and grandson. A servant prays at a well, and Rebekah says, "I will go." Twins struggle even before they are born: Esau the hunter, who sells his birthright for a bowl of stew, and Jacob the quiet one, who takes his brother's blessing by a trick and has to run for his life. Alone in the night, Jacob dreams of a ladder to heaven. He works fourteen years for the woman he loves, is tricked in his turn, and becomes the father of twelve sons. Coming home, he wrestles until dawn with a mysterious stranger and receives a new name: Israel. And at last, the brother he wronged runs to meet him with open arms.

This is a family of sinners, and the Bible does not hide it. Yet God keeps his promise through them, and through Jacob's son Judah the line runs on toward Christ. The threads point to Mary's "yes," the Bread of life, Baptism, Christ the ladder to heaven, God's love for the lowly, persevering prayer, and the Father who runs to meet his returning son.

## Episode 1 · A Bride for Isaac

**Genesis 24 · about 8 minutes · 4 scenes**

### Scene 1.1 · Abraham's charge
*On screen:* Abraham, very old, sits in the shade of his tent at Mamre and speaks with his oldest servant. Camels kneel nearby, loaded with gifts.

1. **[Q] Genesis 24:1** "Now Abraham was old, well advanced in years; and the LORD had blessed Abraham in all things."
2. One thing remained: a wife for Isaac, from Abraham's own people, not from the Canaanites around them.
3. **[Q] Genesis 24:4** "but will go to my country and to my kindred, and take a wife for my son Isaac.""

### Scene 1.2 · A prayer at the well
*On screen:* Evening, outside a town in the north, by a well. The servant's ten camels kneel in the dust. He bows his head. Women come out with jars on their shoulders.

1. **[Q] Genesis 24:10** "Then the servant took ten of his master's camels and departed, taking all sorts of choice gifts from his master; and he arose, and went to Mesopotamia, to the city of Nahor."
2. **[Q] Genesis 24:12** "And he said, "O LORD, God of my master Abraham, grant me success today, I pray thee, and show steadfast love to my master Abraham."
3. He asked God for a sign: the girl who offers water not only to him, but to his camels too.

### Scene 1.3 · Rebekah
*On screen:* A young woman, her jar on her shoulder, goes down to the well. The servant runs to her. She lowers her jar for him, then runs back and forth to the trough for the camels. *(Play: watering the camels.)*

1. **[Q] Genesis 24:15** "Before he had done speaking, behold, Rebekah, who was born to Bethuel the son of Milcah, the wife of Nahor, Abraham's brother, came out with her water jar upon her shoulder."
2. **[Q] Genesis 24:18** "She said, "Drink, my lord"; and she quickly let down her jar upon her hand, and gave him a drink."
3. **[Q] Genesis 24:19** "When she had finished giving him a drink, she said, "I will draw for your camels also, until they have done drinking.""
4. *(Play: watering the camels.)*
5. A thirsty camel can drink a great deal of water. Ten camels meant many trips up and down to the well. Rebekah's kindness was not small.

### Scene 1.4 · "I will go"
*On screen:* Rebekah's family's house: her brother Laban and her father, the servant with gifts of gold. Then the caravan on the road south, and at evening, Isaac walking in a field; Rebekah sees him and covers herself with her veil.

1. The servant told her family everything: his master's prayer, his own, and God's answer at the well.
2. **[Q] Genesis 24:58** "And they called Rebekah, and said to her, "Will you go with this man?" She said, "I will go.""
3. **[Q] Genesis 24:63** "And Isaac went out to meditate in the field in the evening; and he lifted up his eyes and looked, and behold, there were camels coming."
4. **[Q] Genesis 24:67** "Then Isaac brought her into the tent, and took Rebekah, and she became his wife; and he loved her. So Isaac was comforted after his mother's death."

### Play: watering the camels
- **What it is:** pouring and water (spec §13.7, kind 12).
- **How it plays:** Rebekah draws water from the well and pours it into the stone trough. The player lowers the jar into the well, lifts it full (hold, then release), and pours it into the trough (tip, or drag). Ten thirsty camels come up to drink one after another; each one that has had enough kneels down contentedly. A full trough waters faster; a spilled jar only means another trip.
- **Teaches:** generosity that does more than it is asked; the servant's prayer answered.
- **Gentle mode:** the jar fills and pours by itself with each tap.
- **Accessibility:** a pointer, or Space to draw and pour.

**Thread:** *Rebekah's "I will go" → Mary's "let it be."* The glowing object is Rebekah's water jar.
- **[Q] Luke 1:38:** "And Mary said, "Behold, I am the handmaid of the Lord; let it be to me according to your word." And the angel departed from her."
- **What it shows:** Rebekah said yes to a journey she had not planned, to marry a man she had never met, because she saw God's hand in it. Mary said yes to God's messenger, and the Son of God came into the world. The Church prays for the same readiness: to say "I will go" when God calls.
- **Source:** Luke 1:26–38; Catechism 148, 494.

**Where and when:**
- **When.** Isaac's and Jacob's time: roughly 1900–1600 BC, as scholars estimate.
- **The city of Nahor.** In Aram-naharaim ("Aram of the two rivers"), near Haran, where Abraham's brother Nahor's family had stayed.
- **Camels and water.** A camel that has walked through the desert can drink well over 100 liters. Watering ten of them was hours of work.

**Read:** Genesis 24.

## Episode 2 · The Birthright

**Genesis 25:7–34 · about 7 minutes · 4 scenes**

### Scene 2.1 · Two nations
*On screen:* Abraham's burial at the cave of Machpelah: Isaac and Ishmael side by side. Then Isaac praying outside the tent, and Rebekah, troubled, a hand on her womb.

1. **[Q] Genesis 25:8** "Abraham breathed his last and died in a good old age, an old man and full of years, and was gathered to his people."
2. Rebekah, like Sarah, was barren.
3. **[Q] Genesis 25:21** "And Isaac prayed to the LORD for his wife, because she was barren; and the LORD granted his prayer, and Rebekah his wife conceived."
4. **[Q] Genesis 25:23** "And the LORD said to her, "Two nations are in your womb, and two peoples, born of you, shall be divided; the one shall be stronger than the other, the elder shall serve the younger.""

### Scene 2.2 · Twins
*On screen:* Two babies: one red and hairy, crying loudly; the other holding on to his brother's heel.

1. **[Q] Genesis 25:25** "The first came forth red, all his body like a hairy mantle; so they called his name Esau."
2. **[Q] Genesis 25:26** "Afterward his brother came forth, and his hand had taken hold of Esau's heel; so his name was called Jacob. Isaac was sixty years old when she bore them."
3. The name Jacob sounds like the Hebrew word for "heel." It came to mean one who grabs from behind: a supplanter.

### Scene 2.3 · The hunter and the quiet man
*On screen:* Esau, grown, striding out to the hills with his bow; Jacob at the tents, tending the cooking fire. Isaac smiles at Esau; Rebekah watches Jacob.

1. **[Q] Genesis 25:27** "When the boys grew up, Esau was a skilful hunter, a man of the field, while Jacob was a quiet man, dwelling in tents."
2. **[Q] Genesis 25:28** "Isaac loved Esau, because he ate of his game; but Rebekah loved Jacob."

### Scene 2.4 · A bowl of red stew
*On screen:* Jacob stirs a pot of red lentil stew. Esau comes in from the field, exhausted and starving. Jacob holds out the bowl, but not yet. *(Play: what is it worth?)*

1. **[Q] Genesis 25:30** "And Esau said to Jacob, "Let me eat some of that red pottage, for I am famished!" (Therefore his name was called Edom.)"
2. **[Q] Genesis 25:31** "Jacob said, "First sell me your birthright.""
3. The birthright was the eldest son's inheritance: a double share, the leadership of the family, and in this family, God's promise to Abraham.
4. *(Play: what is it worth?)*
5. **[Q] Genesis 25:32** "Esau said, "I am about to die; of what use is a birthright to me?""
6. **[Q] Genesis 25:34** "Then Jacob gave Esau bread and pottage of lentils, and he ate and drank, and rose and went his way. Thus Esau despised his birthright."

### Play: what is it worth?
- **What it is:** a quiet interaction (spec §13.7, kind 20): weighing.
- **How it plays:** a pair of scales. On one pan sits a steaming bowl of stew, and it seems heavy to a hungry man. The player places on the other pan, one by one, what the birthright really meant: the double inheritance; the leadership of the family; the blessing of Abraham; God's promise of a land and a great nation; the line from which a Savior would come. With each, the scales tip further, until the bowl of stew flies up, light as nothing. Then Esau, too hungry to look, takes the bowl anyway.
- **Teaches:** what Esau gave away; the value of God's gifts compared with what we want right now.
- **Gentle mode:** each piece glows and floats to the pan when tapped.
- **Accessibility:** Space places the next piece on the scales.

**Thread:** *Esau sells his birthright for a meal → "Man shall not live by bread alone."* The glowing object is the bowl of red stew.
- **[Q] Matthew 4:4:** "But he answered, "It is written, 'Man shall not live by bread alone, but by every word that proceeds from the mouth of God.'""
- **What it shows:** Esau, starving, gave up God's promise for one meal. Jesus, starving after forty days in the desert, was tempted to turn stones into bread, and refused: God's word mattered more than his hunger. The Letter to the Hebrews warns us not to be like Esau, "who sold his birthright for a single meal."
- **Source:** Matthew 4:1–4; Hebrews 12:16; Catechism 538–540.

**Where and when:**
- **Edom.** Esau's descendants were the Edomites, who lived south of the Dead Sea. "Edom" means "red."
- **The birthright.** By ancient custom, the firstborn son received a double share of his father's goods (Deuteronomy 21:17) and became head of the family.
- **Lentils.** Red lentil stew is still cooked across the Middle East today, much as Jacob made it.

**Read:** Genesis 25:19–34.

## Episode 3 · The Stolen Blessing

**Genesis 27 · about 8 minutes · 4 scenes**

### Scene 3.1 · Isaac grows old
*On screen:* Inside the tent, dim light. Isaac, old and blind, lies on his bed. Esau kneels beside him, bow on his shoulder. At the tent door, Rebekah listens.

1. **[Q] Genesis 27:1** "When Isaac was old and his eyes were dim so that he could not see, he called Esau his older son, and said to him, "My son"; and he answered, "Here I am.""
2. **[Q] Genesis 27:4** "and prepare for me savory food, such as I love, and bring it to me that I may eat; that I may bless you before I die.""
3. In those days a father's blessing before he died was solemn and final. Once given, it could not be taken back.

### Scene 3.2 · Rebekah's plan
*On screen:* Rebekah cooks quickly over the fire. Jacob, uneasy, holds out his arms as she ties goatskins over his hands and neck and dresses him in Esau's best robe.

1. Rebekah remembered what God had said: the elder shall serve the younger. But instead of trusting God, she made a plan of her own.
2. **[Q] Genesis 27:16** "and the skins of the kids she put upon his hands and upon the smooth part of his neck;"
3. Jacob was afraid of being found out. But he went along with the lie.

### Scene 3.3 · The voice is Jacob's voice
*On screen:* Jacob kneels at his father's bedside with the dish. The blind old man reaches out and feels the hairy hands, puzzled, then kisses him and lays his hands on his head. *(Play: the blessing.)*

1. **[Q] Genesis 27:22** "So Jacob went near to Isaac his father, who felt him and said, "The voice is Jacob's voice, but the hands are the hands of Esau.""
2. **[Q] Genesis 27:27** "So he came near and kissed him; and he smelled the smell of his garments, and blessed him, and said, "See, the smell of my son is as the smell of a field which the LORD has blessed!"
3. *(Play: the blessing.)*
4. **[Q] Genesis 27:28** "May God give you of the dew of heaven, and of the fatness of the earth, and plenty of grain and wine."
5. **[Q] Genesis 27:29** "Let peoples serve you, and nations bow down to you. Be lord over your brothers, and may your mother's sons bow down to you. Cursed be every one who curses you, and blessed be every one who blesses you!""

### Scene 3.4 · A bitter cry
*On screen:* Esau comes in with his dish, and stops. Isaac trembles. Esau falls to his knees weeping. Later, Jacob slips away at night with a small bundle, alone.

1. **[Q] Genesis 27:34** "When Esau heard the words of his father, he cried out with an exceedingly great and bitter cry, and said to his father, "Bless me, even me also, O my father!""
2. Esau's grief turned to hatred, and he swore to kill his brother.
3. **[Q] Genesis 27:43** "Now therefore, my son, obey my voice; arise, flee to Laban my brother in Haran,"
4. Everyone in this family did wrong: Isaac favored one son, Rebekah plotted, Jacob lied, and Esau had despised what he now wept for. God's plan went on, but the sin cost them dearly: Rebekah never saw her favorite son again.

### Play: the blessing
- **What it is:** ordering (spec §13.7, kind 9).
- **How it plays:** Isaac's hands rest on Jacob's head, and the words of the blessing float up like light, out of order. The player sets them in the order Isaac spoke them: the dew of heaven; the fatness of the earth; grain and wine; peoples serve you; lord over your brothers; blessed be everyone who blesses you. A word set out of place drifts back with a hint ("First the gifts of the land, then the nations").
- **Teaches:** what the blessing promised, and what Jacob would one day pass on.
- **Gentle mode:** the next words glow.
- **Accessibility:** a pointer, or Space to set the next words.

**Thread:** *Jacob blessed in his elder brother's clothes → clothed in Christ.* The glowing object is Esau's best robe, worn by Jacob.
- **[Q] Galatians 3:27:** "For as many of you as were baptized into Christ have put on Christ."
- **What it shows:** Jacob received the blessing meant for the firstborn because he wore his elder brother's clothes. That was a lie. But it points to something true: we receive the Father's blessing not by our own goodness, but because in Baptism we are clothed in Christ, the true firstborn, and the Father sees his Son in us. St Ambrose saw this long ago.
- **Source:** Galatians 3:26–29; Romans 8:28–29; St Ambrose, *On Jacob and the Happy Life* 2.2.9; Catechism 1227, 1243.

**Where and when:**
- **Blessings.** Ancient documents from Mesopotamia show that a father's deathbed words could decide who inherited. They were legally binding, which is why Isaac could not take his back.
- **Beer-sheba.** Isaac's family lived around Beer-sheba, in the south. Haran, where Jacob fled, is about 800 kilometers north.
- **God works even through sin.** God never approves of lies. But St Paul says that "in everything God works for good with those who love him" (Romans 8:28), even through human failings.

**Read:** Genesis 27.

## Episode 4 · Jacob's Ladder

**Genesis 28 · about 7 minutes · 4 scenes**

### Scene 4.1 · Alone on the road
*On screen:* Sunset in the stony hill country. Jacob walks alone with a staff and a small bundle, looking back over his shoulder. He stops, picks up a stone, sets it down for a pillow, and lies down under the first stars.

1. **[Q] Genesis 28:10** "Jacob left Beer-sheba, and went toward Haran."
2. He had his father's blessing, but no home, no brother, and no idea what lay ahead.
3. **[Q] Genesis 28:11** "And he came to a certain place, and stayed there that night, because the sun had set. Taking one of the stones of the place, he put it under his head and lay down in that place to sleep."

### Scene 4.2 · The dream
*On screen:* Jacob asleep. Above him the night opens: a stairway of light rises from the ground beside him into the sky, and shining figures go up and down it. At the very top, a great light. *(Play: the ladder.)*

1. **[Q] Genesis 28:12** "And he dreamed that there was a ladder set up on the earth, and the top of it reached to heaven; and behold, the angels of God were ascending and descending on it!"
2. *(Play: the ladder.)*
3. **[Q] Genesis 28:13** "And behold, the LORD stood above it and said, "I am the LORD, the God of Abraham your father and the God of Isaac; the land on which you lie I will give to you and to your descendants;"
4. **[Q] Genesis 28:15** "Behold, I am with you and will keep you wherever you go, and will bring you back to this land; for I will not leave you until I have done that of which I have spoken to you.""
5. Jacob had done nothing to deserve this. He was running away from the trouble he had caused. God came to him anyway.

### Scene 4.3 · The gate of heaven
*On screen:* Dawn. Jacob wakes with a start and sits up, staring at the empty hillside where the ladder stood.

1. **[Q] Genesis 28:16** "Then Jacob awoke from his sleep and said, "Surely the LORD is in this place; and I did not know it.""
2. **[Q] Genesis 28:17** "And he was afraid, and said, "How awesome is this place! This is none other than the house of God, and this is the gate of heaven.""

### Scene 4.4 · Bethel
*On screen:* Morning light. Jacob stands his stone pillow upright as a pillar and pours oil from a small flask over its top. He kneels before it.

1. **[Q] Genesis 28:18** "So Jacob rose early in the morning, and he took the stone which he had put under his head and set it up for a pillar and poured oil on the top of it."
2. **[Q] Genesis 28:19** "He called the name of that place Bethel; but the name of the city was Luz at the first."
3. Bethel means "house of God."
4. Jacob made a vow: if God would bring him home in peace, the LORD would be his God, and he would give him a tenth of all he had.

### Play: the ladder
- **What it is:** light and reveal (spec §13.7, kind 11).
- **How it plays:** the dark sky above sleeping Jacob. The player taps (or presses Space) to light the steps of the ladder one by one, from the ground upward. With each new step, an angel steps onto it: some climb toward heaven, others come down toward Jacob. As the ladder grows, the stars around it brighten; when the last step reaches the light at the top, all the angels pause, and the LORD speaks (the scene goes on).
- **Teaches:** heaven and earth joined; God coming down to a man who did not expect him.
- **Gentle mode:** the steps light up by themselves, one after another, once the first is tapped.
- **Accessibility:** a pointer, or Space to light the next step.

**Thread:** *The ladder between heaven and earth → Jesus, "the angels of God ascending and descending upon the Son of man."* The glowing object is the ladder's lowest step, beside Jacob's head.
- **[Q] John 1:51:** "And he said to him, "Truly, truly, I say to you, you will see heaven opened, and the angels of God ascending and descending upon the Son of man.""
- **What it shows:** When Jesus met Nathanael, he called him "an Israelite indeed, in whom is no guile," a man unlike Jacob the trickster. Then he told him that Jacob's dream would come true in Jesus himself. Jesus is the ladder: the one place where heaven and earth meet, God made man, through whom we can climb to the Father. Every church is a "Bethel," a house of God and a gate of heaven, because Christ is there.
- **Source:** John 1:47–51; Catechism 2795; the Rite of Dedication of a Church, which uses Genesis 28:17.

**Where and when:**
- **Bethel.** About 18 kilometers north of Jerusalem, where Abraham had built an altar years before. Jacob's journey north to Haran was long: about 800 kilometers on foot.
- **The "ladder."** The Hebrew word, *sullam*, appears only here in the Bible. It may mean a stairway or a ramp, like the great stepped towers of Mesopotamia, but this one was built by God, not by men as at Babel.
- **Anointing with oil.** Pouring oil set something apart for God. Later Israel's priests and kings were anointed, and the name "Christ" means "the Anointed One."

**Read:** Genesis 28:10–22.

## Episode 5 · Rachel and Leah

**Genesis 29–30 · about 8 minutes · 4 scenes**

### Scene 5.1 · The stone on the well
*On screen:* Midday, open pasture near Haran. Three flocks of sheep lie around a well with a huge round stone over its mouth. The shepherds wait. A young woman leads her father's sheep up. Jacob strides to the well, sets his shoulder to the stone, and rolls it away.

1. **[Q] Genesis 29:2** "As he looked, he saw a well in the field, and lo, three flocks of sheep lying beside it; for out of that well the flocks were watered. The stone on the well's mouth was large,"
2. **[Q] Genesis 29:9** "While he was still speaking with them, Rachel came with her father's sheep; for she kept them."
3. **[Q] Genesis 29:10** "Now when Jacob saw Rachel the daughter of Laban his mother's brother, and the sheep of Laban his mother's brother, Jacob went up and rolled the stone from the well's mouth, and watered the flock of Laban his mother's brother."
4. **[Q] Genesis 29:11** "Then Jacob kissed Rachel, and wept aloud."
5. In those days, a kiss was how relatives greeted each other. Jacob had found his family, and after his long, lonely road, he wept for joy.

### Scene 5.2 · Seven years
*On screen:* Laban's house. Laban, a shrewd man, with his two daughters: Leah, the elder, with gentle eyes, and Rachel, the younger. Then the seasons pass over Jacob as he tends the flocks: spring lambs, summer heat, autumn, winter rain, and again.

1. **[Q] Genesis 29:17** "Leah's eyes were weak, but Rachel was beautiful and lovely."
2. **[Q] Genesis 29:18** "Jacob loved Rachel; and he said, "I will serve you seven years for your younger daughter Rachel.""
3. **[Q] Genesis 29:20** "So Jacob served seven years for Rachel, and they seemed to him but a few days because of the love he had for her."

### Scene 5.3 · The trickster tricked
*On screen:* A wedding feast at night, with lamps and music. The bride is veiled. In the morning light, Jacob lifts the veil and steps back: it is Leah. He storms to Laban, who shrugs.

1. On the wedding night, the bride wore a veil. Laban gave Jacob his elder daughter instead.
2. **[Q] Genesis 29:25** "And in the morning, behold, it was Leah; and Jacob said to Laban, "What is this you have done to me? Did I not serve with you for Rachel? Why then have you deceived me?""
3. **[Q] Genesis 29:26** "Laban said, "It is not so done in our country, to give the younger before the first-born."
4. Jacob had tricked his blind father by pretending to be the firstborn. Now he was tricked, in the dark, by the firstborn. He learned what it felt like.
5. A week later, Laban gave him Rachel too, for seven more years of work. In those days a man could have more than one wife, but the Bible shows how much sadness it brought to this family.

### Scene 5.4 · The LORD saw Leah
*On screen:* Years in Jacob's tents. Leah holds one baby after another while Rachel watches, sad. At last Rachel, too, holds a son. *(Play: the sons' names.)*

1. **[Q] Genesis 29:30** "So Jacob went in to Rachel also, and he loved Rachel more than Leah, and served Laban for another seven years."
2. **[Q] Genesis 29:31** "When the LORD saw that Leah was hated, he opened her womb; but Rachel was barren."
3. "Hated" here means loved less. Jacob did not love Leah as he loved Rachel. But God saw her, and he did not forget her.
4. *(Play: the sons' names.)*
5. **[Q] Genesis 29:35** "And she conceived again and bore a son, and said, "This time I will praise the LORD"; therefore she called his name Judah; then she ceased bearing."
6. **[Q] Genesis 30:22** "Then God remembered Rachel, and God hearkened to her and opened her womb."
7. **[Q] Genesis 30:24** "and she called his name Joseph, saying, "May the LORD add to me another son!""

### Play: the sons' names
- **What it is:** matching and naming (spec §13.7, kind 10).
- **How it plays:** each mother's words float up beside a sleeping baby: "The LORD has looked upon my affliction"; "The LORD has heard"; "My husband will be joined to me"; "This time I will praise the LORD"; "May the LORD add to me another son." The player gives each baby the name that sounds like those words in Hebrew: Reuben ("see, a son"), Simeon ("heard"), Levi ("joined"), Judah ("praise"), Joseph ("may he add"). A name that doesn't fit drifts back, and the meaning glows as a hint.
- **Teaches:** the first sons of Israel, and how their mothers' prayers shaped their names; Leah moving from longing for her husband's love to praising God.
- **Gentle mode:** the right name glows beside each baby.
- **Accessibility:** a pointer, or arrow keys to choose a name and Space to give it.

**Thread:** *Leah, the unloved, becomes the mother of Judah → the Lion of the tribe of Judah.* The glowing object is Leah's fourth son, baby Judah, in her arms.
- **[Q] Revelation 5:5:** "Then one of the elders said to me, "Weep not; lo, the Lion of the tribe of Judah, the Root of David, has conquered, so that he can open the scroll and its seven seals.""
- **What it shows:** Leah was the wife no one chose. Yet God looked on her, and from her son Judah came King David, and from David's family, Jesus. Matthew's Gospel begins with this family: "Abraham was the father of Isaac, and Isaac the father of Jacob, and Jacob the father of Judah and his brothers." As Mary sang, God "has put down the mighty from their thrones, and exalted those of low degree."
- **Source:** Matthew 1:2; Luke 1:52; Genesis 49:10; Catechism 2575.

**Where and when:**
- **Haran.** Abraham's family had stayed here when he went on to Canaan (Level 3). Rebekah grew up nearby, and now her son came back to her brother's house.
- **Shepherds and wells.** A heavy stone kept a well clean and stopped anyone from taking more than their share; the shepherds waited until all the flocks were there to roll it off together. Jacob rolled it away alone.
- **Twelve sons.** Jacob would have twelve sons, with Leah, Rachel and their two maids, Bilhah and Zilpah. They became the twelve tribes of Israel. Benjamin, the last, was born later, back in Canaan.

**Read:** Genesis 29:1–30:24.

## Episode 6 · Wrestling with God

**Genesis 31–32 · about 8 minutes · 4 scenes**

### Scene 6.1 · Go home
*On screen:* Twenty years later. Jacob, older and bearded, stands among great flocks, his wives and children around him. A voice in the evening. Then a long caravan moving south: camels, donkeys, sheep and goats, and children waving.

1. Jacob served Laban twenty years. Laban cheated him again and again, but God blessed Jacob, and his flocks grew great.
2. **[Q] Genesis 31:3** "Then the LORD said to Jacob, "Return to the land of your fathers and to your kindred, and I will be with you.""
3. But going home meant facing Esau.
4. **[Q] Genesis 32:6** "And the messengers returned to Jacob, saying, "We came to your brother Esau, and he is coming to meet you, and four hundred men with him.""

### Scene 6.2 · Jacob prays
*On screen:* Jacob's camp at dusk, divided into two companies. Jacob alone on a rise, on his knees, his face in his hands. Herdsmen drive his gifts of goats, sheep, camels and cattle ahead toward Esau.

1. **[Q] Genesis 32:7** "Then Jacob was greatly afraid and distressed; and he divided the people that were with him, and the flocks and herds and camels, into two companies,"
2. **[Q] Genesis 32:10** "I am not worthy of the least of all the steadfast love and all the faithfulness which thou hast shown to thy servant, for with only my staff I crossed this Jordan; and now I have become two companies."
3. **[Q] Genesis 32:11** "Deliver me, I pray thee, from the hand of my brother, from the hand of Esau, for I fear him, lest he come and slay us all, the mothers with the children."
4. It was the first humble prayer we hear from Jacob. Then he sent herds of animals ahead as a present for his brother, hoping to calm his anger.

### Scene 6.3 · Until the breaking of the day
*On screen:* Night by the Jabbok, a rushing stream in a rocky gorge. Jacob sends his family across the ford, then stands alone on the bank. A figure steps out of the darkness, and the two grapple, locked together, under the stars. *(Play: hold on.)*

1. **[Q] Genesis 32:22** "The same night he arose and took his two wives, his two maids, and his eleven children, and crossed the ford of the Jabbok."
2. **[Q] Genesis 32:24** "And Jacob was left alone; and a man wrestled with him until the breaking of the day."
3. *(Play: hold on.)*
4. **[Q] Genesis 32:25** "When the man saw that he did not prevail against Jacob, he touched the hollow of his thigh; and Jacob's thigh was put out of joint as he wrestled with him."
5. **[Q] Genesis 32:26** "Then he said, "Let me go, for the day is breaking." But Jacob said, "I will not let you go, unless you bless me.""

### Scene 6.4 · Israel
*On screen:* The first light of dawn. The stranger, still holding Jacob, asks him something; Jacob answers, head bowed. Then the stranger is gone. The sun rises over the hills as Jacob limps across the stream toward his family.

1. **[Q] Genesis 32:27** "And he said to him, "What is your name?" And he said, "Jacob.""
2. To say his name was to admit who he had been: Jacob, the heel-grabber, the one who tricked his brother.
3. **[Q] Genesis 32:28** "Then he said, "Your name shall no more be called Jacob, but Israel, for you have striven with God and with men, and have prevailed.""
4. **[Q] Genesis 32:30** "So Jacob called the name of the place Peniel, saying, "For I have seen God face to face, and yet my life is preserved.""
5. **[Q] Genesis 32:31** "The sun rose upon him as he passed Penuel, limping because of his thigh."

### Play: hold on
- **What it is:** timing (spec §13.7, kind 16), in the dark, like a quiet interaction.
- **How it plays:** Jacob and the stranger, locked together, seen as two shadows against the stars. The player holds Space (or holds the pointer down) to hold on. Now and then the stranger pulls away, and a ring of light around Jacob's hands tightens: the player presses again just as it closes to keep his grip. Every moment held, the night moves on: the stars turn, the sky in the east grows pale, gray, then gold. Letting go never ends the game; Jacob simply catches hold again. When the dawn breaks, the stranger says, "Let me go" (the scene goes on).
- **Teaches:** persevering prayer: holding on to God through the dark until he blesses.
- **Gentle mode:** a hold is enough; the grip never slips.
- **Accessibility:** hold Space, or the pointer; a sound marks each pull.

**Thread:** *Jacob would not let go until God blessed him → "they ought always to pray and not lose heart."* The glowing object is the first light of dawn over the hills.
- **[Q] Luke 18:1:** "And he told them a parable, to the effect that they ought always to pray and not lose heart."
- **What it shows:** Jacob held on through the long night and would not let go until he was blessed. Jesus told the parable of the widow who kept asking the judge until he gave in, to teach us to pray like that: to keep praying when the answer is slow and the night is long. The Catechism says the Church has always seen Jacob's wrestling as a picture of prayer: a battle of faith, and the victory of perseverance.
- **Source:** Luke 18:1–8; Hosea 12:4; Catechism 2573.

**Where and when:**
- **The Jabbok.** A stream that flows west into the Jordan through a deep valley, east of the river, in today's Jordan. It is now called the Zarqa.
- **Who was the man?** The prophet Hosea calls him an angel: "He strove with the angel and prevailed, he wept and sought his favor" (Hosea 12:4). Jacob himself said he had seen God face to face.
- **Israel.** The name may mean "he strives with God" or "God strives." From now on, God's people are called by it: the children of Israel.

**Read:** Genesis 32.

### Scene 7.4 · On the way to Bethlehem
*On screen:* The road south, near a village on a ridge. Rachel's tent; the women gather, the midwife holds a newborn. Then Jacob, grieving, sets up a pillar of stones by the road.

2. **[Q] Genesis 35:18** "And as her soul was departing (for she died), she called his name Ben-oni; but his father called his name Benjamin."
3. Ben-oni means "son of my sorrow." Jacob called him Benjamin, "son of the right hand": a name of strength and hope.
4. **[Q] Genesis 35:19** "So Rachel died, and she was buried on the way to Ephrath (that is, Bethlehem),"

### Scene 7.5 · Gathered to his people
*On screen:* Mamre at Hebron, the old oaks. Isaac, very old, lies at rest. Esau and Jacob, side by side, carry their father to the cave of Machpelah. Then the twelve sons of Jacob stand together in a row.

1. **[Q] Genesis 35:22 (in part)** "Now the sons of Jacob were twelve."
2. **[Q] Genesis 35:29** "And Isaac breathed his last; and he died and was gathered to his people, old and full of days; and his sons Esau and Jacob buried him."
3. The brothers who had been enemies buried their father together, in peace.
4. God's promise to Abraham now rested on Jacob, Israel, and his twelve sons. The next level tells of the favorite of them all: Joseph.

### Play: seven bows
- **What it is:** a quiet interaction (spec §13.7, kind 20).
- **How it plays:** a long, open field, with Jacob limping at one end and Esau's company far off at the other. The player presses Space (or taps) to have Jacob step forward and bow to the ground; each bow takes a moment, and he cannot hurry it. Seven times he bows, a little closer each time, while Esau's dust cloud grows nearer. After the seventh bow, Jacob stays on his knees, and the player waits: then Esau breaks away from his men and runs (the next scene begins).
- **Teaches:** humility and the courage to face the one you wronged; the first step of making peace.
- **Gentle mode:** Jacob bows on his own after the first press.
- **Accessibility:** a pointer, or Space for each bow.

**Thread:** *Esau ran to meet his brother and kissed him → the father of the prodigal son ran to meet him.* The glowing object is the lamb Jacob carries as a gift for his brother.
- **[Q] Luke 15:20:** "And he arose and came to his father. But while he was yet at a distance, his father saw him and had compassion, and ran and embraced him and kissed him."
- **What it shows:** Jacob came with gifts, rehearsed words and seven bows, afraid of what he deserved. But Esau ran to him first. Jesus told the parable of the prodigal son with the same words: the father "ran and embraced him and kissed him," before his son could finish his apology. This is how God receives us when we come back to him, above all in the sacrament of Reconciliation. And it is how we are to forgive one another.
- **Source:** Luke 15:11–32; Matthew 5:23–24; Catechism 1439, 2843.

**Where and when:**
- **Edom.** Esau lived in the land of Seir, the mountains south of the Dead Sea, later called Edom.
- **Shechem and Jacob's well.** Jacob bought a field at Shechem (Genesis 33:19). Centuries later, Jesus sat by "Jacob's well" there and spoke with the Samaritan woman (John 4:5–6).
- **Rachel's tomb.** Matthew's Gospel remembers "Rachel weeping for her children" near Bethlehem when Jesus was born (Matthew 2:18). A tomb honored as Rachel's still stands at the entrance to Bethlehem.

**Read:** Genesis 33:1–17; 35:1–29.
