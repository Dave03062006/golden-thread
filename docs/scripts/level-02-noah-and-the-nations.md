# Level 2 script: *Noah and the Nations*

**Bible:** Genesis 6–11
**Age:** I · The Beginning (Level 2 completes it)
**Length:** about 50–55 minutes (7 episodes, then the quiz, then reflection and prayer)
**Status:** draft for Dave's approval (spec §15). Nothing in this level is built from unapproved text.

## How to read this script

- Each **scene** is a checkpoint. Its numbered lines are the narration beats; the player taps to move from one to the next.
- *On screen* describes what the scene shows. It's the brief for the art in both styles.
- **[Q]** marks an exact quotation from the Bible. Its wording in both translations is in the verse table at the end. Everything else is our own retelling.
- **Play** is the episode's interactive moment. **Thread** is its golden thread. **Read** is the passage the game sends the player to.
- "LORD" in capitals stands for God's holy name, printed in small capitals in the RSV.
- **For Dave** marks a choice for you to make before the level is built.

### What this level is about

Level 1 ended with a father's hope: "this one shall bring us relief." Level 2 is the story of that child. The world God made good fills with violence, and God, grieving, washes it clean, but he saves a remnant in the ark: Noah, who walked with God, his family, and every kind of creature. When the waters fall, God makes his first covenant, with every living thing, and hangs his bow in the clouds. Then humanity tries to reach heaven by its own bricks at Babel and is scattered into the nations, until, at the end of the level, one family from the line of Shem sets out from Ur. Through Abram, God will begin to gather the nations back.

The threads point forward to Mary, the Church, Baptism, the Holy Spirit, the new covenant, Pentecost and the blessing of all nations in Christ.

## Episode 1 · A World Gone Wrong, a Man Who Walked with God

**Genesis 6:1–13 · about 6 minutes · 4 scenes**

### Scene 1.1 · The earth filled with violence
*On screen:* A great city at dusk, crowded and loud. Walls and towers, smoke rising, people shoving past each other in silhouette. Nothing violent is shown up close; the mood says it: hard faces, closed doors, a fire left burning.

1. The children of Adam spread across the earth, and their cities grew.
2. But they forgot the God who made them, and they turned on one another.
3. **[Q] Genesis 6:5** "The LORD saw that the wickedness of man was great in the earth, and that every imagination of the thoughts of his heart was only evil continually."
4. **[Q] Genesis 6:6** "And the LORD was sorry that he had made man on the earth, and it grieved him to his heart."
5. This is the first time the Bible tells us that God grieved. Sin is not only breaking a rule. It wounds the heart of the One who loves us.

### Scene 1.2 · But Noah
*On screen:* The camera leaves the crowded streets for the edge of the city, where a man stands alone at sunset, his face lifted to the sky. The noise fades behind him.

1. And yet, in that whole dark world, one man was different.
2. **[Q] Genesis 6:8** "But Noah found favor in the eyes of the LORD."
3. **[Q] Genesis 6:9** "These are the generations of Noah. Noah was a righteous man, blameless in his generation; Noah walked with God."
4. "Walked with God": the same words Genesis used for Enoch. Noah was the great-grandson of Enoch, and he walked the same path.

### Scene 1.3 · Noah's family
*On screen:* Noah's home outside the city: a courtyard with his wife and their three grown sons and their wives, sharing a meal by lamplight.

1. **[Q] Genesis 6:10** "And Noah had three sons, Shem, Ham, and Japheth."
2. Noah was not perfect, but he listened to God, and he taught his family to listen too.
3. **[Q] Genesis 6:11** "Now the earth was corrupt in God's sight, and the earth was filled with violence."
4. *(Play: walk with God.)*

### Scene 1.4 · God speaks to Noah
*On screen:* Night. Noah alone on a hill above the sleeping land. A soft light gathers around him as God speaks.

1. Then God spoke to Noah.
2. **[Q] Genesis 6:13 (in part)** "I have determined to make an end of all flesh; for the earth is filled with violence through them"
3. God would not leave his creation ruined forever. He would wash the earth clean and begin again, and Noah would be part of that new beginning.
4. **[Q] Genesis 6:14 (in part)** "Make yourself an ark of gopher wood"

### Play: walk with God
- **What it is:** a quiet interaction (spec §13.7, kind 20). Noah walks home through the crowded city toward a soft light at its edge, where his family waits.
- **How it plays:** the path is lit by a gentle glow. Voices and crowds drift across it: a merchant cheating, a quarrel, a crowd jeering. The player keeps Noah walking in the light (hold right, or Space). If Noah stops or strays, nothing is lost: the light waits and gathers around him again, and he walks on.
- **Teaches:** to "walk with God" is to keep going God's way while the world goes another.
- **Gentle mode:** Noah keeps to the light on his own; the player only says when to walk.
- **Accessibility:** one button (Space to walk), and the keyboard arrows.

**Thread:** *Noah found favor with God → Mary found favor with God.* The glowing object is the lamp in the window of Noah's house, which shines in every scene until it's found.
- **[Q] Luke 1:30 (in part):** "Do not be afraid, Mary, for you have found favor with God."
- **What it shows:** in a dark world, God's favor (his grace) rested on Noah, and through him the human family was saved. In the fullness of time the angel spoke the same word to Mary, "full of grace," and through her came the Savior of the whole world.
- **Source:** Luke 1:28–30; Catechism 490–493.

**Where and when:**
- **"The sons of God."** Genesis 6:1–4 tells of "the sons of God" taking wives from "the daughters of men," and of mighty men called the Nephilim. These verses have been read in different ways. Some ancient writers saw angels in them; many Church Fathers, like St Augustine, saw the family line of Seth mixing with the line of Cain and losing its faith.
- **Other flood stories.** The peoples of Mesopotamia told their own flood stories, like the tale of Utnapishtim in the *Epic of Gilgamesh*. In them, the gods send the flood because humans are too noisy, and they quarrel among themselves. In Genesis, one God acts because of violence and sin, grieves over it, and saves the righteous.
- **A long life.** Noah was five hundred years old when his sons were born. Like the ages in Level 1, these great numbers have been understood in different ways.

**Read:** Genesis 6:1–13.

## Episode 2 · Building the Ark

**Genesis 6:14–7:5 · about 7 minutes · 4 scenes**

### Scene 2.1 · The plan
*On screen:* A wide clearing among tall trees, far from any sea. Noah draws the plan in the dust with a stick while his sons watch.

1. God told Noah exactly how to build the ark.
2. **[Q] Genesis 6:15** "This is how you are to make it: the length of the ark three hundred cubits, its breadth fifty cubits, and its height thirty cubits."
3. A cubit was about the length of a man's forearm, close to half a meter. The ark would be longer than a football field and as tall as a four-story house.
4. It would have rooms, three decks, a roof, and a door in its side. Inside and out, it would be sealed with pitch to keep out the water.
5. *(Play: building the ark.)*

### Scene 2.2 · A covenant promised
*On screen:* The great frame of the ark rises against the sky, ribs of new wood. Noah stops work to listen.

1. Then God told Noah why.
2. **[Q] Genesis 6:17 (in part)** "For behold, I will bring a flood of waters upon the earth"
3. **[Q] Genesis 6:18** "But I will establish my covenant with you; and you shall come into the ark, you, your sons, your wife, and your sons' wives with you."
4. A covenant is more than a promise. It is a bond of family: God binding himself to his people, and his people to him. This is the first time the word appears in the Bible, and it will run through all the rest of it.

### Scene 2.3 · Noah did all that God commanded
*On screen:* Seasons pass over the clearing. The ark grows plank by plank. Passersby stop, point and laugh at the boat in a dry land; Noah keeps working.

1. There was no sea nearby and no rain in the sky. People must have laughed at the old man and his boat.
2. But Noah kept building.
3. **[Q] Genesis 6:22** "Noah did this; he did all that God commanded him."
4. The Letter to the Hebrews says: **[Q] Hebrews 11:7 (in part)** "By faith Noah, being warned by God concerning events as yet unseen, took heed and constructed an ark for the saving of his household"
5. Faith is trusting God about what we cannot see yet, and acting on it.

### Scene 2.4 · Two of every sort
*On screen:* The finished ark, dark with pitch, its great door open. Food is carried up the ramp in baskets and jars.

1. God told Noah to store food of every kind, for his family and for the animals.
2. **[Q] Genesis 6:19** "And of every living thing of all flesh, you shall bring two of every sort into the ark, to keep them alive with you; they shall be male and female."
3. Of the clean animals, the ones that could be offered to God, Noah was to take seven pairs.
4. **[Q] Genesis 7:5** "And Noah did all that the LORD had commanded him."

### Play: building the ark
- **What it is:** the building kind of play (spec §13.7, kind 3). The ark is built in the order of God's plan, piece by piece.
- **How it plays:** the parts arrive one at a time: the keel, the ribs, the three decks, the rooms, the roof, and the door in its side. The player places each one where its outline glows on the plan. Then the player sweeps pitch over the hull, inside and out, until it shines.
- **Each part placed** shows a few words of the plan: "three hundred cubits long," "lower, second and third decks," "the door in its side."
- **Mistakes:** a part put in the wrong place slides back gently with a hint, such as "The decks go inside the ribs."
- **Teaches:** that Noah followed God's plan exactly, in faith.
- **Gentle mode:** each part's place glows strongly, and a part let go near it snaps into place.

**Thread:** *the ark that saved Noah → the Church, the ark of salvation.* The glowing object is the door in the ark's side.
- **[Q] 1 Peter 3:20 (in part):** "during the building of the ark, in which a few, that is, eight persons, were saved through water."
- **What it shows:** the Church Fathers saw the ark as a picture of the Church: the one boat God provides to carry his people safely through the waters to a new world. Some even noticed that the door was in the ark's side, as the Church was born from the pierced side of Christ on the cross (John 19:34).
- **Source:** 1 Peter 3:20; Catechism 845; St Augustine, *City of God* 15.26.

**Where and when:**
- **Gopher wood.** No one knows for sure what tree "gopher wood" was. It may have been cypress, which shipbuilders long prized because it resists rot.
- **The shape of a barge.** The ark's measurements, 6 times longer than it was wide, are the proportions of a stable barge. It had no sails and no rudder. It didn't need to go anywhere; it only needed to float. God would steer it.
- **The word "ark."** The Hebrew word for the ark, *tevah*, appears only one other time in the Bible: for the basket of reeds, sealed with pitch, that carried the baby Moses on the Nile.

**Read:** Genesis 6:14–7:5.

## Episode 3 · The Flood

**Genesis 7:6–24 · about 8 minutes · 4 scenes**

> **Content note shown before this episode:** "This episode tells of the Flood, in which many died. It is told gently, and no one is shown suffering."

### Scene 3.1 · Into the ark
*On screen:* Animals come from every direction, out of the forests and across the plains, in pairs: lions, deer, sheep, elephants, birds in pairs above them. They climb the ramp into the ark. Then Noah's family. Dark clouds gather.

1. Then the animals came, two and two, as if called.
2. **[Q] Genesis 7:9** "two and two, male and female, went into the ark with Noah, as God had commanded Noah."
3. Noah, his wife, his three sons and their wives went in after them: eight people in all.
4. **[Q] Genesis 7:16 (in part)** "and the LORD shut him in."
5. God himself closed the door. Inside the ark, Noah's family was safe in God's hands.

### Scene 3.2 · The fountains of the deep
*On screen:* The sky goes dark. Water bursts up from the ground and pours down from the sky. The ark lifts gently off the earth.

1. **[Q] Genesis 7:11** "In the six hundredth year of Noah's life, in the second month, on the seventeenth day of the month, on that day all the fountains of the great deep burst forth, and the windows of the heavens were opened."
2. **[Q] Genesis 7:12** "And rain fell upon the earth forty days and forty nights."
3. On the second day of creation, God had separated the waters above from the waters below. Now the waters came together again, as if creation were going back to the beginning, to be made new.

### Scene 3.3 · The ark on the waters
*On screen:* A storm. The ark rides the great swells, rising and falling, lamplight glowing through its single window. *(Play: through the storm.)* Then the rain stops, and the ark floats on a wide, still sea under a gray sky.

1. **[Q] Genesis 7:17** "The flood continued forty days upon the earth; and the waters increased, and bore up the ark, and it rose high above the earth."
2. *(Play: through the storm.)*
3. **[Q] Genesis 7:18** "The waters prevailed and increased greatly upon the earth; and the ark floated on the face of the waters."

### Scene 3.4 · Only Noah was left
*On screen:* Nothing but water to the horizon in every direction, and the ark, small and alone. No bodies, no wreckage; only a vast, quiet sea.

1. The waters covered even the highest mountains. Everything that lived on the land and breathed the air was gone.
2. **[Q] Genesis 7:23 (in part)** "Only Noah was left, and those that were with him in the ark."
3. **[Q] Genesis 7:24** "And the waters prevailed upon the earth a hundred and fifty days."
4. It is a hard story. God's judgment on sin is real. But it is never God's last word. Even here, God was keeping a remnant safe, to begin again.

### Play: through the storm
- **What it is:** the steering kind of play (spec §13.7, kind 7). The ark rides out the storm.
- **How it plays:** great waves roll toward the ark. The player leans the ark into each wave as it comes (left and right, or tilt), so it rides over the crest and the lamps inside stay steady. A wave met badly only rocks the ark harder; it never sinks, and the storm goes on. After about a minute the rain eases and the sea grows calm.
- **Teaches:** the ark carried them through, safe in God's care; "the LORD shut him in."
- **Gentle mode:** the waves are slower and smaller, and the ark leans on its own if the player waits.
- **Accessibility:** one button: press Space as each wave rises.

**Thread:** *the waters of the Flood → the waters of Baptism.* The glowing object is the ark's window, lit from within.
- **[Q] 1 Peter 3:21 (in part):** "Baptism, which corresponds to this, now saves you"
- **What it shows:** the waters of the Flood ended a world of sin and carried the righteous to a new beginning. In Baptism, the old life of sin is drowned, and we rise to new life in Christ. At the Easter Vigil the Church remembers the Flood as she blesses the baptismal water.
- **Source:** 1 Peter 3:20–21; Catechism 701, 1219.

**Where and when:**
- **Forty days.** Forty is the Bible's number for testing and new beginnings: Moses forty days on Mount Sinai, Israel forty years in the desert, Elijah forty days on the way to Horeb, Jesus forty days fasting in the desert, and the forty days of Lent.
- **The whole earth.** Genesis tells the Flood as covering the whole world it knew. Catholics are free to understand its size in different ways; what Genesis teaches is God's judgment on sin and his faithfulness in saving.
- **Eight people.** Noah, his wife, Shem, Ham, Japheth and their wives. The Church Fathers noticed the number eight: the day after the seventh, the day of the Resurrection, the first day of the new creation.

**Read:** Genesis 7:6–24.

## Episode 4 · The Dove Returns

**Genesis 8:1–19 · about 7 minutes · 5 scenes**

### Scene 4.1 · God remembered Noah
*On screen:* A wind passes over the gray water, and the clouds begin to break. A first patch of blue.

1. Inside the ark, the days were long and the waiting longer.
2. **[Q] Genesis 8:1** "But God remembered Noah and all the beasts and all the cattle that were with him in the ark. And God made a wind blow over the earth, and the waters subsided;"
3. "God remembered" does not mean God had forgotten. In the Bible, when God remembers, he acts. A wind blew over the waters, as the Spirit had moved over the waters at the very beginning.

### Scene 4.2 · The mountains of Ararat
*On screen:* The ark grinds gently onto a high mountainside. Over weeks, other peaks rise from the water around it.

1. **[Q] Genesis 8:4** "and in the seventh month, on the seventeenth day of the month, the ark came to rest upon the mountains of Ararat."
2. Months passed, and slowly the tops of the mountains appeared.

### Scene 4.3 · The raven and the dove
*On screen:* Noah opens the window. A raven flies out and circles over the water, never coming back. Then Noah lets a white dove go from his hands.

1. Noah opened the window of the ark and sent out a raven. It flew to and fro until the waters dried up.
2. **[Q] Genesis 8:8** "Then he sent forth a dove from him, to see if the waters had subsided from the face of the ground;"
3. *(Play: the dove, first flight.)*
4. **[Q] Genesis 8:9 (in part)** "but the dove found no place to set her foot, and she returned to him to the ark"

### Scene 4.4 · The olive leaf
*On screen:* Evening. The dove comes back to Noah's window, a fresh green olive leaf in her beak. Noah's family gathers around, laughing and weeping.

1. Noah waited seven days and sent the dove out again.
2. *(Play: the dove, second flight.)*
3. **[Q] Genesis 8:11** "and the dove came back to him in the evening, and lo, in her mouth a freshly plucked olive leaf; so Noah knew that the waters had subsided from the earth."
4. After seven more days he sent her out a third time, and she did not come back. She had found a home in the new world.
5. *(Play: the dove, third flight.)*

### Scene 4.5 · Out of the ark
*On screen:* The great door opens onto a washed, green world. The animals come out, pair by pair, and run, fly and scatter across the hills. Noah's family steps onto the dry ground.

1. **[Q] Genesis 8:16** "Go forth from the ark, you and your wife, and your sons and your sons' wives with you."
2. More than a year after they went in, Noah and his family came out.
3. **[Q] Genesis 8:19** "And every beast, every creeping thing, and every bird, everything that moves upon the earth, went forth by families out of the ark."

### Play: the dove
- **What it is:** the flight kind of play (spec §13.7, kind 1), in three short flights, each from Noah's window.
- **First flight:** the dove flies out over endless water. There is nowhere to land; she circles, and the player brings her home to Noah's open hand.
- **Second flight:** the waters are lower. Treetops show here and there. The player finds the olive tree, plucks a leaf, and carries it back.
- **Third flight:** green hills and valleys below. The dove flies free and settles in a tree. She does not return.
- **The raven** circles in the distance, going to and fro, never landing.
- **Teaches:** the three flights and their meaning; waiting on God's time.
- **Gentle mode:** a soft wind guides the dove toward where she needs to go.
- **Accessibility:** arrows or a pointer to fly; with one button, the dove flies on her own and Space beats her wings.

**Thread:** *the dove with the olive leaf → the Holy Spirit descending like a dove.* The glowing object is the olive leaf in the dove's beak.
- **[Q] Matthew 3:16:** "And when Jesus was baptized, he went up immediately from the water, and behold, the heavens were opened and he saw the Spirit of God descending like a dove, and alighting on him;"
- **What it shows:** over the waters of the Flood, a dove brought news of peace and a new creation. Over the waters of the Jordan, the Spirit came down like a dove upon Jesus, and the new creation began in him.
- **Source:** Matthew 3:16; Catechism 701.

**Where and when:**
- **Ararat.** Genesis says "the mountains of Ararat," a range, not one peak. Ararat is the land the Assyrians called Urartu, in the mountains where Turkey, Armenia and Iran meet today. Its highest peak, now called Mount Ararat, rises over 5,000 meters.
- **The raven and the dove.** A raven can live on what floats on the water. A dove needs dry ground and green trees, so her return with a leaf told Noah that the land was growing again.
- **The olive branch.** Since this story, a dove with an olive branch has been a sign of peace all over the world.

**Read:** Genesis 8:1–19.

## Episode 5 · The Rainbow Covenant

**Genesis 8:20–9:17 · about 7 minutes · 4 scenes**

### Scene 5.1 · The altar
*On screen:* On the mountainside, Noah builds an altar of stones. Smoke rises straight into a clear sky while his family kneels.

1. The first thing Noah did in the new world was to worship.
2. **[Q] Genesis 8:20** "Then Noah built an altar to the LORD, and took of every clean animal and of every clean bird, and offered burnt offerings on the altar."
3. God received his offering, and he made a promise in his heart:
4. **[Q] Genesis 8:22** "While the earth remains, seedtime and harvest, cold and heat, summer and winter, day and night, shall not cease."

### Scene 5.2 · A new beginning
*On screen:* Noah's sons and their wives plant a field and build a home. Children are born. The animals graze around them.

1. **[Q] Genesis 9:1** "And God blessed Noah and his sons, and said to them, 'Be fruitful and multiply, and fill the earth.'"
2. It is the same blessing God gave Adam and Eve at the beginning. The human story was starting again.
3. God also taught them that every human life is sacred:
4. **[Q] Genesis 9:6** "Whoever sheds the blood of man, by man shall his blood be shed; for God made man in his own image."
5. Before the Flood the earth was filled with violence. In the new world, God set a guard around every human life, because every person bears his image.

### Scene 5.3 · The bow in the cloud
*On screen:* Rain clouds roll in, and Noah's family looks up, afraid. Then the sun breaks through, and a rainbow arches across the whole sky. *(Play: colors of the promise.)*

1. Then God made a covenant with Noah, with his family, and with every living creature.
2. **[Q] Genesis 9:11** "I establish my covenant with you, that never again shall all flesh be cut off by the waters of a flood, and never again shall there be a flood to destroy the earth."
3. *(Play: colors of the promise.)*
4. **[Q] Genesis 9:13** "I set my bow in the cloud, and it shall be a sign of the covenant between me and the earth."

### Scene 5.4 · "I will remember"
*On screen:* The rainbow over the whole new world: the ark on the mountain, the fields, the animals, the family looking up together.

1. **[Q] Genesis 9:15** "I will remember my covenant which is between me and you and every living creature of all flesh; and the waters shall never again become a flood to destroy all flesh."
2. Every time we see a rainbow, God's promise is shining in the sky.
3. **For Dave:** see the note below about Noah's vineyard (Genesis 9:18–29).

### Play: colors of the promise
- **What it is:** the light-and-reveal kind of play (spec §13.7, kind 11).
- **How it plays:** after the rain, the sun breaks through. The player draws the rainbow across the sky, one band at a time, from red to violet (a sweep for each band, or Space). Each band, as it appears, lights up a few words of God's promise: "never again," "every living creature," "for all future generations," "I will remember." When all seven bands are drawn, the whole rainbow shines and the promise reads complete.
- **Teaches:** the promises of the covenant with Noah.
- **Gentle mode:** each band draws itself as the player moves anywhere across the sky.
- **Accessibility:** one button (Space draws the next band).

**Thread:** *God's covenant with Noah → the new and everlasting covenant in Christ.* The glowing object is the rainbow's highest point.
- **[Q] Luke 22:20 (in part):** "This cup which is poured out for you is the new covenant in my blood."
- **What it shows:** with Noah, God bound himself to all creation and promised never again to destroy it by a flood. At the Last Supper, Jesus made the new covenant, not with a sign in the clouds but with his own blood, to save the whole world. At every Mass we hear his words again: "the blood of the new and eternal covenant."
- **Source:** Luke 22:20; Catechism 56–58, 71; Revelation 4:3 (a rainbow around God's throne).

**Where and when:**
- **God's bow.** The Hebrew word for "bow" is the word for a warrior's bow. Many readers have seen in the rainbow God hanging up his bow of war in the sky, pointed away from the earth, as a sign of peace.
- **A covenant with everyone.** God's covenant with Noah was made with all of humanity and every living thing. The Catechism says it remains in force for all the nations until the Gospel reaches them (Catechism 58).

### For Dave: Noah's vineyard (Genesis 9:18–29)

After the covenant, Genesis tells a hard story. Noah planted a vineyard, drank too much wine, and lay uncovered in his tent. His son Ham saw him and told his brothers mockingly; Shem and Japheth walked in backward and covered their father without looking. When Noah woke, he cursed Ham's son, Canaan.

**My suggestion:** include it as a short, gentle scene (5.5, below), because it's honest about the fact that even a righteous man sins, and it teaches respect for parents. But the "curse of Canaan" was later twisted to defend slavery, a grave misuse the Church condemns, so if it's included, a *Where and when* note should say so. **You might prefer to leave it out**, as many children's Bibles do; the level works without it.

#### Scene 5.5 (optional) · Even Noah
*On screen:* Years later. A vineyard on the hillside, ripe with grapes. A tent at evening. The scene is shown from outside: the closed tent, then two brothers walking backward with a cloak, faces turned away.

1. Noah became a farmer and planted a vineyard.
2. One day he drank too much of its wine and fell asleep uncovered in his tent.
3. His son Ham saw him and told his brothers, mocking his father. But Shem and Japheth took a cloak and walked in backward, faces turned away, and covered him.
4. Even Noah, who walked with God, was not without sin. Only one man would ever be without sin, and he would come from the line of Shem.
5. **[Q] Genesis 9:29** "All the days of Noah were nine hundred and fifty years; and he died."

*Where and when (if included):* **A curse misused.** When Noah woke, he spoke a curse on Canaan, Ham's son, and blessed Shem and Japheth. Centuries later, some people twisted these words to claim that God approved of slavery for whole peoples. That was a terrible misreading: the curse was about the land of Canaan in Israel's later history, not about any race, and the Church teaches that every human being has equal dignity (Catechism 1935).

**Read:** Genesis 8:20–9:17 (and 9:18–29).

## Episode 6 · The Tower of Babel

**Genesis 10:1–11:9 · about 8 minutes · 5 scenes**

### Scene 6.1 · The families of the earth
*On screen:* A map of the ancient world spreads out like a tapestry. From the mountains of Ararat, three streams of families flow outward: one to the coastlands and islands of the west and north, one south toward Egypt and Canaan, one east to the rivers of Mesopotamia.

1. After the Flood, Noah's sons had children, and their children had children.
2. **[Q] Genesis 10:32** "These are the families of the sons of Noah, according to their genealogies, in their nations; and from these the nations spread abroad on the earth after the flood."
3. The families of Japheth spread to the coastlands; the families of Ham to Egypt, Cush and Canaan; the families of Shem to the lands of the east.
4. Genesis lists seventy nations in all: the whole human family, every one of them coming from Noah.

### Scene 6.2 · One language
*On screen:* A wide, flat plain between two great rivers. A people arrives with their herds and settles. They dig clay, shape it in molds, and fire bricks in kilns.

1. **[Q] Genesis 11:1** "Now the whole earth had one language and few words."
2. **[Q] Genesis 11:2** "And as men migrated from the east, they found a plain in the land of Shinar and settled there."
3. There was no stone on the plain, so they made bricks of clay and baked them hard in fire. With bricks, they could build anything.

### Scene 6.3 · "Let us make a name for ourselves"
*On screen:* A city rising on the plain, and in the middle of it a great stepped tower, terrace above terrace, its top lost in the clouds. Crowds of builders haul bricks up its ramps. *(Play: the tower, first part.)*

1. **[Q] Genesis 11:4** "Then they said, 'Come, let us build ourselves a city, and a tower with its top in the heavens, and let us make a name for ourselves, lest we be scattered abroad upon the face of the whole earth.'"
2. *(Play: the tower, first part.)*
3. God had told the human family to fill the earth. Instead, they wanted to stay together and climb to heaven by their own strength, and to make their own name great instead of God's.

### Scene 6.4 · The LORD came down
*On screen:* From high above, the tower looks tiny. Then the light of God comes down to it. On the tower, builders call to one another and suddenly can't understand: their words appear as different scripts floating in the air.

1. **[Q] Genesis 11:5** "And the LORD came down to see the city and the tower, which the sons of men had built."
2. There is gentle humor here. The tower that was meant to reach the heavens was so small that God had to come down to see it.
3. **[Q] Genesis 11:7** "Come, let us go down, and there confuse their language, that they may not understand one another's speech."
4. The builders could no longer understand one another, and the work stopped.

### Scene 6.5 · Scattered
*On screen:* The half-built tower stands empty, bricks abandoned. Families gather their herds and set out in every direction. *(Play: the tower, second part.)*

1. **[Q] Genesis 11:8** "So the LORD scattered them abroad from there over the face of all the earth, and they left off building the city."
2. *(Play: the tower, second part.)*
3. **[Q] Genesis 11:9 (in part)** "Therefore its name was called Babel, because there the LORD confused the language of all the earth"
4. Babel had tried to make a name for itself and reach heaven by its own bricks. In the next level, God will promise one man, Abram, "I will make your name great." What people could not seize, God would give.

### Play: the tower
- **What it is:** two parts, both from the spec's library (§13.7): building and stacking (kind 3), then courier routes (kind 15).
- **First part, the tower:** the player stacks baked bricks to raise the tower, terrace by terrace. It climbs higher and higher, and the builders cheer, "Let us make a name for ourselves!" The tower never reaches the sky; however high it climbs, the clouds stay out of reach.
- **Second part, scattered:** after the confusion, families stand at the foot of the tower, each now speaking its own language, shown by the script above them. The player guides each family to its new land on the map, following the families of Noah's sons in Genesis 10: Japheth's to the coastlands, Ham's to Egypt and Canaan, Shem's to the east.
- **Mistakes:** a family guided to the wrong land turns back, and a hint names where it belongs.
- **Teaches:** the pride of Babel, and the spread of the nations in Genesis 10.
- **Gentle mode:** each family's land glows on the map.

**Thread:** *Babel, where languages divided people → Pentecost, where the Spirit united them.* The glowing object is a brick left at the foot of the tower.
- **[Q] Acts 2:6:** "And at this sound the multitude came together, and they were bewildered, because each one heard them speaking in his own language."
- **What it shows:** at Babel, pride divided humanity into many languages that could not understand each other. At Pentecost, the Holy Spirit came down, and people "from every nation under heaven" each heard the apostles in their own language. The Church gathers what Babel scattered.
- **Source:** Acts 2:1–11; Catechism 57, 731–732.

**Where and when:**
- **Babel and Babylon.** "Babel" is the Hebrew name for Babylon, the great city of the land of Shinar, in what is now southern Iraq. The Babylonians said its name meant "gate of god." Genesis plays on a Hebrew word that sounds like it, *balal*, "to confuse."
- **Ziggurats.** The cities of Mesopotamia built great stepped towers of baked brick called ziggurats, with a temple on top, where they believed heaven and earth met. Babylon's ziggurat, called Etemenanki, "the house of the foundation of heaven and earth," rose about 90 meters high.
- **Seventy nations.** Genesis 10 names seventy peoples, a number that stands for all the nations of the world. Jesus later sent out seventy (or seventy-two) disciples ahead of him (Luke 10:1).

**Read:** Genesis 10:1–11:9.

**Genesis 11:10–32 · about 6 minutes · 4 scenes**

### Scene 7.1 · The line of Shem
*On screen:* The family tree from Level 1 appears again, grown up to Noah. A new branch springs from Noah: Shem.

1. Out of all the scattered families, Genesis follows one: the line of Shem.
2. **[Q] Genesis 11:10 (in part)** "These are the descendants of Shem."
3. *(Play: the family tree, from Shem to Abram.)*

### Scene 7.2 · Ur of the Chaldeans
*On screen:* A great walled city by the Euphrates, with its own ziggurat and busy harbor: Ur. In a house there, Terah with his three sons, Abram, Nahor and Haran.

1. **[Q] Genesis 11:26** "When Terah had lived seventy years, he became the father of Abram, Nahor, and Haran."
2. Haran died young, in Ur, leaving his son Lot.
3. Abram married Sarai.
4. **[Q] Genesis 11:30** "Now Sarai was barren; she had no child."
5. In a family with no child, God's promise of a Savior seemed to come to a stop. But God loves to begin where people see an end.

### Scene 7.3 · The road to Haran
*On screen:* A caravan of tents, camels and herds sets out along the river, northwest, toward Haran. Abram walks beside his old father.

1. **[Q] Genesis 11:31** "Terah took Abram his son and Lot the son of Haran, his grandson, and Sarai his daughter-in-law, his son Abram's wife, and they went forth together from Ur of the Chaldeans to go into the land of Canaan; but when they came to Haran, they settled there."
2. **[Q] Genesis 11:32** "The days of Terah were two hundred and five years; and Terah died in Haran."

### Scene 7.4 · A voice calling
*On screen:* Haran at night. Abram stands outside his tent under a sky full of stars. A soft light, the same light that came down at Babel, rests on him.

1. In Haran, Abram heard a voice.
2. **[Q] Genesis 12:1** "Now the LORD said to Abram, 'Go from your country and your kindred and your father's house to the land that I will show you.'"
3. At Babel, the nations were scattered. Now, through one man, God would begin to gather them back.
4. That is the next Age's story.

### Play: the family tree, from Shem to Abram
- **What it is:** the ordering kind of play (spec §13.7, kind 9), the same as Level 1's family tree, continuing it. Ten name-leaves drift down, and the player places each on the tree in order from Shem to Abram.
- **Each correct placement** grows the next branch and shows one short fact:
  - Shem: "son of Noah, blessed by his father"
  - Arpachshad: "born two years after the Flood"
  - Eber: "from whose name may come the word 'Hebrew'"
  - Peleg: "in his days the earth was divided"
  - Terah: "father of Abram, Nahor and Haran"
  - Abram: "the LORD will call him"
- **The ten names:** Shem, Arpachshad, Shelah, Eber, Peleg, Reu, Serug, Nahor, Terah, Abram.
- **Mistakes:** a misplaced leaf floats back with a hint, such as "Eber came after Shelah."
- **Teaches:** the line from Noah to Abram, through which God's promise travels.
- **Gentle mode:** the next correct leaf glows.

**Thread:** *the line of Shem, and the call of Abram → all nations blessed in Christ.* The glowing object is the brightest star above Abram's tent.
- **[Q] Galatians 3:8:** "And the scripture, foreseeing that God would justify the Gentiles by faith, preached the gospel beforehand to Abraham, saying, 'In you shall all the nations be blessed.'"
- **What it shows:** the nations scattered at Babel were not forgotten. God chose one family from the line of Shem, so that through it all the families of the earth would be blessed. That blessing came in Jesus, who sends his Church to every nation. St John saw the end of the story: a great multitude "from every nation, from all tribes and peoples and tongues" before the throne of God (Revelation 7:9).
- **Source:** Galatians 3:8; Genesis 12:3; Catechism 59–60.

**Where and when:**
- **Ur.** Ur of the Chaldeans was one of the greatest cities of its time, on the Euphrates near the Persian Gulf, in southern Iraq today. Archaeologists have uncovered its ziggurat and royal tombs full of gold.
- **Haran.** Haran was a trading city far to the northwest, in southern Turkey today, on the road between Mesopotamia and Canaan. Both Ur and Haran honored the moon god, Sin. It was from a world of many gods that the one God called Abram.
- **"Hebrew."** The name Eber may be the root of the word "Hebrew," the name later given to Abram's people.

**Read:** Genesis 11:10–12:1.
