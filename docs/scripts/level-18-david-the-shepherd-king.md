# Level 18 script: *David, the Shepherd King*

**Bible:** 1 Samuel 16–31; 2 Samuel 1–7
**Age:** VI · The Kingdom
**Length:** about 65 minutes (8 episodes, then the quiz, then reflection and prayer)
**Status:** draft for Dave's approval.

## How to read this script

- Each **scene** is a checkpoint. Its numbered lines are the narration beats; the player taps to move from one to the next.
- *On screen* describes what the scene shows. It's the brief for the art in both styles.
- **[Q]** marks an exact quotation from the Bible. Its wording in both translations is in the verse table at the end. Everything else is our own retelling.
- **Play** is the episode's interactive moment. **Thread** is its golden thread. **Read** is the passage the game sends the player to.
- "LORD" in capitals stands for God's holy name, printed in small capitals in the RSV.

### What this level is about

God sends Samuel to Bethlehem to anoint a new king, and passes over seven tall sons of Jesse for the youngest, out with the sheep: "man looks on the outward appearance, but the LORD looks on the heart." The shepherd boy David faces the giant Goliath with a sling and five stones, because "the battle is the LORD's." Saul's son Jonathan loves David as his own soul, and gives him his robe and his sword, though it means David will be king instead of him. Saul, jealous, hunts David for years, but twice David has Saul in his power and spares him: "I will not put forth my hand against the LORD's anointed." After Saul's death, David becomes king over all Israel, takes Jerusalem, and dances before the ark as it comes into the city. And God makes him a promise that will reach all the way to Jesus: "your throne shall be established for ever."

The shepherd of Bethlehem who becomes king is the clearest picture in the Old Testament of Jesus, "the son of David," the Good Shepherd and the King whose kingdom has no end.

**How it's shown:** Episodes 2, 6 and 7 begin with a short note. Goliath falls, seen from far off; Saul's death and Uzzah's are told in a sentence each. No battles are shown.

## Episode 1 · The Shepherd Boy Anointed

**1 Samuel 16 · about 8 minutes · 3 scenes**

### Scene 1.1 · Fill your horn with oil
*On screen:* Ramah. Old Samuel sits alone, grieving for Saul. A light speaks to him. He takes a ram's horn, fills it with oil, and sets out south toward Bethlehem.

1. **[Q] 1 Samuel 16:1** "The LORD said to Samuel, "How long will you grieve over Saul, seeing I have rejected him from being king over Israel? Fill your horn with oil, and go; I will send you to Jesse the Bethlehemite, for I have provided for myself a king among his sons.""
2. Samuel went to Bethlehem and invited Jesse and his sons to a sacrifice.

### Scene 1.2 · The LORD looks on the heart
*On screen:* Jesse's courtyard in Bethlehem. One by one his sons pass before Samuel: Eliab, tall and strong; then Abinadab; then Shammah; seven in all. Samuel shakes his head at each. Then a boy runs in from the fields, ruddy and bright-eyed, with a shepherd's staff, smelling of sheep. *(Play: the LORD looks on the heart.)*

1. **[Q] 1 Samuel 16:6** "When they came, he looked on Eliab and thought, "Surely the LORD's anointed is before him.""
2. **[Q] 1 Samuel 16:7** "But the LORD said to Samuel, "Do not look on his appearance or on the height of his stature, because I have rejected him; for the LORD sees not as man sees; man looks on the outward appearance, but the LORD looks on the heart.""
3. **[Q] 1 Samuel 16:10** "And Jesse made seven of his sons pass before Samuel. And Samuel said to Jesse, "The LORD has not chosen these.""
4. *(Play: the LORD looks on the heart.)*
5. **[Q] 1 Samuel 16:11** "And Samuel said to Jesse, "Are all your sons here?" And he said, "There remains yet the youngest, but behold, he is keeping the sheep." And Samuel said to Jesse, "Send and fetch him; for we will not sit down till he comes here.""
6. **[Q] 1 Samuel 16:12** "And he sent, and brought him in. Now he was ruddy, and had beautiful eyes, and was handsome. And the LORD said, "Arise, anoint him; for this is he.""
7. **[Q] 1 Samuel 16:13** "Then Samuel took the horn of oil, and anointed him in the midst of his brothers; and the Spirit of the LORD came mightily upon David from that day forward. And Samuel rose up, and went to Ramah."

### Scene 1.3 · The shepherd with the lyre
*On screen:* Saul's house at Gibeah. King Saul sits in the dark, troubled and angry. Young David sits at his feet with a lyre and plays, and the darkness lifts from the room.

1. Saul was troubled by an evil spirit, and his servants looked for someone who could play the lyre to soothe him. One of them knew a son of Jesse of Bethlehem, "skilful in playing," and "the LORD is with him."
2. **[Q] 1 Samuel 16:23** "And whenever the evil spirit from God was upon Saul, David took the lyre and played it with his hand; so Saul was refreshed, and was well, and the evil spirit departed from him."

### Play: the LORD looks on the heart
- **What it is:** a quiet interaction, spot the one God chooses (spec §13.7, kind 20).
- **How it plays:** Jesse's sons pass before Samuel one at a time, each taller and finer than the last. For each, the player chooses what Samuel does: anoint him, or pass him by, because "the LORD has not chosen this one." When all seven have passed, the player asks Jesse, "Are all your sons here?"
- **Teaches:** that God chooses by the heart, not by height, looks or strength.
- **Gentle mode:** the right choice glows.
- **Accessibility:** a pointer, or the arrow keys and Space.

**Thread:** *The shepherd boy of Bethlehem, anointed to be the shepherd of God's people → Jesus: "I am the good shepherd."* The glowing object is the horn of oil, poured on David's head.
- **[Q] John 10:11:** "I am the good shepherd. The good shepherd lays down his life for the sheep."
- **[Q] John 10:14:** "I am the good shepherd; I know my own and my own know me,"
- **What it shows:** God took David "from following the sheep" to be the shepherd of Israel. A thousand years later, another child was born in Bethlehem, of David's family, and his birth was announced first to shepherds. Jesus calls himself the Good Shepherd, who knows his sheep by name and lays down his life for them, as David risked his against the lion and the bear.
- **Source:** John 10:11–16; 2 Samuel 5:2; Psalm 23; Ezekiel 34:23; Catechism 437, 754.

**Where and when:**
- **About 1025 BC.** David was perhaps fifteen when Samuel anointed him.
- **Bethlehem.** Ruth's town (Level 16). Jesse was the son of Obed, the son of Ruth and Boaz.
- **A horn of oil.** A hollow ram's horn used as a flask. Kings and priests were anointed with olive oil, often scented with spices.
- **The lyre.** A small harp, the instrument of the Psalms. Many of the Psalms are called "of David."

**Read:** 1 Samuel 16:1–23.

## Episode 2 · David and Goliath

**1 Samuel 17 · about 9 minutes · 3 scenes**

> **A note before this episode:** David kills the giant Goliath. Goliath falls, seen from far off; nothing violent is shown.

### Scene 2.1 · The champion of Gath
*On screen:* The valley of Elah: the Philistine army on one hill, Israel's on the other, a stream between. A giant in bronze armor strides out every morning and shouts across the valley. Saul's soldiers shrink back. Young David arrives with bread and cheese for his brothers, and hears.

1. **[Q] 1 Samuel 17:4** "And there came out from the camp of the Philistines a champion named Goliath, of Gath, whose height was six cubits and a span."
2. For forty days Goliath came out, morning and evening, and challenged Israel to send one man to fight him.
3. **[Q] 1 Samuel 17:11** "When Saul and all Israel heard these words of the Philistine, they were dismayed and greatly afraid."
4. **[Q] 1 Samuel 17:26** "And David said to the men who stood by him, "What shall be done for the man who kills this Philistine, and takes away the reproach from Israel? For who is this uncircumcised Philistine, that he should defy the armies of the living God?""

### Scene 2.2 · "Let no man's heart fail"
*On screen:* Saul's tent. David stands before the king. Saul puts his own armor on him; David can hardly walk in it, and takes it off. He goes down to the brook and chooses five smooth stones.

1. **[Q] 1 Samuel 17:32** "And David said to Saul, "Let no man's heart fail because of him; your servant will go and fight with this Philistine.""
2. **[Q] 1 Samuel 17:33** "And Saul said to David, "You are not able to go against this Philistine to fight with him; for you are but a youth, and he has been a man of war from his youth.""
3. **[Q] 1 Samuel 17:34** "But David said to Saul, "Your servant used to keep sheep for his father; and when there came a lion, or a bear, and took a lamb from the flock,"
4. **[Q] 1 Samuel 17:37** "And David said, "The LORD who delivered me from the paw of the lion and from the paw of the bear, will deliver me from the hand of this Philistine." And Saul said to David, "Go, and the LORD be with you!""
5. **[Q] 1 Samuel 17:40** "Then he took his staff in his hand, and chose five smooth stones from the brook, and put them in his shepherd's bag or wallet; his sling was in his hand, and he drew near to the Philistine."

### Scene 2.3 · The battle is the LORD's
*On screen:* The valley floor. Goliath, huge, with his spear and his shield-bearer; David, small, with a sling. David runs toward him, whirls the sling, and lets go. Seen from the hilltop: the giant falls on his face, and the Philistines run. *(Play: five smooth stones.)*

1. **[Q] 1 Samuel 17:45** "Then David said to the Philistine, "You come to me with a sword and with a spear and with a javelin; but I come to you in the name of the LORD of hosts, the God of the armies of Israel, whom you have defied."
2. **[Q] 1 Samuel 17:47** "and that all this assembly may know that the LORD saves not with sword and spear; for the battle is the LORD's and he will give you into our hand.""
3. *(Play: five smooth stones.)*
4. **[Q] 1 Samuel 17:49** "And David put his hand in his bag and took out a stone, and slung it, and struck the Philistine on his forehead; the stone sank into his forehead, and he fell on his face to the ground."
5. **[Q] 1 Samuel 17:50** "So David prevailed over the Philistine with a sling and with a stone, and struck the Philistine, and killed him; there was no sword in the hand of David."

### Play: five smooth stones
- **What it is:** aiming (spec §13.7, kind 4; the spec names David's sling).
- **How it plays:** David whirls the sling, and a marker swings back and forth across Goliath's great bronze helmet. The player lets go (tap, or Space) when the marker is on the bare forehead. Too early or too late, and David whirls again; a stone is never wasted.
- **Teaches:** that David trusted not in armor or strength, but in the LORD, and that "the battle is the LORD's."
- **Gentle mode:** the target is wide, and the marker swings slowly.
- **Accessibility:** a pointer, or Space.

**Thread:** *"The battle is the LORD's" → "Thanks be to God, who gives us the victory through our Lord Jesus Christ."* The glowing object is the stone in David's sling.
- **[Q] 1 Corinthians 15:57:** "But thanks be to God, who gives us the victory through our Lord Jesus Christ."
- **What it shows:** David won not with Saul's armor, but "in the name of the LORD." The Fathers of the Church saw in David's victory a picture of Christ's: the Son of David went out alone against the greatest enemies of all, sin and death, and won, not with a sword but with the wood of the cross. And he shares his victory with us.
- **Source:** 1 Corinthians 15:54–57; Colossians 2:15; Catechism 1505, 2853.

**Where and when:**
- **The valley of Elah.** A wide valley in the low hills west of Bethlehem, between Israel's hills and Philistine country. It still has a stream with smooth stones.
- **Six cubits and a span.** About three meters (nine and a half feet). Some old copies of the Bible say four cubits.
- **Gath.** One of the five Philistine cities (Level 15).
- **The sling.** A shepherd's weapon: a strip of leather with a pocket, whirled and released. A good slinger could hit a target at a hundred meters.

**Read:** 1 Samuel 17:1–51.

## Episode 3 · Jonathan's Friendship

**1 Samuel 18; 20 · about 8 minutes · 3 scenes**

### Scene 3.1 · Knit to the soul of David
*On screen:* Saul's court. Jonathan, the king's son, a warrior prince, takes off his royal robe and puts it on David, then gives him his armor, his sword, his bow and his belt. Outside, women dance and sing, and Saul scowls.

1. **[Q] 1 Samuel 18:1** "When he had finished speaking to Saul, the soul of Jonathan was knit to the soul of David, and Jonathan loved him as his own soul."
2. **[Q] 1 Samuel 18:3** "Then Jonathan made a covenant with David, because he loved him as his own soul."
3. **[Q] 1 Samuel 18:4** "And Jonathan stripped himself of the robe that was upon him, and gave it to David, and his armor, and even his sword and his bow and his girdle."
4. **[Q] 1 Samuel 18:7** "And the women sang to one another as they made merry, "Saul has slain his thousands, and David his ten thousands.""
5. **[Q] 1 Samuel 18:9** "And Saul eyed David from that day on."

### Scene 3.2 · The sign of the arrows
*On screen:* A field outside Gibeah, early morning. David hides behind a heap of stones. Jonathan comes out with a bow and a small boy. He shoots an arrow far beyond the boy: the sign that David must flee. *(Play: the arrow beyond you.)*

1. Saul's jealousy grew until he wanted David dead. Jonathan promised to find out what his father meant to do, and to tell David by a secret sign: arrows shot in the field.
2. **[Q] 1 Samuel 20:17** "And Jonathan made David swear again by his love for him; for he loved him as he loved his own soul."
3. **[Q] 1 Samuel 20:35** "In the morning Jonathan went out into the field to the appointment with David, and with him a little lad."
4. **[Q] 1 Samuel 20:36** "And he said to his lad, "Run and find the arrows which I shoot." As the lad ran, he shot an arrow beyond him."
5. *(Play: the arrow beyond you.)*
6. **[Q] 1 Samuel 20:37** "And when the lad came to the place of the arrow which Jonathan had shot, Jonathan called after the lad and said, "Is not the arrow beyond you?""

### Scene 3.3 · "Go in peace"
*On screen:* The boy has gone back to the city. David comes out from behind the stones and bows to the ground three times. The two friends embrace and weep. Then they part: David toward the hills, Jonathan back toward the city.

1. **[Q] 1 Samuel 20:41** "And as soon as the lad had gone, David rose from beside the stone heap and fell on his face to the ground, and bowed three times; and they kissed one another, and wept with one another, until David recovered himself."
2. **[Q] 1 Samuel 20:42** "Then Jonathan said to David, "Go in peace, forasmuch as we have sworn both of us in the name of the LORD, saying, 'The LORD shall be between me and you, and between my descendants and your descendants, for ever.'" And he rose and departed; and Jonathan went into the city."

### Play: the arrow beyond you
- **What it is:** aiming (spec §13.7, kind 4; the spec names Jonathan's arrows).
- **How it plays:** Jonathan draws his bow. A marker sweeps out across the field, past the boy, and back. The player lets the arrow go (tap, or Space) when the marker is far beyond the boy: the sign that means "flee." An arrow that falls short means something else, so Jonathan simply shoots again.
- **Teaches:** the secret sign, and that Jonathan chose to save his friend, though it set him against his own father.
- **Gentle mode:** the target is wide, and the marker sweeps slowly.
- **Accessibility:** a pointer, or Space.

**Thread:** *Jonathan, the king's son, gives David his robe and his sword and says, "you shall be king, and I shall be next to you" → John the Baptist: "He must increase, but I must decrease."* The glowing object is Jonathan's royal robe, given to David.
- **[Q] John 3:30:** "He must increase, but I must decrease.""
- **What it shows:** Jonathan was the heir to Saul's throne. But he saw that God had chosen David, and he gave David his own robe and weapons, and gladly stepped aside. John the Baptist did the same for Jesus: crowds followed John, but when Jesus came, John pointed to him and said, "He must increase, but I must decrease." True friends of God are glad when someone else is lifted up.
- **Source:** 1 Samuel 23:16–18; John 3:27–30; Catechism 523, 1829.

**Where and when:**
- **Gibeah.** Saul's home town and capital, a few kilometers north of Jerusalem (Level 15).
- **"Saul has slain his thousands."** A victory song. Saul heard in it that the people loved David more than him.
- **A covenant.** Jonathan and David swore loyalty to each other and to each other's children "for ever." Years later, King David took care of Jonathan's lame son, Mephibosheth (2 Samuel 9).

**Read:** 1 Samuel 18:1–16; 20:1–42.

## Episode 4 · On the Run from Saul

**1 Samuel 21–23 · about 8 minutes · 3 scenes**

### Scene 4.1 · The holy bread
*On screen:* The little town of Nob, where the priests keep the tabernacle. David arrives hungry and alone. The priest Ahimelech brings out the only bread there is: the twelve loaves of the Presence from the holy table.

1. David fled from Saul with nothing, and came to Nob, the town of the priests, where the tabernacle stood. He asked the priest for bread.
2. **[Q] 1 Samuel 21:6** "So the priest gave him the holy bread; for there was no bread there but the bread of the Presence, which is removed from before the LORD, to be replaced by hot bread on the day it is taken away."

### Scene 4.2 · The cave of Adullam
*On screen:* A great cave in the hills. David sits at the mouth of it. One by one, then by the dozen, people come up the path: his brothers, his old father, and then all kinds of people in trouble, in debt, unhappy, until four hundred men camp around him.

1. **[Q] 1 Samuel 22:1** "David departed from there and escaped to the cave of Adullam; and when his brothers and all his father's house heard it, they went down there to him."
2. **[Q] 1 Samuel 22:2** "And every one who was in distress, and every one who was in debt, and every one who was discontented, gathered to him; and he became captain over them. And there were with him about four hundred men."

### Scene 4.3 · The wilderness of Ziph
*On screen:* The bare, rocky hills of the wilderness. Saul's soldiers search the ridges by day; David's men slip from rock to rock just ahead of them. Once Saul's army goes around one side of a mountain while David's men go around the other. At a hidden stronghold, Jonathan comes to visit David. *(Play: the other side of the mountain.)*

1. **[Q] 1 Samuel 23:14** "And David remained in the strongholds in the wilderness, in the hill country of the Wilderness of Ziph. And Saul sought him every day, but God did not give him into his hand."
2. *(Play: the other side of the mountain.)*
3. **[Q] 1 Samuel 23:16** "And Jonathan, Saul's son, rose, and went to David at Horesh, and strengthened his hand in God."
4. **[Q] 1 Samuel 23:17** "And he said to him, "Fear not; for the hand of Saul my father shall not find you; you shall be king over Israel, and I shall be next to you; Saul my father also knows this.""

### Play: the other side of the mountain
- **What it is:** timing (spec §13.7, kind 16).
- **How it plays:** a mountain ridge, with Saul's soldiers on one side and David's men on the other. Saul's lookouts sweep the slopes with their eyes. When they look away, the player moves David's men on to the next rock (tap, or Space); when they look this way, the men keep still. Six rocks, and they are over the ridge and safe.
- **Teaches:** that "God did not give him into his hand": David trusted God to keep him safe through years of running.
- **Gentle mode:** the lookouts look away for longer.
- **Accessibility:** a pointer, or Space.

**Thread:** *David eats the holy bread of the Presence → Jesus: "Have you not read what David did, when he was hungry?"* The glowing object is the holy bread.
- **[Q] Matthew 12:3:** "He said to them, "Have you not read what David did, when he was hungry, and those who were with him:"
- **[Q] Matthew 12:4:** "how he entered the house of God and ate the bread of the Presence, which it was not lawful for him to eat nor for those who were with him, but only for the priests?"
- **What it shows:** the bread of the Presence was holy, for the priests alone. But the priest gave it to David and his hungry men. Jesus himself told this story to show that God's law was given for the good of people, and that mercy comes first. And Jesus would give a holier bread than any on the golden table: himself, the Bread of Life, for everyone who is hungry.
- **Source:** Matthew 12:1–8; John 6:35; Catechism 582, 1338.

**Where and when:**
- **Nob.** A town of priests near Jerusalem, where the tabernacle stood after Shiloh was lost (Level 17).
- **The bread of the Presence.** Twelve loaves set out on the golden table in the holy place every sabbath (Level 11).
- **Adullam.** A cave-riddled hill near the valley of Elah. Psalms 57 and 142 are titled "when he fled from Saul, in the cave."
- **The wilderness of Ziph.** Bare hills south-east of Hebron, sloping down to the Dead Sea.

**Read:** 1 Samuel 21:1–6; 22:1–2; 23:14–18.

## Episode 5 · Sparing the LORD's Anointed

**1 Samuel 24; 26 · about 8 minutes · 3 scenes**

### Scene 5.1 · The cave at En-gedi
*On screen:* The wild goat rocks of En-gedi, above the Dead Sea. Saul comes into a cave alone, not knowing that David and his men are hiding in the dark at the back. David creeps up behind him and silently cuts a corner off his robe.

1. Saul came with three thousand men to hunt David among the rocks of En-gedi. He went into a cave alone, and David and his men were hiding deep inside it.
2. David's men whispered that God had given his enemy into his hand. David crept up behind Saul, but he only cut off the corner of his robe, and afterward his heart troubled him even for that.
3. **[Q] 1 Samuel 24:6** "He said to his men, "The LORD forbid that I should do this thing to my lord, the LORD's anointed, to put forth my hand against him, seeing he is the LORD's anointed.""

### Scene 5.2 · "Is this your voice, my son David?"
*On screen:* Outside the cave. Saul walks away down the path. David comes out behind him, calls after him, and bows to the ground, holding up the piece of robe. Saul turns, sees it, and weeps.

1. **[Q] 1 Samuel 24:10** "Lo, this day your eyes have seen how the LORD gave you today into my hand in the cave; and some bade me kill you, but I spared you. I said, 'I will not put forth my hand against my lord; for he is the LORD's anointed.'"
2. **[Q] 1 Samuel 24:11** "See, my father, see the skirt of your robe in my hand; for by the fact that I cut off the skirt of your robe, and did not kill you, you may know and see that there is no wrong or treason in my hands. I have not sinned against you, though you hunt my life to take it."
3. **[Q] 1 Samuel 24:16** "When David had finished speaking these words to Saul, Saul said, "Is this your voice, my son David?" And Saul lifted up his voice and wept."
4. **[Q] 1 Samuel 24:17** "He said to David, "You are more righteous than I; for you have repaid me good, whereas I have repaid you evil."

### Scene 5.3 · The spear and the jar
*On screen:* Night in the wilderness of Ziph, some time later. Saul's whole army lies asleep in a ring. David and Abishai creep through the sleeping camp to the middle, where Saul lies with his spear stuck in the ground by his head and a jar of water beside it. *(Play: through the sleeping camp.)*

1. **[Q] 1 Samuel 26:7** "So David and Abishai went to the army by night; and there lay Saul sleeping within the encampment, with his spear stuck in the ground at his head; and Abner and the army lay around him."
2. **[Q] 1 Samuel 26:9** "But David said to Abishai, "Do not destroy him; for who can put forth his hand against the LORD's anointed, and be guiltless?""
3. **[Q] 1 Samuel 26:11** "The LORD forbid that I should put forth my hand against the LORD's anointed; but take now the spear that is at his head, and the jar of water, and let us go.""
4. *(Play: through the sleeping camp.)*
5. **[Q] 1 Samuel 26:12** "So David took the spear and the jar of water from Saul's head; and they went away. No man saw it, or knew it, nor did any awake; for they were all asleep, because a deep sleep from the LORD had fallen upon them."
6. **[Q] 1 Samuel 26:23** "The LORD rewards every man for his righteousness and his faithfulness; for the LORD gave you into my hand today, and I would not put forth my hand against the LORD's anointed."

### Play: through the sleeping camp
- **What it is:** stealth and escape (spec §13.7, kind 17).
- **How it plays:** the sleeping camp at night, soldiers lying in rings around Saul. The player leads David and Abishai through the gaps (drag, or the arrow keys; Space goes on to the next gap), picks up the spear and the water jar from beside Saul's head, and slips away. Nobody wakes: "a deep sleep from the LORD had fallen upon them."
- **Teaches:** that David had the power to kill his enemy and chose not to, leaving judgment to God.
- **Gentle mode:** they walk on by themselves.
- **Accessibility:** a pointer, or the arrow keys and Space.

**Thread:** *David spares the king who is hunting him → Jesus: "Love your enemies and pray for those who persecute you."* The glowing object is the corner of Saul's robe, in David's hand.
- **[Q] Matthew 5:44:** "But I say to you, Love your enemies and pray for those who persecute you,"
- **What it shows:** Saul hunted David for years, and twice God put Saul in David's power. Both times David refused to harm him: he left Saul to God. Jesus asks the same of us: not to pay back evil with evil, but to love our enemies and pray for them. On the cross he did it himself, praying for the men who crucified him.
- **Source:** Matthew 5:43–48; Romans 12:17–21; Luke 23:34; Catechism 1825, 2303.

**Where and when:**
- **En-gedi.** A spring and waterfall in the cliffs above the Dead Sea, full of caves and wild goats. Its name means "spring of the kid."
- **The corner of the robe.** A king's robe was a sign of his kingdom (Level 17: Samuel's robe, torn). David's conscience troubled him even for cutting it.
- **Abishai.** David's nephew, one of his bravest men, who wanted to kill Saul in his sleep.

**Read:** 1 Samuel 24:1–22; 26:1–25.

## Episode 6 · King in Jerusalem

**1 Samuel 31; 2 Samuel 1–5 · about 8 minutes · 3 scenes**

> **A note before this episode:** Saul and Jonathan die in a battle with the Philistines. It is told in a sentence, and nothing is shown.

### Scene 6.1 · "How are the mighty fallen!"
*On screen:* Mount Gilboa at dusk, empty, with a broken bow lying in the grass. Then David's camp, where a messenger arrives with the news. David tears his clothes and weeps, and sings a lament.

1. The Philistines fought against Israel on Mount Gilboa. Jonathan and his brothers were killed, and Saul, badly wounded, took his own life rather than be captured.
2. **[Q] 1 Samuel 31:6** "Thus Saul died, and his three sons, and his armor-bearer, and all his men, on the same day together."
3. **[Q] 2 Samuel 1:19** ""Thy glory, O Israel, is slain upon thy high places! How are the mighty fallen!"
4. **[Q] 2 Samuel 1:23** ""Saul and Jonathan, beloved and lovely! In life and in death they were not divided; they were swifter than eagles, they were stronger than lions."

### Scene 6.2 · King at Hebron
*On screen:* Hebron, the old city of Abraham. The men of Judah anoint David king. Years later, all the elders of Israel come and kneel, and David is anointed again, king over all Israel.

1. **[Q] 2 Samuel 2:4** "And the men of Judah came, and there they anointed David king over the house of Judah. When they told David, "It was the men of Jabesh-gilead who buried Saul,""
2. **[Q] 2 Samuel 5:3** "So all the elders of Israel came to the king at Hebron; and King David made a covenant with them at Hebron before the LORD, and they anointed David king over Israel."
3. **[Q] 2 Samuel 5:4** "David was thirty years old when he began to reign, and he reigned forty years."

### Scene 6.3 · The stronghold of Zion
*On screen:* Jerusalem, a small fortress on a steep hill, with the Jebusites mocking from the walls. David's men climb up through the water shaft inside the hill. The next scene: David's banner flies over the stronghold of Zion, the city of David. *(Play: the road to the throne.)*

1. **[Q] 2 Samuel 5:6** "And the king and his men went to Jerusalem against the Jebusites, the inhabitants of the land, who said to David, "You will not come in here, but the blind and the lame will ward you off"—thinking, "David cannot come in here.""
2. **[Q] 2 Samuel 5:7** "Nevertheless David took the stronghold of Zion, that is, the city of David."
3. *(Play: the road to the throne.)*
4. **[Q] 2 Samuel 5:10** "And David became greater and greater, for the LORD, the God of hosts, was with him."

### Play: the road to the throne
- **What it is:** ordering (spec §13.7, kind 9).
- **How it plays:** the long road from the sheepfold to the throne, as a path of steps up the hill of Zion. ### Play: dancing before the LORD
- **What it is:** rhythm (spec §13.7, kind 5).
- **How it plays:** the procession climbs toward the city, and the drum beats. The player dances with David on the beat (tap, or Space), and every six steps the procession stops for a sacrifice, as at the start. A missed beat doesn't matter: David just dances on. When the ark reaches the gate, the trumpets sound.
- **Teaches:** David's joy in God, without caring what anyone thought, and that the ark was now carried the right way, on the shoulders of the Levites.
- **Gentle mode:** any tap counts.
- **Accessibility:** a pointer, or Space.

**Thread:** *The ark comes to the house of Obed-edom for three months, and David leaps for joy before it → Mary, carrying Jesus, comes to Elizabeth's house for three months, and John leaps for joy.* The glowing object is the ark.
- **[Q] Luke 1:41:** "And when Elizabeth heard the greeting of Mary, the babe leaped in her womb; and Elizabeth was filled with the Holy Spirit"
- **[Q] Luke 1:43:** "And why is this granted me, that the mother of my Lord should come to me?"
- **[Q] Luke 1:44:** "For behold, when the voice of your greeting came to my ears, the babe in my womb leaped for joy."
- **[Q] Luke 1:56:** "And Mary remained with her about three months, and returned to her home."
- **What it shows:** Saint Luke tells Mary's visit to Elizabeth with the words of this chapter. David asked, "How can the ark of the LORD come to me?"; Elizabeth asks, "Why is this granted me, that the mother of my Lord should come to me?" David leapt before the ark; John leapt in his mother's womb. The ark stayed three months; Mary stayed three months. The ark held God's word on stone; Mary carried the Word of God made flesh. That is why the Church calls Mary the "Ark of the Covenant."
- **Source:** Luke 1:39–56; Revelation 11:19–12:1; Litany of Loreto; Catechism 2676.

**Where and when:**

- **A linen ephod.** A simple priestly garment. David danced not in royal robes but like a humble servant before God.
- **Michal.** Saul's daughter, David's first wife.

**Read:** 2 Samuel 6:1–23.

- **The Douay-Rheims column** is the 1899 American edition, which is public domain. It calls 1 and 2 Samuel "1 and 2 Kings" (following the Latin Vulgate). One slip in Bible Gateway's Douay-Rheims is corrected: in 1 Samuel 16:12 it prints "brought him Now he was ruddy," without the full stop. Its own numbers are shown in brackets.
