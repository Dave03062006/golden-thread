# Level 12 script: *Forty Years in the Wilderness*

**Bible:** Numbers 1–2; 6; 9–11; 13–14; 16–17; 20–24
**Age:** IV · The Covenant in the Desert
**Length:** about 55 minutes (8 episodes, then the quiz, then reflection and prayer)
**Status:** approved by Dave on 2026-11-08 ("Approved, build it").

## How to read this script

- Each **scene** is a checkpoint. Its numbered lines are the narration beats; the player taps to move from one to the next.
- *On screen* describes what the scene shows. It's the brief for the art in both styles.
- **[Q]** marks an exact quotation from the Bible. Its wording in both translations is in the verse table at the end. Everything else is our own retelling.
- **Play** is the episode's interactive moment. **Thread** is its golden thread. **Read** is the passage the game sends the player to.
- "LORD" in capitals stands for God's holy name, printed in small capitals in the RSV.

### What this level is about

A year after the Passover, God's people leave Mount Sinai. They are counted by name, camped tribe by tribe around the tabernacle, and blessed with the words God gives Aaron: "The LORD bless you and keep you." When the cloud lifts, they set out; when it settles, they camp. But the journey is hard, and again and again the people complain: about the food, about the water, about Moses and Aaron. When the twelve spies come back from the promised land, ten of them say it cannot be taken, and the people refuse to go in. So they wander for forty years, until a new generation has grown up.

Yet God never leaves them. He shares Moses' burden with seventy elders, makes Aaron's dry rod blossom, brings water from the rock, and heals everyone who looks at the bronze serpent. And when a king hires the prophet Balaam to curse them, God turns every curse into a blessing, and Balaam sees, far off, a star rising out of Jacob.

Almost every step of this road points to Jesus: the blessing he gave his disciples as he went up to heaven, the Spirit poured out on all God's people, the rivers of living water, the Son of man lifted up like the serpent, and the star the wise men followed to Bethlehem.

**How it's shown:** God's judgments (the fire at Taberah, the plague after the quail, Korah's fall, the serpents) are told plainly and briefly, never dwelt on. No one is shown dying; the camp is shown afraid, then praying, then saved.

## Episode 1 · The Camp and the Priestly Blessing

**Numbers 1:1–3, 45–46; 2:1–34; 6:22–27; 9:15–23; 10:11–36 · about 7 minutes · 3 scenes**

### Scene 1.1 · Counted by name
*On screen:* The camp at the foot of Mount Sinai, a year after the people came there. Moses and Aaron sit at the door of the tent of meeting with a long scroll, and the leaders of the tribes come one by one. Then, seen from above, the camp takes its shape: the tabernacle in the middle, the Levites around it, and three tribes on each side under their banners.

1. **[Q] Numbers 1:1** "The LORD spoke to Moses in the wilderness of Sinai, in the tent of meeting, on the first day of the second month, in the second year after they had come out of the land of Egypt, saying,"
2. **[Q] Numbers 1:2** ""Take a census of all the congregation of the people of Israel, by families, by fathers' houses, according to the number of names, every male, head by head;"
3. Every family was counted, every name written down. God knew each one of his people.
4. **[Q] Numbers 2:2** ""The people of Israel shall encamp each by his own standard, with the ensigns of their fathers' houses; they shall encamp facing the tent of meeting on every side."
5. Judah, Issachar and Zebulun camped on the east, toward the sunrise; Reuben, Simeon and Gad on the south; Ephraim, Manasseh and Benjamin on the west; and Dan, Asher and Naphtali on the north. In the middle of them all stood the tabernacle, where God lived among them.

### Scene 1.2 · The cloud and the fire
*On screen:* The tabernacle under the cloud by day, and under a glow of fire by night. Then the cloud rises: trumpets sound, the tents come down, and the whole camp sets out behind the ark, tribe by tribe, into the wilderness of Paran. *(Play: follow the cloud.)*

1. **[Q] Numbers 9:15** "On the day that the tabernacle was set up, the cloud covered the tabernacle, the tent of the testimony; and at evening it was over the tabernacle like the appearance of fire until morning."
2. **[Q] Numbers 9:17** "And whenever the cloud was taken up from over the tent, after that the people of Israel set out; and in the place where the cloud settled down, there the people of Israel encamped."
3. **[Q] Numbers 9:18** "At the command of the LORD the people of Israel set out, and at the command of the LORD they encamped; as long as the cloud rested over the tabernacle, they remained in camp."
4. *(Play: follow the cloud.)*
5. **[Q] Numbers 10:33** "So they set out from the mount of the LORD three days' journey; and the ark of the covenant of the LORD went before them three days' journey, to seek out a resting place for them."
6. **[Q] Numbers 10:35** "And whenever the ark set out, Moses said, "Arise, O LORD, and let thy enemies be scattered; and let them that hate thee flee before thee.""
7. **[Q] Numbers 10:36** "And when it rested, he said, "Return, O LORD, to the ten thousand thousands of Israel.""

### Scene 1.3 · "The LORD bless you"
*On screen:* Evening at a new camp. Aaron stands at the door of the tent of meeting and lifts his hands over the people, and a soft golden light spreads over the tents, one after another.

1. **[Q] Numbers 6:23** ""Say to Aaron and his sons, Thus you shall bless the people of Israel: you shall say to them,"
2. **[Q] Numbers 6:24** "The LORD bless you and keep you:"
3. **[Q] Numbers 6:25** "The LORD make his face to shine upon you, and be gracious to you:"
4. **[Q] Numbers 6:26** "The LORD lift up his countenance upon you, and give you peace."
5. **[Q] Numbers 6:27** ""So shall they put my name upon the people of Israel, and I will bless them.""

### Play: follow the cloud
- **What it is:** pathfinding (spec §13.7, kind 6).
- **How it plays:** a map of the wilderness, seen from above, with the camp as a little cluster of tents and the cloud as a soft white shape. When the cloud lifts and moves on, the player leads the camp after it (drag, or the arrow keys), along dry valleys and around the rocks, and when it settles, the camp pitches its tents beneath it. Sometimes the cloud stays a day, sometimes many days; when it stays, the camp stays too, and the player waits (the moon rises and sets over it). After a few stages, the cloud settles in the wilderness of Paran.
- **Teaches:** that God led his people day by day, and they moved only when he moved: "At the command of the LORD they encamped, and at the command of the LORD they set out."
- **Gentle mode:** the camp follows the cloud by itself; the player only taps to set out and to pitch the tents.
- **Accessibility:** a pointer, or the arrow keys and Space.

**Thread:** *Aaron lifts his hands and blesses the people: "The LORD bless you and keep you" → Jesus, at his ascension, "lifting up his hands he blessed them."* The glowing object is Aaron's lifted hands.
- **[Q] Luke 24:50:** "Then he led them out as far as Bethany, and lifting up his hands he blessed them."
- **[Q] Luke 24:51:** "While he blessed them, he parted from them and was carried up into heaven."
- **What it shows:** God told Aaron and his sons to bless the people with his own name. The last thing Jesus did on earth was to bless his disciples, with his hands lifted, and he went up to heaven still blessing them. At the end of every Mass, the priest blesses the people in his name, and sends them out. The blessing of Aaron is still prayed in the Church: on the first day of every year, its words are read at Mass.
- **Source:** Luke 24:50–53; Numbers 6:22–27 (the first reading of the Mass of Mary, Mother of God, 1 January); Catechism 1078–1083, 1671.

**Where and when:**
- **The book of Numbers.** It takes its name from the two times the people are counted (Numbers 1 and 26). Its Hebrew name means "In the wilderness."
- **Six hundred thousand.** The count of men over twenty came to 603,550 (Numbers 1:46). Some scholars think the Hebrew word for "thousand" here may mean a smaller group, like a clan.
- **The wilderness of Paran.** The wide, dry land between Sinai and Canaan, where the people would spend much of their forty years.

**Read:** Numbers 6:22–27; 9:15–23; 10:11–36.

## Episode 2 · Complaints and Quail

**Numbers 11 · about 7 minutes · 3 scenes**

### Scene 2.1 · "O that we had meat to eat!"
*On screen:* The camp at Taberah. People sit at the doors of their tents, grumbling, while the manna lies white on the ground around them. They remember Egypt: fish, cucumbers, melons, leeks, onions and garlic float in a dream above their heads.

1. **[Q] Numbers 11:4** "Now the rabble that was among them had a strong craving; and the people of Israel also wept again, and said, "O that we had meat to eat!"
2. **[Q] Numbers 11:5** "We remember the fish we ate in Egypt for nothing, the cucumbers, the melons, the leeks, the onions, and the garlic;"
3. **[Q] Numbers 11:6** "but now our strength is dried up, and there is nothing at all but this manna to look at.""
4. God had fed them every single morning. But they had grown tired of his gift, and forgot how hard their life in Egypt had been.
5. **[Q] Numbers 11:10** "Moses heard the people weeping throughout their families, every man at the door of his tent; and the anger of the LORD blazed hotly, and Moses was displeased."

### Scene 2.2 · The burden too heavy
*On screen:* Moses alone in the tent of meeting, his head in his hands. Then seventy elders stand in a circle around the tent, and the cloud comes down; one by one, a light rests on each of them. *(Play: the spirit on the seventy.)*

1. **[Q] Numbers 11:11** "Moses said to the LORD, "Why hast thou dealt ill with thy servant? And why have I not found favor in thy sight, that thou dost lay the burden of all this people upon me?"
2. **[Q] Numbers 11:14** "I am not able to carry all this people alone, the burden is too heavy for me."
3. **[Q] Numbers 11:16** "And the LORD said to Moses, "Gather for me seventy men of the elders of Israel, whom you know to be the elders of the people and officers over them; and bring them to the tent of meeting, and let them take their stand there with you."
4. **[Q] Numbers 11:17** "And I will come down and talk with you there; and I will take some of the spirit which is upon you and put it upon them; and they shall bear the burden of the people with you, that you may not bear it yourself alone."
5. *(Play: the spirit on the seventy.)*
6. **[Q] Numbers 11:25** "Then the LORD came down in the cloud and spoke to him, and took some of the spirit that was upon him and put it upon the seventy elders; and when the spirit rested upon them, they prophesied. But they did so no more."

### Scene 2.3 · Would that all were prophets
*On screen:* In the camp, far from the tent, two men, Eldad and Medad, glow with the same light and speak God's words to the people around them. A young man runs to Moses, and Joshua is upset. Then a wind blows from the sea, and quail fall all around the camp.

1. **[Q] Numbers 11:26** "Now two men remained in the camp, one named Eldad, and the other named Medad, and the spirit rested upon them; they were among those registered, but they had not gone out to the tent, and so they prophesied in the camp."
2. **[Q] Numbers 11:28** "And Joshua the son of Nun, the minister of Moses, one of his chosen men, said, "My lord Moses, forbid them.""
3. **[Q] Numbers 11:29** "But Moses said to him, "Are you jealous for my sake? Would that all the LORD's people were prophets, that the LORD would put his spirit upon them!""
4. **[Q] Numbers 11:31** "And there went forth a wind from the LORD, and it brought quails from the sea, and let them fall beside the camp, about a day's journey on this side and a day's journey on the other side, round about the camp, and about two cubits above the face of the earth."
5. The people gathered quail all day and all night, more than they could ever eat. But their craving had become greed, and a plague struck the camp. They called the place "the graves of craving," and moved on.

### Play: the spirit on the seventy
- **What it is:** light and reveal (spec §13.7, kind 11).
- **How it plays:** the seventy elders stand in a wide circle around the tent of meeting, in shadow. The cloud comes down over the tent, and the player draws the light out from Moses to each elder in turn (drag from one to the next, or press Space): each one lights up, lifts his head, and is ready to help carry the people. When the circle is complete, two more lights appear far out in the camp: Eldad and Medad, who were not at the tent at all. The player brings the light to them too.
- **Teaches:** that God shares his Spirit, so that no one has to carry everything alone; and that his Spirit is not kept to one place or a few people.
- **Gentle mode:** a tap anywhere lights the next elder.
- **Accessibility:** a pointer, or the arrow keys and Space.

**Thread:** *"Would that all the LORD's people were prophets, that the LORD would put his spirit upon them!" → At Pentecost: "I will pour out my Spirit upon all flesh."* The glowing object is Eldad and Medad, prophesying in the camp.
- **[Q] Acts 2:17:** "'And in the last days it shall be, God declares, that I will pour out my Spirit upon all flesh, and your sons and your daughters shall prophesy, and your young men shall see visions, and your old men shall dream dreams;"
- **[Q] Acts 2:18:** "yea, and on my menservants and my maidservants in those days I will pour out my Spirit; and they shall prophesy."
- **What it shows:** Moses longed for the day when God's Spirit would rest on all his people, not just on seventy elders. At Pentecost, it happened: the Holy Spirit came down on the whole Church, men and women, young and old. Through Baptism and Confirmation, every Christian receives the Spirit and shares in Christ's work as prophet, speaking his word to the world.
- **Source:** Acts 2:1–21 (quoting Joel 2:28–29); Catechism 1268, 1285–1287, 1546.

**Where and when:**
- **Manna.** "Like coriander seed": a small, round, pale seed. The people ground it, boiled it and baked it into cakes that tasted "like cakes baked with oil" (Numbers 11:7–8).
- **Quail.** Small birds that cross the Sinai each spring and autumn, flying low and landing exhausted after crossing the sea. They are still caught there today.
- **Joshua.** Moses' young helper. Here he wants to protect Moses' honor; Moses wants the Spirit for everyone.

**Read:** Numbers 11.

## Episode 3 · The Twelve Spies

**Numbers 13–14 · about 8 minutes · 4 scenes**

### Scene 3.1 · Go up and see the land
*On screen:* The camp at Kadesh, in the wilderness of Paran. Moses chooses twelve men, one from each tribe, and points them north toward the hills of Canaan. He gives Hoshea the son of Nun a new name: Joshua.

1. **[Q] Numbers 13:2** ""Send men to spy out the land of Canaan, which I give to the people of Israel; from each tribe of their fathers shall you send a man, every one a leader among them.""
2. Moses gave one of the twelve, Hoshea, a new name: Joshua, which means "The LORD saves." In Greek, the same name is Jesus.
3. **[Q] Numbers 13:18** "and see what the land is, and whether the people who dwell in it are strong or weak, whether they are few or many,"
4. **[Q] Numbers 13:20** "and whether the land is rich or poor, and whether there is wood in it or not. Be of good courage, and bring some of the fruit of the land." Now the time was the season of the first ripe grapes."

### Scene 3.2 · A single cluster of grapes
*On screen:* The spies move through a green land: hills, olive trees, walled cities on the heights. In the Valley of Eshcol, two of them cut a cluster of grapes so heavy that they carry it on a pole between them, with pomegranates and figs. *(Play: forty days in Canaan.)*

1. **[Q] Numbers 13:21** "So they went up and spied out the land from the wilderness of Zin to Rehob, near the entrance of Hamath."
2. **[Q] Numbers 13:23** "And they came to the Valley of Eshcol, and cut down from there a branch with a single cluster of grapes, and they carried it on a pole between two of them; they brought also some pomegranates and figs."
3. *(Play: forty days in Canaan.)*
4. **[Q] Numbers 13:25** "At the end of forty days they returned from spying out the land."
5. **[Q] Numbers 13:27** "And they told him, "We came to the land to which you sent us; it flows with milk and honey, and this is its fruit."

### Scene 3.3 · Like grasshoppers
*On screen:* Back at Kadesh. The spies show the fruit to the whole people. Then ten of them shake their heads; behind them the walled cities grow huge, and the people of the land loom like giants, while the spies shrink small. Caleb steps forward. That night, the whole camp weeps.

1. **[Q] Numbers 13:28** "Yet the people who dwell in the land are strong, and the cities are fortified and very large; and besides, we saw the descendants of Anak there."
2. **[Q] Numbers 13:30** "But Caleb quieted the people before Moses, and said, "Let us go up at once, and occupy it; for we are well able to overcome it.""
3. **[Q] Numbers 13:31** "Then the men who had gone up with him said, "We are not able to go up against the people; for they are stronger than we.""
4. **[Q] Numbers 13:33** "And there we saw the Nephilim (the sons of Anak, who come from the Nephilim); and we seemed to ourselves like grasshoppers, and so we seemed to them.""
5. **[Q] Numbers 14:1** "Then all the congregation raised a loud cry; and the people wept that night."
6. **[Q] Numbers 14:4** "And they said to one another, "Let us choose a captain, and go back to Egypt.""

### Scene 3.4 · Forty years
*On screen:* Joshua and Caleb tear their clothes and plead with the people, and the people pick up stones. Then the glory of the LORD shines from the tent of meeting, and everyone falls silent. Then: the long road back into the desert, and a new generation of children growing up in the tents.

1. **[Q] Numbers 14:6** "And Joshua the son of Nun and Caleb the son of Jephunneh, who were among those who had spied out the land, rent their clothes,"
2. **[Q] Numbers 14:7** "and said to all the congregation of the people of Israel, "The land, which we passed through to spy it out, is an exceedingly good land."
3. **[Q] Numbers 14:8** "If the LORD delights in us, he will bring us into this land and give it to us, a land which flows with milk and honey."
4. **[Q] Numbers 14:9** "Only, do not rebel against the LORD; and do not fear the people of the land, for they are bread for us; their protection is removed from them, and the LORD is with us; do not fear them.""
5. **[Q] Numbers 14:10** "But all the congregation said to stone them with stones. Then the glory of the LORD appeared at the tent of meeting to all the people of Israel."
6. Moses prayed for the people again, and God forgave them. But those who had refused to trust him would not enter the land.
7. **[Q] Numbers 14:24** "But my servant Caleb, because he has a different spirit and has followed me fully, I will bring into the land into which he went, and his descendants shall possess it."
8. **[Q] Numbers 14:34** "According to the number of the days in which you spied out the land, forty days, for every day a year, you shall bear your iniquity, forty years, and you shall know my displeasure.'"

### Play: forty days in Canaan
- **What it is:** stealth and escape (spec §13.7, kind 17).
- **How it plays:** the hill country of Canaan, from the side, with walled cities on the heights and watchmen on their walls. The player moves the twelve spies from cover to cover (an olive grove, a vineyard wall, a ravine) while the watchmen look the other way, gathering the fruit of the land as they go: grapes, pomegranates, figs. In the Valley of Eshcol, two of them shoulder the great cluster on its pole, and the player brings them all safely home to Kadesh. Forty days pass as moons in the sky.
- **Teaches:** how good the land was that God had promised; and that the spies were sent to see it, not to judge whether God could keep his word.
- **Gentle mode:** the watchmen turn slowly, and the spies wait safely if sent on at the wrong moment.
- **Accessibility:** a pointer, or the arrow keys and Space.

**Thread:** *Joshua and Caleb trust God, but the people will not go in; they wander forty years, "unable to enter because of unbelief" → "There remains a sabbath rest for the people of God."* The glowing object is the cluster of grapes, the fruit of the land they did not enter.
- **[Q] Hebrews 3:19:** "So we see that they were unable to enter because of unbelief."
- **[Q] Hebrews 4:9:** "So then, there remains a sabbath rest for the people of God;"
- **What it shows:** The people saw the fruit of the promised land with their own eyes, and still they would not trust God to bring them in. The letter to the Hebrews tells Christians not to make the same mistake: God has promised us a greater rest, his own rest in heaven, and we enter it by trusting him. Joshua led the next generation into Canaan; Jesus, whose name is the same, leads us into God's rest.
- **Source:** Hebrews 3:7–4:11 (quoting Psalm 95:7–11); Catechism 1720–1721, 2184.

**Where and when:**
- **Kadesh.** An oasis on the southern edge of Canaan, perhaps Ain el-Qudeirat, with the best springs of the northern Sinai. The people would camp around it for many years.
- **The Valley of Eshcol.** Near Hebron. "Eshcol" means "cluster." The hills around Hebron are still famous for their grapes.
- **"Milk and honey."** A land good for flocks (milk) and full of flowers, date palms and bees (honey).
- **The sons of Anak.** A people known for their great height. Later, Caleb himself drove them out of Hebron (Joshua 15:14).

**Read:** Numbers 13–14.

## Episode 4 · Korah's Rebellion and Aaron's Rod

**Numbers 16–17 · about 7 minutes · 3 scenes**

### Scene 4.1 · "You have gone too far!"
*On screen:* Korah, a Levite, stands before Moses and Aaron with two hundred and fifty leaders, all holding censers. They point at Aaron's priestly robes. Then the ground shakes, and Korah's tents vanish into the earth in a cloud of dust, and the people run.

1. **[Q] Numbers 16:3** "and they assembled themselves together against Moses and against Aaron, and said to them, "You have gone too far! For all the congregation are holy, every one of them, and the LORD is among them; why then do you exalt yourselves above the assembly of the LORD?""
2. **[Q] Numbers 16:9** "is it too small a thing for you that the God of Israel has separated you from the congregation of Israel, to bring you near to himself, to do service in the tabernacle of the LORD, and to stand before the congregation to minister to them;"
3. **[Q] Numbers 16:10** "and that he has brought you near him, and all your brethren the sons of Levi with you? And would you seek the priesthood also?"
4. Korah and his men were not content to serve God; they wanted to take for themselves what God had given to Aaron. Moses left it to God to show whom he had chosen.
5. **[Q] Numbers 16:31** "And as he finished speaking all these words, the ground under them split asunder;"

### Scene 4.2 · Between the dead and the living
*On screen:* The next day the people grumble again, and a sickness spreads through the camp like a dark shadow. Aaron runs into the middle of the crowd with a censer of smoking incense and stands still, his arms wide, and the shadow stops at his feet.

1. **[Q] Numbers 16:41** "But on the morrow all the congregation of the people of Israel murmured against Moses and against Aaron, saying, "You have killed the people of the LORD.""
2. **[Q] Numbers 16:46** "And Moses said to Aaron, "Take your censer, and put fire therein from off the altar, and lay incense on it, and carry it quickly to the congregation, and make atonement for them; for wrath has gone forth from the LORD, the plague has begun.""
3. **[Q] Numbers 16:47** "So Aaron took it as Moses said, and ran into the midst of the assembly; and behold, the plague had already begun among the people; and he put on the incense, and made atonement for the people."
4. **[Q] Numbers 16:48** "And he stood between the dead and the living; and the plague was stopped."

### Scene 4.3 · The rod that budded
*On screen:* Twelve dry staffs, each carved with a leader's name, are laid before the ark in the tent. Night falls. In the morning, Moses brings them out, and one of them, Aaron's, is covered with green buds, white blossoms and ripe almonds. *(Play: the twelve rods.)*

1. **[Q] Numbers 17:2** ""Speak to the people of Israel, and get from them rods, one for each fathers' house, from all their leaders according to their fathers' houses, twelve rods. Write each man's name upon his rod,"
2. **[Q] Numbers 17:5** "And the rod of the man whom I choose shall sprout; thus I will make to cease from me the murmurings of the people of Israel, which they murmur against you.""
3. *(Play: the twelve rods.)*
4. **[Q] Numbers 17:8** "And on the morrow Moses went into the tent of the testimony; and behold, the rod of Aaron for the house of Levi had sprouted and put forth buds, and produced blossoms, and it bore ripe almonds."
5. **[Q] Numbers 17:10** "And the LORD said to Moses, "Put back the rod of Aaron before the testimony, to be kept as a sign for the rebels, that you may make an end of their murmurings against me, lest they die.""

### Play: the twelve rods
- **What it is:** matching and naming (spec §13.7, kind 10).
- **How it plays:** the leaders of the twelve tribes come one by one with their staffs, and each staff is marked with its tribe's sign: Judah's lion, Issachar's donkey, Zebulun's ship, Reuben's water, Simeon's tower, Gad's tent, Joseph's fruitful vine, Benjamin's wolf, Dan's scales, Asher's olive, Naphtali's deer, and Levi's, with Aaron's name. The player matches each leader to his tribe's staff (tap, or the arrow keys and Space), and lays it before the ark. Then night falls over the tent; in the morning, Aaron's rod alone has blossomed.
- **Teaches:** the twelve tribes of Israel; and that the priesthood was God's gift, not something anyone could take.
- **Gentle mode:** the right staff glows.
- **Accessibility:** a pointer, or the arrow keys and Space.

**Thread:** *Aaron's dry rod blossoms, to show whom God has chosen as priest → "One does not take the honor upon himself, but he is called by God, just as Aaron was. So also Christ…"* The glowing object is Aaron's rod.
- **[Q] Hebrews 5:4:** "And one does not take the honor upon himself, but he is called by God, just as Aaron was."
- **[Q] Hebrews 5:5:** "So also Christ did not exalt himself to be made a high priest, but was appointed by him who said to him, "Thou art my Son, today I have begotten thee";"
- **[Q] Hebrews 9:4:** "having the golden altar of incense and the ark of the covenant covered on all sides with gold, which contained a golden urn holding the manna, and Aaron's rod that budded, and the tables of the covenant;"
- **What it shows:** Korah wanted to make himself a priest. Jesus, who truly was the Son of God, did not take any glory for himself: the Father called him. Aaron's rod, dead wood that blossomed overnight, was kept in the ark as a sign. The Fathers of the Church saw in it Christ himself, who came to life from the dead wood of the cross, and Mary, from whom, without any human father, came the flower of Jesse's root.
- **Source:** Hebrews 5:1–10; 9:4; Isaiah 11:1; Catechism 1539–1547, 1578.

**Where and when:**
- **The Levites and the priests.** All the tribe of Levi served at the tabernacle, but only Aaron and his sons were priests. Korah was a Levite, a cousin of Moses and Aaron.
- **Almonds.** The almond is the first tree to blossom in the land of Israel, in late winter. Its Hebrew name means "the watcher," the tree that wakes first.
- **The censers.** The bronze of the rebels' censers was beaten into a covering for the altar, as a reminder (Numbers 16:38–40).

**Read:** Numbers 16:1–35; 16:41–17:13.

## Episode 5 · Water at Meribah

**Numbers 20:1–13, 22–29 · about 7 minutes · 3 scenes**

### Scene 5.1 · No water
*On screen:* Kadesh, many years later. A small grave with stones on it: Miriam's. The springs have run dry. Thirsty families with empty jars, children and flocks, crowd angrily around Moses and Aaron.

1. **[Q] Numbers 20:1** "And the people of Israel, the whole congregation, came into the wilderness of Zin in the first month, and the people stayed in Kadesh; and Miriam died there, and was buried there."
2. **[Q] Numbers 20:2** "Now there was no water for the congregation; and they assembled themselves together against Moses and against Aaron."
3. **[Q] Numbers 20:5** "And why have you made us come up out of Egypt, to bring us to this evil place? It is no place for grain, or figs, or vines, or pomegranates; and there is no water to drink.""

### Scene 5.2 · Speak to the rock
*On screen:* Moses and Aaron fall on their faces at the door of the tent, and the glory of the LORD shines on them. Then the whole people gathers before a great rock. Moses lifts his rod and strikes it, once, twice, and water bursts out. *(Play: water for all.)*

### Play: look and live
- **What it is:** a quiet interaction (spec §13.7, kind 20).
- **How it plays:** the camp at dusk, with the bronze serpent lifted high in the middle. Around it, people who were bitten lie in their tents with their eyes down. - **Accessibility:** a pointer, or the arrow keys and Space.

- **The staff of Asclepius.** A snake on a staff became a sign of healing in the Greek world too, and is still a symbol of medicine.

### Play: the narrow way
- **What it is:** steering (spec §13.7, kind 7).
- **How it plays:** the player is the donkey, carrying Balaam along the road, which grows narrower: first open fields, then a lane between vineyard walls, then a narrow pass in the rocks. Balaam grumbles and threatens; the donkey keeps him safe.
- **Teaches:** that God can open the eyes of anyone, even an animal, and that he protects us from paths we cannot see are dangerous. A gentle, funny story: the wise man is the one who is blind.

- **Balaam the son of Beor.** A famous seer from Pethor, on the Euphrates. In 1967, an inscription about "Balaam son of Beor, a seer of the gods" was found at Deir Alla in Jordan, written about 800 BC.
- **The angel of the LORD.** God's messenger, often speaking with God's own voice. He appears to Hagar, to Abraham on Mount Moriah and to Moses at the burning bush.

### Play: a star out of Jacob
- **What it is:** memory (spec §13.7, kind 14).
- **How it plays:** the night sky over the plains of Moab. Balaam's last oracle is written in the stars, but some words are missing. Phrase by phrase, the player chooses the missing words in order ("I see him, but not now; I behold him, but not nigh: a star shall come forth out of Jacob, and a scepter shall rise out of Israel"), and with each one more stars light up, until the bright star rises over the camp. A wrong word gets a gentle "not quite."
- **Teaches:** Balaam's prophecy of the star, the promise of a king from Israel that the wise men followed.
- **Gentle mode:** the right word glows among the choices.
- **Accessibility:** a pointer, or the arrow keys and Space.

**Thread:** *"A star shall come forth out of Jacob, and a scepter shall rise out of Israel" → the wise men: "We have seen his star in the East," and Jesus: "I am… the bright morning star."* The glowing object is the star.
- **[Q] Matthew 2:1:** "Now when Jesus was born in Bethlehem of Judea in the days of Herod the king, behold, wise men from the East came to Jerusalem, saying,"
- **[Q] Matthew 2:2:** ""Where is he who has been born king of the Jews? For we have seen his star in the East, and have come to worship him.""
- **[Q] Revelation 22:16:** ""I Jesus have sent my angel to you with this testimony for the churches. I am the root and the offspring of David, the bright morning star.""
- **What it shows:** A prophet from the east, who did not even belong to God's people, was made to see the King who would come from Israel. Centuries later, wise men from the east saw his star and came to worship him: the first of all the nations to come to Jesus. At the very end of the Bible, Jesus calls himself "the bright morning star," the star that rises before the dawn of the day that will never end.
- **Source:** Matthew 2:1–12; Revelation 22:16; 2 Peter 1:19; Catechism 528.

**Where and when:**
- **Seven altars.** Balak built seven altars on each hilltop and offered a bull and a ram on each, hoping that a better view, or more offerings, would change God's mind (Numbers 23:1–3, 13–14, 27–30).
- **The scepter.** The staff a king holds as a sign of his rule. Jacob had promised that "the scepter shall not depart from Judah" (Genesis 49:10; Level 5).
- **What became of Balaam?** He did not go home a friend of God: he later taught Moab how to lead Israel into sin, and died in battle (Numbers 31:8, 16; Revelation 2:14).

**Read:** Numbers 23–24.
