# Level 5 script: *Joseph and His Brothers*

**Bible:** Genesis 37–50
**Age:** II · The Patriarchs
**Length:** about 55 minutes (7 episodes, then the quiz, then reflection and prayer)
**Status:** approved by Dave on 2026-10-20 ("Approved").

## How to read this script

- Each **scene** is a checkpoint. Its numbered lines are the narration beats; the player taps to move from one to the next.
- *On screen* describes what the scene shows. It's the brief for the art in both styles.
- **[Q]** marks an exact quotation from the Bible. Its wording in both translations is in the verse table at the end. Everything else is our own retelling.
- **Play** is the episode's interactive moment. **Thread** is its golden thread. **Read** is the passage the game sends the player to.
- "LORD" in capitals stands for God's holy name, printed in small capitals in the RSV.
- The RSV calls Pharaoh's cupbearer his "butler." The script uses the RSV's word.

### What this level is about

Level 4 ended with the twelve sons of Jacob standing together. Level 5 tells what became of the favorite of them all. Joseph, the dreamer in the long robe, is hated by his brothers and sold for twenty pieces of silver. A slave in Egypt, then a prisoner, he stays faithful, and the LORD is with him. He reads the dreams of Pharaoh's servants and of Pharaoh himself, and in a single day rises from the dungeon to rule all Egypt. When famine comes, his brothers come to buy grain from the brother they sold, and bow before him without knowing him. Joseph tests them, and finds them changed: Judah offers himself as a slave in place of the youngest. Then Joseph weeps, and says, "I am Joseph." Jacob comes down to Egypt to see his son again, and before he dies, he blesses his twelve sons, the twelve tribes of Israel.

The Church has always seen in Joseph a picture of Jesus: the beloved son, rejected by his own and sold for silver, counted with criminals, then raised to the right hand of the king, giving bread to a starving world and forgiving those who wronged him. "You meant evil against me; but God meant it for good." The threads point to Judas's thirty pieces of silver, the good thief, Mary at Cana, the Bread of life, the greater love of the cross, "Father, forgive them," and the kingdom of the Son of David, which will have no end.

## Episode 1 · Sold for Silver

**Genesis 37 · about 8 minutes · 4 scenes**

> **Content note shown before this episode:** "In this episode Joseph's brothers turn against him: they throw him into an empty pit and sell him. It is told gently, and no one is shown being hurt."

### Scene 1.1 · The long robe
*On screen:* Jacob's tents near Hebron. Jacob, old now, puts a beautiful long robe on young Joseph. Behind them, ten brothers watch, scowling, then turn away.

1. **[Q] Genesis 37:3** "Now Israel loved Joseph more than any other of his children, because he was the son of his old age; and he made him a long robe with sleeves."
2. **[Q] Genesis 37:4** "But when his brothers saw that their father loved him more than all his brothers, they hated him, and could not speak peaceably to him."

### Scene 1.2 · The dreams
*On screen:* A golden field of wheat at harvest: eleven sheaves bow down around one standing upright. Then a night sky: the sun, the moon and eleven stars bowing to one star.

1. **[Q] Genesis 37:5** "Now Joseph had a dream, and when he told it to his brothers they only hated him the more."
2. **[Q] Genesis 37:7** "behold, we were binding sheaves in the field, and lo, my sheaf arose and stood upright; and behold, your sheaves gathered round it, and bowed down to my sheaf.""
3. **[Q] Genesis 37:8** "His brothers said to him, "Are you indeed to reign over us? Or are you indeed to have dominion over us?" So they hated him yet more for his dreams and for his words."
4. **[Q] Genesis 37:9** "Then he dreamed another dream, and told it to his brothers, and said, "Behold, I have dreamed another dream; and behold, the sun, the moon, and eleven stars were bowing down to me.""
5. **[Q] Genesis 37:11** "And his brothers were jealous of him, but his father kept the saying in mind."

### Scene 1.3 · "I am seeking my brothers"
*On screen:* Joseph in his long robe sets off north alone. Open, rolling fields near Shechem, empty of flocks. A man in the field points the way. *(Play: to Dothan.)*

1. **[Q] Genesis 37:13** "And Israel said to Joseph, "Are not your brothers pasturing the flock at Shechem? Come, I will send you to them." And he said to him, "Here I am.""
2. **[Q] Genesis 37:15** "And a man found him wandering in the fields; and the man asked him, "What are you seeking?""
3. **[Q] Genesis 37:16** ""I am seeking my brothers," he said, "tell me, I pray you, where they are pasturing the flock.""
4. **[Q] Genesis 37:17** "And the man said, "They have gone away, for I heard them say, 'Let us go to Dothan.'" So Joseph went after his brothers, and found them at Dothan."
5. *(Play: to Dothan.)*

### Scene 1.4 · Twenty shekels of silver
*On screen:* The brothers by their flocks at Dothan see the long robe coming, far off. They pull it off Joseph and lower him into a dry pit. A camel caravan of traders comes along the road; silver changes hands. Later, Jacob holds the torn robe and weeps.

1. **[Q] Genesis 37:18** "They saw him afar off, and before he came near to them they conspired against him to kill him."
2. **[Q] Genesis 37:19** "They said to one another, "Here comes this dreamer."
3. **[Q] Genesis 37:21** "But when Reuben heard it, he delivered him out of their hands, saying, "Let us not take his life.""
4. **[Q] Genesis 37:24** "and they took him and cast him into a pit. The pit was empty, there was no water in it."
5. **[Q] Genesis 37:28** "Then Midianite traders passed by; and they drew Joseph up and lifted him out of the pit, and sold him to the Ishmaelites for twenty shekels of silver; and they took Joseph to Egypt."
6. The brothers dipped Joseph's robe in a goat's blood and brought it to their father, and let him believe that a wild animal had killed his son.
7. **[Q] Genesis 37:35** "All his sons and all his daughters rose up to comfort him; but he refused to be comforted, and said, "No, I shall go down to Sheol to my son, mourning." Thus his father wept for him."

### Play: to Dothan
- **What it is:** pathfinding (spec §13.7, kind 6).
- **How it plays:** a map of the hill country seen from above, like a cloth map: fields, walls, olive groves and streams between Shechem and Dothan. The player leads Joseph along the paths toward the faint dust of his brothers' flocks in the north. Signs on the way help: a shepherd's fire still warm, hoofprints, a lost lamb. A wall or a stream that can't be crossed turns him back gently. When he reaches Dothan, the scene goes on.
- **Teaches:** Joseph's obedience to his father, searching for his brothers when they had gone away.
- **Gentle mode:** the right path glows, and Joseph follows it with a tap.
- **Accessibility:** a pointer (tap where to walk), or the arrow keys; Space walks to the next sign.

**Thread:** *Joseph sold for silver by his own brothers → Jesus betrayed for thirty pieces of silver.* The glowing object is the silver paid for Joseph.
- **[Q] Matthew 26:15:** "and said, "What will you give me if I deliver him to you?" And they paid him thirty pieces of silver."
- **What it shows:** Joseph, the beloved son sent by his father to his brothers, was rejected by them and sold for silver. Jesus, the beloved Son sent by the Father, came to his own, and one of his own sold him for silver. In both stories the betrayal did not have the last word: God turned it into salvation for many.
- **Source:** Matthew 26:14–16; John 1:11; Acts 7:9; Catechism 597–598.

**Where and when:**
- **When.** Joseph's time: roughly 1700–1600 BC, as scholars estimate.
- **Dothan.** A town on the trade road between the hills of Samaria and the coast, about 25 kilometers north of Shechem. Caravans of camels carried spices that way down to Egypt.
- **Twenty shekels.** About 230 grams of silver: the price of a young slave in those days.

**Read:** Genesis 37.

## Episode 2 · Faithful in Potiphar's House and in Prison

**Genesis 39–40 · about 8 minutes · 4 scenes**

> **Content note shown before this episode:** "In this episode Joseph is falsely accused and put in prison, and one of the prisoners he meets is put to death. It is told gently, and nothing is shown."

### Scene 2.1 · In Potiphar's house
*On screen:* Egypt: a great house of white plaster and painted columns by the Nile. Joseph, now in a short Egyptian kilt, oversees servants carrying jars and baskets. Potiphar, an officer in a striped headcloth, hands him a ring of keys.

1. **[Q] Genesis 39:1** "Now Joseph was taken down to Egypt, and Poti-phar, an officer of Pharaoh, the captain of the guard, an Egyptian, bought him from the Ishmaelites who had brought him down there."
2. **[Q] Genesis 39:2** "The LORD was with Joseph, and he became a successful man; and he was in the house of his master the Egyptian,"
3. **[Q] Genesis 39:5** "From the time that he made him overseer in his house and over all that he had the LORD blessed the Egyptian's house for Joseph's sake; the blessing of the LORD was upon all that he had, in house and field."

### Scene 2.2 · "How can I sin against God?"
*On screen:* A courtyard at dusk. Joseph turns away and walks quickly out, leaving his cloak behind. Then guards lead him to a dark prison of mud brick.

1. Potiphar's wife wanted Joseph to do wrong with her, behind her husband's back. Day after day she asked him, and day after day he refused.
2. **[Q] Genesis 39:9** "he is not greater in this house than I am; nor has he kept back anything from me except yourself, because you are his wife; how then can I do this great wickedness, and sin against God?""
3. One day he ran from her, leaving his cloak in her hand. She lied, and said Joseph had attacked her.
4. **[Q] Genesis 39:20** "And Joseph's master took him and put him into the prison, the place where the king's prisoners were confined, and he was there in prison."

### Scene 2.3 · The LORD was with him in prison
*On screen:* Inside the prison: thin light through a high window, prisoners sitting against the walls. Joseph moves among them with bread and a water jar. The keeper watches, then hands him the keys. *(Play: caring for the prisoners.)*

1. **[Q] Genesis 39:21** "But the LORD was with Joseph and showed him steadfast love, and gave him favor in the sight of the keeper of the prison."
2. **[Q] Genesis 39:22** "And the keeper of the prison committed to Joseph's care all the prisoners who were in the prison; and whatever was done there, he was the doer of it;"
3. *(Play: caring for the prisoners.)*
4. Joseph had done nothing wrong. He could have been bitter. Instead, he served.

### Scene 2.4 · Two dreams
*On screen:* Two new prisoners, finely dressed: Pharaoh's butler, with a golden cup on his belt, and Pharaoh's baker. They sit troubled. Above them, faintly, their dreams: a vine with three branches of grapes; three baskets of bread with birds pecking at them.

1. **[Q] Genesis 40:8** "They said to him, "We have had dreams, and there is no one to interpret them." And Joseph said to them, "Do not interpretations belong to God? Tell them to me, I pray you.""
2. The butler had dreamed of a vine, and of squeezing its grapes into Pharaoh's cup. The baker had dreamed of birds eating Pharaoh's bread from the baskets on his head.
3. God showed Joseph the meaning: in three days the butler would be set free and given back his work. But the baker would die.
4. **[Q] Genesis 40:14** "But remember me, when it is well with you, and do me the kindness, I pray you, to make mention of me to Pharaoh, and so get me out of this house."
5. **[Q] Genesis 40:21** "He restored the chief butler to his butlership, and he placed the cup in Pharaoh's hand;"
6. **[Q] Genesis 40:23** "Yet the chief butler did not remember Joseph, but forgot him."

### Play: caring for the prisoners
- **What it is:** serving and distributing (spec §13.7, kind 19).
- **How it plays:** the dim prison, with six prisoners along the walls. Each shows what he needs as a small picture above him: bread, water, a blanket, a lamp. Joseph carries a tray; the player takes the right thing from the tray to each prisoner. As each is cared for, his face lifts and a little light comes into the room. The last two prisoners are the butler and the baker, troubled by their dreams, and the scene goes on.
- **Teaches:** faithfulness in small things, even when life is unfair; Jesus' words, "I was in prison and you came to me."
- **Gentle mode:** the thing each prisoner needs glows on the tray.
- **Accessibility:** a pointer (drag, or tap the thing and then the prisoner), or the arrow keys to choose and Space to give.

**Thread:** *Two prisoners beside Joseph, one restored and one not, and "remember me" → the two criminals beside Jesus, and "Jesus, remember me."* The glowing object is Pharaoh's cup in the butler's hand.
- **[Q] Luke 23:42:** "And he said, "Jesus, remember me when you come in your kingly power.""
- **[Q] Luke 23:43:** "And he said to him, "Truly, I say to you, today you will be with me in Paradise.""
- **What it shows:** Joseph, innocent, was counted among criminals, with one man on each side: one set free, one put to death. He asked the butler, "remember me," and the butler forgot him. Jesus, innocent, died between two criminals. One of them asked, "Jesus, remember me," and Jesus did not forget: "Today you will be with me in Paradise."
- **Source:** Luke 23:32–43; Isaiah 53:12; Catechism 1021.

**Where and when:**
- **Potiphar.** His title, "captain of the guard," may mean the chief of Pharaoh's bodyguards, or the official in charge of the royal prisons.
- **The butler.** Pharaoh's cupbearer tasted his wine and poured it for him. He had to be someone the king could trust with his life.
- **Dreams in Egypt.** Egyptians wrote books to explain dreams. Joseph didn't use them: "Do not interpretations belong to God?"

**Read:** Genesis 39–40.

## Episode 3 · Pharaoh's Dreams

**Genesis 41 · about 8 minutes · 4 scenes**

### Scene 3.1 · Two years later
*On screen:* Pharaoh's bedchamber by night; then his dream over the Nile: seven fat cows coming up out of the river, and seven thin ones eating them up; seven full ears of grain on one stalk, and seven thin ears swallowing them. In the morning, the magicians shake their heads.

1. **[Q] Genesis 41:1** "After two whole years, Pharaoh dreamed that he was standing by the Nile,"
2. Out of the river came seven fat, healthy cows, and after them seven thin, ugly cows, and the thin cows ate up the fat ones. Then he dreamed of seven full, good ears of grain, and seven thin ears that swallowed them up.
3. **[Q] Genesis 41:8** "So in the morning his spirit was troubled; and he sent and called for all the magicians of Egypt and all its wise men; and Pharaoh told them his dream, but there was none who could interpret it to Pharaoh."
4. **[Q] Genesis 41:9** "Then the chief butler said to Pharaoh, "I remember my faults today."

### Scene 3.2 · Before Pharaoh
*On screen:* Pharaoh's throne hall: painted pillars, Pharaoh on his throne with the double crown. Joseph, shaved and in clean linen, stands before him. *(Play: Pharaoh's dreams.)*

1. **[Q] Genesis 41:14** "Then Pharaoh sent and called Joseph, and they brought him hastily out of the dungeon; and when he had shaved himself and changed his clothes, he came in before Pharaoh."
2. **[Q] Genesis 41:16** "Joseph answered Pharaoh, "It is not in me; God will give Pharaoh a favorable answer.""
3. *(Play: Pharaoh's dreams.)*
4. **[Q] Genesis 41:25** "Then Joseph said to Pharaoh, "The dream of Pharaoh is one; God has revealed to Pharaoh what he is about to do."
5. **[Q] Genesis 41:29** "There will come seven years of great plenty throughout all the land of Egypt,"
6. **[Q] Genesis 41:30** "but after them there will arise seven years of famine, and all the plenty will be forgotten in the land of Egypt; the famine will consume the land,"

### Scene 3.3 · Over all the land of Egypt
*On screen:* Pharaoh takes the ring from his finger and puts it on Joseph's hand. Joseph in fine white linen, a gold chain around his neck, rides out in a chariot through the crowds, who kneel as he passes.

1. Joseph told Pharaoh to choose a wise man, and store up grain in the good years against the years of famine.
2. **[Q] Genesis 41:38** "And Pharaoh said to his servants, "Can we find such a man as this, in whom is the Spirit of God?""
3. **[Q] Genesis 41:41** "And Pharaoh said to Joseph, "Behold, I have set you over all the land of Egypt.""
4. **[Q] Genesis 41:42** "Then Pharaoh took his signet ring from his hand and put it on Joseph's hand, and arrayed him in garments of fine linen, and put a gold chain about his neck;"
5. **[Q] Genesis 41:43** "and he made him to ride in his second chariot; and they cried before him, "Bow the knee!" Thus he set him over all the land of Egypt."
6. Joseph was thirty years old. Thirteen years had passed since his brothers sold him.

### Scene 3.4 · "Go to Joseph"
*On screen:* Seven golden harvests: grain carried in, poured into great round storehouses. Joseph with his wife and two little sons. Then the fields dry and crack; hungry crowds gather at Pharaoh's palace, and Pharaoh points them to Joseph.

1. **[Q] Genesis 41:49** "And Joseph stored up grain in great abundance, like the sand of the sea, until he ceased to measure it, for it could not be measured."
2. **[Q] Genesis 41:52** "The name of the second he called Ephraim, "For God has made me fruitful in the land of my affliction.""
3. **[Q] Genesis 41:54** "and the seven years of famine began to come, as Joseph had said. There was famine in all lands; but in all the land of Egypt there was bread."
4. **[Q] Genesis 41:55** "When all the land of Egypt was famished, the people cried to Pharaoh for bread; and Pharaoh said to all the Egyptians, "Go to Joseph; what he says to you, do.""
5. **[Q] Genesis 41:57** "Moreover, all the earth came to Egypt to Joseph to buy grain, because the famine was severe over all the earth."

### Play: Pharaoh's dreams
- **What it is:** decoding (spec §13.7, kind 18).
- **How it plays:** the pictures of Pharaoh's dreams float in the throne hall, one after another: seven fat cows, seven thin cows, seven full ears, seven thin ears, and the dream that came twice. For each, the player chooses what God was showing: "seven years of plenty," "seven years of famine," "God has decided it, and will do it soon." A wrong choice gets a gentle word and another try, and Joseph's words, "It is not in me," remind the player that the meaning comes from God.
- **Teaches:** that God is the Lord of history and makes known what he will do; wisdom that prepares in good times for bad.
- **Gentle mode:** the right meaning glows.
- **Accessibility:** a pointer, or the arrow keys and Space to choose.

**Thread:** *"Go to Joseph; what he says to you, do" → Mary at Cana: "Do whatever he tells you."* The glowing object is Pharaoh's ring on Joseph's hand.
- **[Q] John 2:5:** "His mother said to the servants, "Do whatever he tells you.""
- **What it shows:** When Egypt had no bread, Pharaoh sent the hungry to Joseph: "what he says to you, do." When the wedding at Cana had no wine, Mary sent the servants to Jesus with almost the same words: "Do whatever he tells you." They obeyed, and the water became wine. Mary still points us to her Son. (The Church also uses Pharaoh's words, "Go to Joseph," for St Joseph, the guardian of the Holy Family.)
- **Source:** John 2:1–11; Catechism 2618; Pope Leo XIII, *Quamquam pluries* (1889).

**Where and when:**
- **Pharaoh.** The title means "great house," the palace. Genesis doesn't give this Pharaoh's name.
- **The Nile.** Egypt's harvests depended on the river's yearly flood. A few years of low floods meant famine, and Egyptian records tell of such famines.
- **The signet ring.** It carried Pharaoh's seal. Whoever wore it could sign and command with Pharaoh's authority.

**Read:** Genesis 41.

## Episode 4 · The Brothers Come for Grain

**Genesis 42–43 · about 8 minutes · 4 scenes**

### Scene 4.1 · Grain in Egypt
*On screen:* Jacob's tents in Canaan, the fields dry and bare. Old Jacob speaks to his sons; ten of them load empty sacks onto donkeys. Young Benjamin stays at his father's side.

1. **[Q] Genesis 42:2** "And he said, "Behold, I have heard that there is grain in Egypt; go down and buy grain for us there, that we may live, and not die.""
2. **[Q] Genesis 42:4** "But Jacob did not send Benjamin, Joseph's brother, with his brothers, for he feared that harm might befall him."

### Scene 4.2 · They bowed themselves before him
*On screen:* The great storehouse hall in Egypt, crowded with foreigners buying grain. The ten brothers, in their Canaanite cloaks, bow with their faces to the ground before the governor in white linen. The governor is Joseph. They don't know him.

1. **[Q] Genesis 42:6** "Now Joseph was governor over the land; he it was who sold to all the people of the land. And Joseph's brothers came, and bowed themselves before him with their faces to the ground."
2. **[Q] Genesis 42:8** "Thus Joseph knew his brothers, but they did not know him."
3. **[Q] Genesis 42:9** "And Joseph remembered the dreams which he had dreamed of them; and he said to them, "You are spies, you have come to see the weakness of the land.""
4. Joseph wanted to know if his brothers had changed. He kept Simeon in prison, and told the others to come back with their youngest brother, Benjamin.

### Scene 4.3 · "We are guilty concerning our brother"
*On screen:* The brothers talk together in low voices. Joseph, who understands every word, turns aside and wipes his eyes. Servants fill the brothers' sacks with grain and tuck a bag of silver into the top of each one. *(Play: filling the sacks.)*

1. **[Q] Genesis 42:21** "Then they said to one another, "In truth we are guilty concerning our brother, in that we saw the distress of his soul, when he besought us and we would not listen; therefore is this distress come upon us.""
2. **[Q] Genesis 42:24** "Then he turned away from them and wept; and he returned to them and spoke to them. And he took Simeon from them and bound him before their eyes."
3. *(Play: filling the sacks.)*
4. **[Q] Genesis 42:25** "And Joseph gave orders to fill their bags with grain, and to replace every man's money in his sack, and to give them provisions for the journey. This was done for them."
5. **[Q] Genesis 42:28** "and he said to his brothers, "My money has been put back; here it is in the mouth of my sack!" At this their hearts failed them, and they turned trembling to one another, saying, "What is this that God has done to us?""

### Scene 4.4 · Benjamin
*On screen:* Back in Canaan, the grain runs out. Judah kneels before his father with his hand on his heart. Then the brothers stand again before Joseph in Egypt, Benjamin among them. Joseph hurries out of the room and weeps alone. Then a feast: Benjamin's plate piled high.

1. **[Q] Genesis 43:1** "Now the famine was severe in the land."
2. Jacob would not let Benjamin go. Then Judah, the brother who had once wanted to sell Joseph, stood up for his youngest brother.
3. **[Q] Genesis 43:9** "I will be surety for him; of my hand you shall require him. If I do not bring him back to you and set him before you, then let me bear the blame for ever;"
4. **[Q] Genesis 43:30** "Then Joseph made haste, for his heart yearned for his brother, and he sought a place to weep. And he entered his chamber and wept there."
5. **[Q] Genesis 43:34** "Portions were taken to them from Joseph's table, but Benjamin's portion was five times as much as any of theirs. So they drank and were merry with him."

### Play: filling the sacks
- **What it is:** building and stacking (spec §13.7, kind 3).
- **How it plays:** a storehouse heaped with grain, a row of empty sacks, and ten donkeys waiting. The player scoops grain into each sack until it is full (a hold fills it; the sack glows when it's full), drops in the brother's silver, and lifts the sack onto his donkey. The sacks stack two to a donkey. When the last donkey is loaded, the brothers set off for home.
- **Teaches:** Joseph's generosity to the brothers who had wronged him; bread given freely.
- **Gentle mode:** each sack fills and loads itself with one tap.
- **Accessibility:** a pointer, or Space: hold to fill, press to load.

**Thread:** *Grain from Joseph for a starving world → "I am the bread of life."* The glowing object is the brothers' sacks of grain.
- **[Q] John 6:35:** "Jesus said to them, "I am the bread of life; he who comes to me shall not hunger, and he who believes in me shall never thirst."
- **What it shows:** In the famine, the whole world came to Joseph for bread, even the brothers who had sold him, and he gave it to them, and secretly gave back their money too. Jesus gives himself as bread for the whole world, freely, to all who come to him: in the Eucharist, the Bread of life.
- **Source:** John 6:32–35; Matthew 6:11; Catechism 1338, 1406.

**Where and when:**
- **The journey.** From Hebron to the cities of the Nile delta is about 400 kilometers: two or three weeks with loaded donkeys.
- **Storehouses.** Egyptian paintings show round granaries with ladders, filled from the top and emptied through a door at the bottom.
- **Speaking through an interpreter.** Joseph spoke to his brothers through an interpreter, so they didn't know he understood them (Genesis 42:23).

**Read:** Genesis 42–43.

## Episode 5 · Benjamin and the Silver Cup

**Genesis 44 · about 7 minutes · 3 scenes**

### Scene 5.1 · The cup in the sack
*On screen:* Night in Joseph's house. The steward fills the brothers' sacks, and, as Joseph points, slips a shining silver cup into the top of the youngest one's sack. At dawn, the brothers ride out happily; soon, horsemen gallop after them.

1. **[Q] Genesis 44:1** "Then he commanded the steward of his house, "Fill the men's sacks with food, as much as they can carry, and put each man's money in the mouth of his sack,"
2. **[Q] Genesis 44:2** "and put my cup, the silver cup, in the mouth of the sack of the youngest, with his money for the grain." And he did as Joseph told him."
3. The brothers had not gone far when the steward caught up with them. Why had they returned evil for good, he asked, and stolen his lord's silver cup?
4. The brothers were sure they were innocent. If the cup was found with any of them, they said, he should die, and the rest would become slaves.

### Scene 5.2 · The search
*On screen:* By the roadside, eleven sacks in a row, from the biggest to the smallest. The steward opens them one by one. *(Play: the silver cup.)* At the last sack, Benjamin's, the cup gleams. The brothers tear their cloaks.

1. *(Play: the silver cup.)*
2. **[Q] Genesis 44:12** "And he searched, beginning with the eldest and ending with the youngest; and the cup was found in Benjamin's sack."
3. **[Q] Genesis 44:13** "Then they rent their clothes, and every man loaded his ass, and they returned to the city."
4. This was the test. More than twenty years before, they had let their father's favorite son be carried off as a slave. Would they do it again? They could have gone home without Benjamin. Not one of them did.

### Scene 5.3 · Judah's plea
*On screen:* Back in Joseph's hall. The brothers on their faces before Joseph. Judah rises and steps forward alone, his hands open, and stands between Joseph and Benjamin.

1. **[Q] Genesis 44:16** "And Judah said, "What shall we say to my lord? What shall we speak? Or how can we clear ourselves? God has found out the guilt of your servants; behold, we are my lord's slaves, both we and he also in whose hand the cup has been found.""
2. **[Q] Genesis 44:18** "Then Judah went up to him and said, "O my lord, let your servant, I pray you, speak a word in my lord's ears, and let not your anger burn against your servant; for you are like Pharaoh himself."
3. **[Q] Genesis 44:30** "Now therefore, when I come to your servant my father, and the lad is not with us, then, as his life is bound up in the lad's life,"
4. **[Q] Genesis 44:33** "Now therefore, let your servant, I pray you, remain instead of the lad as a slave to my lord; and let the lad go back with his brothers."
5. **[Q] Genesis 44:34** "For how can I go back to my father if the lad is not with me? I fear to see the evil that would come upon my father.""

### Play: the silver cup
- **What it is:** searching (spec §13.7, kind 8). The spec names this very story: Joseph's cup.
- **How it plays:** eleven sacks stand in a row by the road, from Reuben's, the eldest, to Benjamin's, the youngest. The steward searched "beginning with the eldest and ending with the youngest"; the player opens the sacks in that order. Each holds grain and a little bag of silver, and each brother sighs with relief, until the last. A sack opened out of order gets a gentle word: "The steward began with the eldest." When the cup is found in Benjamin's sack, everyone falls silent.
- **Teaches:** how the brothers were tested, and the tension of the moment.
- **Gentle mode:** the next sack to open glows.
- **Accessibility:** a pointer, or the arrow keys to choose a sack and Space to open it.

**Thread:** *Judah offers himself as a slave in place of his brother → Jesus, of the tribe of Judah, lays down his life for us.* The glowing object is Judah, standing between Joseph and Benjamin.
- **[Q] John 15:13:** "Greater love has no man than this, that a man lay down his life for his friends."
- **What it shows:** Judah, who once sold his brother, now offers his own freedom so that his youngest brother can go home. It is the first time in the Bible that someone offers to take another's punishment in his place. Jesus, the Son of David from the tribe of Judah, did that fully: he gave his life in our place, so that we can go home to the Father.
- **Source:** John 15:12–14; 1 Peter 3:18; Hebrews 7:14; Catechism 615–616.

**Where and when:**

### Play: come near to me
- **What it is:** a quiet interaction (spec §13.7, kind 20).
- **How it plays:** Joseph stands with his arms open; his eleven brothers stand back in fear, heads down. The player taps each brother (or presses Space) to help him take a step closer, and as each comes near, his head lifts. Some hang back and need two steps. When all eleven stand close, Benjamin last, Joseph embraces him, and the scene goes on.
- **Teaches:** forgiveness that reaches out first; the courage to come near to someone you have hurt.
- **Gentle mode:** the brothers come near on their own, one after another.
- **Accessibility:** a pointer, or Space for each step.

**Thread:** *Joseph forgives the brothers who sold him → Jesus on the cross: "Father, forgive them."* The glowing object is Joseph's open arms.
- **[Q] Luke 23:34:** "And Jesus said, "Father, forgive them; for they know not what they do." And they cast lots to divide his garments."
- **What it shows:** Joseph could have punished his brothers. Instead he wept, called them near, and told them not to be afraid. Jesus, nailed to the cross by those he came to save, prayed for them: "Father, forgive them." Joseph's forgiveness is a picture of the mercy God offers everyone, and of how we are to forgive those who hurt us.
- **Source:** Luke 23:34; Matthew 5:44; Matthew 6:12; Catechism 2842–2845.

**Where and when:**
- **Goshen.** A green region in the eastern Nile delta, good for flocks. Jacob's family settled there, apart from the Egyptian cities.
- **Seventy.** Genesis counts seventy of Jacob's family who came down into Egypt (Genesis 46:27). From these few, a nation would grow.
- **Wagons.** Carts were rare in Canaan's hills; Egyptian wagons proved to Jacob that his son really was a great lord in Egypt.

**Read:** Genesis 45:1–46:7; 46:28–30.

### Play: the twelve blessings
- **What it is:** matching and naming (spec §13.7, kind 10). The spec names "the twelve tribes" for this kind.
- **How it plays:** Jacob's twelve sons stand in a row around his bed. One by one, a picture from Jacob's blessings rises: a lion (Judah), a ship by the shore (Zebulun), a strong donkey (Issachar), a serpent by the road (Dan), a deer let loose (Naphtali), a fruitful bough over a wall (Joseph), a wolf (Benjamin), rich bread (Asher). The player gives each picture to the son it belongs to, with a hint from Jacob's words when it doesn't fit ("Judah is a lion's whelp"). The four brothers without a picture (Reuben, Simeon, Levi and Gad) are named as the play begins.
- **Teaches:** the twelve tribes of Israel, the people who will carry God's promise.
- **Gentle mode:** the son who fits glows.
- **Accessibility:** a pointer, or the arrow keys to choose and Space to give.

**Thread:** *"The scepter shall not depart from Judah, until he comes to whom it belongs" → the Son of David, whose kingdom will have no end.* The glowing object is the lion over Judah.
- **[Q] Luke 1:32:** "He will be great, and will be called the Son of the Most High; and the Lord God will give to him the throne of his father David,"
- **[Q] Luke 1:33:** "and he will reign over the house of Jacob for ever; and of his kingdom there will be no end.""
- **What it shows:** Jacob blessed Judah with a king's scepter "until he comes to whom it belongs." From Judah came King David, and from David, many kings. But the angel Gabriel told Mary that her son would have the throne of David and reign "over the house of Jacob for ever." The one to whom the scepter belongs is Jesus, the Lion of the tribe of Judah.
- **Source:** Luke 1:31–33; Revelation 5:5; Hebrews 7:14; Catechism 436, 439.

**Where and when:**
- **The twelve tribes.** Jacob's sons became the tribes of Israel, though Joseph's line became two tribes, Ephraim and Manasseh, and Levi became the tribe of priests.
- **Embalming.** Egyptians preserved the bodies of important people with oils and linen wrappings, over forty days (Genesis 50:3). Joseph was honored as a great Egyptian.
- **Joseph's bones.** When Israel left Egypt, Moses took them along (Exodus 13:19), and they were buried at last at Shechem (Joshua 24:32).

**Read:** Genesis 48:1–49:33; 50:15–26.
