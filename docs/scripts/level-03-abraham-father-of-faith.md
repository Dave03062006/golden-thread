# Level 3 script: *Abraham, Father of Faith*

**Bible:** Genesis 12–23
**Age:** II · The Patriarchs (Level 3 opens it)
**Length:** about 65 minutes (10 episodes, then the quiz, then reflection and prayer)
**Status:** draft, waiting for Dave's approval.

## How to read this script

- Each **scene** is a checkpoint. Its numbered lines are the narration beats; the player taps to move from one to the next.
- *On screen* describes what the scene shows. It's the brief for the art in both styles.
- **[Q]** marks an exact quotation from the Bible. Its wording in both translations is in the verse table at the end. Everything else is our own retelling.
- **Play** is the episode's interactive moment. **Thread** is its golden thread. **Read** is the passage the game sends the player to.
- "LORD" in capitals stands for God's holy name, printed in small capitals in the RSV.

### What this level is about

Level 2 ended at night in Haran, with a voice calling Abram. Level 3 is his answer. Abram leaves everything he knows for a land he has never seen, trusting a promise that seems impossible: a great nation, from an old man and a barren wife. He walks with God for a hundred years, sometimes failing, often waiting, always coming back to the altar. He gives Lot the better land, rescues him, is blessed by a priest who brings bread and wine, and counts the stars. God changes his name and Sarai's. Strangers come to his tent, and one of them is the LORD. He pleads for Sodom. At last, laughter: Isaac is born. And then the hardest test of all, on a mountain called Moriah, where God provides the lamb.

Abraham is the father of all who believe. The threads point to the Eucharist, Baptism, the Church, the Annunciation, Christ who intercedes for us, and the Lamb of God who carried the wood of his own sacrifice up a hill near that same mountain.

Two episodes carry a content note: Sodom and Gomorrah (Episode 8) and the binding of Isaac (Episode 10). Both are told honestly and shown with restraint.

## Episode 1 · The Call

**Genesis 12:1–9 · about 6 minutes · 4 scenes**

### Scene 1.1 · Go from your country
*On screen:* Haran at dawn, picking up from Level 2: Abram's tent on the hill, the city walls below, the moon temple's tower faint in the mist. Abram stands outside with his staff.

1. Abram was seventy-five years old, and he had lived all his life among people who worshiped the moon.
2. **[Q] Genesis 12:1** "Now the LORD said to Abram, "Go from your country and your kindred and your father's house to the land that I will show you."
3. God did not tell him where. Only: go, and I will show you.

### Scene 1.2 · The promise
*On screen:* The stars fading at sunrise over the hills. Words of the promise glow faintly in the sky, one after another, then fade.

1. With the command came a promise, the greatest anyone had ever heard.
2. **[Q] Genesis 12:2** "And I will make of you a great nation, and I will bless you, and make your name great, so that you will be a blessing."
3. **[Q] Genesis 12:3** "I will bless those who bless you, and him who curses you I will curse; and by you all the families of the earth shall bless themselves."
4. At Babel, people tried to make a name for themselves. Here God promises to make Abram's name great, and through him, to bless every family on earth.

### Scene 1.3 · So Abram went
*On screen:* The caravan leaves Haran: Abram and Sarai, their nephew Lot, servants, tents rolled onto camels and donkeys, flocks of sheep and goats. They head southwest along the road toward Canaan. *(Play: the journey.)*

1. **[Q] Genesis 12:4** "So Abram went, as the LORD had told him; and Lot went with him. Abram was seventy-five years old when he departed from Haran."
2. *(Play: the journey.)*

### Scene 1.4 · An altar at Shechem
*On screen:* A great oak at Shechem, among green hills. Abram stacks stones into an altar; the family gathers. Then a second altar on a hilltop between Bethel and Ai, the tents pitched below.

1. **[Q] Genesis 12:7** "Then the LORD appeared to Abram, and said, "To your descendants I will give this land." So he built there an altar to the LORD, who had appeared to him."
2. Wherever Abram went in the land, he built an altar. It was his way of saying: this land, and my life, belong to God.
3. **[Q] Genesis 12:8** "Thence he removed to the mountain on the east of Bethel, and pitched his tent, with Bethel on the west and Ai on the east; and there he built an altar to the LORD and called on the name of the LORD."

### Play: the journey
- **What it is:** courier routes (spec §13.7, kind 15), on the map.
- **How it plays:** the caravan travels from Haran to Canaan along the old roads. The player leads it from waypoint to waypoint: the river crossing at the Euphrates, the well at an oasis, the road past Damascus, and down into the hills of Canaan to Shechem. At each waypoint the caravan rests for a moment, and a line about the road appears ("Water for the flocks," "A long way from home"). A caravan led off the road turns back to it.
- **Teaches:** the length and the cost of the journey, and that Abram went "not knowing where he was to go."
- **Gentle mode:** the next waypoint glows, and the caravan follows the road by itself while the player holds.
- **Accessibility:** arrows or a pointer; one button: Space leads the caravan on to the next waypoint.

**Thread:** *the call of Abram → the call to follow Jesus.* The glowing object is Abram's staff, left leaning against the first altar.
- **[Q] Matthew 4:19:** "And he said to them, "Follow me, and I will make you fishers of men.""
- **What it shows:** God called Abram to leave home and go where he would show him. Jesus called Peter and Andrew in the same way, and they left their nets at once. Every Christian is called like Abraham: to leave something behind and follow.
- **Source:** Hebrews 11:8; Matthew 4:19–20; Catechism 59, 145.

**Where and when:**
- **When.** Abraham's time: roughly 2000–1700 BC, as scholars estimate. The Bible does not give dates we can fix to a calendar.
- **Haran to Canaan.** About 650 kilometers on foot, with flocks: a journey of weeks. The road ran south past Damascus into the hill country.
- **Shechem and Bethel.** Two places in the hills of Canaan that will matter again and again in the Bible. Jacob will dream of a ladder at Bethel (Level 4).

**Read:** Genesis 12:1–9.

## Episode 2 · Lot Chooses the Plain

**Genesis 12:10–13:18 · about 7 minutes · 4 scenes**

### Scene 2.1 · Famine, and a fearful lie
*On screen:* Dry fields and dying grass. The caravan goes down to Egypt, green along the Nile. Pharaoh's palace in the distance. Sarai's face, troubled.

1. **[Q] Genesis 12:10** "Now there was a famine in the land. So Abram went down to Egypt to sojourn there, for the famine was severe in the land."
2. In Egypt, Abram was afraid. Sarai was beautiful, and he feared the Egyptians would kill him to take her. So he told her to say she was only his sister.
3. Pharaoh took Sarai into his house, and God sent plagues on Pharaoh's household until the truth came out. Pharaoh sent them away. Even Abram, the man of faith, could be afraid and fail. God kept his promise anyway.

### Scene 2.2 · Too many flocks
*On screen:* Back in the hills near Bethel. Two great camps side by side, flocks crowding the same springs. Herdsmen shout and push at a well. *(Play: dividing the flocks.)*

1. **[Q] Genesis 13:6** "so that the land could not support both of them dwelling together; for their possessions were so great that they could not dwell together,"
2. Abram's herdsmen and Lot's herdsmen began to quarrel over water and grass.
3. **[Q] Genesis 13:8** "Then Abram said to Lot, "Let there be no strife between you and me, and between your herdsmen and my herdsmen; for we are kinsmen."
4. Abram was the elder. He could have chosen first. Instead he let Lot choose.
5. *(Play: dividing the flocks.)*

### Scene 2.3 · Lot chooses
*On screen:* Lot on a high place, looking east: the Jordan valley below, green and well watered, with the cities of the plain shining in the distance. Behind him, the dry hills.

1. **[Q] Genesis 13:10** "And Lot lifted up his eyes, and saw that the Jordan valley was well watered everywhere like the garden of the LORD, like the land of Egypt, in the direction of Zoar; this was before the LORD destroyed Sodom and Gomorrah."
2. Lot chose what looked best. He moved his tents down toward the city of Sodom.
3. **[Q] Genesis 13:13** "Now the men of Sodom were wicked, great sinners against the LORD."

### Scene 2.4 · Look north and south and east and west
*On screen:* Abram alone on the hilltop at evening. As he turns to each direction, the land opens before him: hills, valleys, the sea far to the west. Then he moves his tents to the oaks of Mamre.

1. After Lot had gone, God spoke to Abram.
2. **[Q] Genesis 13:14** "The LORD said to Abram, after Lot had separated from him, "Lift up your eyes, and look from the place where you are, northward and southward and eastward and westward;"
3. **[Q] Genesis 13:15** "for all the land which you see I will give to you and to your descendants for ever."
4. **[Q] Genesis 13:18** "So Abram moved his tent, and came and dwelt by the oaks of Mamre, which are at Hebron; and there he built an altar to the LORD."

### Play: dividing the flocks
- **What it is:** herding (spec §13.7, kind 2), like Abel's flock in Level 1.
- **How it plays:** the two flocks have mixed together at the crowded well. Abram's sheep wear a brown mark and Lot's a white one. The player guides each animal to its own flock, Abram's toward the hills and Lot's toward the valley path, until the two camps are peaceful again. Herdsmen's angry words fade as the flocks are sorted.
- **Teaches:** "Let there be no strife": peace between brothers, and generosity.
- **Gentle mode:** animals drift toward their own flock by themselves; the player only nudges.
- **Accessibility:** a pointer, or Space to send the next animal to its flock.

**Thread:** *Abram lets Lot choose → "seek first his kingdom."* The glowing object is the altar on the hill at Bethel.
- **[Q] Matthew 6:33:** "But seek first his kingdom and his righteousness, and all these things shall be yours as well."
- **What it shows:** Lot chose by what his eyes could see, and moved toward Sodom. Abram put God first and peace first, and God gave him the whole land. Jesus teaches the same: seek God first, and trust him with the rest.
- **Source:** Matthew 6:33; Catechism 2830.

**Where and when:**
- **Egypt.** The Nile made Egypt the breadbasket of the ancient world. In famines, families from Canaan often went down to Egypt for food, as Jacob's sons will in Level 5.
- **The Jordan valley.** The valley of the Jordan River runs down to the Dead Sea, the lowest land on earth. Sodom and Gomorrah were somewhere near its southern end.
- **Mamre and Hebron.** The oaks of Mamre were near Hebron, in the southern hills. Abraham's family tomb is there to this day.

**Read:** Genesis 12:10–13:18.

## Episode 3 · Melchizedek's Bread and Wine

**Genesis 14 · about 6 minutes · 4 scenes**

### Scene 3.1 · Lot taken captive
*On screen:* Dust on the horizon of the valley: an army of four kings marching away with carts of goods and lines of captives. Among them, small and in silhouette, Lot.

1. War came to the cities of the plain. Four kings from the east defeated the king of Sodom and his allies.
2. **[Q] Genesis 14:12** "they also took Lot, the son of Abram's brother, who dwelt in Sodom, and his goods, and departed."
3. A man who escaped ran all the way to Mamre to tell Abram.

### Scene 3.2 · By night
*On screen:* Night in the far north, near Dan. The enemy camp sleeps around its fires. Torches move through the dark hills, closing in from two sides. *(Play: by night.)*

1. **[Q] Genesis 14:14** "When Abram heard that his kinsman had been taken captive, he led forth his trained men, born in his house, three hundred and eighteen of them, and went in pursuit as far as Dan."
2. *(Play: by night.)*
3. **[Q] Genesis 14:16** "Then he brought back all the goods, and also brought back his kinsman Lot with his goods, and the women and the people."

### Scene 3.3 · The priest-king of Salem
*On screen:* The King's Valley at morning. A man in white comes out from a small city on a hill, Salem, carrying bread and a jar of wine. Light rests on him. Abram kneels.

1. On the way home, a mysterious man came out to meet Abram.
2. **[Q] Genesis 14:18** "And Melchizedek king of Salem brought out bread and wine; he was priest of God Most High."
3. **[Q] Genesis 14:19** "And he blessed him and said, "Blessed be Abram by God Most High, maker of heaven and earth;"
4. **[Q] Genesis 14:20** "and blessed be God Most High, who has delivered your enemies into your hand!" And Abram gave him a tenth of everything."

### Scene 3.4 · Not a thread
*On screen:* The king of Sodom, in rich robes, offers Abram carts of treasure. Abram shakes his head and lifts his hand toward heaven.

1. The king of Sodom offered Abram all the riches he had won back.
2. **[Q] Genesis 14:23** "that I would not take a thread or a sandal-thong or anything that is yours, lest you should say, 'I have made Abram rich.'"
3. Abram would be made rich by God alone, not by Sodom.

### Play: by night
- **What it is:** stealth and timing (spec §13.7, kinds 16 and 17).
- **How it plays:** Abram's men carry torches through the dark hills toward the sleeping camp. The player moves the two groups of torches from cover to cover while the camp's watchmen look the other way; when both groups are in place around the camp, the torches flare at once and the captives are freed. No fighting is shown: only torches, shouts in the dark, and the captives running to Abram.
- **Teaches:** Abram's courage and loyalty: he risked everything for the nephew who had taken the better land.
- **Gentle mode:** the watchmen turn slowly, and a group waits safely by itself until the way is clear.
- **Accessibility:** arrows or a pointer; one button: Space moves the next group on when it's safe.

**Thread:** *Melchizedek's bread and wine → the Eucharist.* The glowing object is the bread and wine in Melchizedek's hands.
- **[Q] Psalm 110:4:** "The LORD has sworn and will not change his mind, "You are a priest for ever after the order of Melchizedek.""
- **What it shows:** Melchizedek, king of Salem (later Jerusalem) and priest of God Most High, offered bread and wine and blessed Abram. The Letter to the Hebrews calls Jesus a priest for ever after the order of Melchizedek. At the Last Supper he took bread and wine and made them his Body and Blood. In the First Eucharistic Prayer, the priest still names "the holy sacrifice, the spotless victim" offered by Melchizedek.
- **Source:** Hebrews 7:1–3; Matthew 26:26–28; Catechism 1333, 1350; the Roman Canon.

**Where and when:**
- **Salem.** Ancient tradition, and Psalm 76, identify Salem with Jerusalem, the city of peace (*shalom*).
- **Dan.** At the far north of the land, near the springs of the Jordan. Abram chased the kings nearly 250 kilometers.
- **A tenth.** Abram gave Melchizedek a tenth of everything: the first tithe in the Bible.

**Read:** Genesis 14.

## Episode 4 · Count the Stars

**Genesis 15 · about 6 minutes · 4 scenes**

### Scene 4.1 · Fear not, Abram
*On screen:* Abram's tent at Mamre, at dusk. He sits in the doorway, troubled, an old man with no child. A light comes to rest near him.

1. **[Q] Genesis 15:1** "After these things the word of the LORD came to Abram in a vision, "Fear not, Abram, I am your shield; your reward shall be very great.""
2. But Abram had a sorrow he could not hide.
3. **[Q] Genesis 15:3** "And Abram said, "Behold, thou hast given me no offspring; and a slave born in my house will be my heir.""

### Scene 4.2 · Number the stars
*On screen:* Outside the tent, under the night sky. Stars appear one by one, then by hundreds, then by thousands, until the whole sky is filled. *(Play: count the stars.)*

1. **[Q] Genesis 15:5** "And he brought him outside and said, "Look toward heaven, and number the stars, if you are able to number them." Then he said to him, "So shall your descendants be.""
2. *(Play: count the stars.)*
3. **[Q] Genesis 15:6** "And he believed the LORD; and he reckoned it to him as righteousness."
4. Abram could see no child. He believed anyway. That trust is what the Bible calls faith.

### Scene 4.3 · The covenant between the pieces
*On screen:* Evening. Animals laid out in two rows on the ground, shown simply (no blood). Abram falls into a deep sleep. In the darkness, a smoking fire pot and a flaming torch pass between the rows.

1. Then God made a covenant with Abram in the way of those days: people who made a solemn agreement walked between the halves of animals, as if to say, "May this happen to me if I break my word."
2. **[Q] Genesis 15:17** "When the sun had gone down and it was dark, behold, a smoking fire pot and a flaming torch passed between these pieces."
3. Only God passed between the pieces. Abram slept. God bound himself alone to keep the promise.

### Scene 4.4 · The land promised
*On screen:* Dawn over the land, from the river of Egypt to the great Euphrates, drawn like the tapestry map of Level 2, with the promised land glowing.

1. **[Q] Genesis 15:18** "On that day the LORD made a covenant with Abram, saying, "To your descendants I give this land, from the river of Egypt to the great river, the river Euphrates,"
2. Abram still had no son. But now he had God's oath.

### Play: count the stars
- **What it is:** light and reveal (spec §13.7, kind 11).
- **How it plays:** the player taps stars to count them. Each tap lights a star and the count goes up: 1, 2, 3… Then the stars begin to appear faster than anyone could tap, in clusters, in streams, until the Milky Way spills across the sky and the counter gives up, showing only "So shall your descendants be."
- **Teaches:** the size of God's promise, and faith that trusts beyond what can be counted.
- **Gentle mode:** a sweep across the sky counts many stars at once.
- **Accessibility:** Space counts the next star; holding Space counts faster.

**Thread:** *the stars of Abraham → all who believe are his children.* The glowing object is the brightest star overhead.
- **[Q] Galatians 3:29:** "And if you are Christ's, then you are Abraham's offspring, heirs according to promise."
- **What it shows:** Abraham's children are not only his family by blood. Everyone who believes in Christ becomes a child of Abraham, so the stars God showed him include us. We call him "our father in faith" at every Mass that uses the First Eucharistic Prayer.
- **Source:** Romans 4:3, 18–21; Galatians 3:6–9, 29; Catechism 145–146.

**Where and when:**
- **The stars.** On a clear night, far from any city, a person can see about 3,000 stars. Abram, in the desert air, saw the sky as few of us ever do.
- **"Reckoned as righteousness."** St Paul quotes this verse to explain that we are made right with God through faith (Romans 4). St James quotes it too, adding that Abraham's faith showed itself in what he did (James 2:21–23).
- **A covenant oath.** Cutting animals in two to seal an agreement is known from other ancient documents. The prophet Jeremiah mentions it (Jeremiah 34:18).

**Read:** Genesis 15.

## Episode 5 · Hagar and Ishmael

**Genesis 16 and 21:8–21 · about 7 minutes · 4 scenes**

### Scene 5.1 · A plan of their own
*On screen:* Ten years later at Mamre. Sarai and her Egyptian maid Hagar at the loom. Sarai's sad face; Abram's worried one.

1. Ten years passed in the land, and still Sarai had no child.
2. **[Q] Genesis 16:2** "and Sarai said to Abram, "Behold now, the LORD has prevented me from bearing children; go in to my maid; it may be that I shall obtain children by her." And Abram hearkened to the voice of Sarai."
3. It was a custom of that time, but it was not God's plan. Hagar became pregnant, and jealousy came into the household. Sarai treated Hagar harshly, and Hagar ran away into the desert.

### Scene 5.2 · The God who sees
*On screen:* The desert road to Shur. Hagar alone by a spring, exhausted. A figure of light stands beside her.

1. **[Q] Genesis 16:7** "The angel of the LORD found her by a spring of water in the wilderness, the spring on the way to Shur."
2. **[Q] Genesis 16:11** "And the angel of the LORD said to her, "Behold, you are with child, and shall bear a son; you shall call his name Ishmael; because the LORD has given heed to your affliction."
3. The name Ishmael means "God hears."
4. **[Q] Genesis 16:13** "So she called the name of the LORD who spoke to her, "Thou art a God of seeing"; for she said, "Have I really seen God and remained alive after seeing him?""
5. Hagar was a slave, a foreigner, running away. She is the first person in the Bible to give God a name: the God who sees.

### Scene 5.3 · Sent away
*On screen:* Years later. Abram, now Abraham, at dawn, gives Hagar bread and a skin of water and lifts it onto her shoulder; the boy Ishmael beside her. They walk away into the wilderness of Beer-sheba.

1. After Isaac was born (Episode 9 tells it), Sarah would not let Ishmael stay. Abraham was grieved, but God told him he would care for the boy.
2. **[Q] Genesis 21:14** "So Abraham rose early in the morning, and took bread and a skin of water, and gave it to Hagar, putting it on her shoulder, along with the child, and sent her away. And she departed, and wandered in the wilderness of Beer-sheba."

### Scene 5.4 · God heard the boy
*On screen:* The empty water skin. Hagar sits a bowshot away from the boy, under a bush, her face turned away, weeping. Then light, and a well appears where there was only sand. *(Play: the well.)*

1. The water ran out. Hagar could not bear to watch her son die.
2. **[Q] Genesis 21:17** "And God heard the voice of the lad; and the angel of God called to Hagar from heaven, and said to her, "What troubles you, Hagar? Fear not; for God has heard the voice of the lad where he is."
3. *(Play: the well.)*
4. **[Q] Genesis 21:19** "Then God opened her eyes, and she saw a well of water; and she went, and filled the skin with water, and gave the lad a drink."
5. **[Q] Genesis 21:20** "And God was with the lad, and he grew up; he lived in the wilderness, and became an expert with the bow."

### Play: the well
- **What it is:** searching (spec §13.7, kind 8), with light and reveal.
- **How it plays:** the wilderness shimmers in the heat. The player sweeps across the desert; where the sweep passes, the haze clears a little, and faint signs appear: a green shoot, damp sand, a bird drinking. Following the signs leads to the well. When it's found, the water glints and Hagar runs to fill the skin.
- **Teaches:** God sees and hears those in trouble, and opens our eyes to what he has already provided.
- **Gentle mode:** the signs glow, and the haze clears faster.
- **Accessibility:** a pointer or arrows; one button: Space reveals the next sign.

**Thread:** *the well in the wilderness → living water.* The glowing object is the well.
- **[Q] John 4:14:** "but whoever drinks of the water that I shall give him will never thirst; the water that I shall give him will become in him a spring of water welling up to eternal life."
- **What it shows:** God found Hagar, a foreign woman alone at a spring, and gave her water in the desert. Jesus sat by a well with another foreign woman, the Samaritan, and offered her living water that would never run dry: the grace of the Holy Spirit.
- **Source:** John 4:5–14; Catechism 694, 2560.

**Where and when:**
- **Beer-sheba.** In the dry south of the land, the Negeb, at the edge of the desert. Its name means "well of the oath" (Genesis 21:31).
- **Ishmael.** God kept his word to Hagar too: Ishmael became a great nation, with twelve princes (Genesis 17:20; 25:13–16). Many Arab peoples look to him as their ancestor.
- **The God who sees.** In Hebrew, *El Roi*. The spring where the angel found Hagar was named Beer-lahai-roi, "the well of the Living One who sees me."

**Read:** Genesis 16; 21:8–21.

## Episode 6 · New Names, Everlasting Covenant

**Genesis 17 · about 6 minutes · 4 scenes**

### Scene 6.1 · Walk before me
*On screen:* Mamre, years later. Abram, ninety-nine, white-haired, bowed with his face to the ground before a great light.

1. Abram was ninety-nine years old, and still there was no son from Sarai. Then God spoke again.
2. **[Q] Genesis 17:1** "When Abram was ninety-nine years old the LORD appeared to Abram, and said to him, "I am God Almighty; walk before me, and be blameless."
3. **[Q] Genesis 17:7** "And I will establish my covenant between me and you and your descendants after you throughout their generations for an everlasting covenant, to be God to you and to your descendants after you."

### Scene 6.2 · Abraham and Sarah
*On screen:* The names "Abram" and "Sarai" written in the air in light. A new breath of light touches each, and they change. *(Play: new names.)*

1. **[Q] Genesis 17:5** "No longer shall your name be Abram, but your name shall be Abraham; for I have made you the father of a multitude of nations."
2. **[Q] Genesis 17:15** "And God said to Abraham, "As for Sarai your wife, you shall not call her name Sarai, but Sarah shall be her name."
3. *(Play: new names.)*
4. In the Bible, when God gives someone a new name, he gives them a new mission.

### Scene 6.3 · The sign of the covenant
*On screen:* Abraham's whole household gathered at the tents, men and boys, Ishmael among them, solemn, as Abraham speaks. Nothing more is shown.

1. God gave Abraham a sign of the covenant, to be marked on the body of every male in his family, from generation to generation: circumcision.
2. **[Q] Genesis 17:10** "This is my covenant, which you shall keep, between me and you and your descendants after you: Every male among you shall be circumcised."
3. It marked them as belonging to God's family. Christians receive a new sign of belonging, not cut by hands: Baptism.

### Scene 6.4 · Abraham laughed
*On screen:* Abraham on his face on the ground, his shoulders shaking. He is laughing.

1. Then God promised that Sarah herself would have a son.
2. **[Q] Genesis 17:17** "Then Abraham fell on his face and laughed, and said to himself, "Shall a child be born to a man who is a hundred years old? Shall Sarah, who is ninety years old, bear a child?""
3. **[Q] Genesis 17:21** "But I will establish my covenant with Isaac, whom Sarah shall bear to you at this season next year.""
4. God even chose the child's name: Isaac, which means "he laughs."

### Play: new names
- **What it is:** matching and naming (spec §13.7, kind 10), a quiet one.
- **How it plays:** a soft breath of light floats over the names "Abram" and "Sarai," written as glowing letters. The player gives each name its new letter (drags the light into the name, or chooses it), and the name changes: Abram, "exalted father," becomes Abraham, "father of a multitude"; Sarai becomes Sarah, "princess." Then a third name appears, not yet given to anyone: Isaac, "he laughs."
- **Teaches:** the new names and what they mean; God's promise written into who they are.
- **Gentle mode:** the place for the new letter glows.
- **Accessibility:** Space gives each name its new letter.

**Thread:** *a new name, a new mission → "You are Peter."* The glowing object is the new name "Abraham," shining in the air.
- **[Q] Matthew 16:18:** "And I tell you, you are Peter, and on this rock I will build my church, and the powers of death shall not prevail against it."
- **What it shows:** God renamed Abram "father of a multitude," and from him came God's people. Jesus renamed Simon "Peter," the rock, and built his Church on him. In Baptism and Confirmation, Christians are called by name too, and given a mission.
- **Source:** Matthew 16:17–18; Colossians 2:11–12; Catechism 2156–2159.

**Where and when:**
- **God Almighty.** In Hebrew, *El Shaddai*, one of the oldest names of God in the Bible.
- **Two names explained.** *Abram* means "exalted father"; *Abraham* sounds like "father of a multitude." *Sarai* and *Sarah* both mean "princess"; the new form marks her new mission as mother of nations.
- **Circumcision and Baptism.** St Paul calls Baptism "the circumcision of Christ," a sign made "without hands" (Colossians 2:11–12).

**Read:** Genesis 17.

## Episode 7 · Three Visitors at Mamre

**Genesis 18:1–15 · about 6 minutes · 4 scenes**

### Scene 7.1 · In the heat of the day
*On screen:* Midday at Mamre. The air shimmers. Abraham dozes at his tent door under the great oak. Three figures stand nearby, their outlines edged with light.

1. **[Q] Genesis 18:1** "And the LORD appeared to him by the oaks of Mamre, as he sat at the door of his tent in the heat of the day."
2. **[Q] Genesis 18:2** "He lifted up his eyes and looked, and behold, three men stood in front of him. When he saw them, he ran from the tent door to meet them, and bowed himself to the earth,"
3. An old man, at the hottest hour of the day, ran.

### Scene 7.2 · Abraham hastened

### Play: serving the guests
- **What it is:** serving and distributing (spec §13.7, kind 19).
- **How it plays:** Abraham hurries to make his guests welcome. ### Play: Abraham pleads
- **What it is:** a quiet interaction (spec §13.7, kind 20), with choosing.
- **How it plays:** Abraham asks again and again.
