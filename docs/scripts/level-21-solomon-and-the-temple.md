# Level 21 script: *Solomon and the Temple*

**Bible:** 1 Kings 1–11; 2 Chronicles 1–9
**Age:** VI · The Kingdom
**Length:** about 46 minutes (6 episodes, then the quiz, then reflection and prayer)
**Status:** draft for Dave's approval.

## How to read this script

- Each **scene** is a checkpoint. Its numbered lines are the narration beats; the player taps to move from one to the next.
- *On screen* describes what the scene shows. It's the brief for the art in both styles.
- **[Q]** marks an exact quotation from the Bible. Its wording in both translations is in the verse table at the end. Everything else is our own retelling.
- **Play** is the episode's interactive moment. **Thread** is its golden thread. **Read** is the passage the game sends the player to.
- "LORD" in capitals stands for God's holy name, printed in small capitals in the RSV.

### What this level is about

Solomon, the son of David and Bathsheba (Level 19), is anointed king. God appears to him in a dream and says, "Ask what I shall give you," and Solomon asks not for riches or a long life, but for "an understanding mind." God gives him wisdom, and riches and honor besides. Solomon's wisdom shines in the case of two mothers and one living child. He builds the Temple in Jerusalem, on the hill where David built his altar (Level 19), so quietly that "neither hammer nor axe" was heard; and when the ark is brought in, the glory of the LORD fills the house like a cloud. The Queen of Sheba travels from the ends of the earth to test him with hard questions, and goes home amazed.

But the story doesn't end in glory. In his old age, Solomon's heart turns away from God to the gods of his many foreign wives, and God tells him that the kingdom will be torn in two.

Solomon was the wisest king of Israel, but Jesus said, "something greater than Solomon is here." Jesus is the wisdom of God in person, and he builds a temple not of stone but of living people.

**How it's shown:** Episode 6 begins with a short note. Solomon's idol worship is told simply.

## Episode 1 · Solomon Asks for Wisdom

**1 Kings 1:38–3:15; 2 Chronicles 1 · about 8 minutes · 3 scenes**

### Scene 1.1 · "Long live King Solomon!"
*On screen:* The spring of Gihon, in the valley below Jerusalem. Young Solomon rides King David's own mule down to the spring, with the priest Zadok, the prophet Nathan and the king's guard. Zadok takes a horn of oil and pours it on Solomon's head; trumpets sound, and the crowd plays pipes and dances.

1. King David was old. Before he died, he kept his promise to Bathsheba: their son Solomon would be king after him.
2. **[Q] 1 Kings 1:39** "There Zadok the priest took the horn of oil from the tent, and anointed Solomon. Then they blew the trumpet; and all the people said, "Long live King Solomon!""
3. **[Q] 1 Kings 1:40** "And all the people went up after him, playing on pipes, and rejoicing with great joy, so that the earth was split by their noise."

### Scene 1.2 · "Be strong, and show yourself a man"
*On screen:* The king's room. Old David lies in his bed; Solomon kneels beside him, and David lays his hand on his son's head.

1. Before he died, David gave his son a last charge.
2. **[Q] 1 Kings 2:2** ""I am about to go the way of all the earth. Be strong, and show yourself a man,"
3. **[Q] 1 Kings 2:3** "and keep the charge of the LORD your God, walking in his ways and keeping his statutes, his commandments, his ordinances, and his testimonies, as it is written in the law of Moses, that you may prosper in all that you do and wherever you turn;"
4. David died, and was buried in the city of David. He had reigned over Israel for forty years.

### Scene 1.3 · "Ask what I shall give you"
*On screen:* Gibeon, at night, beside the great altar. Solomon sleeps on the ground in his cloak. A soft light gathers over him, brighter and brighter, until it fills the whole hilltop. *(Play: an understanding mind.)*

1. **[Q] 1 Kings 3:5** "At Gibeon the LORD appeared to Solomon in a dream by night; and God said, "Ask what I shall give you.""
2. **[Q] 1 Kings 3:7** "And now, O LORD my God, thou hast made thy servant king in place of David my father, although I am but a little child; I do not know how to go out or come in."
3. *(Play: an understanding mind.)*
4. **[Q] 1 Kings 3:9** "Give thy servant therefore an understanding mind to govern thy people, that I may discern between good and evil; for who is able to govern this thy great people?""
5. **[Q] 1 Kings 3:11** "And God said to him, "Because you have asked this, and have not asked for yourself long life or riches or the life of your enemies, but have asked for yourself understanding to discern what is right,"
6. **[Q] 1 Kings 3:12** "behold, I now do according to your word. Behold, I give you a wise and discerning mind, so that none like you has been before you and none like you shall arise after you."
7. **[Q] 1 Kings 3:13** "I give you also what you have not asked, both riches and honor, so that no other king shall compare with you, all your days."

### Play: an understanding mind
- **What it is:** ordering and memory (spec §13.7, kinds 9 and 14).
- **How it plays:** God asks, "Ask what I shall give you." The parts of Solomon's prayer appear like stars in the dream, and the player puts them in order: first thanks for God's love to David, then "I am but a little child," then the request, "Give thy servant… an understanding mind." Among them float the things Solomon didn't ask for: a long life, riches, the death of his enemies. With each right choice, the light grows.
- **Teaches:** how to pray like Solomon: thank God, be humble, and ask for what really matters.
- **Gentle mode:** the next part glows.
- **Accessibility:** a pointer, or the arrow keys and Space.

**Thread:** *Solomon asks God for "an understanding mind" → "If any of you lacks wisdom, let him ask God."* The glowing object is the light of the dream.
- **[Q] 1 Kings 3:9:** "Give thy servant therefore an understanding mind to govern thy people, that I may discern between good and evil; for who is able to govern this thy great people?""
- **[Q] James 1:5:** "If any of you lacks wisdom, let him ask God, who gives to all men generously and without reproaching, and it will be given him."
- **What it shows:** God was pleased that Solomon asked for wisdom rather than riches. Wisdom isn't only for kings: James promises that anyone who asks God for wisdom will receive it, "generously and without reproaching." Wisdom is one of the seven gifts of the Holy Spirit, given to every Christian at Confirmation.
- **Source:** James 1:5–8; Wisdom 9:1–11; Catechism 1831, 2633.

**Where and when:**
- **About 970 BC.** Solomon was a young man, perhaps twenty, when he became king.
- **Gihon.** The spring in the Kidron valley that gave Jerusalem its water. Kings of David's family were anointed there.
- **"Long live the king!"** Handel set the words of 1 Kings 1:38–40 to music in "Zadok the Priest" for the coronation of King George II in 1727. It has been sung at every British coronation since.
- **Gibeon.** A town a few kilometers north of Jerusalem, where the tabernacle of Moses then stood (2 Chronicles 1:3).

**Read:** 1 Kings 1:28–40; 2:1–4; 3:3–15.

## Episode 2 · The Two Mothers

**1 Kings 3:16–4:34 · about 7 minutes · 3 scenes**

### Scene 2.1 · Two women before the king
*On screen:* The king's hall of judgment. Solomon sits on his throne. Two young women stand before him, both crying and shouting, one holding a baby tight in her arms.

1. Two women came to King Solomon. They lived in the same house, and each had a baby boy, born three days apart.
2. One night one of the babies died. Now each woman said that the living baby was hers, and the dead baby was the other's. No one else had been in the house; no one could tell who was telling the truth.
3. **[Q] 1 Kings 3:22** "But the other woman said, "No, the living child is mine, and the dead child is yours." The first said, "No, the dead child is yours, and the living child is mine." Thus they spoke before the king."

### Scene 2.2 · "Bring me a sword"
*On screen:* The same hall. A guard brings a sword and holds it up, and the whole court goes silent. One woman falls to her knees, her arms reaching for the baby; the other stands still with her arms folded. *(Play: the living child.)*

1. **[Q] 1 Kings 3:24** "And the king said, "Bring me a sword." So a sword was brought before the king."
2. **[Q] 1 Kings 3:25** "And the king said, "Divide the living child in two, and give half to the one, and half to the other.""
3. Solomon did not mean to harm the child. He was testing the two women, to find out whose heart was a mother's.
4. **[Q] 1 Kings 3:26** "Then the woman whose son was alive said to the king, because her heart yearned for her son, "Oh, my lord, give her the living child, and by no means slay it." But the other said, "It shall be neither mine nor yours; divide it.""
5. *(Play: the living child.)*
6. **[Q] 1 Kings 3:27** "Then the king answered and said, "Give the living child to the first woman, and by no means slay it; she is its mother.""

### Scene 2.3 · The wisdom of God was in him
*On screen:* The true mother walks out of the hall into the sunshine, holding her baby close, while the people of Jerusalem bow to the young king. Then Solomon's court: wise men from far countries listen to him talking of trees, animals, birds and fish.

1. **[Q] 1 Kings 3:28** "And all Israel heard of the judgment which the king had rendered; and they stood in awe of the king, because they perceived that the wisdom of God was in him, to render justice."
2. **[Q] 1 Kings 4:29** "And God gave Solomon wisdom and understanding beyond measure, and largeness of mind like the sand on the seashore,"
3. **[Q] 1 Kings 4:32** "He also uttered three thousand proverbs; and his songs were a thousand and five."
4. **[Q] 1 Kings 4:34** "And men came from all peoples to hear the wisdom of Solomon, and from all the kings of the earth, who had heard of his wisdom."

### Play: the living child
- **What it is:** a quiet judging interaction (spec §13.7, kind 20).
- **How it plays:** the player helps Solomon judge, one question at a time. Who cries out to save the child's life, even if it means losing him? Who says, "divide it"? And so, who is the true mother? For each question the player taps one of the two women. When the right answer is found, the guard lowers the sword, and the baby is placed in his mother's arms.
- **Teaches:** that real love gives itself up for the one it loves, and that God's wisdom sees the heart.
- **Gentle mode:** the right woman glows.
- **Accessibility:** a pointer, or the arrow keys and Space.

**Thread:** *"The wisdom of God was in him, to render justice" → "Christ the power of God and the wisdom of God."* The glowing object is the living child, given back to his mother.
- **[Q] 1 Kings 3:28:** "And all Israel heard of the judgment which the king had rendered; and they stood in awe of the king, because they perceived that the wisdom of God was in him, to render justice."
- **[Q] 1 Corinthians 1:24:** "but to those who are called, both Jews and Greeks, Christ the power of God and the wisdom of God."
- **What it shows:** God gave Solomon wisdom to judge justly, and the people saw it. Saint Paul says that Jesus himself *is* God's wisdom, made man. The wisdom that saw the true mother's love is the same wisdom that will judge the whole world with justice and mercy.
- **Source:** 1 Corinthians 1:18–30; Colossians 2:3; Catechism 216, 1040.

**Where and when:**
- **The king as judge.** In Israel the king was the highest court. Anyone could bring their case to him, as these two poor women did.
- **Three thousand proverbs.** Many of them are collected in the book of Proverbs, the subject of Level 22.
- **"From the cedar… to the hyssop."** Solomon spoke about the whole of creation, from the tallest tree in Lebanon to the little hyssop that grows out of a wall (Level 19).

**Read:** 1 Kings 3:16–28; 4:29–34.

## Episode 3 · Building the Temple

**1 Kings 5–6; 2 Chronicles 2–4 · about 8 minutes · 3 scenes**

### Scene 3.1 · The cedars of Lebanon
*On screen:* The mountains of Lebanon, covered with tall cedars. Woodcutters fell the great trees, and the logs are tied into rafts and floated down the coast of the sea. Then a stone quarry, where masons cut and shape great blocks.

1. David had wanted to build a house for God, but God had promised that his son would build it (Level 18). Now Solomon was ready.
2. **[Q] 1 Kings 5:5** "And so I purpose to build a house for the name of the LORD my God, as the LORD said to David my father, 'Your son, whom I will set upon your throne in your place, shall build the house for my name.'"
3. He made a treaty with Hiram, the king of Tyre, who sent cedar and cypress from the mountains of Lebanon. Thousands of workers cut stone in the hills.

### Scene 3.2 · Neither hammer nor axe
*On screen:* Mount Moriah, the hill where David built his altar. On the bare rock, the Temple rises course by course. Workers lower great finished stones on ropes, gently, without a sound. *(Play: without a sound.)*

1. **[Q] 1 Kings 6:1** "In the four hundred and eightieth year after the people of Israel came out of the land of Egypt, in the fourth year of Solomon's reign over Israel, in the month of Ziv, which is the second month, he began to build the house of the LORD."
2. **[Q] 1 Kings 6:7** "When the house was built, it was with stone prepared at the quarry; so that neither hammer nor axe nor any tool of iron was heard in the temple, while it was being built."
3. *(Play: without a sound.)*
4. **[Q] 1 Kings 6:12** ""Concerning this house which you are building, if you will walk in my statutes and obey my ordinances and keep all my commandments and walk in them, then I will establish my word with you, which I spoke to David your father."
5. **[Q] 1 Kings 6:13** "And I will dwell among the children of Israel, and will not forsake my people Israel.""

### Scene 3.3 · Overlaid with gold
*On screen:* Inside the Temple: walls of cedar carved with flowers, palm trees and cherubim, all covered with gold and shining in the lamplight. At the far end, the doors of the inner sanctuary, the Most Holy Place, stand open, empty and waiting.

1. **[Q] 1 Kings 6:19** "The inner sanctuary he prepared in the innermost part of the house, to set there the ark of the covenant of the LORD."
2. **[Q] 1 Kings 6:22** "And he overlaid the whole house with gold, until all the house was finished. Also the whole altar that belonged to the inner sanctuary he overlaid with gold."
3. **[Q] 1 Kings 6:38** "And in the eleventh year, in the month of Bul, which is the eighth month, the house was finished in all its parts, and according to all its specifications. He was seven years in building it."

### Play: without a sound
- **What it is:** building and timing (spec §13.7, kinds 3 and 16).
- **How it plays:** a finished stone hangs from its rope, swaying gently. The player waits until it is still and in line, then taps to lower it into place. Six courses, and the walls stand. A tap while it swings does nothing; nobody hammers, nothing is forced.
- **Teaches:** that God's house was built with patience and care, every stone prepared beforehand to fit.
- **Gentle mode:** each stone waits steady for the tap.
- **Accessibility:** a pointer, or Space.

**Thread:** *Stones prepared at the quarry, built into the house of the LORD → "Like living stones be yourselves built into a spiritual house."* The glowing object is the stone being set in place.
- **[Q] 1 Kings 6:7:** "When the house was built, it was with stone prepared at the quarry; so that neither hammer nor axe nor any tool of iron was heard in the temple, while it was being built."
- **[Q] 1 Peter 2:5:** "and like living stones be yourselves built into a spiritual house, to be a holy priesthood, to offer spiritual sacrifices acceptable to God through Jesus Christ."
- **What it shows:** Solomon's Temple was built of stones made ready far away, each cut to fit its place. Saint Peter says that Christians are living stones, built by God into a spiritual house on Jesus, the cornerstone (Level 20). God shapes each of us, often quietly and patiently, for the place he has prepared for us in his Church.
- **Source:** 1 Peter 2:4–10; Ephesians 2:19–22; Catechism 756, 1141.

**Where and when:**
- **About 966 BC.** Solomon began the Temple in the fourth year of his reign, and finished it seven years later.
- **Mount Moriah.** Where Abraham was ready to offer Isaac (Level 3), and where David built his altar on Araunah's threshing floor (Level 19; 2 Chronicles 3:1).
- **The cedars of Lebanon.** Tall, fragrant, long-lasting trees from the mountains north of Israel; the cedar is still on the flag of Lebanon.
- **The size of the Temple.** Sixty cubits long and twenty wide, about 30 by 10 meters: twice the size of the tabernacle (Level 11), and built on the same plan.

**Read:** 1 Kings 5:1–12; 6:1–38.

## Episode 4 · The Glory Fills the House

**1 Kings 8; 2 Chronicles 5–7 · about 9 minutes · 3 scenes**

### Scene 4.1 · The ark comes home
*On screen:* The Temple courts (Level 20), crowded with all Israel. The priests carry the ark of the covenant on its poles, up the steps, through the golden doors, into the holy place, and through the veil into the Most Holy Place, under the wide wings of two golden cherubim. *(Play: into the Most Holy Place.)*

1. When the Temple was finished, Solomon gathered all Israel to bring the ark of the covenant up to it, from the city of David.
2. *(Play: into the Most Holy Place.)*
3. **[Q] 1 Kings 8:6** "Then the priests brought the ark of the covenant of the LORD to its place, in the inner sanctuary of the house, in the most holy place, underneath the wings of the cherubim."
4. **[Q] 1 Kings 8:9** "There was nothing in the ark except the two tables of stone which Moses put there at Horeb, where the LORD made a covenant with the people of Israel, when they came out of the land of Egypt."
5. **[Q] 1 Kings 8:10** "And when the priests came out of the holy place, a cloud filled the house of the LORD,"
6. **[Q] 1 Kings 8:11** "so that the priests could not stand to minister because of the cloud; for the glory of the LORD filled the house of the LORD."

### Scene 4.2 · Solomon's prayer
*On screen:* The great altar in the courts. Solomon stands before it with his arms raised to heaven, and the whole people kneel. The cloud of glory shines in the Temple behind him.

1. **[Q] 1 Kings 8:22** "Then Solomon stood before the altar of the LORD in the presence of all the assembly of Israel, and spread forth his hands toward heaven;"
2. **[Q] 1 Kings 8:23** "and said, "O LORD, God of Israel, there is no God like thee, in heaven above or on earth beneath, keeping covenant and showing steadfast love to thy servants who walk before thee with all their heart;"
3. **[Q] 1 Kings 8:27** ""But will God indeed dwell on the earth? Behold, heaven and the highest heaven cannot contain thee; how much less this house which I have built!"
4. **[Q] 1 Kings 8:30** "And hearken thou to the supplication of thy servant and of thy people Israel, when they pray toward this place; yea, hear thou in heaven thy dwelling place; and when thou hearest, forgive."
5. Solomon prayed even for foreigners, from far countries, who would come to pray toward the house of the LORD, "in order that all the peoples of the earth may know thy name."

### Scene 4.3 · Fire from heaven
*On screen:* Fire falls from heaven onto the altar, and the glory of the LORD shines over the whole Temple. All the people bow down with their faces to the pavement.

1. **[Q] 2 Chronicles 7:1** "When Solomon had ended his prayer, fire came down from heaven and consumed the burnt offering and the sacrifices, and the glory of the LORD filled the temple."
2. **[Q] 2 Chronicles 7:3** "When all the children of Israel saw the fire come down and the glory of the LORD upon the temple, they bowed down with their faces to the earth on the pavement, and worshiped and gave thanks to the LORD, saying, "For he is good, for his steadfast love endures for ever.""

### Play: into the Most Holy Place
- **What it is:** a courier route (spec §13.7, kinds 6 and 15).
- **How it plays:** the player leads the priests carrying the ark through the Temple, stage by stage: across the courts, past the great bronze altar and the bronze sea, up the steps to the porch between the two bronze pillars, through the holy place with its golden lampstands and table, and through the veil into the Most Holy Place. At the end, the ark comes to rest under the wings of the cherubim.
- **Teaches:** the plan of God's house, from the outer courts to the place of his presence, and that God chose to live among his people.
- **Gentle mode:** the priests walk the way on their own.
- **Accessibility:** a pointer, or the arrow keys; each stage is announced.

**Thread:** *"Heaven and the highest heaven cannot contain thee; how much less this house" → "Behold, the dwelling of God is with men."* The glowing object is the cloud of glory filling the house.
- **[Q] 1 Kings 8:27:** ""But will God indeed dwell on the earth? Behold, heaven and the highest heaven cannot contain thee; how much less this house which I have built!"
- **[Q] Revelation 21:3:** "and I heard a great voice from the throne saying, "Behold, the dwelling of God is with men. He will dwell with them, and they shall be his people, and God himself will be with them;"
- **[Q] Revelation 21:22:** "And I saw no temple in the city, for its temple is the Lord God the Almighty and the Lamb."
- **What it shows:** Solomon knew that no building could hold God. Yet God chose to dwell among his people. At the end of the Bible, Saint John sees the heavenly city, where there is no temple at all, "for its temple is the Lord God the Almighty and the Lamb." There, God will live with his people for ever, face to face.
- **Source:** Revelation 21:1–4, 21:22–27; Catechism 1045, 2794.

**Where and when:**
- **The dedication.** At the feast of Booths, in the seventh month (1 Kings 8:2), probably in 959 BC.
- **The two tables of stone.** The Ten Commandments given to Moses at Horeb, or Sinai (Level 10).
- **The cherubim.** Two golden angels, each ten cubits (about 5 meters) high, whose wings stretched from wall to wall over the ark.
- **"For he is good."** The song of the Levites, "for his steadfast love endures for ever," is the refrain of Psalm 136.

**Read:** 1 Kings 8:1–30; 2 Chronicles 7:1–3.

## Episode 5 · The Queen of Sheba

**1 Kings 10 · about 7 minutes · 3 scenes**

### Scene 5.1 · From the ends of the earth
*On screen:* The desert road from the far south. A long caravan of camels, laden with spices, gold and jewels, winds toward Jerusalem. At its head rides a queen, under a canopy.

1. **[Q] 1 Kings 10:1** "Now when the queen of Sheba heard of the fame of Solomon concerning the name of the LORD, she came to test him with hard questions."
2. **[Q] 1 Kings 10:2** "She came to Jerusalem with a very great retinue, with camels bearing spices, and very much gold, and precious stones; and when she came to Solomon, she told him all that was on her mind."

### Scene 5.2 · Hard questions
*On screen:* Solomon's throne room, with its golden throne and the twelve lions on its steps. The queen sits facing the king and asks question after question; he answers each one, smiling. *(Play: hard questions.)*

1. **[Q] 1 Kings 10:3** "And Solomon answered all her questions; there was nothing hidden from the king which he could not explain to her."
2. *(Play: hard questions.)*
3. **[Q] 1 Kings 10:6** "And she said to the king, "The report was true which I heard in my own land of your affairs and of your wisdom,"
4. **[Q] 1 Kings 10:7** "but I did not believe the reports until I came and my own eyes had seen it; and behold, the half was not told me; your wisdom and prosperity surpass the report which I heard."

### Scene 5.3 · The half was not told me
*On screen:* The queen's gifts are carried into the hall: chests of gold, jars of spices, precious stones. The queen bows, then turns her caravan for home.

1. **[Q] 1 Kings 10:9** "Blessed be the LORD your God, who has delighted in you and set you on the throne of Israel! Because the LORD loved Israel for ever, he has made you king, that you may execute justice and righteousness.""
2. **[Q] 1 Kings 10:10** "Then she gave the king a hundred and twenty talents of gold, and a very great quantity of spices, and precious stones; never again came such an abundance of spices as these which the queen of Sheba gave to King Solomon."
3. **[Q] 1 Kings 10:23** "Thus King Solomon excelled all the kings of the earth in riches and in wisdom."
4. **[Q] 1 Kings 10:24** "And the whole earth sought the presence of Solomon to hear his wisdom, which God had put into his mind."

### Play: hard questions
- **What it is:** riddles, a decoding play (spec §13.7, kind 18).
- **How it plays:** the queen asks riddles about the things Solomon spoke of, "from the cedar… to the hyssop," the beasts, the birds and the fish. For each riddle the player chooses the answer from three. Each answer appears in the hall as a picture: the cedar of Lebanon, the hyssop on the wall, the lion of the throne, the dove, the fish of the sea.
- **Teaches:** that wisdom begins with looking carefully at God's world and wondering at it.
- **Gentle mode:** the right answer glows.
- **Accessibility:** a pointer, or the arrow keys and Space.

**Thread:** *The Queen of Sheba comes from the ends of the earth to hear Solomon's wisdom → Jesus: "something greater than Solomon is here."* The glowing object is the queen's gift of gold.
- **[Q] 1 Kings 10:1:** "Now when the queen of Sheba heard of the fame of Solomon concerning the name of the LORD, she came to test him with hard questions."
- **[Q] Matthew 12:42:** "The queen of the South will arise at the judgment with this generation and condemn it; for she came from the ends of the earth to hear the wisdom of Solomon, and behold, something greater than Solomon is here."
- **[Q] Matthew 2:11:** "and going into the house they saw the child with Mary his mother, and they fell down and worshiped him. Then, opening their treasures, they offered him gifts, gold and frankincense and myrrh."
- **What it shows:** the queen traveled from the ends of the earth to hear a wise man. Jesus said that something greater than Solomon had come: God's own wisdom in person. And at his birth, wise men came from the east, as the queen had, bringing gold and spices, to worship not a king of Israel but the King of kings.
- **Source:** Matthew 12:42; Matthew 2:1–11; Isaiah 60:6; Psalm 72:10–11; Catechism 528.

**Where and when:**
- **Sheba.** A rich kingdom in the far south of Arabia, in today's Yemen, famous for frankincense and myrrh. Some traditions place it in Ethiopia.
- **Solomon's throne.** Made of ivory and overlaid with gold, with six steps and twelve lions (1 Kings 10:18–20).
- **A hundred and twenty talents of gold.** About four tonnes of gold.

**Read:** 1 Kings 10:1–13; Matthew 12:42.

## Episode 6 · Solomon's Heart Turns Away

**1 Kings 11 · about 7 minutes · 3 scenes**

> **A note before this episode:** This is a sad story: the wise king turns away from God. His worship of other gods is told simply.

### Scene 6.1 · His heart was not wholly true
*On screen:* Solomon, now old and gray, walks with his foreign wives up the hill east of Jerusalem. On its slopes, little shrines to other gods have been built, with smoke rising from their altars. Below, the Temple shines on its hill, and Solomon turns his back on it.

1. As he grew richer and more powerful, Solomon married many foreign princesses, against the command of God, who had warned that they would turn his heart to their own gods.
2. **[Q] 1 Kings 11:4** "For when Solomon was old his wives turned away his heart after other gods; and his heart was not wholly true to the LORD his God, as was the heart of David his father."
3. On the hill east of Jerusalem, Solomon built shrines to the gods of his wives.
4. **[Q] 1 Kings 11:9** "And the LORD was angry with Solomon, because his heart had turned away from the LORD, the God of Israel, who had appeared to him twice,"

### Scene 6.2 · "I will surely tear the kingdom from you"
*On screen:* Night in the palace. Old Solomon sits alone on his golden throne; the hall is dark and empty. A voice speaks to him out of the darkness. *(Play: a heart wholly true.)*

1. **[Q] 1 Kings 11:11** "Therefore the LORD said to Solomon, "Since this has been your mind and you have not kept my covenant and my statutes which I have commanded you, I will surely tear the kingdom from you and will give it to your servant."
2. **[Q] 1 Kings 11:12** "Yet for the sake of David your father I will not do it in your days, but I will tear it out of the hand of your son."
3. *(Play: a heart wholly true.)*
4. **[Q] 1 Kings 11:13** "However I will not tear away all the kingdom; but I will give one tribe to your son, for the sake of David my servant and for the sake of Jerusalem which I have chosen.""

### Scene 6.3 · Twelve pieces
*On screen:* An open road in the country. The prophet Ahijah, in a new cloak, meets a young man named Jeroboam. He takes off his cloak and tears it into twelve pieces, and hands ten of them to Jeroboam.

1. **[Q] 1 Kings 11:29** "And at that time, when Jeroboam went out of Jerusalem, the prophet Ahijah the Shilonite found him on the road. Now Ahijah had clad himself with a new garment; and the two of them were alone in the open country."
2. **[Q] 1 Kings 11:30** "Then Ahijah laid hold of the new garment that was on him, and tore it into twelve pieces."
3. **[Q] 1 Kings 11:31** "And he said to Jeroboam, "Take for yourself ten pieces; for thus says the LORD, the God of Israel, 'Behold, I am about to tear the kingdom from the hand of Solomon, and will give you ten tribes"
4. Solomon died after reigning forty years, and his son Rehoboam became king. The story of the kingdom torn in two is told in Level 23.

### Play: a heart wholly true
- **What it is:** a quiet choosing interaction (spec §13.7, kind 20).
- **How it plays:** Solomon's life comes back to him as a road with crossroads. At each one, the player chooses what a heart wholly true to God would do: when God says "Ask," ask for wisdom; when the Temple is built, give it to God; when foreign wives bring their gods, keep God's command; when they ask for shrines on the hill, worship the LORD alone. With each true choice, a lamp lights along the road.
- **Teaches:** that hearts don't usually turn away all at once, but one small choice at a time, and that we can choose God again at every crossroads.
- **Gentle mode:** the true choice glows.
- **Accessibility:** a pointer, or the arrow keys and Space.

**Thread:** *Solomon's "heart was not wholly true to the LORD his God" → Jesus: "No one can serve two masters."* The glowing object is Ahijah's torn cloak.
- **[Q] 1 Kings 11:4:** "For when Solomon was old his wives turned away his heart after other gods; and his heart was not wholly true to the LORD his God, as was the heart of David his father."
- **[Q] Matthew 6:24:** ""No one can serve two masters; for either he will hate the one and love the other, or he will be devoted to the one and despise the other. You cannot serve God and mammon."
- **[Q] Matthew 6:29:** "yet I tell you, even Solomon in all his glory was not arrayed like one of these."
- **What it shows:** Solomon had everything: wisdom, riches, glory. But his heart was divided, and a divided heart can't hold together; neither could his kingdom. Jesus said that even "Solomon in all his glory" wasn't dressed as beautifully as a lily that trusts God. He asks for our whole heart, because only he can make it whole.
- **Source:** Matthew 6:19–33; Deuteronomy 6:5; Catechism 2113, 2544.

**Where and when:**
- **About 931 BC.** Solomon died after reigning forty years, like his father David.
- **The hill east of Jerusalem.** The Mount of Olives (Level 19). Later it was called "the mount of corruption" because of Solomon's shrines (2 Kings 23:13).
- **Ahijah of Shiloh.** A prophet from the town where the tabernacle had stood (Level 17).
- **Jeroboam.** A capable young man whom Solomon had put in charge of the forced labor of the northern tribes. He will be the first king of the north (Level 23).

**Read:** 1 Kings 11:1–13, 26–43.
