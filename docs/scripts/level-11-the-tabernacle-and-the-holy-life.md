# Level 11 script: *The Tabernacle and the Holy Life*

**Bible:** Exodus 25–31; 35–40; Leviticus 1–3; 6; 8–9; 16; 19; 23
**Age:** IV · The Covenant in the Desert
**Length:** about 45 minutes (7 episodes, then the quiz, then reflection and prayer)
**Status:** draft, waiting for Dave's approval.

## How to read this script

- Each **scene** is a checkpoint. Its numbered lines are the narration beats; the player taps to move from one to the next.
- *On screen* describes what the scene shows. It's the brief for the art in both styles.
- **[Q]** marks an exact quotation from the Bible. Its wording in both translations is in the verse table at the end. Everything else is our own retelling.
- **Play** is the episode's interactive moment. **Thread** is its golden thread. **Read** is the passage the game sends the player to.
- "LORD" in capitals stands for God's holy name, printed in small capitals in the RSV.

### What this level is about

God's people are still camped at Mount Sinai. God has forgiven them after the golden calf, and now he does something astonishing: he asks them to make him a tent, so that he can live in the middle of their camp. Everyone gives, and the craftsmen God has filled with his Spirit build the tabernacle, with the ark of the covenant at its heart. When it is finished, the cloud of God's glory comes down and fills it. Then God shows his people how to live close to him: priests to serve him, offerings to give him, a day each year when their sins are carried away, feasts to mark the year, and a holy life of love for one another: "You shall love your neighbor as yourself."

The tabernacle and its worship can seem far away from us, but almost everything in it points to Jesus: God dwelling among us in his body, the great high priest who offered himself once for all, the veil torn open so that everyone can come near to God. Mary is the new ark, the Church keeps the feasts of Easter and Pentecost on the old feasts, and the law of love comes alive in the Good Samaritan.

**How it's shown:** the offerings are never shown being killed. They are shown as smoke rising from the altar.

## Episode 1 · The Pattern of the Tabernacle

**Exodus 25:1–9; 31:1–6; 35:20–36:7; 39:32–43 · about 7 minutes · 3 scenes**

### Scene 1.1 · "That I may dwell in their midst"
*On screen:* Mount Sinai in its cloud. High on the mountain, Moses listens, and lines of golden light draw a plan in the air before him: a court, a tent, and a chest with two winged figures on its lid. Far below, the camp of tents.

1. On the mountain, during his forty days with God, Moses had been shown something more than the commandments. God had shown him a plan.
2. **[Q] Exodus 25:2** ""Speak to the people of Israel, that they take for me an offering; from every man whose heart makes him willing you shall receive the offering for me."
3. **[Q] Exodus 25:8** "And let them make me a sanctuary, that I may dwell in their midst."
4. **[Q] Exodus 25:9** "According to all that I show you concerning the pattern of the tabernacle, and of all its furniture, so you shall make it."
5. The people lived in tents. God wanted a tent too, in the middle of theirs. Now that he had forgiven them, it was time to build it.

### Scene 1.2 · Gifts for the sanctuary
*On screen:* Moses speaks to the people. Then families come from their tents carrying their gifts to a workshop at the edge of the camp: gold earrings and bracelets, silver, bronze, blue, purple and scarlet yarn, fine linen, goats' hair, rams' skins, acacia wood, jars of oil, spices and onyx stones. The heap grows and grows. *(Play: gifts for the sanctuary.)*

1. **[Q] Exodus 35:21** "And they came, every one whose heart stirred him, and every one whose spirit moved him, and brought the LORD's offering to be used for the tent of meeting, and for all its service, and for the holy garments."
2. **[Q] Exodus 35:22** "So they came, both men and women; all who were of a willing heart brought brooches and earrings and signet rings and armlets, all sorts of gold objects, every man dedicating an offering of gold to the LORD."
3. The same people who had given their gold for a calf now gave it gladly for God's house.
4. *(Play: gifts for the sanctuary.)*
5. **[Q] Exodus 36:5** "and said to Moses, "The people bring much more than enough for doing the work which the LORD has commanded us to do.""
6. **[Q] Exodus 36:6** "So Moses gave command, and word was proclaimed throughout the camp, "Let neither man nor woman do anything more for the offering for the sanctuary." So the people were restrained from bringing;"
7. **[Q] Exodus 36:7** "for the stuff they had was sufficient to do all the work, and more."

### Scene 1.3 · Filled with the Spirit of God
*On screen:* The workshop, busy. Bezalel hammers gold at a little forge; Oholiab weaves blue, purple and scarlet; women spin goats' hair; men saw acacia boards. At the end, all the finished pieces are set out before Moses, and he lifts his hands to bless the workers.

1. **[Q] Exodus 31:2** ""See, I have called by name Bezalel the son of Uri, son of Hur, of the tribe of Judah:"
2. **[Q] Exodus 31:3** "and I have filled him with the Spirit of God, with ability and intelligence, with knowledge and all craftsmanship,"
3. Bezalel and Oholiab taught the others, and men and women worked side by side, each with the gift God had given.
4. **[Q] Exodus 39:42** "According to all that the LORD had commanded Moses, so the people of Israel had done all the work."
5. **[Q] Exodus 39:43** "And Moses saw all the work, and behold, they had done it; as the LORD had commanded, so had they done it. And Moses blessed them."

### Play: gifts for the sanctuary
- **What it is:** herding and gathering (spec §13.7, kind 2).
- **How it plays:** the workshop, with a row of empty places for everything on God's list (Exodus 25:3–7): gold, silver, bronze, blue, purple and scarlet yarn, fine linen, goats' hair, rams' skins, acacia wood, oil for the lamps, spices and onyx stones. People keep coming from the tents with their gifts. The player gathers each gift and brings it to its place (drag it, or choose with the arrow keys and Space). When every place is filled, the people go on coming, and the heap grows past the list until the craftsmen run to Moses: "much more than enough."
- **Teaches:** that the tabernacle was built from gifts given freely, from willing hearts; that every gift, great or small, had its place.
- **Gentle mode:** a tap on a gift sends it to its place by itself.
- **Accessibility:** a pointer, or the arrow keys and Space.

**Thread:** *"Let them make me a sanctuary, that I may dwell in their midst" → Jesus: "Destroy this temple, and in three days I will raise it up," and "You are God's temple."* The glowing object is the plan of the tabernacle, drawn in light before Moses on the mountain.
- **[Q] John 2:19:** "Jesus answered them, "Destroy this temple, and in three days I will raise it up.""
- **[Q] John 2:21:** "But he spoke of the temple of his body."
- **[Q] 1 Corinthians 3:16:** "Do you not know that you are God's temple and that God's Spirit dwells in you?"
- **What it shows:** God wanted to live among his people, so he had them build him a tent in the middle of their camp. Later, Solomon would build him a temple of stone in Jerusalem. But Jesus spoke of a greater temple: his own body. In Jesus, God came to live among us in person. And through Baptism, the Holy Spirit makes every Christian a temple of God, and every church is God's house among the houses of his people.
- **Source:** John 2:13–22; John 1:14; 1 Corinthians 3:16–17; 6:19; Revelation 21:3; Catechism 586, 797, 1197.

**Where and when:**
- **The tabernacle.** The word means "dwelling." It was a tent about 13.5 meters long and 4.5 meters wide (30 cubits by 10), standing in a court of linen hangings about 45 meters by 22 (Exodus 27:18). It could be taken down and carried, so God went with his people on their journey.
- **Acacia wood.** One of the few trees that grow in the Sinai desert. Its wood is hard, and insects leave it alone.
- **Bezalel** was the grandson of Hur, perhaps the same Hur who held up Moses' hands at Rephidim (Level 9).

**Read:** Exodus 25:1–9; 35:20–36:7; 39:32–43.

## Episode 2 · The Ark of the Covenant

**Exodus 25:10–40; 27:20–21; 40:1–38 · about 8 minutes · 4 scenes**

### Scene 2.1 · The ark and the mercy seat
*On screen:* The workshop. A chest of acacia wood covered with gold, with golden rings and carrying poles. Bezalel sets its lid of pure gold on top: the mercy seat, with two golden cherubim facing each other, their wings spread over it.

1. **[Q] Exodus 25:10** ""They shall make an ark of acacia wood; two cubits and a half shall be its length, a cubit and a half its breadth, and a cubit and a half its height."
2. **[Q] Exodus 25:20** "The cherubim shall spread out their wings above, overshadowing the mercy seat with their wings, their faces one to another; toward the mercy seat shall the faces of the cherubim be."
3. **[Q] Exodus 25:21** "And you shall put the mercy seat on the top of the ark; and in the ark you shall put the testimony that I shall give you."
4. **[Q] Exodus 25:22** "There I will meet with you, and from above the mercy seat, from between the two cherubim that are upon the ark of the testimony, I will speak with you of all that I will give you in commandment for the people of Israel."
5. The ark was the heart of the whole tabernacle: the place where God would meet his people.

### Scene 2.2 · Bread and light
*On screen:* A golden table with twelve loaves in two rows. Beside it, the golden lampstand, with seven branches like the branches of an almond tree, and a flame on each one.

1. **[Q] Exodus 25:30** "And you shall set the bread of the Presence on the table before me always."
2. Twelve loaves, one for each tribe, were always before God, and every sabbath they were made new.
3. **[Q] Exodus 25:37** "And you shall make the seven lamps for it; and the lamps shall be set up so as to give light upon the space in front of it."
4. **[Q] Exodus 27:20** ""And you shall command the people of Israel that they bring to you pure beaten olive oil for the light, that a lamp may be set up to burn continually."

### Scene 2.3 · Raising the tabernacle
*On screen:* The first day of spring, a year after the people left Egypt. Bare ground in the middle of the camp, with all the finished pieces laid out around it, and the people gathered to watch. *(Play: raise the tabernacle.)*

1. **[Q] Exodus 40:2** ""On the first day of the first month you shall erect the tabernacle of the tent of meeting."
2. **[Q] Exodus 40:17** "And in the first month in the second year, on the first day of the month, the tabernacle was erected."
3. *(Play: raise the tabernacle.)*
4. **[Q] Exodus 40:33** "And he erected the court round the tabernacle and the altar, and set up the screen of the gate of the court. So Moses finished the work."

### Scene 2.4 · The glory of the LORD
*On screen:* The finished tabernacle in its white court, in the middle of the camp. The cloud comes down and covers the tent, and light fills it from within; Moses steps back from the door. Night falls, and the cloud glows like fire over the sleeping camp.

1. **[Q] Exodus 40:34** "Then the cloud covered the tent of meeting, and the glory of the LORD filled the tabernacle."
2. **[Q] Exodus 40:35** "And Moses was not able to enter the tent of meeting, because the cloud abode upon it, and the glory of the LORD filled the tabernacle."
3. **[Q] Exodus 40:36** "Throughout all their journeys, whenever the cloud was taken up from over the tabernacle, the people of Israel would go onward;"
4. **[Q] Exodus 40:38** "For throughout all their journeys the cloud of the LORD was upon the tabernacle by day, and fire was in it by night, in the sight of all the house of Israel."
5. God had come to live among his people, as he had promised.

### Play: raise the tabernacle
- **What it is:** building and stacking (spec §13.7, kind 3; the spec names the tabernacle).
- **How it plays:** the bare ground, and the pieces Bezalel made laid out around it. The player raises the tabernacle in the order of Exodus 40, choosing at each step which piece comes next (tap, or the arrow keys and Space): the bases and the frames; the coverings of the tent; the ark, with the tablets inside and the mercy seat on top; the veil in front of it; the table with its bread; the lampstand; the golden altar of incense; the screen for the door; the altar of burnt offering; the laver of water; and last, the hangings of the court. Each piece rises into its place, and the words "as the LORD had commanded Moses" shine out. A wrong choice gets a gentle "not yet."
- **Teaches:** the parts of the tabernacle and where each one stands, from the ark in the innermost room out to the court; that it was made just as God had shown.
- **Gentle mode:** the piece that comes next glows.
- **Accessibility:** a pointer, or the arrow keys and Space.

**Thread:** *The ark of the covenant, overshadowed by the cloud of God's glory → Mary: "the power of the Most High will overshadow you."* The glowing object is the ark.
- **[Q] Luke 1:35:** "And the angel said to her, "The Holy Spirit will come upon you, and the power of the Most High will overshadow you; therefore the child to be born will be called holy, the Son of God."
- **[Q] Revelation 11:19:** "Then God's temple in heaven was opened, and the ark of his covenant was seen within his temple; and there were flashes of lightning, loud noises, peals of thunder, an earthquake, and heavy hail."
- **What it shows:** The ark held God's word, written on stone, and the cloud of his glory covered it. In the Greek Bible the apostles read, the cloud "overshadowed" the tabernacle, the same word the angel used to Mary. The Word of God himself came to live within her. That is why the Church calls Mary the Ark of the Covenant. In the book of Revelation, Saint John sees the ark of the covenant in heaven, and straight after it, "a woman clothed with the sun."
- **Source:** Luke 1:35, 39–45 (compare 2 Samuel 6:9–11); Revelation 11:19–12:1; Catechism 2676; the Litany of Loreto.

**Where and when:**
- **The ark.** A chest about 1.1 meters long and 0.7 meters wide and high, carried on poles so that no one would touch it. Inside were the tablets of the covenant; later a jar of manna and Aaron's rod were kept with it (Hebrews 9:4).
- **The mercy seat.** Its Hebrew name means "the place of atonement" (see Episode 5).
- **What became of it?** The ark was lost when the Babylonians destroyed Jerusalem in 587 BC. The second book of Maccabees says the prophet Jeremiah hid it in a cave on the mountain where Moses saw the promised land (2 Maccabees 2:4–8).
- **A lamp always burning.** In every Catholic church, the Eucharist is kept in a tabernacle, with a lamp burning beside it.

**Read:** Exodus 25:10–40; 40:1–38.

## Episode 3 · Priests and Their Vestments

**Exodus 28:1–38; Leviticus 8:1–13; 9:22–23 · about 7 minutes · 3 scenes**

### Scene 3.1 · For glory and for beauty
*On screen:* Aaron and his four sons before the tabernacle. Beside them, the holy garments the craftsmen have made: a robe of blue with golden bells and pomegranates on its hem, an ephod with an onyx stone on each shoulder, a breastpiece with twelve precious stones in four rows, a linen turban and a plate of gold.

1. **[Q] Exodus 28:1** ""Then bring near to you Aaron your brother, and his sons with him, from among the people of Israel, to serve me as priests—Aaron and Aaron's sons, Nadab and Abihu, Eleazar and Ithamar."
2. **[Q] Exodus 28:2** "And you shall make holy garments for Aaron your brother, for glory and for beauty."
3. On Aaron's shoulders were two onyx stones, and on his heart twelve precious stones, each engraved with the name of one of the tribes.
4. **[Q] Exodus 28:29** "So Aaron shall bear the names of the sons of Israel in the breastpiece of judgment upon his heart, when he goes into the holy place, to bring them to continual remembrance before the LORD."
5. **[Q] Exodus 28:36** ""And you shall make a plate of pure gold, and engrave on it, like the engraving of a signet, 'Holy to the LORD.'"

### Scene 3.2 · Moses clothes Aaron
*On screen:* The door of the tent of meeting, with all the people gathered. Moses washes Aaron and his sons with water from the laver, and dresses Aaron, piece by piece. Then he pours oil on Aaron's head. *(Play: clothed for glory.)*

1. **[Q] Leviticus 8:4** "And Moses did as the LORD commanded him; and the congregation was assembled at the door of the tent of meeting."
2. **[Q] Leviticus 8:6** "And Moses brought Aaron and his sons, and washed them with water."
3. *(Play: clothed for glory.)*
4. **[Q] Leviticus 8:12** "And he poured some of the anointing oil on Aaron's head, and anointed him, to consecrate him."

### Scene 3.3 · Aaron blesses the people
*On screen:* Aaron, in his robes, lifts his hands over the people. Moses and Aaron go into the tent, then come out together, and a light shines over the whole camp.

1. **[Q] Leviticus 9:22** "Then Aaron lifted up his hands toward the people and blessed them; and he came down from offering the sin offering and the burnt offering and the peace offerings."
2. **[Q] Leviticus 9:23** "And Moses and Aaron went into the tent of meeting; and when they came out they blessed the people, and the glory of the LORD appeared to all the people."
3. Aaron stood between God and his people. He brought their gifts to God, and God's blessing to them.

### Play: clothed for glory
- **What it is:** ordering (spec §13.7, kind 9).
- **How it plays:** Aaron stands at the door of the tent in plain linen. The holy garments hang on a rail beside him, and the player dresses him in the order of Leviticus 8:7–9 (tap, or the arrow keys and Space): the coat, the girdle, the blue robe, the ephod with its two onyx stones, the breastpiece with its twelve stones, the turban, and last, the golden plate, "Holy to the LORD." As each piece goes on, its meaning is shown: the names of the tribes on his shoulders and on his heart, the bells on his hem, the holy name on his forehead. A wrong choice gets a gentle "not yet."
- **Teaches:** the high priest's garments, and what they meant: Aaron carried the whole people before God.
- **Gentle mode:** the next garment glows.
- **Accessibility:** a pointer, or the arrow keys and Space.

**Thread:** *Aaron the high priest, carrying the names of his people on his heart → "We have a great high priest… Jesus, the Son of God."* The glowing object is the breastpiece with the twelve names.
- **[Q] Hebrews 4:14:** "Since then we have a great high priest who has passed through the heavens, Jesus, the Son of God, let us hold fast our confession."
- **[Q] Hebrews 4:15:** "For we have not a high priest who is unable to sympathize with our weaknesses, but one who in every respect has been tempted as we are, yet without sinning."
- **What it shows:** Aaron carried the names of all twelve tribes on his heart when he went in before God. Jesus is the great high priest. He does not carry our names on stones: he took our human nature, was tempted in every way as we are, yet without sin, and carries each of us before the Father. The priests of the new covenant share in his one priesthood.
- **Source:** Hebrews 4:14–5:10; 7:23–27; Catechism 1539–1545.

**Where and when:**
- **The tribe of Levi.** Moses and Aaron belonged to the tribe of Levi. Aaron's sons became the priests, and the rest of the tribe served at the tabernacle (Numbers 3).
- **The Urim and the Thummim.** They were kept in the breastpiece and used to ask God's will. No one knows today what they looked like.
- **Golden bells.** They hung on the hem of the robe, so that Aaron would be heard when he went into the holy place (Exodus 28:33–35).
- **Vestments today.** At Mass, priests wear vestments too: a white linen alb like Aaron's coat, a stole and a chasuble.

**Read:** Exodus 28:1–38; Leviticus 8:1–13; 9:22–23.

## Episode 4 · Offerings and Sacrifices

**Leviticus 1–3; 6:8–13; 9:24 · about 6 minutes · 3 scenes**

### Scene 4.1 · When any man of you brings an offering
*On screen:* The tent of meeting, with God's voice as light from within. A family brings a lamb to the gate of the court, and the father lays his hand on its head. The scene moves to smoke rising from the altar into the sky.

1. **[Q] Leviticus 1:1** "The LORD called Moses, and spoke to him from the tent of meeting, saying,"
2. **[Q] Leviticus 1:2** ""Speak to the people of Israel, and say to them, When any man of you brings an offering to the LORD, you shall bring your offering of cattle from the herd or from the flock."
3. **[Q] Leviticus 1:4** "he shall lay his hand upon the head of the burnt offering, and it shall be accepted for him to make atonement for him."
4. A burnt offering was given to God whole, and went up in smoke from the altar: a sign of giving God everything.

### Scene 4.2 · Flour, oil and frankincense
*On screen:* A poor widow brings a bowl of fine flour, a little jar of oil and some frankincense. The priest takes a handful and burns it on the altar. Then, outside their tent, a family shares a meal of thanks.

1. **[Q] Leviticus 2:1** ""When any one brings a cereal offering as an offering to the LORD, his offering shall be of fine flour; he shall pour oil upon it, and put frankincense on it,"
2. **[Q] Leviticus 2:2** "and bring it to Aaron's sons the priests. And he shall take from it a handful of the fine flour and oil, with all of its frankincense; and the priest shall burn this as its memorial portion upon the altar, an offering by fire, a pleasing odor to the LORD."
3. With a peace offering, part was burned on the altar, and the family who brought it ate the rest together, a meal of thanks before God.
4. Some offerings gave thanks; some asked forgiveness for sin. All of them said: everything we have comes from God, and belongs to him.

### Scene 4.3 · The fire shall not go out
*On screen:* The altar of burnt offering at dawn; a priest lays wood on the fire, and it burns through the day and the night. Then the day of the first offerings: fire flashes out from the tent and consumes the offering on the altar, and the people shout and fall on their faces. *(Play: keep the fire burning.)*

1. **[Q] Leviticus 6:12** "The fire on the altar shall be kept burning on it, it shall not go out; the priest shall burn wood on it every morning, and he shall lay the burnt offering in order upon it, and shall burn on it the fat of the peace offerings."
2. **[Q] Leviticus 6:13** "Fire shall be kept burning upon the altar continually; it shall not go out."
3. *(Play: keep the fire burning.)*
4. **[Q] Leviticus 9:24** "And fire came forth from before the LORD and consumed the burnt offering and the fat upon the altar; and when all the people saw it, they shouted, and fell on their faces."

### Play: keep the fire burning
- **What it is:** timing (spec §13.7, kind 16).
- **How it plays:** the altar fire, through one day and one night, as the sun crosses the sky and the stars come out. The fire slowly sinks, and a ring of light around it shows when it needs wood. The player lays on wood at the right moment (tap, or Space): too early and the priest waits, too late and the flames grow small, but the fire never goes out. Morning and evening, the priest lays the offering on it, and the smoke goes up.
- **Teaches:** the fire on the altar that never went out; the offering of each morning and evening, like the morning and evening prayer of the Church.
- **Gentle mode:** the moment for wood lasts much longer.
- **Accessibility:** a pointer, or Space.

**Thread:** *The offerings on the altar, "a pleasing odor to the LORD" → "Christ loved us and gave himself up for us, a fragrant offering and sacrifice to God."* The glowing object is the fire on the altar.
- **[Q] Ephesians 5:2:** "And walk in love, as Christ loved us and gave himself up for us, a fragrant offering and sacrifice to God."
- **[Q] Hebrews 10:10:** "And by that will we have been sanctified through the offering of the body of Jesus Christ once for all."
- **What it shows:** Every offering on the altar was a gift to God, and a sign of a greater gift to come. The blood of animals could never truly take away sin (Hebrews 10:4). Jesus offered himself: his whole life, given in love on the cross, "once for all." At every Mass, that one sacrifice is made present, and we offer ourselves with him.
- **Source:** Hebrews 10:1–14; Ephesians 5:1–2; Romans 12:1; Catechism 613–614, 1366–1367, 2099–2100.

**Where and when:**
- **Five kinds of offering.** The burnt offering, given whole; the cereal offering of flour and oil; the peace offering, shared as a meal; and the sin offering and the guilt offering, asking forgiveness (Leviticus 1–7).
- **Without blemish.** Only the best of the flock or the herd was offered.
- **The offering of the poor.** A family too poor for a lamb could bring two turtledoves or two pigeons (Leviticus 12:8). Mary and Joseph brought this offering when they presented Jesus in the Temple (Luke 2:24).
- **A hand on its head.** The giver laid a hand on the offering, as if to say, "This stands for me."

**Read:** Leviticus 1:1–9; 2:1–3; 6:8–13; 9:22–24.

## Episode 5 · The Day of Atonement

**Leviticus 16 · about 7 minutes · 4 scenes**

### Scene 5.1 · Once in the year
*On screen:* Aaron alone at dawn. He bathes, then puts on plain white linen, with no gold at all. Inside the tent, the light of the lampstand falls on the veil.

1. **[Q] Leviticus 16:2** "and the LORD said to Moses, "Tell Aaron your brother not to come at all times into the holy place within the veil, before the mercy seat which is upon the ark, lest he die; for I will appear in the cloud upon the mercy seat."
2. Only one man, on one day a year, could pass the veil and come before the ark: the high priest, on the Day of Atonement.
3. **[Q] Leviticus 16:4** "He shall put on the holy linen coat, and shall have the linen breeches on his body, be girded with the linen girdle, and wear the linen turban; these are the holy garments. He shall bathe his body in water, and then put them on."
4. **[Q] Leviticus 16:34** "And this shall be an everlasting statute for you, that atonement may be made for the people of Israel once in the year because of all their sins." And Moses did as the LORD commanded him."

### Scene 5.2 · Within the veil
*On screen:* Aaron lifts the veil and steps through, a censer of glowing coals in his hand. A cloud of incense fills the little room and hides the ark and the cherubim. Seven drops of light fall before the mercy seat.

1. **[Q] Leviticus 16:12** "And he shall take a censer full of coals of fire from the altar before the LORD, and two handfuls of sweet incense beaten small; and he shall bring it within the veil"
2. **[Q] Leviticus 16:13** "and put the incense on the fire before the LORD, that the cloud of the incense may cover the mercy seat which is upon the testimony, lest he die;"
3. **[Q] Leviticus 16:15** ""Then he shall kill the goat of the sin offering which is for the people, and bring its blood within the veil, and do with its blood as he did with the blood of the bull, sprinkling it upon the mercy seat and before the mercy seat;"
4. **[Q] Leviticus 16:16** "thus he shall make atonement for the holy place, because of the uncleannesses of the people of Israel, and because of their transgressions, all their sins; and so he shall do for the tent of meeting, which abides with them in the midst of their uncleannesses."

### Scene 5.3 · The goat that was sent away
*On screen:* The court. Two goats stand before the tent; Aaron casts lots. Then he lays both hands on the head of one, and a man leads it out of the camp into the empty desert, smaller and smaller, until it is gone. *(Play: carried far away.)*

1. **[Q] Leviticus 16:8** "and Aaron shall cast lots upon the two goats, one lot for the LORD and the other lot for Azazel."
2. **[Q] Leviticus 16:21** "and Aaron shall lay both his hands upon the head of the live goat, and confess over him all the iniquities of the people of Israel, and all their transgressions, all their sins; and he shall put them upon the head of the goat, and send him away into the wilderness by the hand of a man who is in readiness."
3. *(Play: carried far away.)*
4. **[Q] Leviticus 16:22** "The goat shall bear all their iniquities upon him to a solitary land; and he shall let the goat go in the wilderness."

### Scene 5.4 · Clean before the LORD
*On screen:* Evening. Aaron comes out in his golden garments. The people, who have fasted all day, lift their heads.

1. **[Q] Leviticus 16:30** "for on this day shall atonement be made for you, to cleanse you; from all your sins you shall be clean before the LORD."
2. Every year the people were made clean, and every year they needed it again. They were waiting for something more.

### Play: carried far away
- **What it is:** a quiet interaction (spec §13.7, kind 20).
- **How it plays:** Aaron's hands rest on the head of the live goat. Around the camp, the people's sins are shown as small dark stones, each with a word: pride, lies, anger, stealing, unkindness, forgetting God. The player brings each one to the goat (drag it, or choose with the arrow keys and Space) as Aaron confesses them. When all are laid on it, the player holds Space (or presses and holds) to send it away, and the goat walks off into the desert, carrying every one, smaller and smaller until it is out of sight. The camp is clear.
- **Teaches:** that God does not just cover sin; he takes it away, far from us.
- **Gentle mode:** a tap on a stone carries it to the goat by itself.
- **Accessibility:** a pointer, or the arrow keys and Space.

**Thread:** *The high priest passes the veil once a year with the blood of the offering → Christ "entered once for all into the Holy Place, taking not the blood of goats and calves but his own blood," and the curtain of the temple is torn in two.* The glowing object is the veil before the ark.
- **[Q] Hebrews 9:11:** "But when Christ appeared as a high priest of the good things that have come, then through the greater and more perfect tent (not made with hands, that is, not of this creation)"
- **[Q] Hebrews 9:12:** "he entered once for all into the Holy Place, taking not the blood of goats and calves but his own blood, thus securing an eternal redemption."
- **[Q] Mark 15:37:** "And Jesus uttered a loud cry, and breathed his last."
- **[Q] Mark 15:38:** "And the curtain of the temple was torn in two, from top to bottom."
- **What it shows:** Once a year, one man could pass the veil into God's presence, and only for a short time. When Jesus died on the cross, the curtain of the Temple was torn in two, from top to bottom: torn by God, from above. Jesus, our high priest, has gone into the presence of God for us, not with the blood of goats, but with his own, and now the way to God is open to everyone.
- **Source:** Hebrews 9:1–14, 24–28; 10:19–22; Romans 3:25; Catechism 433, 662.

**Where and when:**
- **Yom Kippur.** The Day of Atonement fell on the tenth day of the seventh month, in September or October. It is still the holiest day of the Jewish year, kept with fasting and prayer.
- **Azazel.** No one is sure what the word means: perhaps the name of a spirit of the desert, or "the goat that goes away." William Tyndale translated it "scapegoat" in 1530, and that is where the English word comes from.
- **The holy of holies.** The inner room of the tabernacle, behind the veil, probably a perfect cube. It held only the ark.

**Read:** Leviticus 16.

### Scene 6.1 · The sabbath and the passover
*On screen:* The year as a great wheel of twelve moons around the tabernacle. A family resting on the sabbath. Then a spring evening: a family at the Passover meal, with unleavened bread on the table.

*On screen:* Autumn. Rams' horns sound at the new moon; then a quiet day of fasting. Then the whole camp is full of shelters made of branches of palm and willow, and families eat under their leafy roofs, looking up at the stars through the branches. *(Play: the feasts of the year.)*

3. **[Q] Leviticus 23:42** "You shall dwell in booths for seven days; all that are native in Israel shall dwell in booths,"

5. *(Play: the feasts of the year.)*

### Play: the feasts of the year
- **What it is:** matching and naming (spec §13.7, kind 10).
- **How it plays:** the year is a wheel of twelve moons around the tabernacle. One by one the feasts come up, each with its sign: unleavened bread (the passover), the first sheaf (the first fruits), two loaves of new wheat (the feast of weeks), a ram's horn (the day of trumpets), the veil (the Day of Atonement), and a shelter of branches (the feast of booths). The player matches each one to what it remembers or gives thanks for (tap, or the arrow keys and Space): the night God set his people free from Egypt; the first of the harvest, given back to God; the wheat harvest, fifty days later; a day for remembering, at the turn of the year; the day their sins were taken away; the years of living in tents in the wilderness. Each feast then takes its place on the wheel, at its season.
- **Teaches:** the feasts of Israel, and how the whole year became a way of remembering God.
- **Gentle mode:** the right match glows.
- **Accessibility:** a pointer, or the arrow keys and Space.

**Thread:** *The first sheaf, waved before the LORD "on the morrow after the sabbath," and the feast fifty days later → Christ raised from the dead, "the first fruits," and the Holy Spirit given at Pentecost.* The glowing object is the first sheaf.
- **[Q] 1 Corinthians 15:20:** "But in fact Christ has been raised from the dead, the first fruits of those who have fallen asleep."
- **[Q] Acts 2:1:** "When the day of Pentecost had come, they were all together in one place."
- **What it shows:** The first sheaf of the harvest was given to God on the day after the sabbath, in the week of the Passover. Jesus rose from the dead on that very day, the first day of the week: the first fruits of all who will rise. Fifty days later, at the feast of weeks (in Greek, Pentecost, "the fiftieth"), the Holy Spirit came down on the apostles, and the great harvest began: about three thousand were baptized that day. The Church still keeps Easter and Pentecost on these feasts.
- **Source:** 1 Corinthians 15:20–23; Acts 2:1–41; Catechism 731, 1096, 1169.

**Where and when:**
- **Three pilgrim feasts.** Passover, the feast of weeks and the feast of booths. On each, every man was to go up to appear before the LORD (Deuteronomy 16:16), and Jesus went up to Jerusalem for them (John 2:13; 7:10).
- **A calendar of moons.** Each month began with the new moon. The first month fell in March or April, the seventh in September or October.
- **The feast of booths.** Jewish families still build shelters of branches each autumn. On the last day of this feast, Jesus stood up in the Temple and invited everyone who is thirsty to come to him and drink (John 7:37).

**Read:** Leviticus 23.

*On screen:* Scenes of the camp: a neighbor moves a stone from the path of a blind man; a young man stands up as an old man passes; a family welcomes a stranger to their meal.

1. **[Q] Leviticus 19:14** "You shall not curse the deaf or put a stumbling block before the blind, but you shall fear your God: I am the LORD."

3. **[Q] Leviticus 19:32** ""You shall rise up before the hoary head, and honor the face of an old man, and you shall fear your God: I am the LORD."

5. Again and again God said, "I am the LORD." Loving their neighbor was how his people would show whose they were.

### Play: the edges of the field
- **What it is:** serving and distributing (spec §13.7, kind 19).
- **How it plays:** a field of ripe grain. The player reaps it row by row (drag the sickle, or the arrow keys and Space), and the sheaves are bound and carried home. But the edges of the field are left standing, and the stalks that fall stay on the ground: at the border, the sickle stops, with "Leave this for the poor and the stranger." When the harvest is in, the gleaners come, and the player shows each of them to the grain left for them, until the widow, the poor man and the stranger all go home with enough.
- **Teaches:** that God's law built kindness into everyday work; that he cares for the poor and the stranger. (Ruth will glean in Boaz's field under this same law, in Level 16.)
- **Gentle mode:** the sickle keeps to the middle of the field by itself.
- **Accessibility:** a pointer, or the arrow keys and Space.

**Thread:** *"You shall love your neighbor as yourself," and the stranger "you shall love him as yourself" → Jesus' parable of the Good Samaritan: "Go and do likewise."* The glowing object is the stranger gleaning at the edge of the field.
- **[Q] Luke 10:29:** "But he, desiring to justify himself, said to Jesus, "And who is my neighbor?""
- **[Q] Luke 10:36:** "Which of these three, do you think, proved neighbor to the man who fell among the robbers?""
- **[Q] Luke 10:37:** "He said, "The one who showed mercy on him." And Jesus said to him, "Go and do likewise.""
- **What it shows:** When a lawyer asked Jesus what he must do to inherit eternal life, Jesus had him answer from the law: love God, and love your neighbor as yourself. "And who is my neighbor?" Jesus told of a Samaritan, a foreigner, who stopped for a wounded stranger when a priest and a Levite had passed him by. Our neighbor is anyone who needs us, even a stranger or an enemy. And Jesus himself is the Good Samaritan, who stopped for us.
- **Source:** Luke 10:25–37; Matthew 25:35–40; Catechism 1825, 1931–1932.

**Where and when:**
- **"You shall be holy."** God says it again and again in Leviticus (11:44; 19:2; 20:26), and Saint Peter repeats it to Christians (1 Peter 1:16).
- **Gleaning.** The poor followed the reapers and picked up what was left. Ruth the Moabite will glean in the field of Boaz (Level 16).
- **"Love your neighbor as yourself"** is Leviticus 19:18. Jesus, Saint Paul and Saint James all quote it (Mark 12:31; Romans 13:9; James 2:8).
- **The stranger.** "For you were strangers in the land of Egypt": the people knew what it was to be foreigners.

**Read:** Leviticus 19:1–18, 32–37.
