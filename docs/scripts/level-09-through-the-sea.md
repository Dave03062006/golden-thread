# Level 9 script: *Through the Sea*

**Bible:** Exodus 14–18
**Age:** III · Out of Egypt
**Length:** about 45 minutes (7 episodes, then the quiz, then reflection and prayer)
**Status:** draft, waiting for Dave's approval.

## How to read this script

- Each **scene** is a checkpoint. Its numbered lines are the narration beats; the player taps to move from one to the next.
- *On screen* describes what the scene shows. It's the brief for the art in both styles.
- **[Q]** marks an exact quotation from the Bible. Its wording in both translations is in the verse table at the end. Everything else is our own retelling.
- **Play** is the episode's interactive moment. **Thread** is its golden thread. **Read** is the passage the game sends the player to.
- "LORD" in capitals stands for God's holy name, printed in small capitals in the RSV.

### What this level is about

God's people have walked out of Egypt, free. But Pharaoh changes his mind and comes after them with his chariots, and they are caught between the army and the sea. God opens a path through the water, and they cross on dry ground. On the far shore they sing, and Miriam leads the women in dancing. Then comes the long walk through the wilderness, and the people learn, day by day, that God will take care of them: he sweetens bitter water, sends bread from heaven every morning, brings water out of a rock, gives them victory over Amalek while Moses prays, and sends Jethro with wise advice.

The people are often afraid, and they grumble, again and again. God is patient with them. Each gift in the desert points ahead to Jesus: the sea to Baptism, the manna to the Eucharist, the rock to Christ himself, from whose side flowed water and blood. The level shows that God makes a way where there seems to be none, and gives his people what they need, one day at a time.

## Episode 1 · Trapped at the Sea

**Exodus 14:1–14, 19–20 · about 6 minutes · 3 scenes**

### Scene 1.1 · Camped by the sea
*On screen:* Evening. The camp of Israel spread along the shore: tents, flocks, cooking fires, and the pillar of cloud standing over it. Behind the camp, the wilderness; in front of it, the sea. Far away, Pharaoh in his palace, turning to his servants, then climbing into his chariot.

1. God led his people to camp by the sea, with the wilderness behind them and the water in front of them. It looked as if they were trapped. But God knew what he would do.
2. **[Q] Exodus 14:5** "When the king of Egypt was told that the people had fled, the mind of Pharaoh and his servants was changed toward the people, and they said, "What is this we have done, that we have let Israel go from serving us?""
3. **[Q] Exodus 14:6** "So he made ready his chariot and took his army with him,"
4. **[Q] Exodus 14:7** "and took six hundred picked chariots and all the other chariots of Egypt with officers over all of them."

### Scene 1.2 · The chariots are coming
*On screen:* The people on the shore look back: on the horizon, a cloud of dust and the glint of chariots. Mothers pull their children close. The people crowd around Moses, shouting. Moses raises his rod, calm.

1. **[Q] Exodus 14:10** "When Pharaoh drew near, the people of Israel lifted up their eyes, and behold, the Egyptians were marching after them; and they were in great fear. And the people of Israel cried out to the LORD;"
2. **[Q] Exodus 14:11** "and they said to Moses, "Is it because there are no graves in Egypt that you have taken us away to die in the wilderness? What have you done to us, in bringing us out of Egypt?"
3. **[Q] Exodus 14:13** "And Moses said to the people, "Fear not, stand firm, and see the salvation of the LORD, which he will work for you today; for the Egyptians whom you see today, you shall never see again."
4. **[Q] Exodus 14:14** "The LORD will fight for you, and you have only to be still.""

### Scene 1.3 · The pillar moves
*On screen:* Night falls. The pillar of cloud lifts from in front of the camp and moves behind it, between Israel and the Egyptians. On the Egyptians' side it is dark cloud; on Israel's side it shines with light all night. *(Play: the light in the camp.)*

1. **[Q] Exodus 14:19** "Then the angel of God who went before the host of Israel moved and went behind them; and the pillar of cloud moved from before them and stood behind them,"
2. **[Q] Exodus 14:20** "coming between the host of Egypt and the host of Israel. And there was the cloud and the darkness; and the night passed without one coming near the other all night."
3. *(Play: the light in the camp.)*
4. All night long, the Egyptians could not come near. God himself stood between his people and their enemies.

### Play: the light in the camp
- **What it is:** light and reveal (spec §13.7, kind 11).
- **How it plays:** night by the sea. First the player moves the pillar of cloud from in front of the camp to behind it, between the people and the chariots (drag it, or press Space). On the Egyptians' side it turns to darkness. Then its light reaches into the camp, and the player guides it over the tents (drag, or the arrow keys). Each frightened family the light finds looks up and grows calm, until the whole camp is lit and at rest.
- **Teaches:** God stands between his people and what they fear; "Fear not, stand firm."
- **Gentle mode:** the light is wide, and drifts toward the next family still in the dark.
- **Accessibility:** a pointer, the arrow keys, or Space to light the next tent.

**Thread:** *"Fear not, stand firm" by the sea → Jesus, walking on the sea: "Take heart, it is I; have no fear."* The glowing object is the dark water of the sea.
- **[Q] Matthew 14:25:** "And in the fourth watch of the night he came to them, walking on the sea."
- **[Q] Matthew 14:27:** "But immediately he spoke to them, saying, "Take heart, it is I; have no fear.""
- **What it shows:** God's people stood at the edge of the sea at night, terrified, and Moses told them, "Fear not." Long after, Jesus' disciples were in a boat on a stormy sea at night, terrified, and Jesus came to them walking on the water, and said, "Have no fear." The God who made a path through the sea walks on the sea himself, and comes to us in our fear.
- **Source:** Matthew 14:22–33; Psalm 77:19; Catechism 1817.

**Where and when:**
- **Which sea?** Exodus calls it *Yam Suph*, usually translated "the Red Sea"; the words can also mean "the Sea of Reeds." No one knows exactly where the people crossed: perhaps at one of the lakes north of the Gulf of Suez, perhaps at the gulf itself.
- **Chariots.** Egypt's chariots were the fastest weapons of their time: light wooden cars pulled by two horses, carrying a driver and an archer. Six hundred picked chariots was a fearsome army against people on foot.
- **The angel of God.** Exodus says that the angel of God moved behind the people with the pillar (14:19). God himself was guarding them.

**Read:** Exodus 14:1–14; 14:19–20.

## Episode 2 · The Waters Part

**Exodus 14:15–31 · about 7 minutes · 4 scenes**

> **Content note shown before this episode:** "In this episode the sea comes back over Pharaoh's army as it follows God's people. It is told gently, and nothing is shown."

### Scene 2.1 · "Go forward"
*On screen:* Moses at the water's edge in the night, his rod raised over the sea. A strong east wind roars; the water heaps up and draws back, and a path of dry seabed opens through the middle.

1. **[Q] Exodus 14:15** "The LORD said to Moses, "Why do you cry to me? Tell the people of Israel to go forward."
2. **[Q] Exodus 14:16** "Lift up your rod, and stretch out your hand over the sea and divide it, that the people of Israel may go on dry ground through the sea."
3. **[Q] Exodus 14:21** "Then Moses stretched out his hand over the sea; and the LORD drove the sea back by a strong east wind all night, and made the sea dry land, and the waters were divided."

### Scene 2.2 · On dry ground
*On screen:* The people walk into the sea on dry ground by the light of the pillar of fire. Walls of water stand on either side, fish glinting inside them. Families with children on their shoulders, flocks and carts. *(Play: the path through the sea.)*

1. **[Q] Exodus 14:22** "And the people of Israel went into the midst of the sea on dry ground, the waters being a wall to them on their right hand and on their left."
2. *(Play: the path through the sea.)*
3. All night they walked, and before morning the whole people had come across, the old and the young, the flocks and the herds. Not one was left behind.

### Scene 2.3 · The sea returns
*On screen:* Dawn on the far shore. Moses stretches out his hand over the sea again, and the walls of water fall back into place. The path is gone, and the sea lies calm in the morning light. Nothing of Pharaoh's army is seen.

1. **[Q] Exodus 14:25** "clogging their chariot wheels so that they drove heavily; and the Egyptians said, "Let us flee from before Israel; for the LORD fights for them against the Egyptians.""
2. **[Q] Exodus 14:26** "Then the LORD said to Moses, "Stretch out your hand over the sea, that the water may come back upon the Egyptians, upon their chariots, and upon their horsemen.""
3. **[Q] Exodus 14:28** "The waters returned and covered the chariots and the horsemen and all the host of Pharaoh that had followed them into the sea; not so much as one of them remained."

### Scene 2.4 · They believed
*On screen:* The people on the shore in the morning sun, looking back at the calm sea. Children run on the sand. Moses stands among them.

1. **[Q] Exodus 14:29** "But the people of Israel walked on dry ground through the sea, the waters being a wall to them on their right hand and on their left."
2. **[Q] Exodus 14:31** "And Israel saw the great work which the LORD did against the Egyptians, and the people feared the LORD; and they believed in the LORD and in his servant Moses."
3. The day before, they had been slaves running for their lives. Now they were free, and the sea lay between them and Egypt.

### Play: the path through the sea
- **What it is:** pathfinding (spec §13.7, kind 6).
- **How it plays:** night on the seabed, between the walls of water. The player leads the people across by guiding Moses along the winding path (drag, or the arrow keys), around rocks and pools, while the families follow in a line behind him by the light of the pillar of fire. Along the way there are stragglers: a child who has stopped to look at the fish in the wall of water, an old man resting on a rock, a lamb that has wandered off. Each one joins the line when Moses reaches them. When everyone is across, dawn breaks on the far shore.
- **Teaches:** God made a way where there was none, and brought all his people through; not one was left behind.
- **Gentle mode:** the path glows ahead, and Moses walks on along it by himself if the player waits.
- **Accessibility:** a pointer, the arrow keys, or Space to walk on along the path.

**Thread:** *The people pass through the sea, from slavery to freedom → Baptism.* The glowing object is the dry path through the sea.
- **[Q] 1 Corinthians 10:1:** "I want you to know, brethren, that our fathers were all under the cloud, and all passed through the sea,"
- **[Q] 1 Corinthians 10:2:** "and all were baptized into Moses in the cloud and in the sea,"
- **What it shows:** God's people went down into the sea as slaves and came up on the other side free. Saint Paul says they were "baptized into Moses in the cloud and in the sea." In Baptism we pass through the water too: from the slavery of sin into the freedom of God's children. That is why this reading is never left out at the Easter Vigil, the night when new Christians are baptized.
- **Source:** 1 Corinthians 10:1–2; Catechism 1094, 1221.

**Where and when:**
- **The east wind.** Exodus says God drove the sea back with a strong east wind all night (14:21). God works his wonders through the very things he has made.
- **The morning watch.** The last watch of the night, before dawn (14:24). By then the people were across.
- **At the Easter Vigil.** Of all the readings at the Easter Vigil, the crossing of the sea is the one that is never left out.

**Read:** Exodus 14:15–31.

## Episode 3 · The Song of Moses and Miriam

**Exodus 15:1–21 · about 6 minutes · 3 scenes**

### Scene 3.1 · "I will sing to the LORD"
*On screen:* Morning on the far shore. The people gather on the sand. Moses lifts his hands and sings, and the people sing with him, their voices rolling out over the calm sea.

1. On the far shore, the people sang. Their song is one of the oldest in the whole Bible.
2. **[Q] Exodus 15:1** "Then Moses and the people of Israel sang this song to the LORD, saying, "I will sing to the LORD, for he has triumphed gloriously; the horse and his rider he has thrown into the sea."
3. **[Q] Exodus 15:2** "The LORD is my strength and my song, and he has become my salvation; this is my God, and I will praise him, my father's God, and I will exalt him."
4. **[Q] Exodus 15:11** ""Who is like thee, O LORD, among the gods? Who is like thee, majestic in holiness, terrible in glorious deeds, doing wonders?"

### Scene 3.2 · Miriam's timbrel
*On screen:* Miriam, the sister of Moses and Aaron, now old, steps out with a timbrel. The women follow her with their timbrels, dancing in a circle on the sand. *(Play: Miriam's song.)*

1. **[Q] Exodus 15:20** "Then Miriam, the prophetess, the sister of Aaron, took a timbrel in her hand; and all the women went out after her with timbrels and dancing."
2. *(Play: Miriam's song.)*
3. **[Q] Exodus 15:21** "And Miriam sang to them: "Sing to the LORD, for he has triumphed gloriously; the horse and his rider he has thrown into the sea.""

### Scene 3.3 · "The LORD will reign"
*On screen:* Evening. The camp by the sea, the pillar glowing over it. Far off to the east, the mountains of the wilderness, where God is leading them.

1. **[Q] Exodus 15:13** ""Thou hast led in thy steadfast love the people whom thou hast redeemed, thou hast guided them by thy strength to thy holy abode."
2. **[Q] Exodus 15:17** "Thou wilt bring them in, and plant them on thy own mountain, the place, O LORD, which thou hast made for thy abode, the sanctuary, O LORD, which thy hands have established."
3. **[Q] Exodus 15:18** "The LORD will reign for ever and ever.""

### Play: Miriam's song
- **What it is:** rhythm (spec §13.7, kind 5; the spec's own example).
- **How it plays:** Miriam beats a short pattern on her timbrel, and the player answers it with the same pattern (tap, or Space). The women echo it on their timbrels. Each phrase answered brings more women into the dance, and the patterns grow a little longer, until the whole shore is singing "Sing to the LORD." A missed beat doesn't stop the song; Miriam simply plays the phrase again. (Level 8's march was keeping a steady beat; this is call and response.)
- **Teaches:** thanksgiving: when God saves us, we praise him, and we praise him together.
- **Gentle mode:** any answer with the right number of taps counts.
- **Accessibility:** a pointer (tap), or Space; each pattern also shows as pulses on screen.

**Thread:** *The song of Moses by the sea → the saints in heaven "sing the song of Moses, the servant of God, and the song of the Lamb."* The glowing object is Miriam's timbrel.
- **[Q] Revelation 15:2:** "And I saw what appeared to be a sea of glass mingled with fire, and those who had conquered the beast and its image and the number of its name, standing beside the sea of glass with harps of God in their hands."
- **[Q] Revelation 15:3:** "And they sing the song of Moses, the servant of God, and the song of the Lamb, saying, "Great and wonderful are thy deeds, O Lord God the Almighty! Just and true are thy ways, O King of the ages!"
- **What it shows:** In the Book of Revelation, Saint John sees the saints in heaven standing beside a sea of glass, with harps in their hands. They sing "the song of Moses" and "the song of the Lamb." The song God's people sang on the shore of the Red Sea is sung again in heaven, by everyone Jesus has brought safely through, from death to life. And every Easter, after the reading of the crossing, the Church sings Moses' song again.
- **Source:** Revelation 15:2–4; Catechism 2642.

**Where and when:**
- **Miriam.** The sister of Moses and Aaron, who watched over the baby Moses in his basket among the reeds (Level 7). She is the first woman the Bible calls a prophetess.
- **A timbrel.** A small hand drum, like a tambourine, played by women at celebrations.
- **An old song.** Many scholars think the Song of the Sea is among the oldest poems in the Bible, sung for centuries before it was written down.

**Read:** Exodus 15:1–21.

## Episode 4 · Manna and Quail

**Exodus 15:22–16:36 · about 8 minutes · 4 scenes**

### Scene 4.1 · Bitter water
*On screen:* Three days into the wilderness, the people reach a pool at Marah and spit out the water: it is bitter. Moses prays, then throws a piece of wood into the water, and the people drink. Then palms and springs at Elim, the camp resting in the shade.

1. **[Q] Exodus 15:22** "Then Moses led Israel onward from the Red Sea, and they went into the wilderness of Shur; they went three days in the wilderness and found no water."
2. **[Q] Exodus 15:23** "When they came to Marah, they could not drink the water of Marah because it was bitter; therefore it was named Marah."
3. **[Q] Exodus 15:25 (in part)** "And he cried to the LORD; and the LORD showed him a tree, and he threw it into the water, and the water became sweet."
4. **[Q] Exodus 15:27** "Then they came to Elim, where there were twelve springs of water and seventy palm trees; and they encamped there by the water."

### Scene 4.2 · Hungry in the wilderness
*On screen:* The wilderness of Sin, dry and stony. The people sit by their tents, hungry and grumbling; children hold empty bowls. Moses and Aaron listen to them.

1. **[Q] Exodus 16:2** "And the whole congregation of the people of Israel murmured against Moses and Aaron in the wilderness,"
2. **[Q] Exodus 16:3** "and said to them, "Would that we had died by the hand of the LORD in the land of Egypt, when we sat by the fleshpots and ate bread to the full; for you have brought us out into this wilderness to kill this whole assembly with hunger.""
3. **[Q] Exodus 16:4** "Then the LORD said to Moses, "Behold, I will rain bread from heaven for you; and the people shall go out and gather a day's portion every day, that I may prove them, whether they will walk in my law or not."

### Scene 4.3 · "What is it?"
*On screen:* Evening: quails come down and cover the camp. Morning: the dew lifts, and the ground all around is covered with fine white flakes, like frost. The people come out of their tents and stare. *(Play: enough for each.)*

1. **[Q] Exodus 16:13** "In the evening quails came up and covered the camp; and in the morning dew lay round about the camp."
2. **[Q] Exodus 16:14** "And when the dew had gone up, there was on the face of the wilderness a fine, flake-like thing, fine as hoarfrost on the ground."
3. **[Q] Exodus 16:15** "When the people of Israel saw it, they said to one another, "What is it?" For they did not know what it was. And Moses said to them, "It is the bread which the LORD has given you to eat."
4. **[Q] Exodus 16:16** "This is what the LORD has commanded: 'Gather of it, every man of you, as much as he can eat; you shall take an omer apiece, according to the number of the persons whom each of you has in his tent.'""
5. *(Play: enough for each.)*
6. **[Q] Exodus 16:18** "But when they measured it with an omer, he that gathered much had nothing over, and he that gathered little had no lack; each gathered according to what he could eat."

### Scene 4.4 · Bread from heaven
*On screen:* Morning after morning the people gather; on the sixth day they gather twice as much; on the seventh day the camp rests, and no one goes out. Aaron sets a jar of manna before the LORD.

1. **[Q] Exodus 16:21** "Morning by morning they gathered it, each as much as he could eat; but when the sun grew hot, it melted."
2. On the sixth day God gave twice as much, so that on the seventh day, the sabbath, the whole people could rest.
3. **[Q] Exodus 16:31** "Now the house of Israel called its name manna; it was like coriander seed, white, and the taste of it was like wafers made with honey."
4. **[Q] Exodus 16:33** "And Moses said to Aaron, "Take a jar, and put an omer of manna in it, and place it before the LORD, to be kept throughout your generations.""
5. **[Q] Exodus 16:35** "And the people of Israel ate the manna forty years, till they came to a habitable land; they ate the manna, till they came to the border of the land of Canaan."

### Play: enough for each
- **What it is:** herding and gathering (spec §13.7, kind 2; the spec names the manna).
- **How it plays:** morning in the camp, the ground white with manna. Outside one tent stands a family of six (a grandmother, a father, a mother and three children) with an empty omer jar for each. The player gathers the manna (tap, or sweep across it), and it fills the jars, one for each person. However much or little is gathered, every jar comes out exactly full: nothing over, no lack. When the sun grows hot, the manna left on the ground melts away. Then comes the sixth day, with two jars for each. On the seventh day the ground is bare, and the family rests together.
- **Teaches:** God gives his people their bread each day, enough for each; they can trust him for tomorrow; the sabbath is a gift of rest. In Level 8, Pharaoh's stubble was never enough. God's bread is always enough.
- **Gentle mode:** a sweep anywhere gathers, and the jars fill quickly.
- **Accessibility:** a pointer (tap or sweep), or Space to fill the next jar.

**Thread:** *The manna, bread from heaven in the desert → Jesus, the living bread which came down from heaven.* The glowing object is the jar of manna kept before the LORD.
- **[Q] John 6:49:** "Your fathers ate the manna in the wilderness, and they died."
- **[Q] John 6:50:** "This is the bread which comes down from heaven, that a man may eat of it and not die."
- **[Q] John 6:51 (in part):** "I am the living bread which came down from heaven;"
- **What it shows:** God fed his people in the desert with bread from heaven, every morning for forty years. Jesus said that he himself is the true bread from heaven: whoever eats this bread will live for ever. In the Eucharist, God still feeds his people on their journey through life. And as Aaron kept a jar of manna before the LORD, every church keeps the Eucharist in the tabernacle.
- **Source:** John 6:31–51; Hebrews 9:4; Catechism 1094, 1334, 1379.

**Where and when:**
- **Manna.** Its name sounds like the Hebrew *man hu*, "What is it?", the people's first words when they saw it (16:15).
- **An omer.** About two liters: a day's food for one person. Exodus explains it as a tenth of an ephah (16:36).
- **Forty years.** The manna fed the people every day of their journey. It stopped only the day after they first ate the grain of the promised land (Joshua 5:12).
- **Marah and Elim.** Marah means "bitter." Elim, with its twelve springs, is often placed at an oasis in the west of the Sinai peninsula, a day or two's walk south of Marah.

**Read:** Exodus 15:22–27; 16:1–35.

## Episode 5 · Water from the Rock

**Exodus 17:1–7 · about 5 minutes · 3 scenes**

### Scene 5.1 · No water at Rephidim
*On screen:* Rephidim, a dry valley among red rocks. Empty water jars, tired animals lying in the dust. The people crowd angrily around Moses.

1. **[Q] Exodus 17:1** "All the congregation of the people of Israel moved on from the wilderness of Sin by stages, according to the commandment of the LORD, and camped at Rephidim; but there was no water for the people to drink."
2. **[Q] Exodus 17:2** "Therefore the people found fault with Moses, and said, "Give us water to drink." And Moses said to them, "Why do you find fault with me? Why do you put the LORD to the proof?""
3. **[Q] Exodus 17:3** "But the people thirsted there for water, and the people murmured against Moses, and said, "Why did you bring us up out of Egypt, to kill us and our children and our cattle with thirst?""

### Scene 5.2 · Strike the rock
*On screen:* Moses walks ahead with some of the elders to a great rock at Horeb. He raises the rod and strikes the rock, and water bursts out and runs down the valley. *(Play: streams in the desert.)*

1. **[Q] Exodus 17:4** "So Moses cried to the LORD, "What shall I do with this people? They are almost ready to stone me.""
2. **[Q] Exodus 17:5** "And the LORD said to Moses, "Pass on before the people, taking with you some of the elders of Israel; and take in your hand the rod with which you struck the Nile, and go."
3. **[Q] Exodus 17:6** "Behold, I will stand before you there on the rock at Horeb; and you shall strike the rock, and water shall come out of it, that the people may drink." And Moses did so, in the sight of the elders of Israel."
4. *(Play: streams in the desert.)*

### Scene 5.3 · "Is the LORD among us?"
*On screen:* The people drinking at the stream, children splashing, the flocks and cattle at the water's edge. Moses looks up at the rock.

1. **[Q] Exodus 17:7** "And he called the name of the place Massah and Meribah, because of the faultfinding of the children of Israel, and because they put the LORD to the proof by saying, "Is the LORD among us or not?""
2. The LORD had been among them all along: in the pillar of cloud, in the bread each morning, and now in the water from the rock.

### Play: streams in the desert
- **What it is:** pouring and water (spec §13.7, kind 12; the spec names the water from the rock).
- **How it plays:** water gushes from the rock and runs down the dry valley. Where the stream comes to a fork in the sand, the player turns it (tap the stone that blocks one way, or the arrow keys and Space) to send the water to those who are thirsty: the families with their jars, the children, the flocks, the cattle. Everyone who drinks brightens, and the valley turns green where the water runs, until all have drunk.
- **Teaches:** God provides even in the driest place, and his gifts reach everyone.
- **Gentle mode:** each fork turns by itself toward whoever is still thirsty.
- **Accessibility:** a pointer, or the arrow keys and Space.

**Thread:** *The rock that gave water in the desert → "the Rock was Christ," from whose pierced side came blood and water.* The glowing object is the rock.
- **[Q] 1 Corinthians 10:4:** "and all drank the same supernatural drink. For they drank from the supernatural Rock which followed them, and the Rock was Christ."
- **[Q] John 19:34:** "But one of the soldiers pierced his side with a spear, and at once there came out blood and water."
- **What it shows:** Saint Paul says the rock that gave water in the desert was a sign of Christ: "the Rock was Christ." When Jesus died on the cross, a soldier pierced his side, and out came blood and water. The Church has always seen in them the sacraments that flow from Jesus: the water of Baptism and the Blood of the Eucharist. Jesus himself said, "If any one thirst, let him come to me and drink" (John 7:37).
- **Source:** 1 Corinthians 10:4; John 7:37–38; John 19:34; Catechism 694, 1094, 1225.

**Where and when:**
- **Rephidim.** A camp in the south of the Sinai peninsula, near Horeb. It is often placed in Wadi Feiran, the largest oasis valley in Sinai.
- **Massah and Meribah.** The names mean "testing" and "quarreling." Psalm 95 remembers them, and the Church prays that psalm at the start of each day in the Liturgy of the Hours: "Harden not your hearts."
- **A rock that followed them.** An old Jewish story told of a rock that went with the people all through the desert, giving them water. Saint Paul takes up the story in 1 Corinthians 10:4.

**Read:** Exodus 17:1–7.

## Episode 6 · Hands Raised Against Amalek

**Exodus 17:8–16 · about 6 minutes · 3 scenes**

> **Content note shown before this episode:** "In this episode Israel fights a battle against the Amalekites. Nothing of the fighting is shown."

### Scene 6.1 · Amalek comes
*On screen:* Rephidim. Warriors of Amalek appear on the ridge above the camp. Moses calls Joshua, a strong young man, and speaks to him.

1. **[Q] Exodus 17:8** "Then came Amalek and fought with Israel at Rephidim."
2. **[Q] Exodus 17:9** "And Moses said to Joshua, "Choose for us men, and go out, fight with Amalek; tomorrow I will stand on the top of the hill with the rod of God in my hand.""
3. **[Q] Exodus 17:10** "So Joshua did as Moses told him, and fought with Amalek; and Moses, Aaron, and Hur went up to the top of the hill."

### Scene 6.2 · On the top of the hill
*On screen:* Moses on the hilltop with the rod of God, his arms raised; Aaron and Hur beside him. Far below in the valley, only dust and banners can be seen. When his hands sink, the banners of Amalek rise. *(Play: hands held high.)*

3. **[Q] Exodus 17:12** "But Moses' hands grew weary; so they took a stone and put it under him, and he sat upon it, and Aaron and Hur held up his hands, one on one side, and the other on the other side; so his hands were steady until the going down of the sun."

### Scene 6.3 · "The LORD is my banner"
*On screen:* Sunset. Joshua and his men come back up the valley. Moses builds an altar of stones on the hill, and they stand around it.

1. By evening, Joshua and his men had won the battle.
2. **[Q] Exodus 17:15** "And Moses built an altar and called the name of it, The LORD is my banner,"
3. Moses knew who had won the battle. It was not the sword, but the LORD, while his people prayed.

### Play: hands held high
- **What it is:** timing (spec §13.7, kind 16).
- **How it plays:** Moses stands on the hilltop with the rod of God raised. Slowly his arms grow tired and begin to sink, and down in the valley the battle turns. Each time they start to sink, the player lifts them again (tap, or Space) before they fall. Each lift is harder than the last, until Aaron and Hur come: the player sets a stone for Moses to sit on, and Aaron and Hur hold up his hands, one on each side. Then his hands stay steady until the sun goes down.
- **Teaches:** prayer is part of the battle; we need one another to keep on praying.
- **Gentle mode:** the arms sink slowly, and Aaron and Hur come sooner.
- **Accessibility:** a pointer (tap), or Space.

**Thread:** *Moses on the hill, his arms stretched out until sunset → Jesus on the cross, his arms stretched out for all.* The glowing object is Moses' raised hands.
- **[Q] John 12:32:** "and I, when I am lifted up from the earth, will draw all men to myself.""
- **[Q] John 12:33:** "He said this to show by what death he was to die."
- **What it shows:** The first Christians saw in Moses on the hill, his arms held wide until the sun went down, a picture of Jesus on the cross. Jesus' arms were stretched out between heaven and earth, and there he won the victory over sin and death for all of us. And the man who led the battle below was named Joshua, the same name as Jesus in Hebrew: "the LORD saves."
- **Source:** John 12:32–33; the Letter of Barnabas 12; Saint Justin Martyr, *Dialogue with Trypho* 90; Catechism 2574–2577.

**Where and when:**
- **Amalek.** A people of the desert, descended from Esau's grandson Amalek (Genesis 36:12).
- **Joshua.** His name means "the LORD saves." This is the first time we meet him; one day he will lead God's people into the promised land. In Greek his name is written *Iesous*, Jesus.
- **Hur.** One of the leaders of the people, who stood with Aaron beside Moses on the hill.

**Read:** Exodus 17:8–16.

### Scene 7.3 · Able men
*On screen:* Moses chooses men from among the people and sets them over groups. The long line breaks up into small circles around them, and only a few people come to Moses. Then Jethro sets off home. *(Play: sharing the load.)*

1. **[Q] Exodus 18:21** "Moreover choose able men from all the people, such as fear God, men who are trustworthy and who hate a bribe; and place such men over the people as rulers of thousands, of hundreds, of fifties, and of tens."
2. **[Q] Exodus 18:22** "And let them judge the people at all times; every great matter they shall bring to you, but any small matter they shall decide themselves; so it will be easier for you, and they will bear the burden with you."
3. *(Play: sharing the load.)*
4. **[Q] Exodus 18:24** "So Moses gave heed to the voice of his father-in-law and did all that he had said."
5. Moses was the greatest leader his people ever had, and he was humble enough to listen to good advice.

### Play: sharing the load
- **What it is:** serving and distributing (spec §13.7, kind 19).
- **How it plays:** the people wait in a long line, each with something to bring: a lost sheep, a borrowed pot that broke, a quarrel over a boundary stone, a question about God's law. Small matters go to the leaders Moses has chosen; great matters go to Moses. The player sends each person to the right one (tap a leader, or the arrow keys and Space). Each leader helps the people sent to him, the line grows shorter, and Moses is no longer worn out. When the line is gone, the sun is still high.
- **Teaches:** the wisdom of sharing work; the humility to accept good advice; leaders are there to serve.
- **Gentle mode:** the right one to go to glows.
- **Accessibility:** a pointer, or the arrow keys and Space.

**Thread:** *Moses chooses able men to share the work → Jesus appoints the Twelve, and sends out laborers into his harvest.* The glowing object is the circle of the new leaders.
- **[Q] Mark 3:14:** "And he appointed twelve, to be with him, and to be sent out to preach"
- **[Q] Luke 10:2:** "And he said to them, "The harvest is plentiful, but the laborers are few; pray therefore the Lord of the harvest to send out laborers into his harvest."
- **What it shows:** Moses could not carry the whole people alone, and God gave him helpers. Jesus, too, chose twelve apostles to be with him and to be sent out, and seventy others besides. The Church still has bishops, priests and deacons to serve God's people, and every Christian has a part in the work: "The harvest is plentiful, but the laborers are few."
- **Source:** Mark 3:13–15; Luke 10:1–2; Acts 6:1–6; Catechism 551, 873.

**Where and when:**
- **Jethro.** The priest of Midian and Moses' father-in-law, who took Moses in when he fled from Egypt (Level 7). He is not an Israelite, yet he praises the LORD and offers him sacrifice.
- **Gershom and Eliezer.** Moses' two sons. Their names mean "a sojourner there" and "my God is help" (18:3–4).
- **Thousands, hundreds, fifties and tens.** Leaders for groups of every size, so that everyone had someone close by to go to.

**Read:** Exodus 18.
