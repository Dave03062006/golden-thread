# Level 16 script: *Ruth*

**Bible:** Ruth 1–4
**Age:** V · The Promised Land
**Length:** about 35 minutes (5 episodes, then the quiz, then reflection and prayer)
**Status:** draft for Dave's approval.

## How to read this script

- Each **scene** is a checkpoint. Its numbered lines are the narration beats; the player taps to move from one to the next.
- *On screen* describes what the scene shows. It's the brief for the art in both styles.
- **[Q]** marks an exact quotation from the Bible. Its wording in both translations is in the verse table at the end. Everything else is our own retelling.
- **Play** is the episode's interactive moment. **Thread** is its golden thread. **Read** is the passage the game sends the player to.
- "LORD" in capitals stands for God's holy name, printed in small capitals in the RSV.

### What this level is about

"In the days when the judges ruled," while Israel was forgetting God again and again, a quiet story happened in the little town of Bethlehem. It has no battles and no miracles. A widow, Naomi, loses everything and comes home bitter and empty. Her daughter-in-law Ruth, a foreigner from Moab, refuses to leave her: "where you go I will go… your God my God." Ruth gleans barley in the field of a kind man, Boaz, who turns out to be one of the family's nearest kin, and he redeems the family's land and marries her. Their son Obed becomes the grandfather of King David.

Ruth's faithful love, *hesed* in Hebrew, mirrors God's own. The foreign widow becomes an ancestor of Jesus, and Saint Matthew names her in his family tree:

- **[Q] Matthew 1:5:** "and Salmon the father of Boaz by Rahab, and Boaz the father of Obed by Ruth, and Obed the father of Jesse,"
- **[Q] Matthew 1:6:** "and Jesse the father of David the king. And David was the father of Solomon by the wife of Uriah,"

Boaz, the kinsman who buys back what was lost, is a picture of Christ our Redeemer. And the story happens in Bethlehem, "the house of bread," where Jesus would be born.

**How it's shown:** gently, with the warmth of a harvest. The night at the threshing floor (Episode 4) is told simply: Ruth asks Boaz to marry her and protect her family, as the custom of the next of kin allowed, and Boaz honors her.

## Episode 1 · Naomi's Sorrow

**Ruth 1:1–7 · about 6 minutes · 3 scenes**

### Scene 1.1 · Famine in the house of bread
*On screen:* Bethlehem on its hill, the fields brown and cracked, empty baskets by the doors. A family of four, Elimelech, Naomi and their two sons, load a donkey and walk east, down toward the Dead Sea and the hills of Moab beyond.

1. **[Q] Ruth 1:1** "In the days when the judges ruled there was a famine in the land, and a certain man of Bethlehem in Judah went to sojourn in the country of Moab, he and his wife and his two sons."
2. **[Q] Ruth 1:2** "The name of the man was Elimelech and the name of his wife Naomi, and the names of his two sons were Mahlon and Chilion; they were Ephrathites from Bethlehem in Judah. They went into the country of Moab and remained there."

### Scene 1.2 · In the country of Moab
*On screen:* The high green plateau of Moab. Elimelech's grave under a lone tree. Then two weddings: the sons marry two young Moabite women, Orpah and Ruth. Years pass: the tree grows, the seasons turn.

1. **[Q] Ruth 1:3** "But Elimelech, the husband of Naomi, died, and she was left with her two sons."
2. **[Q] Ruth 1:4** "These took Moabite wives; the name of the one was Orpah and the name of the other Ruth. They lived there about ten years;"
3. Ten years passed in Moab. Naomi's daughters-in-law were good to her, and she loved them like her own daughters.

### Scene 1.3 · Three widows
*On screen:* Three graves under the tree now. Three women in dark cloaks sit together in silence. A traveler passes with news; far to the west, over the hills of Judah, a soft light rises where Bethlehem lies. Naomi stands, and the three set out on the road. *(Play: the long road home.)*

1. **[Q] Ruth 1:5** "and both Mahlon and Chilion died, so that the woman was bereft of her two sons and her husband."
2. **[Q] Ruth 1:6** "Then she started with her daughters-in-law to return from the country of Moab, for she had heard in the country of Moab that the LORD had visited his people and given them food."
3. **[Q] Ruth 1:7** "So she set out from the place where she was, with her two daughters-in-law, and they went on the way to return to the land of Judah."
4. *(Play: the long road home.)*

### Play: the long road home
- **What it is:** a quiet interaction (spec §13.7, kind 20).
- **How it plays:** the three widows walk west along the road from Moab, down to the Jordan and up toward the hills of Judah. The player walks them on, step by step (tap, or Space), and at each resting place one of Naomi's memories comes back, a few words each: her husband, her sons, the famine, the news of bread. The light over Bethlehem grows a little brighter with each stop.
- **Teaches:** Naomi's grief, and that the road home began with hearing that "the LORD had visited his people."
- **Gentle mode:** the women walk on by themselves.
- **Accessibility:** a pointer, or Space.

**Thread:** *Naomi's sorrow, and the news that God has visited his people → Jesus: "your sorrow will turn into joy."* The glowing object is the light over Bethlehem.
- **[Q] John 16:20:** "Truly, truly, I say to you, you will weep and lament, but the world will rejoice; you will be sorrowful, but your sorrow will turn into joy."
- **[Q] John 16:22:** "So you have sorrow now, but I will see you again and your hearts will rejoice, and no one will take your joy from you."
- **What it shows:** Naomi lost her husband and both her sons, and thought God had turned against her. But the story doesn't end there: by the end of the book, her arms are full again. Jesus promised his friends the same on the night before he died: their sorrow would not just go away, it would turn into joy, a joy no one could take from them. God does not waste our tears.
- **Source:** John 16:20–22; Psalm 30:11; Revelation 21:4; Catechism 1820, 2090.

**Where and when:**
- **Bethlehem.** Its name means "house of bread": a village in the hills of Judah, about 8 kilometers south of Jerusalem. A famine there was a bitter irony.
- **Moab.** The high plateau east of the Dead Sea. The Moabites were descendants of Lot, Abraham's nephew (Genesis 19:37), and for a long time enemies of Israel.
- **Ephrathites.** Ephrath was the old name of Bethlehem. Rachel was buried on the road to it (Level 4).
- **When?** "In the days when the judges ruled" (Level 15), perhaps around 1100 BC.

**Read:** Ruth 1:1–7.

## Episode 2 · "Where You Go I Will Go"

**Ruth 1:8–22 · about 7 minutes · 3 scenes**

### Scene 2.1 · At the crossroads
*On screen:* A crossroads on the plain, where one road turns back toward Moab. Naomi stops, takes her daughters-in-law by the hands, and kisses them. All three weep. Orpah kisses Naomi and walks slowly back toward Moab, turning once to wave.

1. **[Q] Ruth 1:8** "But Naomi said to her two daughters-in-law, "Go, return each of you to her mother's house. May the LORD deal kindly with you, as you have dealt with the dead and with me."
2. **[Q] Ruth 1:9** "The LORD grant that you may find a home, each of you in the house of her husband!" Then she kissed them, and they lifted up their voices and wept."
3. **[Q] Ruth 1:10** "And they said to her, "No, we will return with you to your people.""
4. **[Q] Ruth 1:13** "would you therefore wait till they were grown? Would you therefore refrain from marrying? No, my daughters, for it is exceedingly bitter to me for your sake that the hand of the LORD has gone forth against me.""
5. **[Q] Ruth 1:14** "Then they lifted up their voices and wept again; and Orpah kissed her mother-in-law, but Ruth clung to her."

### Scene 2.2 · Ruth's promise
*On screen:* Ruth kneels in the road and holds on to Naomi's hands and will not let go. Above them the sky opens, wide and blue. *(Play: where you go.)*

1. **[Q] Ruth 1:15** "And she said, "See, your sister-in-law has gone back to her people and to her gods; return after your sister-in-law.""
2. **[Q] Ruth 1:16** "But Ruth said, "Entreat me not to leave you or to return from following you; for where you go I will go, and where you lodge I will lodge; your people shall be my people, and your God my God;"
3. **[Q] Ruth 1:17** "where you die I will die, and there will I be buried. May the LORD do so to me and more also if even death parts me from you.""
4. *(Play: where you go.)*
5. **[Q] Ruth 1:18** "And when Naomi saw that she was determined to go with her, she said no more."

### Scene 2.3 · "Call me Mara"
*On screen:* Bethlehem's gate, with the barley turning gold in the fields below. The women of the town crowd round: "Is this Naomi?" Naomi, thin and gray, shakes her head. Behind her, Ruth stands quietly with the bundle.

1. **[Q] Ruth 1:19** "So the two of them went on until they came to Bethlehem. And when they came to Bethlehem, the whole town was stirred because of them; and the women said, "Is this Naomi?""
2. **[Q] Ruth 1:20** "She said to them, "Do not call me Naomi, call me Mara, for the Almighty has dealt very bitterly with me."
3. **[Q] Ruth 1:21** "I went away full, and the LORD has brought me back empty. Why call me Naomi, when the LORD has afflicted me and the Almighty has brought calamity upon me?""
4. **[Q] Ruth 1:22** "So Naomi returned, and Ruth the Moabitess her daughter-in-law with her, who returned from the country of Moab. And they came to Bethlehem at the beginning of barley harvest."

### Play: where you go
- **What it is:** memory (spec §13.7, kind 14).
- **How it plays:** Ruth's promise, one of the most beautiful in the Bible, is laid out in pieces: "where you go I will go," "where you lodge I will lodge," "your people shall be my people," "and your God my God," "where you die I will die," "and there will I be buried." The player puts it back together in order (choose the next part, or the arrow keys and Space), and as each part is set, Ruth takes a step closer to Naomi.
- **Teaches:** Ruth's promise by heart, and what it meant: she chose not only Naomi, but Naomi's people and Naomi's God.
- **Gentle mode:** the next part glows.
- **Accessibility:** a pointer, or the arrow keys and Space.

**Thread:** *Ruth, a foreigner, chooses the people and the God of Israel → "You are no longer strangers and sojourners, but… members of the household of God."* The glowing object is Ruth's hands, holding on to Naomi's.
- **[Q] Ephesians 2:19:** "So then you are no longer strangers and sojourners, but you are fellow citizens with the saints and members of the household of God,"
- **What it shows:** Ruth was a Moabite, an outsider, from a people Israel's law kept at a distance. But she chose the God of Israel, and he welcomed her completely: she became the great-grandmother of King David. Saint Paul says that in Jesus everyone, from every nation, can belong to God's family in the same way. No one is a stranger to him. The Church has always loved Ruth as a sign that God's promises are for all the nations.
- **Source:** Ephesians 2:11–22; Isaiah 56:6–7; Matthew 28:19; Catechism 543, 831.

**Where and when:**
- **"Mara."** Naomi's name means "pleasant"; Mara means "bitter." It is the same word as the bitter water of Marah in the wilderness (Level 9), which God made sweet.
- **The barley harvest.** The first harvest of the year, in April or May, around the time of Passover. The wheat harvest came a few weeks later.
- **"May the LORD do so to me."** A solemn oath: Ruth calls on the God of Israel by his own name, as one of his people.

**Read:** Ruth 1:8–22.

## Episode 3 · Gleaning in Boaz's Field

**Ruth 2 · about 8 minutes · 3 scenes**

### Scene 3.1 · She happened to come to Boaz's field
*On screen:* Golden barley fields below Bethlehem, reapers cutting with curved sickles, young women tying sheaves. At the edge of the field, Ruth bends to pick up the stalks they drop. A tall, gray-bearded man, Boaz, walks out from the town and greets his workers.

1. **[Q] Ruth 2:1** "Now Naomi had a kinsman of her husband's, a man of wealth, of the family of Elimelech, whose name was Boaz."
2. **[Q] Ruth 2:2** "And Ruth the Moabitess said to Naomi, "Let me go to the field, and glean among the ears of grain after him in whose sight I shall find favor." And she said to her, "Go, my daughter.""
3. **[Q] Ruth 2:3** "So she set forth and went and gleaned in the field after the reapers; and she happened to come to the part of the field belonging to Boaz, who was of the family of Elimelech."
4. **[Q] Ruth 2:4** "And behold, Boaz came from Bethlehem; and he said to the reapers, "The LORD be with you!" And they answered, "The LORD bless you.""

### Scene 3.2 · Under his wings
*On screen:* Boaz speaks to the foreman, then crosses to Ruth. She falls on her face before him; he lifts her up. The sun comes out bright over the field, and its light spreads around Ruth like the wings of a great bird.

1. **[Q] Ruth 2:5** "Then Boaz said to his servant who was in charge of the reapers, "Whose maiden is this?""
2. **[Q] Ruth 2:8** "Then Boaz said to Ruth, "Now, listen, my daughter, do not go to glean in another field or leave this one, but keep close to my maidens."
3. **[Q] Ruth 2:10** "Then she fell on her face, bowing to the ground, and said to him, "Why have I found favor in your eyes, that you should take notice of me, when I am a foreigner?""
4. **[Q] Ruth 2:11** "But Boaz answered her, "All that you have done for your mother-in-law since the death of your husband has been fully told me, and how you left your father and mother and your native land and came to a people that you did not know before."
5. **[Q] Ruth 2:12** "The LORD recompense you for what you have done, and a full reward be given you by the LORD, the God of Israel, under whose wings you have come to take refuge!""

### Scene 3.3 · Handfuls on purpose
*On screen:* Midday under an awning: the reapers eat bread and roasted grain, and Ruth sits with them. Boaz quietly tells the young men to pull out stalks from their bundles and leave them for her. All afternoon she gleans, and at evening she beats out the grain with a stick: a whole heap of barley. *(Play: gleaning.)*

1. **[Q] Ruth 2:14** "And at mealtime Boaz said to her, "Come here, and eat some bread, and dip your morsel in the wine." So she sat beside the reapers, and he passed to her parched grain; and she ate until she was satisfied, and she had some left over."
2. **[Q] Ruth 2:15** "When she rose to glean, Boaz instructed his young men, saying, "Let her glean even among the sheaves, and do not reproach her."
3. **[Q] Ruth 2:16** "And also pull out some from the bundles for her, and leave it for her to glean, and do not rebuke her.""
4. *(Play: gleaning.)*
5. **[Q] Ruth 2:17** "So she gleaned in the field until evening; then she beat out what she had gleaned, and it was about an ephah of barley."
6. **[Q] Ruth 2:20** "And Naomi said to her daughter-in-law, "Blessed be he by the LORD, whose kindness has not forsaken the living or the dead!" Naomi also said to her, "The man is a relative of ours, one of our nearest kin.""

### Play: gleaning
- **What it is:** herding and gathering (spec §13.7, kind 2; the spec names gleaning).
- **How it plays:** the field behind the reapers. Stalks of barley fall to the ground as they work, and Boaz's young men drop whole handfuls "on purpose." The player gathers them before the evening (tap each stalk, or Space for the next), and Ruth's bundle grows until it fills an ephah.
- **Teaches:** God's law for the poor, that the edges of the field and the fallen stalks were left for the widow and the foreigner (Leviticus 19:9–10, Level 11), and Boaz's generosity, which went beyond the law.
- **Gentle mode:** a tap anywhere gathers the next stalk.
- **Accessibility:** a pointer, or Space.

**Thread:** *"Under whose wings you have come to take refuge" → Jesus: "as a hen gathers her brood under her wings."* The glowing object is the light spreading around Ruth as Boaz blesses her.
- **[Q] Matthew 23:37:** ""O Jerusalem, Jerusalem, killing the prophets and stoning those who are sent to you! How often would I have gathered your children together as a hen gathers her brood under her wings, and you would not!"
- **What it shows:** Boaz blessed Ruth for coming to shelter under the wings of the God of Israel. The Psalms often speak of God's wings, like a mother bird's, sheltering those who trust him. Jesus used the same picture of himself, longing to gather his people under his wings as a hen gathers her chicks. Ruth found that shelter in God, and Boaz's kindness was how God gave it to her.
- **Source:** Matthew 23:37; Psalm 91:4; Psalm 36:7; Catechism 2563.

**Where and when:**
- **Gleaning.** God's law told farmers not to reap to the very edges of their fields, and not to go back for stalks that fell, but to leave them "for the poor and for the sojourner" (Leviticus 19:9–10).
- **A man of wealth.** Boaz was a landowner, and a relative of Naomi's dead husband. He was also the son of Rahab of Jericho (Matthew 1:5; Level 14): he too had a foreign mother.
- **An ephah.** About 20 to 25 liters of grain: enough to feed two women for weeks, gathered in one day.
- **"The LORD be with you."** Boaz's greeting to his workers, and their answer, are still heard at every Mass: "The Lord be with you." "And with your spirit."

**Read:** Ruth 2:1–23.

## Episode 4 · The Threshing Floor

**Ruth 3 · about 7 minutes · 3 scenes**

### Scene 4.1 · Winnowing barley tonight
*On screen:* Evening at the threshing floor, a flat hard place outside the town where the wind blows. Boaz and his workers toss the beaten barley into the air with wooden forks; the chaff blows away and the grain falls in a golden heap. *(Play: winnowing.)*

1. **[Q] Ruth 3:1** "Then Naomi her mother-in-law said to her, "My daughter, should I not seek a home for you, that it may be well with you?"
2. **[Q] Ruth 3:2** "Now is not Boaz our kinsman, with whose maidens you were? See, he is winnowing barley tonight at the threshing floor."
3. *(Play: winnowing.)*
4. **[Q] Ruth 3:5** "And she replied, "All that you say I will do.""

### Scene 4.2 · At midnight
*On screen:* Night. The heap of grain, the workers asleep, Boaz lying at the end of the heap under the stars. Ruth comes quietly and lies down at his feet. At midnight he wakes, startled. Ruth asks him to spread his cloak over her: to marry her, as the next of kin. Boaz stands and blesses her.

1. **[Q] Ruth 3:6** "So she went down to the threshing floor and did just as her mother-in-law had told her."
2. In the custom of that time, Ruth went and lay down at the feet of Boaz as he slept: a way of asking him, humbly and in secret, to take her as his wife.
3. **[Q] Ruth 3:8** "At midnight the man was startled, and turned over, and behold, a woman lay at his feet!"
4. **[Q] Ruth 3:9** "He said, "Who are you?" And she answered, "I am Ruth, your maidservant; spread your skirt over your maidservant, for you are next of kin.""
5. **[Q] Ruth 3:10** "And he said, "May you be blessed by the LORD, my daughter; you have made this last kindness greater than the first, in that you have not gone after young men, whether poor or rich."
6. **[Q] Ruth 3:11** "And now, my daughter, do not fear, I will do for you all that you ask, for all my fellow townsmen know that you are a woman of worth."
7. **[Q] Ruth 3:12** "And now it is true that I am a near kinsman, yet there is a kinsman nearer than I."
8. **[Q] Ruth 3:13** "Remain this night, and in the morning, if he will do the part of the next of kin for you, well; let him do it; but if he is not willing to do the part of the next of kin for you, then, as the LORD lives, I will do the part of the next of kin for you. Lie down until the morning.""

### Scene 4.3 · Not empty-handed
*On screen:* Before dawn, gray light. Boaz pours six big measures of barley into Ruth's shawl, and helps her lift it. She walks up to Bethlehem in the first light, and Naomi opens the door to a woman with her arms full.

1. **[Q] Ruth 3:15** "And he said, "Bring the mantle you are wearing and hold it out." So she held it, and he measured out six measures of barley, and laid it upon her; then she went into the city."
2. **[Q] Ruth 3:16** "And when she came to her mother-in-law, she said, "How did you fare, my daughter?" Then she told her all that the man had done for her,"
3. **[Q] Ruth 3:17** "saying, "These six measures of barley he gave to me, for he said, 'You must not go back empty-handed to your mother-in-law.'""
4. **[Q] Ruth 3:18** "She replied, "Wait, my daughter, until you learn how the matter turns out, for the man will not rest, but will settle the matter today.""

### Play: winnowing
- **What it is:** timing (spec §13.7, kind 16).
- **How it plays:** the threshing floor at evening, with the beaten barley on it. The evening wind comes in gusts from the sea, and the player tosses the grain into the air while the wind blows (tap, or Space): the light chaff flies away and the heavy grain falls back. A toss when the air is still lands in a cloud of dust and chaff, and nothing is lost: just wait for the next gust. The heap of clean barley grows.
- **Teaches:** how grain was cleaned, which Jesus and John the Baptist used as a picture of judgment (Matthew 3:12), and Boaz's hard work: a wealthy man who worked through the night with his workers.
- **Gentle mode:** the wind blows longer, and every toss counts.
- **Accessibility:** a pointer, or Space.

**Thread:** *Naomi went away full and came back empty; now Ruth comes home with her arms full of barley → "from his fulness have we all received, grace upon grace."* The glowing object is the barley in Ruth's shawl.
- **[Q] John 1:16:** "And from his fulness have we all received, grace upon grace."
- **What it shows:** "I went away full, and the LORD has brought me back empty," Naomi had said. But Boaz would not let Ruth go back "empty-handed": he filled her shawl with barley from Bethlehem, the house of bread. Saint John says that God has done the same for us in Jesus, born in that same Bethlehem: "from his fulness have we all received, grace upon grace." Jesus is the true bread from Bethlehem, and no one who comes to him goes home empty.
- **Source:** John 1:14–16; John 6:35; Luke 1:53; Catechism 1406, 2837.

**Where and when:**
- **A threshing floor.** A flat, hard place, often on a hilltop, where grain was beaten and winnowed. The evening breeze from the Mediterranean was the best time to winnow, and the owner slept by the heap to guard it.
- **"Spread your skirt over your maidservant."** The Hebrew word for "skirt," *kanaph*, is the same word as "wings" in Ruth 2:12. Ruth asks Boaz to be the answer to his own blessing.
- **Next of kin.** In Hebrew, *go'el*: a relative with the duty to protect the family, to buy back land that had been sold, and to give a dead man's widow a home and a name.
- **A woman of worth.** The same words describe the "good wife" of Proverbs 31, which comes just before the book of Ruth in the Hebrew Bible.

**Read:** Ruth 3:1–18.

### Scene 5.3 · From Perez to David
*On screen:* A great family tree grows across the sky above Bethlehem, branch by branch, from Perez, son of Judah, down to a shepherd boy with a harp: David. And beyond him, faintly, the branches go on. *(Play: Perez to David.)*

1. **[Q] Ruth 4:18** "Now these are the descendants of Perez: Perez was the father of Hezron,"
2. *(Play: Perez to David.)*
3. **[Q] Ruth 4:21** "Salmon of Boaz, Boaz of Obed,"
4. **[Q] Ruth 4:22** "Obed of Jesse, and Jesse of David."

### Play: Perez to David
- **What it is:** ordering (spec §13.7, kind 9: genealogies).
- **How it plays:** the family tree, with ten empty places from Perez down to David. The player sets the names in order, generation by generation (choose the next name, or the arrow keys and Space): Perez, Hezron, Ram, Amminadab, Nahshon, Salmon, Boaz, Obed, Jesse, David. Each one set grows a new branch. Two names glow as they are placed: Salmon, the husband of Rahab, and Boaz, the husband of Ruth.
- **Teaches:** that this little family story is part of the great story: the line of King David, and of Jesus. Nahshon was the leader of the tribe of Judah in the wilderness (Numbers 1:7).
- **Gentle mode:** the next name glows.
- **Accessibility:** a pointer, or the arrow keys and Space.

**Thread:** *Boaz the kinsman-redeemer buys back what was lost, at his own cost → "…you were ransomed… with the precious blood of Christ…"* The glowing object is the sandal, the sign that Boaz has redeemed.
- **[Q] 1 Peter 1:18:** "You know that you were ransomed from the futile ways inherited from your fathers, not with perishable things such as silver or gold,"
- **[Q] 1 Peter 1:19:** "but with the precious blood of Christ, like that of a lamb without blemish or spot."
- **What it shows:** the nearer kinsman would not risk his own inheritance to save Naomi's family. Boaz did: he paid the price to buy back the land, and took Ruth as his wife, so that the dead would not be forgotten. Christians have always seen in Boaz, the *go'el*, a picture of Jesus, our Redeemer. He became our kinsman by becoming man, and he bought us back, not with silver or gold, but with his own life, to make us his own.
- **Source:** 1 Peter 1:18–19; Job 19:25; Isaiah 54:5; Ephesians 5:25; Catechism 517, 601, 1612.

**Where and when:**
- **The gate.** The open place inside a town's gate was its meeting hall and law court. Business done there before the elders was binding.
- **The sandal.** Taking off a sandal and handing it over sealed the deal: "where my foot stood, now yours stands."
- **Perez and Tamar.** Perez was a son of Judah and Tamar (Genesis 38). Like Rahab and Ruth, Tamar is named in Jesus' family tree.
- **Obed, Jesse and David.** Obed means "servant," as in servant of God. His grandson David, the shepherd boy of Bethlehem, will be anointed king in Level 17.

**Read:** Ruth 4:1–22; Matthew 1:1–6.
