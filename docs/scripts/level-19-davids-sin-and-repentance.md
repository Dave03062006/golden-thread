# Level 19 script: *David's Sin and Repentance*

**Bible:** 2 Samuel 11–24; Psalm 51
**Age:** VI · The Kingdom
**Length:** about 41 minutes (5 episodes, then the quiz, then reflection and prayer)
**Status:** draft for Dave's approval.

## How to read this script

- Each **scene** is a checkpoint. Its numbered lines are the narration beats; the player taps to move from one to the next.
- *On screen* describes what the scene shows. It's the brief for the art in both styles.
- **[Q]** marks an exact quotation from the Bible. Its wording in both translations is in the verse table at the end. Everything else is our own retelling.
- **Play** is the episode's interactive moment. **Thread** is its golden thread. **Read** is the passage the game sends the player to.
- "LORD" in capitals stands for God's holy name, printed in small capitals in the RSV.

### What this level is about

David, the shepherd king, falls. While his army is at war, he takes Bathsheba, the wife of his loyal soldier Uriah, and then has Uriah killed to hide what he has done. But nothing is hidden from God. The prophet Nathan tells David a story about a rich man who stole a poor man's only lamb, and when David is angry with the rich man, Nathan says, "You are the man." David doesn't make excuses: "I have sinned against the LORD." And God forgives him. From his sorrow comes Psalm 51, the great prayer of repentance: "Create in me a clean heart, O God."

Forgiveness doesn't erase every consequence. David's own son Absalom turns against him, and the king flees Jerusalem barefoot and weeping, across the brook Kidron and up the Mount of Olives, where Jesus would one day walk on the night he was betrayed. At the end of his life David sings that "the LORD is my rock," and buys the threshing floor where the Temple will stand, because he will not give God an offering "which cost me nothing."

This is a level about sin and mercy: how hiding sin makes it grow, how confessing it sets us free, and how God's love is bigger than our worst failures. Bathsheba, "the wife of Uriah," is named in Jesus' own family tree.

**How it's shown:** Episodes 1, 3, 4 and 5 begin with a short note. David's sin is told with restraint, focusing on its meaning. The deaths of Uriah, of David's baby son, and of Absalom, and the plague, are each told in a sentence. Nothing violent is shown.

## Episode 1 · Bathsheba and Uriah

**2 Samuel 11 · about 8 minutes · 3 scenes**

> **A note before this episode:** King David does something very wrong: he takes another man's wife, and then has her husband killed. It is told simply, and nothing is shown.

### Scene 1.1 · The time when kings go forth to battle
*On screen:* Spring in Jerusalem. The army marches out of the city gate toward the east, banners flying; David watches from the palace and turns back inside. Late in the afternoon he walks on the palace roof, alone, above the quiet city. Across the rooftops, a lamp is lit in a house.

1. **[Q] 2 Samuel 11:1** "In the spring of the year, the time when kings go forth to battle, David sent Joab, and his servants with him, and all Israel; and they ravaged the Ammonites, and besieged Rabbah. But David remained at Jerusalem."
2. One evening, walking on the palace roof, David saw a very beautiful woman.
3. **[Q] 2 Samuel 11:3** "And David sent and inquired about the woman. And one said, "Is not this Bathsheba, the daughter of Eliam, the wife of Uriah the Hittite?""
4. David wanted her for himself, though she was another man's wife. He sent for her and took her, and soon she sent him word that she was going to have his child.

### Scene 1.2 · Uriah at the king's door
*On screen:* The palace. Uriah, a soldier in dusty armor, stands before the king; David smiles and sends him home with a gift. That night Uriah lies down on a mat at the palace door with the servants, his spear beside him. His own house, just down the street, stays dark.

1. David sent for Uriah from the battlefield, and told him to go home and rest. He hoped that people would think the child was Uriah's.
2. **[Q] 2 Samuel 11:9** "But Uriah slept at the door of the king's house with all the servants of his lord, and did not go down to his house."
3. Uriah said that while the ark of God and his fellow soldiers were camping in the open field, he would not go home to eat and drink and be with his wife: "As you live, and as your soul lives, I will not do this thing."
4. David kept him another day, and made him drunk, but still Uriah slept with the servants at the king's door.

### Scene 1.3 · The letter
*On screen:* Night. David at a table, writing by lamplight; he rolls up a letter and seals it. In the morning, Uriah rides out of the city carrying the sealed letter, not knowing what it says. Then a messenger, then mourning: a woman in dark clothes. Last, the palace at night, every window dark but one lamp. *(Play: nothing is hidden.)*

1. **[Q] 2 Samuel 11:14** "In the morning David wrote a letter to Joab, and sent it by the hand of Uriah."
2. In it he told Joab to put Uriah where the fighting was hardest, and then to leave him there alone. And so Uriah, David's faithful soldier, was killed in battle.
3. When Bathsheba's time of mourning was over, David brought her to the palace, and she became his wife and had a son. David thought no one would ever know.
4. *(Play: nothing is hidden.)*
5. **[Q] 2 Samuel 11:27** "And when the mourning was over, David sent and brought her to his house, and she became his wife, and bore him a son. But the thing that David had done displeased the LORD."

### Play: nothing is hidden
- **What it is:** light and reveal, a quiet searching interaction (spec §13.7, kinds 8 and 11).
- **How it plays:** the palace is dark. The player carries a lamp, and its circle of light moves through the rooms. Hidden in the shadows are the things David tried to cover up: the gift he sent after Uriah, the mat at the door where Uriah slept, the sealed letter, an empty helmet. As the light finds each one, a line of the story appears beside it. When all four are found, the whole palace fills with light.
- **Teaches:** that nothing is hidden from God. Covering up a sin only makes it grow; bringing it into the light is the first step to healing.
- **Gentle mode:** the lamp's circle is wide, and the hidden things glow faintly.
- **Accessibility:** a pointer, or the arrow keys to move the lamp; each find is announced.

**Thread:** *"The thing that David had done displeased the LORD" → "He who does what is true comes to the light."* The glowing object is the lamp in the palace window.
- **[Q] John 3:20:** "For every one who does evil hates the light, and does not come to the light, lest his deeds should be exposed."
- **[Q] John 3:21:** "But he who does what is true comes to the light, that it may be clearly seen that his deeds have been wrought in God."
- **[Q] 1 John 1:9:** "If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just, and will forgive our sins and cleanse us from all unrighteousness."
- **What it shows:** David hid in the dark, and his sin grew from one wrong to a worse one. Jesus said that those who do evil hate the light, but the light is not there to shame us: it is there to heal us. "If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just, and will forgive our sins." The name "Uriah" means "the LORD is my light."
- **Source:** John 3:19–21; 1 John 1:5–10; Proverbs 28:13; Catechism 1455, 1847.

**Where and when:**
- **About 990 BC.** David had been king for about twenty years.
- **Rabbah.** The capital of the Ammonites, east of the Jordan. Today it is Amman, the capital of Jordan.
- **Uriah the Hittite.** A foreigner whose family came from the north, but who served Israel's God: his name means "the LORD is my light." He was one of David's "thirty," his bravest soldiers (2 Samuel 23:39).
- **Joab.** David's nephew and the commander of his army.

**Read:** 2 Samuel 11:1–27.

## Episode 2 · Nathan's Parable

**2 Samuel 12:1–14 · about 8 minutes · 3 scenes**

### Scene 2.1 · Two men in a certain city
*On screen:* David on his throne. The prophet Nathan, an old man with a staff, stands before him and begins a story. As he tells it, the story appears in the air between them like a painted scroll: a rich man's house with flocks covering the hills; a poor man's hut, his children laughing, and a little white ewe lamb drinking from his cup.

1. **[Q] 2 Samuel 12:1** "And the LORD sent Nathan to David. He came to him, and said to him, "There were two men in a certain city, the one rich and the other poor."
2. **[Q] 2 Samuel 12:2** "The rich man had very many flocks and herds;"
3. **[Q] 2 Samuel 12:3** "but the poor man had nothing but one little ewe lamb, which he had bought. And he brought it up, and it grew up with him and with his children; it used to eat of his morsel, and drink from his cup, and lie in his bosom, and it was like a daughter to him."
4. **[Q] 2 Samuel 12:4** "Now there came a traveler to the rich man, and he was unwilling to take one of his own flock or herd to prepare for the wayfarer who had come to him, but he took the poor man's lamb, and prepared it for the man who had come to him.""

### Scene 2.2 · "You are the man"
*On screen:* David leaps up from his throne, red with anger, pointing at the rich man in the story. Nathan turns slowly, and the painted scroll fades: now the rich man's face is David's own. David's pointing hand falls. *(Play: you are the man.)*

1. **[Q] 2 Samuel 12:5** "Then David's anger was greatly kindled against the man; and he said to Nathan, "As the LORD lives, the man who has done this deserves to die;"
2. *(Play: you are the man.)*
3. **[Q] 2 Samuel 12:7** "Nathan said to David, "You are the man. Thus says the LORD, the God of Israel, 'I anointed you king over Israel, and I delivered you out of the hand of Saul;"
4. Nathan reminded David that God had given him everything: the kingdom, a palace, and more besides, if he had asked. And yet he had taken Uriah's wife, and had Uriah killed, and hidden it.

### Scene 2.3 · "I have sinned against the LORD"
*On screen:* David kneels on the floor of the throne room, his crown set down beside him. Nathan lays his hand on David's head. Through the window, morning light comes in.

1. David didn't make excuses, or blame anyone else.
2. **[Q] 2 Samuel 12:13** "David said to Nathan, "I have sinned against the LORD." And Nathan said to David, "The LORD also has put away your sin; you shall not die."
3. God forgave David at once. But Nathan warned him that his sin would still bring sorrow to his family, and the trouble would come from his own house.

### Play: you are the man
- **What it is:** matching (spec §13.7, kind 10).
- **How it plays:** on one side, the people and things of Nathan's story, as painted cards: the rich man with his great flocks, the poor man, the little ewe lamb, and the traveler. On the other side, the real people they stand for: King David, with his palace and his wives; Uriah, the faithful soldier who had only one wife; Bathsheba; and a shadow, David's own wanting. The player matches each card to the person it stands for. The last card, the rich man, turns around in the player's hand, and David sees his own face.
- **Teaches:** that it's easy to see sin in others, and hard to see it in ourselves; and that God sends his word to open our eyes, not to crush us.
- **Gentle mode:** the right match glows.
- **Accessibility:** a pointer, or the arrow keys and Space.

**Thread:** *Nathan's word, "The LORD also has put away your sin" → "If you forgive the sins of any, they are forgiven."* The glowing object is Nathan's hand on David's head.
- **[Q] John 20:22:** "And when he had said this, he breathed on them, and said to them, "Receive the Holy Spirit."
- **[Q] John 20:23:** "If you forgive the sins of any, they are forgiven; if you retain the sins of any, they are retained.""
- **What it shows:** God sent a prophet to bring David to repentance, and to tell him that he was forgiven. On the evening of the first Easter, the risen Jesus breathed on his apostles and gave them the power to forgive sins in his name. In the Sacrament of Confession, we tell our sins to a priest, as David told his to Nathan, and hear the words David heard: your sin is put away.
- **Source:** John 20:19–23; 2 Corinthians 5:18–20; Catechism 1441–1442, 1461, 1468.

**Where and when:**
- **Nathan the prophet.** The same prophet who brought God's promise to David, "your throne shall be established for ever" (Level 18).
- **A ewe lamb.** A young female sheep. Shepherds' families sometimes raised an orphaned lamb at home, like a pet.
- **"Fourfold."** God's law said that a thief who killed a stolen sheep must pay back four sheep (Exodus 22:1). Zacchaeus promised the same when he met Jesus (Luke 19:8).

**Read:** 2 Samuel 12:1–14.

## Episode 3 · "Have Mercy on Me, O God"

**2 Samuel 12:15–25; Psalm 51 · about 8 minutes · 3 scenes**

> **A note before this episode:** David's baby son becomes ill and dies. It is told in a sentence, with David's hope that he will see him again.

### Scene 3.1 · All night upon the ground
*On screen:* Night in the palace. David lies face down on the floor in sackcloth, a candle burning low; the elders of his house stand around him, trying to lift him up. Then morning: David washes, changes his clothes, and walks to the tent of the ark.

1. Bathsheba's baby boy fell very ill.
2. **[Q] 2 Samuel 12:16** "David therefore besought God for the child; and David fasted, and went in and lay all night upon the ground."
3. On the seventh day the child died. Then David got up, washed, and went to worship God.
4. David said, "I shall go to him, but he will not return to me": one day, he would be with his son again.

### Scene 3.2 · Whiter than snow
*On screen:* David alone, kneeling with a lyre in his lap, writing a song. Around him, the room is gray and stained. As the player plays, the stains wash away and snow begins to fall outside the window, covering Jerusalem in white. *(Play: whiter than snow.)*

1. David poured out his sorrow to God in a song. It is Psalm 51, which the Church still prays every Friday morning.
2. **[Q] Psalm 51:1** "Have mercy on me, O God, according to thy steadfast love; according to thy abundant mercy blot out my transgressions."
3. **[Q] Psalm 51:2** "Wash me thoroughly from my iniquity, and cleanse me from my sin!"
4. *(Play: whiter than snow.)*
5. **[Q] Psalm 51:7** "Purge me with hyssop, and I shall be clean; wash me, and I shall be whiter than snow."
6. **[Q] Psalm 51:10** "Create in me a clean heart, O God, and put a new and right spirit within me."
7. **[Q] Psalm 51:17** "The sacrifice acceptable to God is a broken spirit; a broken and contrite heart, O God, thou wilt not despise."

### Scene 3.3 · Beloved of the LORD
*On screen:* Spring again. Bathsheba holds a newborn baby; David bends over them, smiling for the first time in a long time. Nathan comes in, raises the baby, and speaks a name over him.

1. In time, God gave David and Bathsheba another son. They called him Solomon, and the LORD loved him.
2. God sent Nathan with a second name for the baby: Jedidiah, which means "beloved of the LORD." The son of David's sorrow would be the king who built God's Temple.
3. **[Q] Psalm 51:13** "Then I will teach transgressors thy ways, and sinners will return to thee."

### Play: whiter than snow
- **What it is:** pouring and water (spec §13.7, kind 12).
- **How it plays:** David's robe lies across a stone basin, dark with stains. The player dips a branch of hyssop in clean water and sprinkles it over the robe; wherever the drops fall, the stain fades. Each part of the robe made clean shows one line of Psalm 51. When the robe is white, the window opens, and snow falls over the whole city.
- **Teaches:** that we can't wash away sin by ourselves, but God can make us completely clean, "whiter than snow."
- **Gentle mode:** each sprinkle cleans a wider circle.
- **Accessibility:** a pointer, or the arrow keys and Space; the lines of the psalm are read aloud.

**Thread:** *"Create in me a clean heart, O God" → "If any one is in Christ, he is a new creation."* The glowing object is the snow-white robe.
- **[Q] 2 Corinthians 5:17:** "Therefore, if any one is in Christ, he is a new creation; the old has passed away, behold, the new has come."
- **What it shows:** David didn't only ask God to forgive him; he asked for a new heart. God answers that prayer in Jesus. In Baptism, the soul is washed clean and made new, and the newly baptized are dressed in a white garment. And whenever we sin again, Confession makes us clean and new once more.
- **Source:** Psalm 51; Ezekiel 36:25–26; Revelation 7:14; Catechism 1243, 1265, 1428, 1431.

**Where and when:**
- **Psalm 51.** Its title in the Bible says it is "a Psalm of David, when Nathan the prophet came to him."
- **Hyssop.** A small bushy plant. Its branches were used to sprinkle the blood of the Passover lamb on the doorposts (Level 8), and a sponge on hyssop was lifted to Jesus on the cross (John 19:29).
- **The *Miserere*.** Psalm 51 is called the *Miserere* from its first word in Latin, "Have mercy." Allegri's setting was sung only in the Pope's chapel and kept secret, until fourteen-year-old Mozart heard it in 1770 and wrote it out from memory.
- **Solomon.** His name sounds like *shalom*, the Hebrew for "peace." He is the hero of Level 21.

**Read:** 2 Samuel 12:15–25; Psalm 51.

## Episode 4 · Absalom's Rebellion

**2 Samuel 15–18 · about 9 minutes · 3 scenes**

> **A note before this episode:** David's son Absalom rebels against him, and is killed in the battle that follows. It is told in a sentence, and no fighting is shown.

### Scene 4.1 · Absalom at the gate
*On screen:* Early morning at the gate of Jerusalem. Absalom, handsome, with long flowing hair, his chariot and fifty runners behind him, greets everyone who comes with a complaint for the king. He shakes their hands, embraces them and kisses them; one by one, the people turn their backs on the palace.

1. Absalom was David's son, the most handsome man in all Israel. Every morning he stood by the city gate and told people with a complaint that the king would never listen to them, but that if he were judge, he would give them justice.
2. **[Q] 2 Samuel 15:6** "Thus Absalom did to all of Israel who came to the king for judgment; so Absalom stole the hearts of the men of Israel."
3. Then he went to Hebron and had himself proclaimed king.
4. **[Q] 2 Samuel 15:13** "And a messenger came to David, saying, "The hearts of the men of Israel have gone after Absalom.""

### Scene 4.2 · Across the brook Kidron
*On screen:* David's household streams out of the city gate and down into the Kidron valley: servants, soldiers, children, Ittai with his six hundred men, and the priests carrying the ark. David crosses the brook and climbs the Mount of Olives barefoot, his head covered, weeping. At the top he looks back at the city. *(Play: up the Mount of Olives.)*

1. David would not let a battle be fought in the city, so he left Jerusalem with everyone loyal to him.
2. Ittai, a foreign soldier from Gath, could have gone home, but he said, "Wherever my lord the king shall be, whether for death or for life, there also will your servant be."
3. **[Q] 2 Samuel 15:23** "And all the country wept aloud as all the people passed by, and the king crossed the brook Kidron, and all the people passed on toward the wilderness."
4. *(Play: up the Mount of Olives.)*
5. **[Q] 2 Samuel 15:25** "Then the king said to Zadok, "Carry the ark of God back into the city. If I find favor in the eyes of the LORD, he will bring me back and let me see both it and his habitation;"
6. **[Q] 2 Samuel 15:26** "but if he says, 'I have no pleasure in you,' behold, here I am, let him do to me what seems good to him.""
7. **[Q] 2 Samuel 15:30** "But David went up the ascent of the Mount of Olives, weeping as he went, barefoot and with his head covered; and all the people who were with him covered their heads, and they went up, weeping as they went."

### Scene 4.3 · "O my son Absalom"
*On screen:* The gate of Mahanaim, across the Jordan. David stands at the gate as his army marches out. Later, a runner comes from the forest; David climbs the stairs to the room over the gate, and weeps.

1. Absalom's army came after David across the Jordan. Before the battle, David gave his commanders one order:
2. **[Q] 2 Samuel 18:5** "And the king ordered Joab and Abishai and Ittai, "Deal gently for my sake with the young man Absalom." And all the people heard when the king gave orders to all the commanders about Absalom."
3. Absalom's army was defeated. As Absalom fled through the forest on his mule, his head caught in the branches of a great oak, and Joab's men killed him, against the king's command.
4. **[Q] 2 Samuel 18:33** "And the king was deeply moved, and went up to the chamber over the gate, and wept; and as he went, he said, "O my son Absalom, my son, my son Absalom! Would I had died instead of you, O Absalom, my son, my son!""

### Play: up the Mount of Olives
- **What it is:** a courier route (spec §13.7, kinds 6 and 15).
- **How it plays:** the player leads David's weeping household out of the city gate, down into the Kidron valley, across the brook and up the slope of the Mount of Olives. Along the way, at each meeting place, someone stops the king: Ittai, who will not leave him; Zadok and the priests with the ark, whom David sends back to the city; the people weeping at the roadside; and, at the summit, his friend Hushai. Each stop brings a line of the story.
- **Teaches:** that even in the worst of times, David trusted God ("here I am, let him do to me what seems good to him"), and that God gave him faithful friends along the way.
- **Gentle mode:** the path glows ahead of the player.
- **Accessibility:** a pointer, or the arrow keys; each stop is announced.

**Thread:** *David, betrayed, crosses the brook Kidron and goes up the Mount of Olives weeping → Jesus, on the night he was betrayed, crosses the Kidron to the garden.* The glowing object is the brook Kidron.
- **[Q] John 18:1:** "When Jesus had spoken these words, he went forth with his disciples across the Kidron valley, where there was a garden, which he and his disciples entered."
- **[Q] Romans 5:8:** "But God shows his love for us in that while we were yet sinners Christ died for us."
- **What it shows:** a thousand years later, the Son of David walked the same road, across the Kidron to the Mount of Olives, on the night he was betrayed by a friend. David went up weeping for his own sins; Jesus went to pray in agony for ours. And when David cried, "Would I had died instead of you," he wished for something he couldn't do. Jesus could, and did: he died in our place, so that his rebellious children could come home.
- **Source:** John 18:1–2; Luke 22:39–44; Psalm 3 (its title: "A Psalm of David, when he fled from Absalom his son"); Catechism 612, 2606.

**Where and when:**
- **Absalom.** David's third son. The Bible says there was no one in Israel as handsome, "from the sole of his foot to the crown of his head" (2 Samuel 14:25).
- **The brook Kidron.** The valley just east of Jerusalem, between the city and the Mount of Olives. In summer it is dry.
- **The Mount of Olives.** The long hill east of Jerusalem, covered with olive trees. Jesus prayed in the garden of Gethsemane on its slope, and rose into heaven from it (Acts 1:12).
- **Mahanaim.** A town east of the Jordan, where Jacob once saw the angels of God (Level 4).

**Read:** 2 Samuel 15:1–37; 18:1–33.

## Episode 5 · David's Last Words

**2 Samuel 22–24 · about 8 minutes · 3 scenes**

> **A note before this episode:** A plague strikes Israel after David's sin of pride. It is told in a sentence, and nothing is shown.

### Scene 5.1 · The LORD is my rock
*On screen:* David, now old and gray, sits on the rocks of the stronghold of Zion with his lyre. As he sings, the scenes of his life rise around him in the clouds: the sheepfold, the valley of Elah, the cave, the throne, the Mount of Olives. All of them are held in a great hand of light.

1. **[Q] 2 Samuel 22:1** "And David spoke to the LORD the words of this song on the day when the LORD delivered him from the hand of all his enemies, and from the hand of Saul."
2. **[Q] 2 Samuel 22:2** "He said, "The LORD is my rock, and my fortress, and my deliverer,"
3. **[Q] 2 Samuel 22:20** "He brought me forth into a broad place; he delivered me, because he delighted in me."
4. **[Q] 2 Samuel 22:29** "Yea, thou art my lamp, O LORD, and my God lightens my darkness."

### Scene 5.2 · The sweet psalmist of Israel
*On screen:* David's room at sunrise. The old king, in bed, speaks his last words; Nathan and young Solomon listen. Through the window, the sun rises over the hills, and dew sparkles on the grass.

1. **[Q] 2 Samuel 23:1** "Now these are the last words of David: The oracle of David, the son of Jesse, the oracle of the man who was raised on high, the anointed of the God of Jacob, the sweet psalmist of Israel:"
2. **[Q] 2 Samuel 23:3** "The God of Israel has spoken, the Rock of Israel has said to me: When one rules justly over men, ruling in the fear of God,"
3. **[Q] 2 Samuel 23:4** "he dawns on them like the morning light, like the sun shining forth upon a cloudless morning, like rain that makes grass to sprout from the earth."
4. **[Q] 2 Samuel 23:5** "Yea, does not my house stand so with God? For he has made with me an everlasting covenant, ordered in all things and secure. For will he not cause to prosper all my help and my desire?"

### Scene 5.3 · The threshing floor of Araunah
*On screen:* A rocky hilltop north of the city, where Araunah's oxen tread out grain. A shining angel stands above it with a raised hand, then lowers it. David climbs up, pays Araunah in silver, and builds an altar of uncut stones. Fire falls on it. *(Play: what costs me nothing.)*

1. Near the end of his reign, David proudly had all his fighting men counted, as if Israel's strength were in its numbers and not in God. Then his conscience troubled him.
2. **[Q] 2 Samuel 24:10** "But David's heart smote him after he had numbered the people. And David said to the LORD, "I have sinned greatly in what I have done. But now, O LORD, I pray thee, take away the iniquity of thy servant; for I have done very foolishly.""
3. A plague struck Israel. But when the angel reached Jerusalem, God said, "It is enough," and the angel stopped by the threshing floor of Araunah.
4. **[Q] 2 Samuel 24:17** "Then David spoke to the LORD when he saw the angel who was smiting the people, and said, "Lo, I have sinned, and I have done wickedly; but these sheep, what have they done? Let thy hand, I pray thee, be against me and against my father's house.""
5. Araunah offered to give the king his threshing floor, his oxen and the wood for the fire, for nothing.
6. **[Q] 2 Samuel 24:24** "But the king said to Araunah, "No, but I will buy it of you for a price; I will not offer burnt offerings to the LORD my God which cost me nothing." So David bought the threshing floor and the oxen for fifty shekels of silver."
7. *(Play: what costs me nothing.)*
8. **[Q] 2 Samuel 24:25** "And David built there an altar to the LORD, and offered burnt offerings and peace offerings. So the LORD heeded supplications for the land, and the plague was averted from Israel."

### Play: what costs me nothing
- **What it is:** building and stacking (spec §13.7, kind 3).
- **How it plays:** first, Araunah holds out the threshing floor as a gift; the player turns it down and counts fifty silver shekels onto his scales instead. Then the player builds the altar on the bare rock, stone by stone, each stone fitting where it falls. When the last stone is in place, fire falls from heaven, and the angel puts his sword away.
- **Teaches:** that a gift to God means most when it costs us something: our time, our effort, our love.
- **Gentle mode:** the stones snap into place.
- **Accessibility:** a pointer, or the arrow keys and Space.

**Thread:** *"I will not offer burnt offerings to the LORD my God which cost me nothing" → "You were ransomed… with the precious blood of Christ."* The glowing object is the altar on the threshing floor.
- **[Q] 2 Chronicles 3:1:** "Then Solomon began to build the house of the LORD in Jerusalem on Mount Moriah, where the LORD had appeared to David his father, at the place that David had appointed, on the threshing floor of Ornan the Jebusite."
- **[Q] 1 Peter 1:18:** "You know that you were ransomed from the futile ways inherited from your fathers, not with perishable things such as silver or gold,"
- **[Q] 1 Peter 1:19:** "but with the precious blood of Christ, like that of a lamb without blemish or spot."
- **What it shows:** David's threshing floor was on Mount Moriah, where Abraham had once been ready to offer his son Isaac (Level 3), and where Solomon would build the Temple. There, for a thousand years, sacrifices were offered for sin. But the sacrifice that takes away the sins of the world was offered near the same hill, on Calvary, and it cost God everything: his only Son. We were bought "not with perishable things such as silver or gold," but with the blood of the Lamb.

**Where and when:**

- **The threshing floor.** A flat rock on a windy hilltop where grain was beaten and winnowed, like Boaz's (Level 16). Araunah was a Jebusite, one of the people who lived in Jerusalem before David took it.
